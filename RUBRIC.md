# RUBRIC.md

Binding standard for every grade report. Site copy of the rubric lives in `rubric.html`. Keep them consistent when this file changes.

## Purpose

Equip a musical worship leader at Berean Community Church (~1000, Southern Baptist, Irvine) to:

1. Identify songs accurately (title, writers, year written, arrangement year, CCLI) from chord sheets and setlist data.
2. Grade lyrics for theological richness, accuracy, Christ-exalting focus, and congregational usefulness.
3. Keep a catalog of the church’s actual repertoire so sets can serve verse-by-verse preaching.
4. Watch the diet: richness vs thinness, families, era, themes.

Musical and audio analysis is out of scope.

## Principles (Kauflin, *Worship Matters*)

- Lyrics are primary. Music serves the text.
- Songs should help the congregation remember, proclaim, and celebrate God’s worthiness, works, and Word.
- Objective truth about God and the gospel fuels affection.
- Songs should let the word of Christ dwell richly (Col. 3:16): teaching and admonishing one another.
- Prefer God-centered, corporate, clear, theologically substantial language over subjective experience, emotionalism, or vague spirituality.

## Red flags (Avoid, or heavy caution)

- Prosperity gospel or health-and-wealth implications
- Charismatic excess, or language that treats private revelation as weightier than the written Word
- Romanticized “Jesus is my boyfriend” intimacy language
- Minimizing of sin, repentance, or substitutionary atonement
- A heavily man-centered core
- Strong association with Hillsong, Elevation, or Bethel (never used)
- Vague spiritual language a non-Christian could sing without tension

## How to grade this room

Grade for a congregation that wants to emulate the Bereans (Acts 17:11). Do not grade for a seeker-friendly service. “Nothing in this lyric is heretical” is the floor, not a 4 or a 5.

Every song gets a written analysis, not only a score. Quote lyrics. Connect them to Scripture when the link is real. Include setlist notes: sermon placement, pairings, thematic strength.

The church chord sheet is the authority for which text we grade. Web lyrics may draft a report only for a stable, high-confidence setting. The published page waits on confirmation that BCC sings that same text.

First-person vow or desire lyrics are scored under Objectivity and Congregational Usefulness. Psalms model honest thirst. High-claim present-tense vows without gospel grounding, or flattening lament into sweet arrival, cap a song. Songs that need a weekly “only sing if you mean it” disclaimer are usually Weak or Acceptable with notes.

## Five scores (1–5 each)

1. **Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity** (highest weight). Accurate to the whole counsel of Scripture? Precision matters. Impressionistic lines (“darkness tries to hide”) are weaker than language that can be tied tightly to a text. Stacking generic true statements is not the same as unfolding Scripture.
2. **Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated.** Focus on the person *and finished work* of Christ. Titles without the cross, substitution, resurrection, or mediation land at 2–3, not 4–5.
3. **Objectivity vs. Subjectivity.** Does the song declare truths about God and Christ, or is it mostly personal feeling?
4. **Description of God’s Character.** Balanced attributes, or overweight on love and feelings while holiness, justice, and sovereignty drop out? “God is great / good / love” without specificity is thin.
5. **Congregational Usefulness and Clarity.** Clear *and* doctrinally rich. Singability alone does not earn a 4 or 5.

## What the numbers mean

- **5** dense, precise, Scripture-shaped; models what this church wants to sing
- **4** solid and usable; minor gaps, still teaches
- **3** true but thin, vague, or incomplete; seeker-accessible more than Berean-rigorous
- **2** serious omission or imbalance (missing gospel, or mushy poetry in place of doctrine)
- **1** error, man-centered core, or disqualifying confusion

Use the full range. Do not inflate.

## Overall recommendation

- **Excellent / Prefer** gold-standard texts; feature freely (Getty, CityAlight, Sovereign Grace, *In Christ Alone*, *Before the Throne*, *Is He Worthy*, *Yet Not I*, and songs like them)
- **Strong** rich enough to use regularly; small notes only
- **Acceptable with notes** orthodox and usable sparingly when the rest of the set supplies what the song lacks
- **Weak** popular, generically true, low rigor; do not feature. At most a brief filler after Scripture has already done the work. *How Great Is Our God* (Tomlin / Cash / Reeves, CCLI 4348399) is the calibration example of Weak: true fragments, thin gospel, impressionistic light/darkness line, seeker-shaped hook.
- **Avoid** red-flag theology or banned-ministry association

If a song thrives mainly because it is easy, emotional, and broadly theistic, cap it at Weak or low Acceptable.

- Dataset artists are the source of truth for related fields.

- *He Is Worthy* (Nathan Stiff / David Zimmer, SGM, CCLI 7138112) is distinct from *Is He Worthy* (Peterson).
- Create in Me a Clean Heart is CCLI 96927 (Bannister), not Keith Green’s 12996.
- Make Us a Prayer is CCLI 855388 (Rita Springer / Vineyard).
- Go Tell It has no CCLI. Habitation has no CCLI (Sovereign Grace family).

## Prose style

Write like a careful pastor-musician briefing a colleague.

Do not use:

- Em dashes. Use a period, comma, colon, parentheses, or a short new sentence.
- “Not this, but that” as a stylistic tic. State the claim once.
- Stock AI cadence: “At its core,” “It’s important to note,” “In a world where,” “Let’s dive in,” “a tapestry of,” “a journey of,” “unpacks,” “speaks into,” “leans into,” “sits with,” “holds space,” “invites us,” “nothing short of,” “stands as a testament,” “rich tapestry,” “breathes life,” “deeply resonant,” “profoundly,” “robust yet accessible,” “both/and” filler.
- Inflated adjectives that do not add a theological point.
- Parallel slogan pairs written for punch rather than precision.

Do:

- Short, concrete sentences.
- Quote the lyric, say what it does or fails to do, name the Scripture if the connection is real.
- Ordinary church words: sin, cross, substitution, resurrection, congregation, thin, vague, useful, weak.
- Let the grade rest on evidence.
- Scannable reports: headings, scores, then paragraphs a busy leader can skim.

## Workflow for a new title or chart

1. Identify title, writers, year, arrangement, CCLI from the church chart when you have it.
2. Write the full grade against this rubric.
3. Note how it fits (or does not fit) typical sermon themes.
4. Do not publish to the site until Theodore approves.
