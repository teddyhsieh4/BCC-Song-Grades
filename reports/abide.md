## Thesis

The believer’s whole life, from daily bread through death and resurrection, hangs on remaining in Christ the Vine. The song asks Him to teach that remaining.

---

## Overall recommendation

**Acceptable with notes**

Use it sparingly, and keep the last verse. That is the only place the lyric names being raised with Christ. The rest is true dependence language built on John 14–15.

The church has already sung it for at least four years (14 logs in the 2023–2026 snapshot). It should not carry a Sunday by itself. Pair it after a text that has already named sin and the cross. Do not let “Where Your Spirit leads as I’m following” drift into private-impression talk.

## Voice and addressee

Almost every line is “I” speaking to “You.” The addressee is Christ: Way, Truth, Life, well, Vine. The congregation is overhearing a personal prayer of dependence. There is no “we” in the printed text.

---

## How the text moves

Three verse movements, one chorus, then an extended chorus.

1. **Ordinary life.** Waking breath, daily bread, sunrise, sleep. Dependence as creatureliness.
2. **Discipleship.** “Where Your Spirit leads as I’m following,” then “victories still in front of me.”
3. **Death and resurrection.** Passing through death, entering rest, eternal life, raised with Christ.

The chorus stays the same until the later pass adds strength, treasure, prize, and “I am Yours, forever You’re mine.” The argument does move: from bread to the grave. It moves as a list of needs more than as a gospel sequence.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

**Do not drop the last verse.** “When I pass through death / as I enter rest” and “for eternal life / to be raised with Christ” are the only gospel-event lines. Without them the song is John 15 plus providence.

**Watch the Spirit line.** “Where Your Spirit leads as I’m following” can be sung as Galatians 5 and Romans 8. It can also be heard as a private inner voice. Keep it attached to the Word if you speak before the song.

No single line is false enough to sink the song.

## Persons of the Trinity named

The Son is addressed by Johannine titles (Way, Truth, Life, Vine) and named at the resurrection line (“raised with Christ”). The Spirit is named once (“Where Your Spirit leads”). The Father is unnamed.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Much of it, yes. A vague theist can sing dependence for breath, bread, sunrise, and sleep. “You’re the Way, the Truth and the Life” and “You are the vine” and “raised with Christ” are the lines that stop that. If the last verse is dropped, the remaining song is easier to sing without the gospel.

“I am Yours, forever You’re mine” could be sung to almost any cherished object if the titles were swapped.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

**Unbiblical.** Nothing contradicts Scripture when “Spirit leads” is read as the Spirit who leads the sons of God (Romans 8:14) and guides into the truth already given (John 16:13). The line becomes a problem only if the room hears a second authority beside the Word.

**Non-biblical.** “The well that never runs dry” is an image drawn from John 4 and 7, not a new claim. “Song in the night” is Job 35:10 and Psalm 42 color.

**Thin.** The whole middle of the gospel is missing: sin, cross, substitution, wrath. “Victories still in front of me” is true and underfed. It never says what kind of victory.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 4

**Verse 1.** “For my waking breath / for my daily bread / I depend on You” is Job 12:10, Matthew 6:11, and Acts 17:25. Creaturely dependence is real doctrine. “For the sun to rise / for my sleep at night” is Matthew 5:45 and Psalm 4:8. God is the giver of ordinary life.

**Chorus.** “You’re the Way, the Truth and the Life” is John 14:6, quoted. “I’m the branch and You are the vine / draw me close and teach me to abide” is John 15:1–5. Abiding in that chapter is remaining in Christ’s word and love, which bears fruit. The lyric asks to be taught that remaining. It does not explain what abiding costs.

**Verse 2.** “Where Your Spirit leads as I’m following” can sit with Romans 8:14 and Galatians 5:16–18. In this church the Spirit leads by the written Word, not by private flashes. The lyric does not say that. “For the victories still in front of me” is the thinnest doctrinal line. It can mean sanctification, mission, or a vaguely triumphant year. The text does not decide.

**Last verse.** “When I pass through death / as I enter rest” is Psalm 23:4 and Hebrews 4:9–11. “To be raised with Christ” is Romans 6:5, Colossians 3:1, and 1 Thessalonians 4:16. That is the one line that ties dependence to union with the risen Christ.

The accuracy score is a 4 because John 14–15 and providence are handled in their ordinary sense. It is not a 5 because verse 2 leaves the Spirit line and the “victories” line under-specified.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 3

Christ is named by His own titles and as the Vine. The last verse names resurrection with Him. The category is still a 3.

The rubric is plain. Titles without the finished work stay in the 2–3 range. There is no cross, no “my sin,” no substitution, no Lamb. “Raised with Christ” assumes a death. It does not preach it. A congregation that already heard the gospel that morning can use the song as a prayer of remaining. The lyric itself will not teach the gospel to anyone who missed it.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 3

The subject of almost every line is the singer’s need. “I depend on You” is the claim the song keeps repeating. Desire language fills the later chorus: strength, song, treasure, prize, “You’re mine.”

Psalms do this. Psalm 63 is thirst. Here the thirst is only lightly attached to named works of God. We hear what the singer needs. We hear almost nothing of what Christ has done, except the last verse. First-person dependence this complete, without a gospel stanza, is why the category is a 3.

“I am Yours, forever You’re mine” is covenant speech (Song of Songs language has been used this way; also Isaiah 43:1). In a room that already knows the Vine, it reads as belonging. Isolated, it leans intimate.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 3

Named: Way, Truth, Life, inexhaustible well, Vine, strength, treasure, the Christ who raises the dead. Those are real titles.

Unnamed: holiness, justice, wrath, mercy at the cross, Fatherhood. “Good” and “love” are not even stacked. The thinness is different. The song names Christ as source and stays there. That is specific enough to avoid “God is great” fog, and still too narrow for a 4.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 4

The words are easy to sing and already known (at least four years in the snapshot). Keys on file include G and F#. That is a band choice.

The score is a 4 because the voice is solo-shaped. A thousand people can sing “I depend on You” together. They are still singing a private prayer in unison. The last verse is the stanza most worth keeping and the stanza easiest to treat as optional when time is short. Do not treat it as optional.

A weekly “only sing if you mean it” speech should not be needed. A one-sentence reminder that abiding in John 15 means remaining in Christ’s word would help verse 2.

---

## Tone

Gentle, earnest, slightly intimate in the later chorus. Not flippant, not sensual in the boyfriend sense. The risk is ungrounded softness: a room enjoying dependence language without being asked to confess sin.

“Teach me to abide” is a right request. It should not be sung as if closeness itself were the gospel.

---

## What the congregation never says

They never confess sin.  
They never name the cross, the blood, or substitution.  
They never name the Father.  
They never name repentance.  
They never name justification.

If this is the only sung prayer before the sermon, the hour has not yet put the gospel in the people’s mouth.

---

## Setlist notes

**When it serves the sermon.** John 15. John 14:6. Psalm 23. Matthew 6 on daily bread. A closing prayer after the sermon has already done the work of sin and atonement.

**Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** Sin, the cross, substitution. Almost everything in the gospel inventory except resurrection-with-Christ.

**What it adds to the year’s diet.** Creaturely dependence and John 15 abide language. It never adds a passion stanza or a confession of guilt.

**What not to do.** Do not open the service with it and then skip the last verse. Do not treat “Spirit leads” as a cue for unmediated guidance talk.
