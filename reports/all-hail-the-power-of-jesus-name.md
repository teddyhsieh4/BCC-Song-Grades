## Thesis

Angels, the ransomed, and every tribe are called to crown Jesus Lord of all. He saves by grace, he is God incarnate and man divine, and sinners who remember the gall are told to lay their trophies at his feet.

---

## Overall recommendation

Excellent / Prefer

Feature it. The church has logged it 3 times across at least three years (2023, 2025, 2026). "Crown Him Lord of all" has to stay, and so does "ye ransomed from the fall, hail Him who saves you by His grace." Many hymnals print only four stanzas and drop "the God incarnate, Man divine" and "the wormwood and the gall." If the chart has those stanzas, sing them. The refrain already tells the room who is Lord.

## Voice and addressee

The hymn calls groups to act: angels, martyrs, the seed of Israel, heirs of David's line, sinners, every tribe. The last stanza turns to "we" at his feet. Jesus is spoken about, and the command is to crown him. The lines give orders for a coronation.

---

## How the text moves

It opens on the name and the royal diadem. Later stanzas call the ransomed, name the incarnate God, set the gall before sinners, and then gather every tribe into one song. The line that returns is "crown Him Lord of all." A short hymnal printing keeps the angels, the ransom, the tribes, and the everlasting song, and leaves the incarnation and the gall on the page unused.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

"Ye seed of Israel's chosen race, ye ransomed from the fall, hail Him who saves you by His grace" is the stanza the gospel in this hymn depends on, with "The God incarnate, Man divine" and "the wormwood and the gall" when the chart includes them. "Crown Him Lord of all" is the line the room will remember. If the band keeps only the angels and the diadem, the hymn is a coronation with the ransom left out. The full CCLI text is not false in any line. A four-stanza cut can hide the incarnation and the gall.

## Persons of the Trinity named

Jesus is named, and he is called Lord of all, the Stem of Jesse's rod, the God incarnate, and man divine. "Your God" is the martyrs' God. The Father and the Spirit are unnamed. The coronation is of the Son.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. "Crown Him Lord of all," "ye ransomed from the fall," and "The God incarnate, Man divine" cannot be moved to another lord. A worshiper who wants a generic king can try the angels and the diadem by themselves. Those lines are the opening. The ransom stanza stops the swap.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** "Let highborn seraphs tune the lyre." The angels' worship is biblical. The lyre is the hymn's picture of their song.
- **Thin:** none in the long text. A printing that keeps only "Bring forth the royal diadem" has less to say. The short printing is a cut of the CCLI text.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

"Crown Him Lord of all" agrees with Philippians 2:9-11, the name above every name and every knee bowing. "Ye ransomed from the fall" agrees with the ransom of Revelation 5:9 and with salvation from Adam's fall in Romans 5. "Hail Him who saves you by His grace" is that rescue named as grace. "Extol the Stem of Jesse's Rod" is Isaiah 11:1. "The God incarnate, Man divine" says the two natures in a line. "The wormwood and the gall" is the bitterness of the passion. Matthew 27:34 says they offered Jesus wine mixed with gall. "Let every tribe and every tongue" agrees with Revelation 7:9. The hymn stacks those claims as a coronation. They can be tied to texts.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

Jesus is the one crowned Lord of all. He ransoms from the fall and saves by grace. He is God incarnate and man divine, the heir of David, the Stem of Jesse. Sinners are pointed to the gall. The word cross is absent, and the resurrection is absent. The ransom, the incarnation, and the passion are in the stanzas. That is the finished work sung as a coronation.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 5

The hymn declares who Jesus is and commands the creation to crown him. "Ye ransomed," "the God incarnate," and "every tribe" are outside the singer's mood. The closing "we at His feet may fall" is the church joining a song that is already about him. No line is a private feeling offered as the doctrine.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 4

He is Lord of all, worthy of the diadem, the strength of Israel, the Stem of Jesse, God incarnate and man divine, the Savior by grace, and the one who ransoms from the fall. Kingship and saving grace are the portrait. Holiness, wrath, and faithfulness are unnamed. The gall implies a real suffering. The attributes that are named are specific.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

Three logs across at least three years means the room has sung it and does not live on it. The refrain is plain: crown him Lord of all. A congregation can learn the name, the ransom, the incarnation, and the passion if those stanzas are sung. "Diadem" may need a word of explanation once. The lesson is the coronation, and the refrain will not teach the ransom if the ransom stanza is dropped.

---

## Tone

The tone is formal and glad. The line that sets it is "All hail the power of Jesus' Name! Let angels prostrate fall." The gall stanza is graver than the angels. Nothing in the hymn is playful. The repeated coronation is the weight.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never says the word cross, never says that Jesus rose, and never names the Spirit.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Philippians 2, Revelation 5, or the kingship of Christ, with the ransom stanza sung.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The resurrection, if the morning is Easter or the empty tomb. Pair it with *In Christ Alone* (logged 8 times) when the cross has to be named in plain words.
- **Diet sentence.** Three times in at least three years, it adds a coronation of Jesus as Lord of all, a ransom from the fall, and, in the long text, the incarnate God and the gall.
- **What not to do.** Do not sing only the angels and the diadem. Do not drop "ransomed from the fall."

## Identification notes

One hymn. CCLI 25400 matches the catalog and the Word to Worship printing of the long text. The catalog names Edward Perronet and Oliver Holden and leaves John Rippon off the writer line. Perronet wrote the 1779 text. Holden wrote CORONATION in 1793. Rippon revised the hymn in 1787 and supplied stanzas that many books now sing, including the closing "Oh, that with yonder sacred throng." A four-stanza hymnal often keeps the angels, the ransom, the tribes, and that closing, and omits the incarnation and the gall. This grade uses the longer CCLI text and says so where a short cut would change what the room hears. Setlist keys include E. No arrangement year is on file.

## Why this overall label

The hymn calls angels and sinners to crown Jesus Lord of all, and it says he ransoms from the fall, saves by grace, and is God incarnate, with the gall set before the church. The resurrection is for another song in the hour. With the ransom stanza sung, this is a text to feature.
