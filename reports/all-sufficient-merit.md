## Thesis

The singer inherits a merit earned by no work of the singer's own. Righteousness was forfeited at the Savior's cross, where that merit did what the singer could not. The law could not save. The spotless Lamb came, the debt is paid, and the singer stands accepted in Jesus' righteousness.

---

## Overall recommendation

Excellent / Prefer

Feature it. The church has logged it 5 times, all in 2026 in this window. "My righteousness I forfeit at my Savior's cross / Where all sufficient merit did what I could not" and "Paid in full, all sufficient merit now my own" have to stay. The song already says the exchange. It does not need another song to explain imputation, though the resurrection is still worth singing in the same hour.

## Voice and addressee

First person, speaking about the Savior and then to the finished work: "no more debt I owe." "My Savior's cross," "my Lord Jesus," "I'll gaze upon my Jesus." Verse 2 shifts to "us" and "our lawlessness." The church would be singing one person's inheritance of a merit that the verses have already said belongs to the Lamb's work.

---

## How the text moves

It starts with merit the singer did not earn, forfeited righteousness, and the cross that did what the singer could not. The next stanza is the incarnation and the spotless life, the law's failure, and the Lamb. The chorus says the debt is finished and the merit is now the singer's. A later stanza lays down empty boasts, calls good works corrupted, and dresses the singer in Jesus' righteousness, with no fear of judgment. The close is acceptance before the throne and thanks for the cross. The chorus does not add a new doctrine. It gives the singer the merit the verses have described.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

"It is done, it is finished, no more debt I owe / Paid in full, all sufficient merit now my own" is the line the grade depends on, with "Where all sufficient merit did what I could not" and "I'm dressed in my Lord Jesus / His righteousness is mine." "In love He condescended, eternal now in time" belongs with them. If the band keeps "shining like the sun" and drops the cross, the debt, and the robe, the song is a bright word about merit with the cross removed. No line is false. The cross and the robe are the lines a chorus-only use might rush past, and the chorus itself still says the debt is paid.

## Persons of the Trinity named

Jesus is named as Savior and as the spotless Lamb, the Maker who was made to die. "The throne of God" names God as the one before whom the singer stands accepted. The Father and the Spirit are not given those titles. The merit is the Son's.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. "My Savior's cross," "the pure and spotless Lamb," and "dressed in my Lord Jesus" cannot be moved to another savior. "All sufficient merit shining like the sun," sung alone, could be a vague boast. The cross and the robe stop that reading.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** "Shining like the sun" as a picture of merit. The brightness is the song's image. The merit is then tied to the cross.
- **Thin:** none. "A fortune I inherit" is a picture, and the next lines say the fortune is a merit the singer did not work for.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

"By no work I have done" and "The law could never save us" agree with Galatians 2:16, justification not by works of the law. "Did what I could not" and "His righteousness is mine" agree with 2 Corinthians 5:21, the exchange of sin and righteousness. "It is done, it is finished" agrees with John 19:30. "No more debt I owe / Paid in full" agrees with Colossians 2:14, the record of debt cancelled. "Eternal now in time" and "A life without a blemish" say the incarnation and the spotless life. "A crimson robe made white" agrees with Isaiah 1:18. "No more fear of judgment" and "stand accepted before the throne of God" agree with Romans 8:1 and with approach to God in Hebrews 4:16. The lines can be tied to those texts. The song says them in order: no works, the cross, the Lamb, the paid debt, the robe.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

The Savior's cross is where human righteousness is forfeited and Christ's merit does what the singer could not. Christ condescends, lives without blemish, dies as the Maker, and is the Lamb the law could not replace. The singer is dressed in Christ, owes no debt, and thanks Christ for the cross before the throne. The resurrection is not named. The active obedience, the death, the imputation, and the acceptance are. That is a saturated gospel.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

"Did what I could not," "the law could never save us," and "His righteousness is mine" declare what Christ has done. "A fortune I inherit," "I lay down my garments," and "I'll gaze upon my Jesus" put the singer in the place of heir. The score stays at 4 because the inheritance is told in the singer's voice from the first line.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 5

He is the Maker, eternal, condescending in love, spotless, the Lamb, the one whose merit pays a real debt, and the one before whose throne a sinner can stand accepted without fear of judgment. Justice is the debt and the judgment. Mercy is the love that condescended and the robe. Holiness is the life without blemish. The portrait holds the payment and the welcome together.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

Five logs in a single year of this window means the room has started to know it. The chorus is memorable and it teaches the point: the debt is paid and the merit is Christ's, now counted to the singer. A congregation can learn imputation, the failure of the law, and the spotless life from the verses. "Sinful host" and "empty boast" are plain enough. The song is worth featuring while it is still new here.

---

## Tone

The tone is settled and glad. The line that sets it is "It is done, it is finished, no more debt I owe." "No more fear of judgment" is the relief under that gladness. "Shining like the sun" is brighter than the law stanza. The thanks at the throne is quieter than the chorus.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never says that Jesus rose, and never names the Spirit.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Galatians 2, 2 Corinthians 5:21, or justification. The cross lines are already in the song.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The resurrection. Pair it with *Christ Our Hope in Life and Death* (logged 8 times) or *Yet Not I but Through Christ in Me* (logged 10 times) when the empty tomb has to be sung.
- **Diet sentence.** Five times in 2026, it adds a plain account of merit the singer did not earn, a cancelled debt, and an imputed righteousness. It never adds the resurrection.
- **What not to do.** Do not loop "shining like the sun" and drop "did what I could not." Do not treat "now my own" as a self-made righteousness. The line says the merit was paid in full by another.
