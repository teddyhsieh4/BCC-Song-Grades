## Thesis

God pursued and chose the singer before the world existed, redeems the cold heart, and works all things for His glory and the singer’s good. He defends in the wilderness and will keep until the end.

---

## Overall recommendation

Strong

Use it regularly when the sermon needs the congregation to rest in God’s electing love and Romans 8:28 providence. The first verse is the one that must stay: the cold heart, the pursuit, the choice before the world, and the redeeming love. The chorus is true but thin without that verse. Pair it with a song that names the cross if the service has not already done so.

## Voice and addressee

First-person singular speaking to the Lord. It is confession of what God has done and is doing, not a report of present feelings as the main claim.

---

## How the text moves

It starts with the past: cold heart, blindness, pursuit, election, redeeming love. The chorus confesses present and future working of all things for glory and good. The second verse faces present enemies and wilderness. The third verse looks to the day of power or the final hour, with God as Author and faithful keeper. The claim moves from election to providence to perseverance.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“When my heart was cold and lifeless / And I wandered in my blindness / You pursued me / And before the world was breathing / You had chosen to redeem me / For You loved me, Lord, You love me.”

Without that verse the chorus is a true but generic statement about providence. The election and pursuit lines are what give the song its doctrinal weight for this church.

## Persons of the Trinity named

The Lord is addressed. Father, Son, and Spirit are not distinguished. “Author of my story” and “chosen to redeem” stay within the one God of Romans 8 and Ephesians 1.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

A theist who believes in a purposeful god could sing the chorus. The first verse requires the God who pursues the blind and chooses to redeem before the foundation of the world. That keeps the whole song from being usable by a vague theist, though the chorus alone is thinner.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** none.
- **Thin:** the finished work. Election, pursuit, and providence are present and true. The lyric never says how the redemption was accomplished or names the cross.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 4

“Before the world was breathing / You had chosen to redeem me” is the ordinary sense of Ephesians 1:4 and Romans 8:29–30. “Working all things / All things for Your glory and my good” is Romans 8:28. “You will keep me” is Philippians 1:6 and 1 Thessalonians 5:23–24. “A thousand fall beside me” is Psalm 91:7. The lines stay inside the texts. The score is 4 rather than 5 because the gospel mechanism is left out.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 3

Christ is not named. The cross is not named. “Chosen to redeem” and “You pursued me” point to the electing love of God, but the lyric does not preach the finished work. That keeps the score at 3.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

The lyric declares what God has done (pursued, chosen, works all things, defends, keeps). “I feel the weight of darkness” names a feeling, but the answer is the objective “You are with me.” The vows of praise are grounded in the Author who is faithful. The score is 4 because the voice stays singular and some lines report the singer’s condition.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 4

God is the one who pursues, chooses, redeems, loves, works all things, accomplishes what He promises, defends, is with the singer, comes in power, keeps, authors the story, and is faithful and for the singer. The attributes are specific and tied to election and providence rather than generic praise. Holiness and justice are not in view.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 4

The language is plain. The congregation can confess electing love and Romans 8:28 providence together. The singular voice and the missing cross are the limits. Singability is high, and the teaching is real, so the score is 4.

---

## Tone

Reverent and steady. It does not ask the room to confer on God anything He does not already possess.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never says the name of Jesus, the cross, substitution, or the resurrection. Those confessions have to come from the rest of the service.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Romans 8, Ephesians 1, or any text on election, providence, or perseverance. Useful after a sermon that has already named the cross and now needs the congregation to rest in the God who works all things.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The name of Jesus, the cross, and the resurrection.
- **Diet sentence.** It adds a clear confession of electing love and Romans 8:28 providence. It never adds the finished work of Christ.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop the first verse. Do not let the chorus stand alone as a soft providence tag.

---

## Identification notes

One song. Distinct from the older Mark Altrogge “All Things” (CCLI 2898645). This is the 2024 Nathan Stiff / David Zimmer setting. CCLI 7236940 stands. Variants are key differences only.

---

## Why this overall label

The lyric names election before the world, pursuit of the cold heart, and the working of all things for glory and good. That is enough for regular use. The absence of the cross and the name of Jesus keeps it at Strong rather than Excellent / Prefer. Keep the first verse and supply the finished work elsewhere.
