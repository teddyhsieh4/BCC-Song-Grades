## Thesis

God pursued and chose the singer before the world existed, redeems the cold heart, and works all things for His glory and the singer’s good. He defends in the wilderness and will keep until the end.

---

## Overall recommendation

Strong

Use it regularly when the sermon calls the congregation to rest in God’s electing love and the providence of Romans 8:28. Keep the first verse, which names the cold heart, God’s pursuit, and His choice to redeem before the world began. Without it the chorus is true but thin. When the service has not named the cross, pair it with a song that does. The church has sung it 10 times across at least three years of the 2023–2026 window.

## Voice and addressee

First-person singular, addressed to the Lord. It confesses what God has done and is doing, and the singer’s feelings are never the main claim.

---

## How the text moves

The first verse looks back to election and God’s pursuit of a blind and lifeless heart, and the chorus confesses that He works all things for His glory and the singer’s good. The second verse faces present enemies, and the third looks to the end, with God as the faithful Author who keeps His own. The song moves from election to providence to perseverance.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“When my heart was cold and lifeless / And I wandered in my blindness / You pursued me / And before the world was breathing / You had chosen to redeem me / For You loved me, Lord, You love me.”

Without this verse the chorus is a general statement about providence. Election and pursuit are what give the song its doctrinal weight for this church.

## Persons of the Trinity named

The Lord is addressed, but Father, Son, and Spirit are not named separately. “Author of my story” and “chosen to redeem” use the language of Romans 8 and Ephesians 1.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Partly. Anyone who believes in a purposeful god could sing the chorus. The first verse, with a God who pursues the blind and chose to redeem before the world began, is what a vague theist would have to change.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** none.
- **Thin:** the finished work. Election, pursuit, and providence are present and true. The lyric never says how the redemption was accomplished or names the cross.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 4

“Before the world was breathing / You had chosen to redeem me” is Ephesians 1:4 and Romans 8:29–30. “Working all things / All things for Your glory and my good” is Romans 8:28. “You will keep me” is Philippians 1:6 and 1 Thessalonians 5:23–24. “A thousand fall beside me” is Psalm 91:7. The score is 4 and not 5 because the song never says how the redemption was accomplished.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 3

Christ and the cross are not named. “Chosen to redeem” and “You pursued me” point to God’s electing love. The lyric stops short of the finished work, so the score is 3.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

The lyric declares what God has done: pursued, chosen, works all things, defends, keeps. “I feel the weight of darkness” names a feeling, and the answer is the objective “You are with me.” The score is 4 because the voice stays singular and some lines describe the singer’s condition.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 4

God pursues, chooses, redeems, and loves. He works all things, keeps His promises, defends, stays near, comes in power, and authors the story to its end. Holiness and justice are not in view, which is why this is a 4.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 4

The words are plain, and a congregation that sings it learns electing love and Romans 8:28 providence together. The singular voice and the missing cross hold it at 4.

---

## Tone

Reverent and steady, the quiet confidence of someone who knows who is writing the story.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never says the name of Jesus, the cross, substitution, or the resurrection. Pair it with It Was Finished Upon That Cross when the service needs them.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Romans 8, Ephesians 1, or any text on election, providence, or perseverance. It works well after a sermon that has already named the cross.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The name of Jesus, the cross, and the resurrection.
- **Diet sentence.** Adds a clear confession of electing love and Romans 8:28 providence. It does not add the finished work of Christ.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop the first verse and sing only the chorus.

---

## Identification notes

One song. Distinct from the older Mark Altrogge “All Things” (CCLI 2898645). This is the 2024 Nathan Stiff / David Zimmer setting. CCLI 7236940 stands. Variants are key differences only.

---

## Why this overall label

The lyric names election before the world, God’s pursuit of a cold heart, and His working of all things for His glory and our good. That is enough for regular use. It is Strong and not Excellent / Prefer because neither the cross nor the name of Jesus appears.
