## Thesis

Amazing grace saved a wretch, once lost and now found, once blind and now seeing. Grace taught fear and then relieved it. The chorus says the chains are gone because God the Savior has ransomed the singer, and mercy reigns. The Lord is the singer's shield and portion, and God who called the singer here below will be the singer's forever.

---

## Overall recommendation

Strong

Use it regularly when the service is on grace, and keep the chorus. The church has logged this setting once, in 2025. "My God, my Savior has ransomed me" is the line the Tomlin text adds to Newton, and it has to stay. Newton's stanzas never say the cross. The ransom word is as close as this lyric comes. Pair it with a song that tells the death if the sermon is the atonement.

## Voice and addressee

First person testimony. "Saved a wretch like me," "my chains," "my Savior," "the Lord has promised good to me." The last tag turns to "You are forever mine." God is addressed only at that tag. The rest tells the church what grace has done for the singer.

---

## How the text moves

It opens with Newton's sweet sound, the wretch, the lost and found, the blind and seeing. The next stanza is grace that taught fear and then relieved it, at the hour of believing. The chorus answers with freedom: chains gone, a ransom, mercy that reigns. A later Newton stanza calls the Lord the singer's shield and portion for as long as life endures. The closing stanza sets the earth's dissolving against "God, who called me here below, will be forever mine," and the tag repeats that possession as "You are forever mine." The chorus is the new claim. Newton still carries the wretch and the call.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

"My chains are gone, I've been set free / My God, my Savior has ransomed me" is the line this setting adds, and the grade of the chorus depends on it, with Newton's "That saved a wretch like me." "The Lord has promised good to me / His word my hope secures" belongs with them. If the band keeps "unending love, amazing grace" and drops the wretch and the ransom, the chorus is a feeling of freedom. No line is false. The ransom is the line a long tag can bury under "forever mine."

## Persons of the Trinity named

God and the Lord are named, and the Savior is "my God, my Savior." The Father and the Spirit are unnamed. Jesus' name is not used. The ransom is the Savior's.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

The Newton stanzas can be sung by someone who means a vague mercy, which is an old risk of this hymn when it is torn from the gospel. "My God, my Savior has ransomed me" asks for a Savior who paid a ransom. It still does not say the cross. A worshiper who will not have a ransoming Savior has to stop at the chorus.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** "And like a flood His mercy reigns." A flood is the song's measure of mercy. The reigning is the claim.
- **Thin:** "Unending love, amazing grace" at the end of the chorus, after the ransom has already been stated. "You are forever mine" repeats Newton's "will be forever mine" as a direct address. The possession is the singer's, and the stanza has already said God called the singer.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

"Amazing grace, how sweet the sound / That saved a wretch like me / I once was lost, but now I'm found / Was blind, but now I see" is Newton's stanza. Lost and found agree with Luke 15:24. "Twas grace that taught my heart to fear / And grace my fears relieved" agrees with the fear of the Lord and with grace that answers it. "My God, my Savior has ransomed me" agrees with Mark 10:45, the Son of Man giving his life as a ransom. "The Lord has promised good to me / His word my hope secures / He will my shield and portion be" is Newton's confidence in the promise, the word, and the portion. "The earth shall soon dissolve like snow" stands near 2 Peter 3:10, the heavens passing away and the earth exposed. "God, who called me here below" agrees with the call of grace. The chorus adds a ransom to stanzas that were already Scripture-shaped. The lines can be tied to those texts.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 4

A wretch is saved. A Savior ransoms. Mercy reigns, and the singer is set free. The name Jesus is absent, and the cross, the blood, and the resurrection are absent. The ransom is the finished work in one line. Newton's hymn has always left the death for the preacher or for another text. This chorus names the ransom and still does not tell it. The score is 4 because the Savior and the ransom are explicit and the cross is not.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

"Saved a wretch like me," "has ransomed me," and "His word my hope secures" declare grace, a ransom, and a promise. "My chains are gone" and "the hour I first believed" are the singer's story. "You are forever mine" is the singer holding the God who called. The testimony is Newton's, and it is about what grace did. The tag is the most personal line in the setting.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 4

Grace saves a wretch, teaches fear, and relieves it. The Savior ransoms. Mercy reigns. The Lord promises good, secures hope by his word, and is shield and portion. He calls, and he remains when the earth dissolves. Love is "unending love" after the ransom. Holiness and wrath are unnamed, though the fear grace taught is the fear of God. The portrait is grace, ransom, promise, and keeping.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 4

One log, in 2025, means this setting is rare here even if Newton is familiar. The chorus is easy and it teaches a ransom if that line is sung. A congregation can learn that grace saves a wretch, that the Savior ransomed, and that God's word secures hope. "Forever mine" will teach belonging. It will not teach the cross. The words are plain. The ransom line is the one this arrangement adds, and it is the one to keep audible.

---

## Tone

The tone is grateful and steady. The line that sets it is "Amazing grace, how sweet the sound / That saved a wretch like me." The chorus is freer: "My chains are gone." "The earth shall soon dissolve like snow" is the sober line under the tag. The sweetness is Newton's, and the wretch keeps it from being only sweet.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never says the name Jesus, never says the word cross, and never says that the ransom was a death.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Ephesians 2 or the grace that saves, with the chorus sung. It does not carry a sermon on the death by itself.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The cross. Pair it with *Hallelujah, What a Savior* (logged 6 times) or *And Can It Be* when that hymn is in the same service.
- **Diet sentence.** Once in this window, the setting adds Newton's wretch and a chorus in which the Savior ransoms. It never adds the cross.
- **What not to do.** Do not sing this as the older Amazing Grace row and drop the chorus, or drop the chorus's ransom and keep only "unending love." Do not loop "You are forever mine" until it replaces the wretch.

## Why this overall label

Newton's stanzas tell a wretch saved by grace, and the chorus adds that the Savior has ransomed the singer and the chains are gone. The cross and the name Jesus are for another song in the hour. With the ransom line sung, the church can use this setting regularly.
