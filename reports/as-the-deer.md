## Thesis

The singer’s soul thirsts for God the way a deer thirsts for water. God alone is the heart’s desire, strength, and joy.

---

## Overall recommendation

Weak

This is a true fragment of Psalm 42:1 set to a memorable chorus. The congregation can sing the opening lines without stretching the text. The rest of the lyric stays inside personal desire and does not name sin, the cross, or the finished work. Use it sparingly, if at all, after a sermon or reading has already put the congregation in the text. Do not feature it.

## Voice and addressee

First person singular. The singer addresses God directly (“Thee,” “You alone”). The lyric reports the singer’s longing more than it declares what God has done.

---

## How the text moves

It opens with the deer image from Psalm 42:1. The chorus repeats the claim that God alone is strength, shield, and heart’s desire. Later stanzas add “friend and brother,” “more than gold or silver,” and “the apple of my eye.” The refrain never changes the claim. It stays a personal vow of desire.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

The first verse is the only line that can be tied to a text in its ordinary sense: “As the deer panteth for the water / So my soul longeth after Thee.” Drop that and the song is pure desire language with no Scripture anchor. The “friend and brother” stanza is the one most likely to be dropped in practice, and dropping it does not change the grade.

## Persons of the Trinity named

None named. The “You” is addressed as King and friend. No Father, Son, or Spirit is distinguished.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

A vague theist or moralist could sing the whole song without tension. “You alone are my heart’s desire” and “Only You can satisfy” work for any object the singer has already decided is ultimate. The deer image is biblical, but the rest of the lyric does not force the singer to the God of the Bible.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** “the apple of my eye,” “the real joy giver.” These are affectionate phrases that cannot be checked by a text.
- **Thin:** the whole song. The Psalm 42 line is true. The rest stacks desire language without the surrounding lament or the gospel that answers it.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 3

“As the deer panteth for the water / So my soul longeth after Thee” is Psalm 42:1 in ordinary English. The psalm continues into tears, the taunt “Where is your God?,” and the hope of seeing the face of God. The song does not carry any of that. “You alone are my strength, my shield” echoes several psalms, but the line stands alone. The lyric is accurate where it quotes and thin everywhere else.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 2

Christ is not named. “You are a King” and “You are my brother” are titles a Christian can apply to the Son, but the song never says why. No cross, no substitution, no resurrection. Naming the King without the finished work stays in the 2–3 range.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 2

The lyric is almost entirely the singer’s inner state. “My soul longeth,” “I long to worship,” “I want You more than gold,” “I love You more than any other.” Psalm 42 models honest thirst. This setting flattens the thirst into a present-tense vow of desire without the gospel grounding the psalm expects. Songs that need a weekly “only sing if you mean it” disclaimer are usually Weak.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 3

Strength, shield, King, joy giver, the one who satisfies. These are true. They are not developed. Holiness, justice, sovereignty, and the character of God that produces the thirst are absent. “You alone” is repeated, but the “You” stays generic.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 3

The first verse is clear and the congregation already knows the chorus. The song does not teach. It reports desire. A seeker-friendly room grades this higher. This church does not. Use it only when the reading or sermon has already put Psalm 42 in the people’s mouths.

---

## Tone

Gentle, affectionate, intimate. The “friend and brother” language is not sensual, but it sits closer to personal affection than to the corporate confession of the psalm.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names its sin, never confesses the cross, never says that God has acted in Christ to answer the thirst. The song never puts the finished work in the people’s mouth.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** A reading of Psalm 42 or a sermon on longing for God that has already named the gospel.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** Sin, substitution, the cross, the resurrection. Pair with a hymn that actually says those things.
- **Diet sentence.** This title adds a familiar expression of desire. It never adds the gospel that makes the desire safe to sing.
- **What not to do.** Do not use it as the main song of the set. Do not drop the first verse.
