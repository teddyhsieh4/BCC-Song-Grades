## Thesis

The singer’s soul thirsts for God the way a deer thirsts for water. God alone is the heart’s desire, strength, and joy.

---

## Overall recommendation

Weak

Do not feature it. If it is used, put it after a reading or sermon has already set Psalm 42 and the gospel before the congregation. The first verse is a true line from Psalm 42:1, and the chorus is easy to remember. The rest of the lyric is the singer’s desire, and it never names sin, the cross, or Christ’s work. The church has sung it 10 times across at least four years of the 2023–2026 window.

---

## Alternative song suggestions

The lyric is reaching for a soul that thirsts for God and finds Him its only desire. Two of these the church already sings. Two are not in the catalog and would be new to the congregation.

**Already sung**

- **All I Have Is Christ** (Jordan Kauflin, Sovereign Grace, CCLI 5174122, graded Excellent / Prefer). Says Christ is the singer’s only treasure and shows why: Christ suffered in the singer’s place and bore the wrath reserved for him.
- **My Soul Will Wait (Psalm 62)** (Keaton Bunting / Bob Kauflin, Sovereign Grace, CCLI 7190363, graded Strong). A psalm of a soul that waits for God alone in trouble, like Psalm 42, and it ends by trusting the perfect Savior.

**Not yet logged**

- **Christ Our Treasure** (Jonathan Jackson / Jordan Kauflin / Grace Nixon / Marc Willerton, Sovereign Grace, CCLI 7138120). Not in the catalog. It confesses that we wandered the earth unsatisfied, names Jesus as Living Water and Word of Life, and sings grace for the guilty. It answers the thirst this lyric describes with Christ by name.
- **I Heard the Voice of Jesus Say** (Horatius Bonar, 1846, hymn, public domain; no SongSelect number confirmed). Not in the catalog. Jesus offers living water to the thirsty, and the singer comes, drinks, and lives in Him. It takes the deer’s thirst to the one who satisfies it.

## Voice and addressee

First-person singular, addressed to God (“Thee,” “You alone”). The lyric reports the singer’s longing more than it declares what God has done.

---

## How the text moves

It opens with the deer of Psalm 42:1, and the chorus repeats that God alone is strength, shield, and heart’s desire. Later stanzas add “friend and brother,” “more than gold or silver,” and “the apple of my eye.” The claim never moves past the singer’s desire.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

The first verse is the only part that rests on a text: “As the deer panteth for the water / So my soul longeth after Thee.” Without it the song has no Scripture under it at all. The “friend and brother” stanza is the one bands most often drop, and dropping it does not change the grade.

## Persons of the Trinity named

None. The “You” is addressed as King and friend, and Father, Son, and Spirit are never named.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Yes. A vague theist or a moralist could sing every line. “You alone are my heart’s desire” and “Only You can satisfy” fit whatever the singer already treats as ultimate. The deer image is biblical, but nothing else requires the God of the Bible.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** “the apple of my eye” and “the real joy giver.” These are affectionate phrases with no text behind them.
- **Thin:** the whole song. The Psalm 42 line is true, but the rest stacks desire language without the psalm’s lament or the gospel that answers it.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 3

“As the deer panteth for the water / So my soul longeth after Thee” is Psalm 42:1. The psalm goes on into tears, the taunt “Where is your God?,” and the hope of seeing God’s face, and the song carries none of that. “You alone are my strength, my shield” echoes several psalms but stands alone. The lyric is accurate where it quotes and thin everywhere else.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 2

Christ is not named. “You are a King” and “You are my brother” are titles a Christian can give the Son. The song never says why He holds them. With no cross, substitution, or resurrection, it scores 2.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 2

Nearly every line is the singer’s inner state: “My soul longeth,” “I long to worship,” “I want You more than gold,” “I love You more than any other.” Psalm 42 models honest thirst, but this setting turns it into a vow of desire with nothing under it. Songs that need an “only sing it if you mean it” warning are usually Weak.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 3

God is strength, shield, King, joy giver, and the one who satisfies. These are true but not developed. Holiness, justice, sovereignty, and the reasons God is worth thirsting for are absent, and the repeated “You alone” never says who He is.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 3

The first verse is clear and the room already knows the chorus. But the song teaches almost nothing and mostly reports desire. A seeker-friendly church would grade it higher, and this church does not. Use it only when a reading or sermon has already put Psalm 42 before the people.

---

## Tone

Gentle and affectionate. The “friend and brother” stanza is personal affection more than the corporate confession of the psalm.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names its sin, never confesses the cross, and never says that God has answered the thirst in Christ.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** After a reading of Psalm 42, or a sermon on longing for God that has already named the gospel.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** Sin, substitution, the cross, and the resurrection. Pair it with In Christ Alone.
- **Diet sentence.** Adds a familiar expression of desire for God. It does not add the gospel that answers that desire.
- **What not to do.** Do not make it the main song of the set, and do not drop the first verse.

## Why this overall label

The opening line is Psalm 42:1. The rest is the singer’s desire with no gospel under it, and a true first line and a familiar chorus are not enough to feature a song. It is Weak.
