## Thesis

There is a place where mercy reigns and grace runs deep, and the love the singer has found comes down like a flood. At the cross the singer surrenders, stands in awe, and says that love ran red and sin washed white. Sin and shame are powerless there. The heart has peace with God and forgiveness. Arms open wide, and Jesus saved the singer's life.

---

## Overall recommendation

Strong

Use it when the sermon is the cross, and do not let the surrender line replace the washing. The church has logged it once, in 2025. "Where Your love ran red and my sin washed white" and "Here You saved my life" have to stay. "I surrender my life" and "I'm in awe of You" are the singer's answer. They are not the doctrine. The blood is pictured as love running red. The exchange is not spelled out beyond the washing and the saving.

## Voice and addressee

First person, speaking to Jesus by the end of the chorus: "I owe all to You, Jesus." "My sin," "my life," "my heart," "my hope." The verses begin as a description of a place and then become the singer's love, peace, and forgiveness. The church would be singing one person's awe at a cross the chorus has named.

---

## How the text moves

It opens on a place of mercy and of grace flowing wide, then on "all the love I've ever found" coming down like a flood. The chorus puts that place at the cross, adds the surrender and the awe, and says love ran red and sin washed white. The next verse says sin and shame are powerless and the heart has peace with God and forgiveness. The bridge is hope, holy ground, a bow, arms open wide, and a saved life. The chorus returns. The cross is named in the refrain. The verses describe the place. The bridge is the singer bowing there.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

"Where Your love ran red and my sin washed white" and "Here You saved my life" are the lines the grade depends on, with "At the cross" and "I owe all to You, Jesus." "Where my heart has peace with God and forgiveness" belongs with them. "I surrender my life" and "I'm in awe of You" are the lines the chorus will repeat around the washing. If those are all that remain, the cross is a feeling of surrender. No line is false. The washing is the line a long awe-tag can cover.

## Persons of the Trinity named

Jesus is named as the one the singer owes, the one whose love ran red, the one who saved the singer's life. "Peace with God" names God. The Father and the Spirit are not given further titles. The cross is Jesus' cross.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. "At the cross," "my sin washed white," and "I owe all to You, Jesus" cannot be moved to another lord. "There's a place where mercy reigns and never dies," sung before the chorus, could be a vague mercy. The chorus ties the place to the cross and to Jesus.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** "Comes like a flood, comes flowing down" and "Here on holy ground." The flood is the song's measure of love. Holy ground is the singer's name for the place of the cross.
- **Thin:** "I'm in awe of You" and "all the love I've ever found." The awe is real if the washing stays. The line itself does not say what he did. "Arms open wide" is a picture beside "You saved my life."

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 4

"Where Your love ran red and my sin washed white" agrees with Isaiah 1:18, sins as scarlet made white as snow, and it puts that washing at the cross. "Peace with God and forgiveness" agrees with Romans 5:1, peace with God through Christ, and with the forgiveness preached in his name. "Here You saved my life" says the rescue happened there. "Sin and shame are powerless" is the song's conclusion from that rescue. "I surrender my life" is the singer's response. The washing is the rescue the chorus has already named. The cross, the washing, and the peace can be tied to texts. "Mercy reigns and never dies" and "streams of grace flow deep and wide" are praise around that washing. The score is 4 because the washing at the cross is clear and the verses spend their time on the size of the mercy.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 4

Jesus is named. The cross is named. Sin is washed white there, shame loses its power, and Jesus saved the singer's life. Peace with God and forgiveness are located at that place. The blood is in the phrase "love ran red." Wrath is absent, and the resurrection is absent. The finished work is the cross that washes and saves, sung in the chorus, with the cost left in the color of the love.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 3

"At the cross" and "my sin washed white" and "You saved my life" declare what happened. "I surrender my life," "I'm in awe of You," "all the love I've ever found," and "here I bow down" are the singer's answer, and they take the repeated lines of the chorus and the bridge. The cross is objective. The awe and the surrender are what a long chorus will keep in the room's ear.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 3

Mercy reigns and does not die. Grace flows. Love ran red. He forgives, makes peace, and saves. Jesus is the one owed. Holiness in the lyric is the phrase "holy ground," a name for the place. Justice and wrath are unnamed. The washing implies a real stain. The portrait the chorus repeats is mercy, awe, and a love that ran red.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 4

One log, in 2025, means the room does not know it well yet. The chorus is clear: at the cross, sin washed white, I owe all to Jesus. A congregation can learn that from one pass if the washing is sung and not replaced by the surrender. "Love ran red" needs no long explanation once the cross is in the line. The song will teach awe if the washing is dropped. It will teach the cross if the washing stays.

---

## Tone

The tone is awed and warm. The line that sets it is "I'm in awe of You," set next to "Where Your love ran red and my sin washed white." The flood of love is the warmth. The washing is the graver line inside it. The bow at the end keeps the singer low.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never says the word blood, never says that wrath was borne, and never says that Jesus rose.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Isaiah 1:18 or the cross, with the washing line sung.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The resurrection, and wrath if the sermon is propitiation. Pair it with *In Christ Alone* (logged 8 times) or *Hallelujah, What a Savior* (logged 6 times).
- **Diet sentence.** Once in this window, it adds a cross where sin is washed white and Jesus saves. It never adds the resurrection.
- **What not to do.** Do not loop "I surrender my life" and "I'm in awe of You" and drop "my sin washed white." Do not treat "holy ground" as the doctrine.
