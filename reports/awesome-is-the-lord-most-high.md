## Thesis

The Lord is great and mighty in strength, faithful, and worthy of praise for his glory. The nations are told to raise their hands and creation is told to shout, because the Lord most high is awesome. Where he sends, the singers will go. He is called the answer the world should know. They will trust him when he calls their name and follow where he leads, and they will praise him together now and forever.

---

## Overall recommendation

Weak

Do not feature it. The church has logged it 3 times across at least three years (2023, 2024, 2026). "Great are You, Lord, mighty in strength / You are faithful, You will ever be" is the line that has to stay if it is sung at all. The chorus is "How awesome is the Lord most high," and the second verse is a vow to go, to trust, and to follow. Jesus is not named. A worshiper who does not confess Jesus can sing the song unchanged. Keep it off the list, or sing a short pass of the first verse after Scripture has already named the Son.

## Voice and addressee

The verses address God as "You." "We will praise You," "where You send us," "when You call our name." The chorus turns outward: "Raise your hands, all you nations / Shout to God, all creation." The greatness is his. The going and the trusting are the church's vow.

---

## How the text moves

It starts with greatness, might, and faithfulness, and with praise offered for his glory. The chorus calls the nations and creation to say how awesome the Lord most high is. The next verse leaves the attributes and makes a promise: we will go where you send, you are the answer we want the world to know, we will trust you when you call, we will follow all the way. A second chorus adds praise together, now and forever. The bridge is hallelujah and the same awesomeness. The first verse says something about God. The second verse says what the singers will do.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

"Great are You, Lord, mighty in strength / You are faithful, You will ever be" is the line the grade depends on. "How awesome is the Lord most high" is the line the room will keep. "Where You send us, God, we will go" is the vow. If the band keeps the awesome chorus and the vow and drops the faithfulness, the song is a shout and a missions promise. No line is false. The faithfulness line is short, and the chorus will not protect it.

## Persons of the Trinity named

"Lord," "God," and "Lord most high" are the names. Father, Son, and Spirit are unnamed. "You're the answer" does not say the answer is the Son. The one who sends and calls is not given Jesus' name.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Yes. "Great are You, Lord, mighty in strength," "How awesome is the Lord most high," and "Where You send us, God, we will go" can be sung by any worshiper of one God who is willing to be sent. Nothing in the lyric names Jesus, a cross, or a resurrection. The song would not correct that worshiper.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** "Raise your hands, all you nations." Lifted hands are a biblical gesture. The line is the song's instruction to the nations, and it does not state a doctrine by itself.
- **Thin:** "How awesome is the Lord most high," "You're the answer we want the world to know," and "We will praise You together for now and forever." Awesomeness, "the answer," and unending praise are as far as those lines go. They never say a work of God.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 3

"How awesome is the Lord most high" stands near Psalm 47:2, "the LORD, the Most High, is to be feared, a great king over all the earth," and near Psalm 47:1, the clapping and the shout. The song says awesome and most high, and it says shout. It does not say "feared," and it does not say "a great king." "You are faithful, You will ever be" agrees with the faithfulness of God in Lamentations 3:22-23 in shorter words. "Mighty in strength" is true. "Where You send us, God, we will go" is a missions vow in the shape of Isaiah 6:8, without Isaiah's "Here I am" or the word that sent him. The opening can be tied to Psalm 47 in part. The rest of the song does not stay with the psalm. That is why the score stays at 3.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 2

Jesus is not named. The cross, the blood, sin, and the resurrection are absent. "Lord most high" is a title any worshiper of the one God can use. "You're the answer" does not say what question he answers or what he has done. The song exalts an unnamed Lord and leaves the gospel out.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 3

"Mighty in strength" and "You are faithful" declare something about God. "How awesome is the Lord most high" is praise aimed at him. "We will praise You," "we offer everything," "we will go," "we will trust You," and "we'll follow all the way" are the church's vow, and the second verse is almost entirely that vow. The attributes are two lines. The response is the rest.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 3

He is great, mighty in strength, faithful forever, glorious, and most high. He sends, calls by name, and leads. "The answer" is the singers' name for his sufficiency. Holiness, justice, wrath, and love are unnamed. Faithfulness and might are real. The chorus does not say them again. It says awesome.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 3

Three logs across at least three years means some of the room knows the chorus. The words are easy. A congregation can learn that the Lord is mighty and faithful and that his people ought to go where he sends. They cannot learn who the Lord is in Christ. "How awesome is the Lord most high" is the line they will take home, and it teaches the least. The faithfulness line is the one worth keeping, and only after Scripture has already named the Son.

---

## Tone

The tone is loud and eager. The line that sets it is "Raise your hands, all you nations / Shout to God, all creation / How awesome is the Lord most high." The faithfulness line is quieter than the shout. The second verse is earnest about going. The song stays in the shout.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names Jesus, never says that the Most High is the Holy One who saves, and never says a mighty act that would show why he is awesome.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Psalm 47, after the reading has said that the Most High is king, and only as a short response. It does not serve a sermon on the cross.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The Son and his work. Pair it with *Only a Holy God* or *Behold Our God* so "awesome" is not left without the God those songs name.
- **Diet sentence.** Three times in at least three years, it adds a shout that the Lord most high is awesome, and a vow to go where he sends. It never adds the gospel.
- **What not to do.** Do not loop the chorus and drop "You are faithful." Do not use "You're the answer" as the gospel.
