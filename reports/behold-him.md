## Thesis

The eternal Son made the world, became a child, lived among sinners, paid a blood-debt on a criminal’s cross, and rose. The church’s work is to be still and behold Him.

---

## Overall recommendation

**Strong**

Use it regularly, and keep the second half of verse 2 (the criminal’s end, the debt, the rising). If that block is dropped, the remaining song is a title list with an infancy line and a ministry line.

The church has sung it for at least three years in the 2023–2026 snapshot (12 logs, first appearing in 2024). It is God-centered and clear. It stays at Strong because a few images are decorative, substitution is stated as payment, and the bridge can run long as a holy-holy stack.

## Voice and addressee

Third person about the Son, then a corporate command: “Behold Him,” “be still and behold Him.” The bridge addresses “the Lord God Almighty.” There is no solo “I,” and the singer’s feelings are not the subject.

---

## How the text moves

Two long verses (often printed as four short ones), two chorus shapes, then a holy-holy bridge.

1. **Eternity and incarnation.** Before light, maker of living things, heard the cry, left the throne, woke as a child, became like the least.
2. **Ministry, cross, resurrection.** Dined with sinners, healed, is in our midst now, chose a criminal’s end, paid the debt with blood, buried death as He rose.

Chorus 1 names Jesus as Son of God, Messiah, Lamb, roaring Lion. Chorus 2 adds Alpha and Omega, risen Savior. The bridge is Isaiah 6 / Revelation 4 language. The song begins before creation and ends with beholding the risen One. The second verse is what pays off the titles.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

**Do not drop “He who chose a criminal’s end / paid with blood to settle our debt / buried death as He rose to life.”** That is the gospel block. Without it the church has beheld a Creator-child-healer.

**The thin line** is “walked across the pages of time.” See the accuracy section.

The bridge can be shortened. It should not replace the gospel block.

No single line is false enough to sink the song.

## Persons of the Trinity named

The Son is named as Jesus, Son of God, Messiah, Lamb, Lion, Alpha and Omega, risen Savior. The bridge’s “Lord God Almighty” is the title of Isaiah 6 and Revelation 4, here aimed at the same Lord the verses have been describing. The Father and the Spirit are unnamed.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Not the whole song. The child on the throne-left, the criminal’s end, the blood-debt, and the risen Savior are Christian claims. “Jesus, Son of God, Messiah” stops a vague theist.

The first half of verse 1 (“before there was light,” “made every living thing”) could be sung by a moral monotheist. Keep the later lines attached to the hook.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

**Unbiblical.** Nothing contradicts Scripture. “Buried death as He rose to life” is a compressed picture of 2 Timothy 1:10 and 1 Corinthians 15:54–57. He abolished death. He did not inter a second character named Death. Read as poetry for the resurrection’s victory, the line holds.

**Non-biblical.** “Walked across the pages of time” is decoration. Scripture says He made the ages (Hebrews 1:2). It does not say He walked across a book called Time. The image does not teach a false doctrine. It also does not teach a real one.

**Thin.** “Even now He is in our midst” is Matthew 18:20 and Revelation 1:13 in a soft line. True, and easy to hear as a mood line rather than a claim about Christ’s presence with the church. “The Lamb, the roaring Lion” is Revelation 5:5–6 as titles. The work those titles belong to has to come from the verse around them.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 4

**Verse 1.** “He who was before there was light” is John 1:1–3 and Genesis 1:3: the Son is not a creature inside the first day. “He who made every living thing” is John 1:3 and Colossians 1:16. “Left His throne to wake as a child / He became like the least of us” is Philippians 2:6–8 and Luke 2. That is incarnation in ordinary words.

“Walked across the pages of time” is the line that keeps this category at 4. It is Bible-ish. It is not a sentence you can tie to a text in its ordinary sense.

**Verse 2.** “Dined with sinners and saints” is Mark 2:15–17. “Healed the blind, the lost and the lame” is the ministry in the Gospels. “Even now He is in our midst” is Matthew 18:20 and Matthew 28:20. “Chose a criminal’s end” is Luke 23:32–33 and Philippians 2:8. “Paid with blood to settle our debt” is 1 Peter 1:18–19 and Colossians 2:14. “Buried death as He rose to life” is the resurrection, compressed.

**Chorus and bridge.** Messiah, Lamb, Lion, Alpha and Omega, risen Savior are John 1:29, Revelation 5:5–6, Revelation 22:13, and 2 Timothy 2:8. “Holy, holy, holy is the Lord God Almighty” is Isaiah 6:3 and Revelation 4:8.

The main claims are sound. The decorative time-line and the compressed “buried death” line are why the score is a 4.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 4

The titles are paid for by the second verse: child, criminal’s cross, blood-debt, resurrection.

Substitution is stated as payment. The lyric never says “in our place.” Wrath is unnamed. Sin is present as debt and as the people He ate with. A taught room will hear the gospel. Heard only as chorus plus bridge, the song is titles and “holy, holy.”

Keep the debt-and-rising lines. If they go, this category falls.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 5

The lyric points at Christ. “Behold Him” and “be still” are commands to look, not reports of how looking felt. There is no first-person vow and no romance. “In our midst” is the only line that brings the room into the frame, and it is a claim about His presence, not about our intensity.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 4

Named: eternal, Creator, incarnate humility, healer, present, the One who pays a blood-debt, risen, Lamb, Lion, Alpha and Omega, holy, worthy.

Unnamed: Fatherhood, the Spirit, wrath, justice as a word. Love as a word is also unnamed, which is unusual and not a defect. The portrait is a sequence of works. It is not a balanced attribute list. Holiness arrives in the bridge, after the works. That is enough for a 4.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 4

The words are clear. The hook is short. The church has sung it for at least three years in this snapshot. Keys on file include B and C.

The score stays at 4 because the printed form makes a long holy-holy stack easy, and because “pages of time” and “buried death” ask the room to interpret images. Keep the gospel lines on the screen. Do not let the bridge become the song.

The popular recording features another singer. That is a recording fact. It does not change the text this church sings.

---

## Tone

Reverent, with a quiet command at the center: be still and behold. That is a good tone if the band actually leaves space for the words. It goes wrong if “be still” is sung at full volume as a hook.

Nothing sensual. Nothing cute. The room is not asked to confer worth on Him. The bridge ascribes worth He already has.

---

## What the congregation never says

They never confess “our sin” as a sentence.  
They never say “in our place,” wrath, or justification.  
They never name the Father or the Spirit.  
They never name repentance.  
They never name a present High Priest.

---

## Setlist notes

**When it serves the sermon.** John 1. Philippians 2. The Gospel passion and resurrection narratives. Revelation 1, 4, or 5. Colossians 1 or 2. An opener that tells the room whom to look at. A Christmas-to-cross movement on a Sunday that needs both.

**Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** A personal confession of sin. Substitution in plain words. Wrath and justification when the text is Romans 3 or Isaiah 53.

**What it adds to the year’s diet.** Incarnation-to-resurrection narrative, “behold” as the congregation’s work, holy-holy language. It will not supply repentance or “our sin” as a confession.

**What not to do.** Do not program verse 1, the titles, and a long bridge. That cut leaves Creator-child-Lion without Calvary.
