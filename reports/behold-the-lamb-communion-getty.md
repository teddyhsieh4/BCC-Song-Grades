## Thesis

The Lamb bears our sins away, slain for us. All who come in faith find forgiveness at the cross. The body of our Savior Jesus Christ was torn for us. The blood cleanses every stain of sin. He drained death’s cup that all may enter in to receive the life of God. The congregation shares in the bread of life and drinks of His sacrifice as a sign of bonds of peace, love, and grace around the table of the King, then rises to follow in the steps of Christ and proclaim that Christ will come again.

---

## Overall recommendation

Excellent / Prefer

Feature it, especially at the Lord’s Table. Keep the lines on the Lamb who bears our sins away, the blood that cleanses, and the cup He drained. The church has sung it 7 times across at least three years of the 2023–2026 window.

## Voice and addressee

Mostly first-person plural, spoken among the congregation as it remembers the cross at the table. It ends with a call to follow Christ as His body on earth.

---

## How the text moves

The hymn moves through the Supper itself. It begins with the Lamb slain for us, then the body torn and the blood shed, and then the cup He drained so we could have life. It ends by sending the church out to follow Christ until He comes again.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“Behold the Lamb who bears our sins away, / Slain for us” and “The blood that cleanses every stain of sin, / Shed for you” and “He drained death’s cup that all may enter in / To receive the life of God.” Without them, the bread and cup are shared with no gospel named.

## Persons of the Trinity named

Jesus Christ is named as the Savior and the Lamb. The Father and the Spirit are not named. The work of the Son is explicit.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. A vague theist could not sing “Behold the Lamb who bears our sins away, / Slain for us” without changing it.

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“Behold the Lamb who bears our sins away, / Slain for us” is John 1:29 and 1 Peter 2:24. “The promise made that all who come in faith / Find forgiveness at the cross” is Acts 10:43 and Ephesians 1:7. “The body of our Savior Jesus Christ, / Torn for you” and “The blood that cleanses every stain of sin, / Shed for you” is 1 Corinthians 11:24–25 and Hebrews 9:14. “He drained death’s cup that all may enter in / To receive the life of God” is Matthew 26:39 and John 10:10. The table as a sign of bonds of peace, love, and grace is 1 Corinthians 10:16–17.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

Every stanza is about Christ’s sacrifice: “slain for us,” “torn for you,” “shed for you,” and the cup of death He drained. Substitution is stated in the words of the Supper itself.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 5

The text declares what the Lamb has done and gives the church’s response at the table. The “we” is corporate, and there are no personal vows.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 4

Jesus is the Lamb who bears sins, the Savior whose body was torn, and the King at whose table His people eat. Holiness and justice appear in the sins borne and the blood that cleanses. The score is 4 because the hymn stays with the sacrifice and names few other attributes.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The plural voice and the Supper’s own words make it easy to sing at the table. It teaches a congregation what the bread and cup mean while they take them.

---

## Tone

The tone is reverent and quiet, as fits the table, and it turns outward at the end with the call to follow.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names the Spirit. The main gospel elements are present.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** 1 Corinthians 11, John 1:29, 1 Peter 2:24, Hebrews 9, Matthew 26, or any text on the Lord’s Supper or the cross. It is especially useful at the table.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The Spirit, if the service has not named Him.
- **Diet sentence.** Adds a hymn for the Lord’s Supper that states substitution in the Supper’s own words. It adds little on the Spirit.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop the lines on the Lamb, the blood, and the cup, and do not sing the refrain alone.
