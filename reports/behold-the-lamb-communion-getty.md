## Thesis

The Lamb bears our sins away, slain for us. All who come in faith find forgiveness at the cross. The body of our Savior Jesus Christ was torn for us. The blood cleanses every stain of sin. He drained death’s cup that all may enter in to receive the life of God. The congregation shares in the bread of life and drinks of His sacrifice as a sign of bonds of peace, love, and grace around the table of the King, then rises to follow in the steps of Christ and proclaim that Christ will come again.

---

## Overall recommendation

Excellent / Prefer

Feature it, especially at the Lord’s Table. The lyric names the Lamb who bears our sins away, the body torn for us, the blood that cleanses every stain of sin, the death that brings life, and the drained cup. Keep those lines. The whole text is dense, precise, and Scripture-shaped for the ordinance.

## Voice and addressee

Mostly first-person plural addressing the congregation and remembering the finished work. It is declaration and response at the table. The congregation can own the confession of the Lamb slain for us, the body torn, the blood shed, and the call to follow as His body here on earth.

---

## How the text moves

It opens by commanding the congregation to behold the Lamb who bears our sins away, slain for us, and to remember the promise that all who come in faith find forgiveness at the cross. The refrain names the sharing in the bread of life and the drinking of His sacrifice as a sign of bonds of peace around the table of the King. Verse 2 names the body of our Savior Jesus Christ torn for us, the wounds that heal, and the death that brings life and makes us one. Verse 3 names the blood that cleanses every stain of sin, shed for us, and that He drained death’s cup that all may enter in to receive the life of God. Verse 4 is the response: with thankfulness and faith we rise to follow in the steps of Christ as His body here on earth, share in His suffering, proclaim that Christ will come again, and join in the feast of heaven.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“Behold the Lamb who bears our sins away, / Slain for us” and “The blood that cleanses every stain of sin, / Shed for you” and “He drained death’s cup that all may enter in / To receive the life of God.”

Dropping those lines leaves the sharing of the bread and cup without the named Lamb who bears sins away, the blood that cleanses, and the drained cup that brings life. The refrain still names the sacrifice, but the specific gospel acts that make the table meaningful are in those lines.

## Persons of the Trinity named

Jesus Christ is named as the Savior and the Lamb. The Father and the Spirit are not named. The work of the Son is explicit.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. The Lamb who bears our sins away, the body torn for us, the blood that cleanses every stain of sin, the drained cup, and the forgiveness at the cross cannot be sung by a vague theist without correction. The table is tied to that finished work.

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“Behold the Lamb who bears our sins away, / Slain for us” is the ordinary sense of John 1:29 and 1 Peter 2:24. “The promise made that all who come in faith / Find forgiveness at the cross” is the ordinary sense of Acts 10:43 and Ephesians 1:7. “The body of our Savior Jesus Christ, / Torn for you” and “The blood that cleanses every stain of sin, / Shed for you” is the ordinary sense of 1 Corinthians 11:24–25 and Hebrews 9:14. “He drained death’s cup that all may enter in / To receive the life of God” is the ordinary sense of Matthew 26:39 and John 10:10. The lines stay inside ordinary biblical sense. The table as a sign of bonds of peace, love, and grace is the ordinary sense of 1 Corinthians 10:16–17.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

Jesus is named as the Savior and the Lamb. Substitution is stated: bears our sins away, slain for us, torn for you, shed for you. The cross is named. The blood that cleanses is named. The drained cup is named. The song does not stop at the ordinance. It preaches the finished work that makes the bread and the cup a sign of the gospel.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 5

The text is declaration of what the Lamb has done and of the ordinary response at the table. The first-person plural is corporate. There is no high-claim present-tense personal vow. The whole text rests on the Lamb who bears sins away, the body torn, the blood shed, and the drained cup.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 4

Jesus is the Lamb who bears our sins away, the Savior whose body was torn, whose blood cleanses every stain of sin, who drained death’s cup, and who is the King at whose table the congregation gathers. The attributes are specific and tied to the sacrifice and the table. Holiness and justice appear as the Lamb who bears sins and the blood that cleanses. The score is 4 because the lyric stays focused on the work of the Lamb at the table rather than a broader list of attributes.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The language is plain. The lyric teaches the Lamb who bears sins away, the body torn, the blood that cleanses, the drained cup, and the ordinary response of sharing the bread and cup as a sign of the gospel. The congregation can learn the finished work by singing those lines at the table. The plural voice is corporate. Singability is high, and the teaching is the reason for the 5. Keep the lines about the Lamb, the blood, and the drained cup so the table stays attached to the gospel.

---

## Tone

Reverent and declarative. It does not ask the room to confer on the Lamb what He has not already done. The intensity stays inside the sacrifice, the blood, the drained cup, and the ordinary response at the table.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names the Spirit. That is a small gap. The main gospel elements of the Lamb who bears sins away, the body torn, the blood that cleanses, and the drained cup are present.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** 1 Corinthians 11, John 1:29, 1 Peter 2:24, Hebrews 9, Matthew 26, any text on the Lord’s Supper, the Lamb, the blood, or the cross. Especially useful at the table after a sermon that has named the Lamb who bears sins away.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The Spirit if the service has not already supplied it.
- **Diet sentence.** It adds a dense confession of the Lamb who bears our sins away, the body torn for us, the blood that cleanses every stain of sin, and the drained cup that brings life, then the ordinary response of sharing the bread and cup as a sign of the gospel. It does not add a full narrative of the Spirit’s ongoing work.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop the lines about the Lamb, the blood, and the drained cup. Do not let the refrain stand alone without the named sacrifice.
