## Thesis

The church looks at the cross and sees the Great I AM under a crown of thorns. They confess that they cursed his name, and that he bore their shame and paid the cost. The Lamb's wounds are the redemption they behold. He died for sin, rose, reigns, and will be seen when death is finished.

---

## Overall recommendation

Excellent / Prefer

Feature it. The church has logged it 3 times, all in 2026, so the room is still learning the words, and the words are worth the repetition. “You bore our shame and paid the cost” and “Our God has risen from the grave” have to stay with “Oh God, we thank You for the cross.” “The Father's heart displayed for us” is the love of God shown in that cost. Sing it in the same verse as the shame and the payment. The chorus can be repeated. The verse about the grave should not be skipped on Easter, or on any other Sunday the song is used.

## Voice and addressee

The opening tells the church to look: “See Him there.” The thanks is corporate and addressed to God: “Oh God, we thank You for the cross.” “We cursed Your name” puts the singers in the company of the mockers. The chorus speaks to Jesus: “Jesus, You will reign forevermore.” “We sing,” “We'll see,” and “We'll bow” stay plural. This is the church speaking to God and to the Son about what he has done for them.

---

## How the text moves

It starts at the cross: the Great I AM, the crown of thorns, the Father's heart displayed, and thanks for the cross. The next lines stay on Calvary. The church cursed his name, and he bore their shame and paid the cost, and the thanks is repeated. The chorus tells the church to behold the Lamb, says the redemption is written on his hands, and answers that Jesus will reign and the victory is his. A later verse says the sacrifice was offered for every sin, the Savior died, and the Lord of Life has risen from the grave. The last verse waits until death's age is done, when the church will see his face and bow before the King of kings. The chorus does not thin the claim out. It keeps the Lamb, the reign, and the victory in front of the room after each of those scenes.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“You bore our shame and paid the cost” is the line the grade depends on, with “Our God has risen from the grave” and “For every sin, our Savior died.” “See Him there, the Great I AM / A crown of thorns upon His head” has to stay, because it says who was on the cross. “The Father's heart displayed for us” belongs in that same verse, beside the shame and the cost. If the band keeps the heart line and the thanks and drops the shame, the cost, and the grave, the cross becomes a display of affection with the payment unspoken. No line is false. The resurrection line is the one a short arrangement is most likely to lose, and losing it would leave the Lamb on the cross with the reign asserted and the empty grave unsung.

## Persons of the Trinity named

The Son is the Great I AM, the Lamb, Jesus, the Lord of Life, and the King of kings. The Father is named in “The Father's heart displayed for us,” and “Oh God” receives the thanks. The Spirit is unnamed. The cross is where the Father's heart and the Son's suffering are sung in one verse.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. “See Him there, the Great I AM / A crown of thorns upon His head” requires Jesus, and requires him on the cross. “We cursed Your name and even still / You bore our shame and paid the cost” cannot be moved to another savior. “Our God has risen from the grave” and “We'll bow before the King of kings” close the same door. A hearer who wants a teacher to admire has to stop at the crown of thorns.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** none.
- **Thin:** “The story of redemption written on His hands.” The wounds are the claim, and John 20:27 has Thomas look at those hands. “Story of redemption” is the song's summary of what the wounds mean. “The Lord of Life can't be contained” is a loose wording next to “Our God has risen from the grave,” which says the same news plainly.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“See Him there, the Great I AM” puts the name of John 8:58, “before Abraham was, I am,” on the one the church is told to see. “A crown of thorns upon His head” is John 19:2, the soldiers' crown. “Lifted up on Calvary's hill” is the crucifixion, and “lifted up” is the phrase Jesus uses for that death in John 12:32. “Oh God, we thank You for the cross” is a plain sentence.

“The Father's heart displayed for us” is Romans 5:8 in a short form: “God shows his love for us in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us.” The song's word is “heart.” The next lines put the shame and the cost in the same verse. “We cursed Your name and even still / You bore our shame and paid the cost” puts the church among the enemies of Romans 5:10, “while we were enemies,” and among those who derided him in Matthew 27:39. “Bore our shame” and “paid the cost” are the exchange. First Peter 2:24 says he bore our sins in his body on the tree. First Corinthians 6:20 says “you were bought with a price.”

“Behold the Lamb” is John 1:29. “For every sin, our Savior died” says the sacrifice was for sin, after the song has already said “our shame” and “we cursed.” Hebrews 10:12 says Christ offered a single sacrifice for sins. “The Lord of Life” is the song's title beside Acts 3:15, the Author of life, whom God raised from the dead. “Can't be contained” is looser than Acts 2:24, “it was not possible for him to be held by it.” “Our God has risen from the grave” is the plain line. “You will reign forevermore” and “King of kings” agree with Revelation 19:16. “When the age of death is done” agrees with 1 Corinthians 15:26, death the last enemy, and with Revelation 21:4, “death shall be no more.” “We'll see Your face, bright as the sun” joins Revelation 22:4, “They will see his face,” and Revelation 1:16, “his face was like the sun shining in full strength.”

The two summary phrases, the Father's heart and the story on his hands, sit inside that account. They do not replace it.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

The person on the cross is the I AM, under the thorns. The work is the cross, the shame borne, the cost paid, the death for sin, the resurrection, the reign, and the day when his face is seen. The church thanks God for the cross and confesses that the cursing was theirs. Wrath is not the word the song uses. Shame, cost, and “every sin” are the penalty it does use. This is the finished work, sung by the people it was for.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 5

“See Him,” “we thank You,” “we cursed,” “You bore,” “behold,” “You will reign,” “our Savior died,” “has risen,” “We'll see,” “We'll bow.” The verbs belong to God and to the whole church. “The Father's heart displayed” is about the Father, in the death. There is no line about how moved the singer feels, and no vow about the singer's future obedience. The confession “we cursed Your name” is corporate and historical. The song declares what Christ has done and what the church will see.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 4

The Son is the Great I AM, the Lamb, the Lord of Life, and the King of kings, and his name is holy. The Father's heart is displayed in the cross where shame is borne and the cost is paid. Love is shown there, in the sense of Romans 5:8, and it is shown as a payment. Holiness is in the “holy name” of the chorus. The reign and the victory are his. Wrath is not named. The justice of God is present as shame, cost, and a death for sin, without the word. That is a full portrait of the Son in his work, with one name for the Father's justice left for another song.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

Three logs, all in 2026, means the church has only begun to sing it in this window. The lines are still clear enough to teach on a first hearing. A congregation learns who was crucified, that the crucifixion was for their own cursing and shame, that he paid the cost, that he rose, that he reigns, and that they will see his face. “Calvary,” “Lamb,” and “King of kings” are ordinary church words. “The story of redemption written on His hands” may need the wounds explained once. After that, the verse carries it. This is a text the church can feature while the room is still learning it.

---

## Tone

The tone is grave and thankful. The line that sets it is “We cursed Your name and even still / You bore our shame and paid the cost,” and then “Oh God, we thank You for the cross.” The thanks does not lighten the shame. The last verse is quieter and forward: the age of death done, his face like the sun, the bowing. Nothing in the song is playful.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never says the word wrath, never says “justified,” and never names the Spirit.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** The passion narratives, John 1:29, Romans 5:8-10, the resurrection, or the last day. Communion belongs with “Behold the Lamb” and the thanks for the cross. Easter belongs with the grave.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The word wrath, if the sermon is propitiation in those terms. Pair it with *Before the Throne of God Above* (logged 8 times) or *It Was Finished Upon That Cross* (logged 11 times) when the text of the morning needs the curse named as curse.
- **Diet sentence.** Three times in one year, it adds a congregational look at the I AM on the cross, the church's own cursing, the shame he bore, the resurrection, and the reign. It never uses the word wrath.
- **What not to do.** Do not keep “The Father's heart displayed for us” and drop “You bore our shame and paid the cost.” Do not skip “Our God has risen from the grave.” Do not treat this as the Getty communion hymn. That is a different text and a different catalog row.
