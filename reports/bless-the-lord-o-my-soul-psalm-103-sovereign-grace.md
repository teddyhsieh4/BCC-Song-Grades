## Thesis

The singer commands the soul to bless the Lord and forget not all He has done. He forgives all iniquities, heals all infirmities, redeems life from the pit, and crowns with mercy. As far as the east is from the west, so far has He taken our sins from us. As high as the heavens are over the earth, so great is His steadfast love. As a father shows compassion to his children, so the Lord shows compassion to those who fear Him.

## Voice and addressee

Mostly first-person singular commanding the soul, with first-person plural in the chorus. It is declaration of what the Lord has done. The congregation can own the command to bless the Lord and the confession that He has taken our sins from us as far as east from west.

---

## How the text moves

It opens with the command of Psalm 103:1–2: “Bless the Lord / O my soul / And all that’s within me praise Him / Bless the Lord / O my soul / And forget not all He’s done.” It continues with the ordinary sense of Psalm 103:3–4: “He forgives all your iniquities / And He heals all your infirmities / He redeems your life from the pit / And crowns you with mercy.” The chorus names the ordinary sense of Psalm 103:11–12: “As far as the east is from the west / So far have You taken our sins from us / And as high as the heavens are over the earth / So great is Your steadfast love toward us.” Verse 2 names the ordinary sense of Psalm 103:13: “As a father shows compassion / To his beloved children / So the Lord shows compassion to us / To those who fear Him.”

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“As far as the east is from the west / So far have You taken our sins from us” and “He forgives all your iniquities.”

Dropping those lines leaves the command to bless the Lord without the named removal of sins and the forgiveness of iniquities. The rest of the lyric still names the steadfast love and the fatherly compassion, but the specific gospel act that shows why the soul can bless is in those lines.

## Persons of the Trinity named

The Lord is named. The Father is implied in the compassion of a father. The Son and the Spirit are not named. The work of the Lord in forgiving and removing sins is explicit.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. The forgiveness of all iniquities and the removal of sins as far as east from west cannot be sung by a vague theist without correction. The command to bless the Lord is tied to that finished work.

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“Bless the Lord / O my soul / And all that’s within me praise Him / And forget not all He’s done” is the ordinary sense of Psalm 103:1–2. “He forgives all your iniquities / And He heals all your infirmities / He redeems your life from the pit / And crowns you with mercy” is the ordinary sense of Psalm 103:3–4. “As far as the east is from the west / So far have You taken our sins from us / And as high as the heavens are over the earth / So great is Your steadfast love toward us” is the ordinary sense of Psalm 103:11–12. “As a father shows compassion / To his beloved children / So the Lord shows compassion to us / To those who fear Him” is the ordinary sense of Psalm 103:13. The lines stay inside ordinary biblical sense. The score is 5 because the lyric names the ordinary sense of those texts.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 4

The Lord is named as the one who forgives all iniquities and has taken our sins from us as far as east from west. The removal of sins is the ordinary sense of the psalm. The score is 4 rather than 5 because the lyric does not name the Son, the cross, or the resurrection in further detail. The gospel is present in the named forgiveness and removal of sins, which is why the score is 4 rather than 3.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

The text is declaration of what the Lord has done. The command to bless the soul is grounded in the named forgiveness of iniquities and the removal of sins as far as east from west. The singer does not make high-claim present-tense vows without grounding. The score is 4 because the lyric stays focused on the benefits of the Lord rather than a fuller narrative of the finished work.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 4

The Lord forgives all iniquities, heals all infirmities, redeems life from the pit, crowns with mercy, takes sins as far as east from west, and shows compassion as a father to those who fear Him. The attributes are specific and tied to the forgiveness, the steadfast love, and the fatherly compassion. Holiness and justice appear as the removal of sins. The score is 4 because the lyric stays focused on the compassion and the steadfast love rather than a broader list of attributes.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The language is plain. The lyric teaches that the Lord forgives all iniquities, takes sins as far as east from west, and shows compassion as a father to those who fear Him. The congregation can learn the ordinary sense of Psalm 103 by singing these lines. The singular and plural voices are declaration the whole church can own. Singability is high, and the teaching is the reason for the 5. Keep the lines about the forgiveness of iniquities and the removal of sins so the command to bless stays attached to the gospel.

---

## Tone

Reverent and declarative. It does not ask the room to confer on the Lord what He has not already done. The intensity stays inside the forgiveness of iniquities, the removal of sins, the steadfast love, and the fatherly compassion.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names the Son or the Spirit. That is a small gap. The main gospel elements of the forgiveness of iniquities and the removal of sins as far as east from west are present.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Psalm 103, any text on the forgiveness of sins, the steadfast love of the Lord, or the fatherly compassion toward those who fear Him. Especially useful after a sermon that has named the removal of sins.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** A fuller naming of the Son, the cross, and the empty tomb if the service has not already supplied them. The lyric names the removal of sins; the rest of the hour can name the cross and the resurrection in further detail.
- **Diet sentence.** It adds a clear confession that the Lord forgives all iniquities, takes sins as far as east from west, and shows compassion as a father to those who fear Him. It does not add a full narrative of the cross or the empty tomb.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop the lines about the forgiveness of iniquities and the removal of sins. Do not let the command to bless stand alone without the named removal of sins.
