## Thesis

The singer commands the soul to bless the Lord and forget not all He has done. He forgives all iniquities, heals all infirmities, redeems life from the pit, and crowns with mercy. As far as the east is from the west, so far has He taken our sins from us. As high as the heavens are over the earth, so great is His steadfast love. As a father shows compassion to his children, so the Lord shows compassion to those who fear Him.

---

## Overall recommendation

Strong

Use it regularly. It is a faithful setting of Psalm 103 that names God forgiving all our iniquities, removing our sins as far as east is from west, and showing a father’s compassion to those who fear Him. Keep the chorus and the forgiveness lines. They are where the gospel is. The church has sung it 7 times across at least four years of the 2023–2026 window.

## Voice and addressee

Mostly first-person singular, the singer commanding his own soul as the psalm does, with a corporate “us” in the chorus. It declares what the Lord has done, and the whole congregation can make that confession.

---

## How the text moves

The first verse sings Psalm 103:1–4, the call to bless the Lord and the list of what He has done. The chorus turns to Psalm 103:11–12, sins removed and steadfast love as high as the heavens. The second verse adds Psalm 103:13, the Lord’s fatherly compassion for those who fear Him.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“As far as the east is from the west / So far have You taken our sins from us” and “He forgives all your iniquities.”

Without these lines the call to bless the Lord has no reason behind it. Forgiveness and the removal of sin are what the soul is told not to forget.

## Persons of the Trinity named

The Lord is named, and the Father is pictured in the father who shows compassion. The Son and the Spirit are not named.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. A vague theist would have to change God forgiving all iniquities and removing sins as far as east is from west.

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“Bless the Lord / O my soul / And all that’s within me praise Him / And forget not all He’s done” is Psalm 103:1–2. “He forgives all your iniquities / And He heals all your infirmities / He redeems your life from the pit / And crowns you with mercy” is Psalm 103:3–4. “As far as the east is from the west / So far have You taken our sins from us / And as high as the heavens are over the earth / So great is Your steadfast love toward us” is Psalm 103:11–12. “As a father shows compassion / To his beloved children / So the Lord shows compassion to us / To those who fear Him” is Psalm 103:13. The song stays close to the psalm throughout.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 4

The Lord forgives all iniquity and removes our sins as far as east is from west. The score is 4 and not 5 because the Son, the cross, and the resurrection are not named. It is 4 and not 3 because forgiveness and the removal of sin are stated plainly.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

The song declares what the Lord has done, and the command to the soul rests on that forgiveness. The singer makes no vow. The score is 4 because the song lists the Lord’s benefits and does not reach the work that secures them.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 4

The Lord forgives, heals, redeems from the pit, crowns with mercy, removes sin, loves steadfastly, and shows a father’s compassion. Justice is implied in the removal of sin. The score is 4 because the lyric dwells on His compassion and love and says less of His other attributes.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The words are the psalm’s own and easy to sing. A congregation that sings it learns Psalm 103, that God forgives all our sin and removes it completely, which is why it earns a 5.

---

## Tone

Grateful and joyful, the tone of a soul reminding itself what God has done.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names the Son, the cross, or the Spirit. The psalm’s forgiveness is clear, and the service can show where that forgiveness was won. Pair it with In Christ Alone.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Psalm 103, or any text on the forgiveness of sins, God’s steadfast love, or His fatherly compassion.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The Son, the cross, and the empty tomb, if the service has not already named them.
- **Diet sentence.** Adds a clear confession that the Lord forgives all iniquity, removes our sins as far as east is from west, and pities His children like a father. It does not add the cross or the resurrection.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop the chorus or the forgiveness lines and sing only the call to bless.
