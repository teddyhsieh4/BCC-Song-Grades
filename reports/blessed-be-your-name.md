## Thesis

The Lord's name is blessed in plenty and in the desert, when the sun is shining and on the road marked with suffering. Every blessing is turned back to praise. When darkness closes in, the singer will still say the name. The bridge takes Job's words: the Lord gives and takes away, and the heart will choose to bless his name.

---

## Overall recommendation

Acceptable with notes

Use it when the service is on blessing God in loss, and do not let it be the only song about who God is. The church has logged it 5 times across at least four years (2023, 2024, 2025, 2026). "You give and take away / My heart will choose to say / Lord, blessed be Your name" has to stay. The verses are circumstances. Jesus is not named. Sing the bridge, and let another song name the Son.

## Voice and addressee

First person, speaking to the Lord. "Blessed be Your name," "when I'm found in the desert place," "when the sun's shining down on me," "my heart will choose to say." The chorus is "Blessed be the name of the Lord." The blessing is aimed at him. The wilderness, the suffering, and the choice are the singer's.

---

## How the text moves

It blesses the name in a plentiful land where streams of abundance flow, then in the desert and the wilderness. A turn says every blessing poured out will be turned back to praise, and that when darkness closes in the singer will still say the name. The chorus blesses the name of the Lord and calls it glorious. The next pair is the sun shining, "the world's all as it should be," and then the road marked with suffering, pain in the offering. The bridge is give and take away, and the heart's choice to bless. The name is blessed in both weathers. The bridge is the only line that says God is the one who gives and who takes.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

"You give and take away / My heart will choose to say / Lord, blessed be Your name" is the line the grade depends on. "Blessed be the name of the Lord" is the line the room will sing. "On the road marked with suffering / Though there's pain in the offering" is the hard circumstance. If the band keeps the plentiful land and the sun and drops the bridge, the song blesses God for abundance and never says he is Lord when he takes away. No line is false. The bridge is the one a chorus-only use will skip.

## Persons of the Trinity named

"Lord" and "Your name" are the address. Father, Son, and Spirit are unnamed. The name that is blessed is not said to be the Son's name. Job's Lord is the one who gives and takes away, and the song does not go on to say that Lord became flesh.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Yes. "Blessed be Your name," "You give and take away," and "on the road marked with suffering" can be sung by any worshiper of the one God who is willing to bless him in loss. Nothing in the lyric names Jesus, a cross, or a resurrection. The song would not correct that worshiper. It would still teach Job's line.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** "When the world's all as it should be." That is the singer's ease, set opposite the suffering road. It does not state a doctrine.
- **Thin:** "In the land that is plentiful," "streams of abundance," "the desert place," and "pain in the offering." The circumstances are real. They do not say who the Lord is beyond the name, until the bridge says he gives and takes away.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 4

"You give and take away" and "Lord, blessed be Your name" agree with Job 1:21, "The LORD gave, and the LORD has taken away; blessed be the name of the LORD." The song puts that line in the mouth of a heart that chooses to say it, which is Job's choice after the loss. "Blessed be the name of the Lord" agrees with Psalm 113:2, "Blessed be the name of the LORD from this time forth and forevermore." "Though I walk through the wilderness" uses the wilderness as the place of want. The plentiful land and the desert are the song's pair of circumstances around the name. They do not misquote Job. They also do not unfold Job's dispute, his friends, or the Lord's answer from the whirlwind. The bridge is the text. The verses are the weather around it. The score is 4 because Job's sentence is intact and the song never says more than the name and the giving and the taking.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 2

Jesus is not named. The cross, the blood, sin, and the resurrection are absent. "The name of the Lord" can be sung as the God of Israel with no reference to the Son. Blessing that name in suffering is a right act. It is not the gospel. The score is 2 because the finished work is not in the lyric.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 3

"You give and take away" declares what the Lord does. "Blessed be Your name" blesses him. "When I'm found in the desert place," "when the sun's shining down on me," "there's pain in the offering," and "my heart will choose to say" are the singer's place and the singer's choice. The circumstances take four verses. The Lord's act is the bridge. Both are in the song the church actually repeats, and the verses are the larger share.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 3

His name is blessed and glorious. He pours out blessing. He gives and he takes away, which is his freedom over the singer's plenty and the singer's loss. Faithfulness is in the choice to bless him when the darkness closes in, and it is the singer's resolve as much as his attribute. Holiness, justice, wrath, and love are unnamed. The sovereignty in the bridge is specific. The verses do not add another attribute.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 4

Five logs across at least four years means the room knows it. The chorus is easy, and the bridge is the lesson: bless the Lord who gives and who takes. A congregation can learn Job 1:21 from this song if the bridge is sung. They cannot learn the Son. "Pain in the offering" is clear enough. The wilderness verses will teach endurance. They will not teach why the name is glorious. The bridge is what makes the song more than a mood for hard weeks.

---

## Tone

The tone is steady in both weathers. The line that sets it is "You give and take away / My heart will choose to say / Lord, blessed be Your name." The plentiful land is bright. The suffering road is plain about pain. The song does not rush the pain into a smile. It blesses the name on that road.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names Jesus, never says why the Lord may take away, and never says that the name above the loss is the name of the crucified Son.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Job 1, or a service of lament that still blesses God, with the bridge sung.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The Son. Pair it with *It Is Well with My Soul* if that hymn is in the hour, or with *Jesus, Your Mercy* (logged 14 times), so the loss is not left without the cross.
- **Diet sentence.** Five times in at least four years, it adds Job's "give and take away" and a blessing of the Lord's name in plenty and in pain. It never adds Christ.
- **What not to do.** Do not skip the bridge. Do not use the sun-shining verse as the whole song. Do not treat the name as a comfort that replaces the gospel.
