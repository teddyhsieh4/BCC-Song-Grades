## Thesis

By grace alone the singer stands where angels fear to tread, invited by redeeming love before the throne, pulled close by nail-scarred hands. When condemnation and Satan press in, the Great I Am is here and fights for the singer. By his blood the singer approaches, blameless, welcomed as his own. The Son is risen, spotless in righteousness, and the celebration is freedom from condemnation because he made an end to the singer's sin.

---

## Overall recommendation

Excellent / Prefer

Feature it. The church has logged it once, in 2023, so it is not yet a habit here, and the text is strong enough to become one. "By Your blood I come" and "He pulls me close with nail-scarred hands" and "free from condemnation" have to stay. "Running home" and "face to face with Love Himself" are the warmer lines. They come in the same song as the blood and the scars. Sing the verses with the chorus so the run toward the throne keeps the blood and the scars.

## Voice and addressee

First person, and the chorus speaks to God: "Boldly I approach Your throne," "By Your blood I come." The verses speak about him: "He pulls me close," "the Great I Am," "the bright and risen Son." The church would be singing one sinner's approach, on the grounds the verses have given.

---

## How the text moves

It starts with grace alone, a standing where angels fear, redeeming love, the throne, and nail-scarred hands. The next stanza is condemnation, Satan's temptation to despair, the voice of the Great I Am, and the one who fights and shields. The chorus is the approach: blameless, running home, by blood, welcomed, into majesty. A later stanza beholds the risen Son, his beauty, Love himself, and a spotless righteousness that a thousand years of praise cannot exhaust. The bridge names the celebration as knowing freedom from condemnation, and praise to the one who made an end to sin. The chorus is the approach. The verses and the bridge say why the approach is allowed.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

"By Your blood I come / Welcomed as Your own" and "He pulls me close with nail-scarred hands" and "Who made an end to all my sin" are the lines the grade depends on. "Knowing we're free from condemnation" and "Behold the bright and risen Son" belong with them. "Blameless now I'm running home" is the line the chorus will repeat. If that run is all that remains, the welcome has lost the blood and the scars. No line is false. The blood is one phrase in a chorus about running home, and it has to stay audible.

## Persons of the Trinity named

The Son is named, risen, with nail-scarred hands and blood, the Lord who is the Great I Am, Love himself, spotless in righteousness. "The throne of God" names God. The Father and the Spirit are not given further titles. The one who welcomes is the one whose blood is the way in.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. "Nail-scarred hands," "by Your blood I come," and "the bright and risen Son" cannot be moved to another lord. "Boldly I approach Your throne," without the blood, could be a vague confidence. The chorus includes the blood in the same breath as the approach.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** "Where even angels fear to tread." The angels' reserve is the song's contrast with the sinner's welcome. Hebrews 4 does not describe the angels that way.
- **Thin:** "I'm face to face with Love Himself" and "Blameless now I'm running home." Love himself is then "His perfect spotless righteousness." The run is the chorus's picture of the approach Hebrews 4:16 describes as drawing near.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

"Boldly I approach Your throne" agrees with Hebrews 4:16, drawing near with confidence to the throne of grace. "By grace alone somehow I stand" agrees with Romans 5:2, access by faith into this grace. "By Your blood I come" agrees with Hebrews 10:19, confidence to enter by the blood of Jesus. "Nail-scarred hands" agree with the risen Christ who shows his hands, as in John 20:27. "Free from condemnation" is Romans 8:1. "The bright and risen Son" is the resurrection. "His perfect spotless righteousness" is the righteousness in which the singer stands. "Made an end to all my sin" says the sin has been dealt with by him. "The Great I Am, the Lord is here" uses the name of Exodus 3:14 for the one who scatters fear. The approach, the blood, the scars, and the end of condemnation can be tied to those texts.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

Redeeming love brings the singer before the throne. Nail-scarred hands pull the singer close. Blood is the way in. The Son is risen, spotless, and he has made an end to sin so that condemnation is over and the singer is blameless and welcomed. The word cross is absent. The scars and the blood are the cross in the lyric. The resurrection is explicit. That is a full gospel of approach.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

"By Your blood I come," "nail-scarred hands," "the risen Son," and "an end to all my sin" declare his work. "Somehow I stand," "when condemnation grips my heart," "I'm running home," and "face to face with Love Himself" are the singer's approach and the singer's fear. The grounds of the boldness are his blood and his scars. The voice is one sinner coming in.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 5

He is redeeming love, the Lord on the throne, the Great I Am who scatters fear, the one who fights and shields, majestic, the risen Son, spotless in righteousness, and the one who ends sin and condemns no longer. Justice is the condemnation that is finished and the blood that answers it. Mercy is the welcome and the arms. Holiness is the spotless righteousness. The portrait holds the scars and the welcome together.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 4

One log, in 2023, means the room has barely met it. The chorus is clear once "by Your blood" is heard: approach, blameless, welcomed, by blood. A congregation can learn Hebrews 4, the scars, the end of condemnation, and the risen Son from the verses and the bridge. "The art of celebration" is the song's name for that freedom. It teaches if the bridge is sung. It is only a run home if the blood is dropped. The lyric is worth teaching the room.

---

## Tone

The tone is bold and warm. The line that sets it is "Boldly I approach Your throne / Blameless now I'm running home / By Your blood I come." The condemnation stanza is darker, with Satan tempting to despair. The scars keep the welcome attached to the wounds. The celebration is that freedom.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never says the word cross, and never names the Spirit. The blood and the scars are sung in place of the word.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Hebrews 4:16, Romans 8:1, or the access believers have by the blood.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** Little, if the scars, the blood, and the bridge are sung. The incarnation's birth narrative is outside the song. Pair it with *Come Behold the Wondrous Mystery* (logged 7 times) if the sermon begins at the manger.
- **Diet sentence.** Once in this window, it would add nail-scarred hands, approach by blood, a risen Son, and freedom from condemnation. One use has not yet put that into the year's diet. It is worth singing until it does.
- **What not to do.** Do not loop "running home" and drop "by Your blood" and "nail-scarred hands." Do not drop the bridge. The celebration is the end of sin, and the bridge is where that is said.
