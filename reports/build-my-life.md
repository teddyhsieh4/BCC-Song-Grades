## Thesis

Jesus is worthy of song, praise, and breath, the only One who can save, holy and without rival. The singer then asks to be shown and filled, and vows to build a life on His love.

---

## Overall recommendation

**Weak**

Do not feature it. The church has already sung it for at least four years (12 logs in the 2023–2026 snapshot). That history does not raise the lyric. If it is used at all, put it after Scripture and another song have already named sin and the cross, and keep it short.

The main reason for Weak is the missing gospel. Jesus is “the only One who could ever save,” and the song never says from what, by what, or at what cost. The chorus asks for an inner experience. The bridge is a first-person vow that treats “Your love” as the foundation. Matthew 7 ties a wise life to hearing and doing Jesus’ words. This lyric names love and stops.

---

## Alternative song suggestions

The lyric is reaching for worthiness, a Savior with no rival, and a life set on a firm foundation. Two of these the church has already sung. Two are not in the catalog, and would be new to the congregation. All four name the work of Christ that this lyric leaves out.

**Already sung**

**The Solid Rock (My Hope Is Built)** (Edward Mote / William Bradbury, hymn, CCLI 25417). Logged 9 times across at least four years in this snapshot. The congregation stands on Christ’s blood and righteousness. “On Christ the solid rock I stand” is the foundation line this bridge is trying to say, and the verse keeps the cross in view.

**In Christ Alone** (Keith Getty / Stuart Townend, Getty, CCLI 3350395). Logged 8 times across at least four years. The cornerstone line keeps the “only Savior” claim, then stays with the cross, the wrath borne, and the resurrection. Use it when the sermon is Matthew 7 or 1 Corinthians 3:11.

**Not yet logged**

**Christ Is Mine Forevermore** (Jonny Robinson / Rich Thompson, CityAlight, CCLI 7036096). Not in the catalog. The life in view belongs to Christ: bought, kept, and raised. It covers the “build my life” desire and names the cross and the resurrection. A fair introduction when the church is ready to retire this title.

**The Church’s One Foundation** (Samuel J. Stone, hymn, CCLI 55377). Not in the catalog. The foundation is Christ, the chief cornerstone, in the sense of Ephesians 2:20. The song is about the church, so it is a better fit when the sermon is the church built on Christ than when the sermon is only a private vow. It still says what this bridge does not.

## Voice and addressee

Verse 1 is corporate (“we live for You”). Verse 2 names Jesus and stays “we.” The chorus and bridge switch to “I”: open my eyes, fill me, lead me, I will build, I will put my trust. The addressee is the Lord, mostly as “You,” with Jesus named in verse 2. The second half of the song is a private prayer sung in unison.

---

## How the text moves

It does not move far.

1. **Worthiness in general.** Songs, praise, breath. We live for You.
2. **Jesus named.** Name above every name. Only One who could save. Same worthiness lines again.
3. **Chorus request.** Holy, no one like You. Open my eyes. Fill me with Your heart. Lead me in Your love to those around me.
4. **Bridge vow.** I will build my life on Your love. I will trust and not be shaken.

The last two movements are where the song spends its time. They are also where the doctrine thins out. The refrain does not add to the claim of verse 2. It moves from Jesus’ worth to the singer’s formation.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

There is no stanza that, if kept, would raise the gospel score. Verse 2 is the strongest block, and it is still only titles plus “save.”

The bridge is the line that holds the song at Weak: “I will build my life upon Your love, it is a firm foundation.” If the band loops that line, the room is treating “love” as the rock. Matthew 7 puts the wise life on hearing and doing Jesus’ words. Cutting the bridge would leave a shorter praise song that is still thin. That is the smaller loss.

---

## Persons of the Trinity named

Jesus is named in verse 2. Father and Spirit are unnamed. “You” in the chorus could be any of the three. The lyric never decides.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Verse 1 plus the holiness lines of the chorus, yes. A vague theist can sing “worthy of every song,” “holy, there is no one like You,” and “build my life upon Your love.”

Verse 2 stops that if it is actually sung: “Jesus, the Name above every other name / Jesus, the only One who could ever save.” Drop verse 2, or bury it under the bridge, and the remaining song is usable in almost any spiritual room.

The name of Jesus can be swapped for another cherished name in the chorus and bridge and the sentences still work. That is a problem this church cares about.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

**Unbiblical.** Nothing is heresy. “Lead me in Your love to those around me” is a fair prayer for neighbor-love (John 13:34). It becomes a problem only if that line is treated as the whole Christian life.

**Non-biblical.** “Open up my eyes in wonder / show me who You are and fill me with Your heart” is mood language. You cannot check “fill me with Your heart” against a text in its ordinary sense. Ephesians 3:17–19 is about being rooted in love and knowing Christ’s love. This line is vaguer than that.

**Thin.** Almost the entire gospel inventory. “Worthy” stacked three times. “Holy” without the works that interpret holiness. “Save” without the cross. “Love” as foundation without the Word.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 3

**Verse 1.** “Worthy of every song… praise… breath” is Revelation 5:12 and 4:11 in a flattened form. True. Generic. “We live for You” is Romans 14:8 and 2 Corinthians 5:15 without the death that those verses name.

**Verse 2.** “Jesus, the Name above every other name” is Philippians 2:9. In Philippians 2 the name is given after obedience to death on a cross. Here the name arrives without that sentence. “The only One who could ever save” is Acts 4:12. Acts 4:12 sits next to the crucified and risen Christ. Here “save” is left hanging.

**Chorus.** “Holy, there is no one like You, there is none beside You” is Exodus 15:11 and Isaiah 45:5. True. Then the lyric leaves ascription and asks for wonder, sight, and an inner filling.

**Bridge.** Matthew 7:24–25: the wise man hears Jesus’ words and does them. The rock is that hearing and doing. Psalm 62: the rock is God Himself. This lyric makes “Your love” the firm foundation. God’s love is real (1 John 4:10), and 1 John defines it by the sending of the Son as propitiation. The bridge never gets to that definition. Read in this church’s ordinary sense of Matthew 7, the line is the wrong foundation even though every word is pious.

That is why the category is a 3. The lines that can be tied to a text are true, and they stop short. The foundation line does not match Matthew 7.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 2

Jesus is named. He is called the only Savior. Titles without the finished work stay in the 2–3 range. There is no cross, no blood, no sin, no resurrection, no wrath, no place-taking. “Save” is the entire gospel vocabulary.

The chorus then turns from His work to our formation. The bridge turns to our vow. Christ is named as worthy. His death and resurrection are not preached.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 2

Verse 1–2 declare worth. From the chorus on, the song is mostly the singer’s inner life: open my eyes, fill me, lead me, I will build, I will not be shaken.

The vow is present-tense and high-claim. “I will build my life upon Your love” and “I will not be shaken” need a congregation that has already heard the gospel. On their own they push the leader to say, before the song, that people should sing them only if they mean them. That is one reason this church keeps a song like this out of the regular rotation.

There is no romance in the boyfriend sense. The problem is formation-and-vow language standing where doctrine should stand.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 3

Named: worthy, the saving One, holy, without rival, love as foundation.

That is a short list, and three of the five words are praise adjectives. Holiness is said and not shown. Love is said and not defined by the cross. Justice, wrath, faithfulness, wisdom, and Fatherhood are unnamed. The portrait is the thin “great / holy / love” stack with Jesus’ name attached.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 3

The words are easy. The room already knows the song (at least four years in the snapshot). Keys on file are often E. Singability is not the question. The question is whether the lyric teaches and admonishes the body (Colossians 3:16). It does not, except at the level of “Jesus is worthy and we should trust Him.”

The chorus is the part people remember. That is also the part that does the least doctrinal work. The bridge is the part bands like to loop. That loop is the worst use.

A seeker-shaped room loves this hook. That is not congregational usefulness here.

---

## Tone

Earnest and warm. Not flippant, not sensual. The intensity sits on “wonder,” “fill me,” and “I will not be shaken.” Those are ungrounded if the hour has not already given the people something solid to stand on.

The room is quietly asked to confer a life-project on God (“I will build”). The finished work is never confessed.

---

## What the congregation never says

They never confess sin.  
They never name the cross, the blood, the resurrection, or wrath.  
They never name the Father or the Spirit.  
They never name substitution or justification.  
They never name repentance.

If this song is the only sung prayer in an hour, the gospel has not been sung.

---

## Setlist notes

**When it serves the sermon.** Almost never as the carrier. At most after a Matthew 7 sermon, and only if the leader is willing to say out loud that the lyric’s “foundation” is thinner than the text. Neighbor-love in the last chorus line can answer a brief application. It cannot carry the doctrine.

**Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The entire gospel inventory except the bare word “save.”

**What it adds to the year’s diet.** Almost nothing this catalog cannot get from stronger texts: a worthiness refrain and a neighbor-love request. It never adds sin, cross, or resurrection.

**What not to do.** Do not open with it. Do not loop the bridge. Do not use it as the song after a sermon that already lacked atonement language.

## Why this overall label

True fragments, a Savior named without His work, and a vow built on “love.” Matthew 7 ties the wise life to hearing and doing Jesus’ words. Weak means brief filler after Scripture has already done the work, and better replaced.
