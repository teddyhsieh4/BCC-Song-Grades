## Thesis

The singer asks God to change the singer's heart, to make it ever true, and to make the singer like God. God is the potter. The singer is the clay. The prayer is to be molded and made.

---

## Overall recommendation

Weak

Do not feature it. The church has logged it once, in 2024. "You are the potter, I am the clay" is the line that has to stay if it is sung at all. The rest is a request to be changed and to be like God. Jesus is not named. The cross is not named. A worshiper who does not confess Jesus can sing every line unchanged. Keep it off the list, or sing it only as a brief response after a psalm that has already said why the heart must change.

## Voice and addressee

First person singular, speaking to God. "Change my heart," "may I be like You," "I am the clay," "this is what I pray." The potter line is about God. Everything else is the request. The church would be singing one person's prayer to be remade.

---

## How the text moves

It asks twice for a changed heart, ever true, and to be like God. Then it gives the picture: potter and clay, mold me and make me, this is what I pray. The prayer returns. Nothing after the clay says how God changes a heart, or names the Son, or mentions the blood. The song moves from the request to the potter and stays inside the request.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

"You are the potter, I am the clay / Mold me and make me, this is what I pray" is the line the grade depends on. "Change my heart, O God, make it ever true / May I be like You" is the line the room will repeat. If the band keeps the change and drops the potter, the song is a wish to be different. No line is false. The potter is the only claim about God, and a chorus-only use can sing the wish without it.

## Persons of the Trinity named

"God" is the one addressed, and he is the potter. Father, Son, and Spirit are unnamed. "Like You" does not say which person the singer would resemble. The clay does not name the hands.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Yes. "Change my heart, O God, may I be like You" and "You are the potter, I am the clay" can be sung by any worshiper who wants God to reshape the heart. Nothing in the lyric names Jesus, a cross, or a resurrection. The song would not correct that worshiper.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** none. The potter and the clay are a biblical picture, used here as the whole prayer.
- **Thin:** "Make it ever true" and "May I be like You." A true heart and likeness to God are right desires. The chorus does not say what is untrue in the heart, or what likeness would look like, or what God has done to make it so.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 3

"You are the potter, I am the clay / Mold me and make me" agrees with Isaiah 64:8, "We are the clay, and you are our potter; we are all the work of your hand," and with Jeremiah 18:6, the house of Israel as clay in the potter's hand. The song makes that picture a personal prayer. "Change my heart, O God" stands near Psalm 51:10, "Create in me a clean heart, O God," and it says change and ever true rather than create and clean. "May I be like You" is a desire for conformity to God, which Scripture does command, and the line does not say the conformity is to the image of the Son. The potter line can be tied to Isaiah. The rest is the request in shorter words. The score stays at 3 because the picture is biblical and the song never says the gospel that gives a new heart.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 2

Jesus is not named. The cross, the blood, and the resurrection are absent. The potter is God unnamed as Father or Son. A changed heart, in Scripture, is tied to the new covenant and to Christ. This lyric asks for the change and does not say how God gives it. The score is 2 because the finished work is absent.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 2

"You are the potter, I am the clay" declares something about God and about the singer. "Change my heart," "make it ever true," "may I be like You," and "this is what I pray" are the request, and they are most of the song. The prayer is honest. It is also the whole confession. The clay line is one sentence inside a chorus about the singer's heart.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 3

He is the potter who molds and makes. He is the one the singer asks to be like, so the likeness is the hope that he is true and good. Holiness, justice, wrath, love, and faithfulness are unnamed. The potter is a real picture of his freedom over the clay. The chorus does not say any other attribute.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 3

One log, in 2024, means it is rare here. The words are few and easy to repeat without thinking. A congregation can learn to ask God to change the heart and to call him the potter. They cannot learn Christ or the new covenant from this chorus. "Ever true" and "like You" will be what they remember if the potter line is not sung. The potter is the line worth keeping, and only after a text that has already named the Son.

---

## Tone

The tone is quiet and earnest. The line that sets it is "Change my heart, O God, make it ever true." The potter line is plainer and heavier than the wish to be like him. Nothing in the chorus is harsh. The prayer is small on purpose, and the smallness is the whole song.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names Jesus, never says that the heart is changed by the Spirit through the gospel, and never says that the potter's hands are the hands that were pierced.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Isaiah 64:8 or Jeremiah 18, after the reading, and only for a short response. It does not serve a sermon on the new birth by itself.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The Son and the means of a new heart. Pair it with *Rock of Ages* or *Jesus, Your Mercy* so the clay is not the only word about how God deals with the singer.
- **Diet sentence.** Once in this window, it adds a potter-and-clay prayer. It never adds the gospel.
- **What not to do.** Do not loop "may I be like You" and drop "You are the potter." Do not use the chorus as the invitation that explains salvation.
