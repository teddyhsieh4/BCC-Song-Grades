## Thesis

The only hope and confidence in life and death is Christ alone: the Redeemer whose blood makes God’s goodness known, the Rock who holds faith in trial, and the living Lord who will raise his people and destroy sin and death.

---

## Overall recommendation

Excellent / Prefer

Feature it freely, and keep the second and third verses: one grounds God’s goodness in the Redeemer’s blood, and the other answers the grave with the living Christ. It can anchor a service on 1 Corinthians 15, Romans 14:7–9, or the first question of the Heidelberg Catechism. The church has sung it 8 times across at least four years of the 2023–2026 window.

## Voice and addressee

First-person plural (“our hope,” “our souls,” “we confess”). The congregation answers the questions in the verses, and the chorus calls the room to sing and confess together.

---

## How the text moves

Each verse asks a question and answers it with Christ. Verse 1 asks about our hope, verse 2 about where God’s goodness is known, and verse 3 about what we will sing at the grave. The answers move from belonging to Christ, to His blood, to His resurrection and ours.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

The second verse (“Where is his grace and goodness known? / In our great Redeemer’s blood”) and the third (“Unto the grave, what will we sing? / ‘Christ, he lives; Christ, he lives!’ / … Then sin and death will be destroyed”). The first verse alone names Christ as hope but leaves the blood and the empty grave unsaid.

## Persons of the Trinity named

Christ is named throughout as hope, Redeemer, Rock, and the living Lord. God is named as good and as the one whose command holds the days. The Spirit is not named.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. A vague theist could not sing “In our great Redeemer’s blood” or “Christ, he lives” at the grave without changing them.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** none that affect the claim.
- **Thin:** “God is good, God is good” is true but thin on its own. The next line grounds it in the Redeemer’s blood.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“What is our hope in life and death? / Christ alone” and “our souls to him belong” are Romans 14:7–9, in the words of the Heidelberg answer. “In our great Redeemer’s blood” states the ground of grace. “Christ, he lives” and “we will rise to meet the Lord / Then sin and death will be destroyed” are 1 Corinthians 15:20–26 and 1 Thessalonians 4:16–17.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

Christ is the only hope, the Redeemer whose blood shows God’s goodness, and the living Lord who will raise His people. The blood and the resurrection are both stated, so the titles are tied to His work.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 5

The questions are answered with facts about Christ, and the “we” is the whole church confessing them. There are no private vows.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 4

God is good, and the song shows where: in the Redeemer’s blood. Christ holds the days, stands above the trial, and is the Rock. The score is 4 because holiness and justice are not named, and “God is good” is repeated before it is grounded.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The question-and-answer form is easy to follow, and the chorus repeats one confession. A congregation that sings it learns to answer the first question of the Heidelberg Catechism with Christ, His blood, and His resurrection.

---

## Tone

The tone is reverent and settled. Verse 3 faces the grave plainly, and the answer is “Christ, he lives.”

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names the Spirit or wrath, and substitution goes no further than “Redeemer’s blood.” When the sermon needs propitiation, pair it with In Christ Alone, whose second verse states it.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** 1 Corinthians 15, Romans 14:7–9, Hebrews 9–10, or any text on our only comfort in life and death.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The Spirit, if the sermon needs Him named, and a fuller statement of propitiation on a Romans 3 or cross text.
- **Diet sentence.** Adds a corporate confession of belonging to Christ and resurrection hope at the grave. It is not a song of repentance.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop the second or third verse, and do not follow it with a vague chorus about hope that drops the blood and the empty grave.
