## Thesis

Christ is our wisdom. His purposes are unnumbered and good. He rules with power. The church clings to His commandments, strengthened by His faithful Word. Once in foolishness they scorned Him, but His blood has ransomed them. They adore Him for the beauty of the cross.

---

## Overall recommendation

Excellent / Prefer

Feature it, and sing every verse. The fourth verse is the one that must stay: it names the cross, our former scorn, and the blood that ransomed us. The earlier verses teach trust in Christ’s purposes when His ways are hidden. The church has sung it 9 times across at least three years of the 2023–2026 window.

## Voice and addressee

Corporate throughout (“we,” “us”), addressed to Christ as wisdom. It is a prayer for trust and a confession of what His blood has done.

---

## How the text moves

The first three verses ask for trust and gladness when God’s ways are hidden, and for the strength to follow His Word. The fourth verse gives the reason to trust Him: the cross, where the blood ransomed people who once scorned Him.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“Christ our wisdom, we adore You / For the beauty of the cross / Once in foolishness we scorned You / But Your blood has ransomed us.”

Without that stanza the song is a good prayer of trust in God’s wisdom with no cross in it.

## Persons of the Trinity named

Christ is named throughout. God is named as the one He is. The Spirit is not named. The blood and the cross are Christ’s.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Not the whole song. A vague theist could sing the earlier verses about God’s purposes. “But Your blood has ransomed us” in the fourth verse would stop them.

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“Christ our wisdom” is 1 Corinthians 1:24 and 1:30. “Your blood has ransomed us” is 1 Peter 1:18–19 and Mark 10:45. “Strengthened by Your faithful word” confesses the sufficiency of Scripture, and “You are God and we are not” is the line between Creator and creature.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

The fourth verse names the cross, our sin as scorn, and the blood that ransomed. That joins the song’s praise of Christ’s wisdom to His saving work, as 1 Corinthians 1 does.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 5

The requests for trust and clearer sight answer what the song has already said about Christ. The voice is corporate, and the singers make no vows.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 5

Christ’s purposes are unnumbered and good, He ordains joy and sadness to fulfill His plan, and He rules every raging flood. He gives a faithful Word, ransoms by His blood, and has a humble heart to save. Wisdom, sovereignty, faithfulness, and mercy are all named.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The corporate voice fits the gathering, and the lines are dense without being hard to sing. The song teaches a congregation to trust God’s wisdom when life is hard, and to see that wisdom at the cross.

---

## Tone

The tone is reverent and steady. It names hardship honestly, sadness and floods included, and answers it with trust.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names the Spirit or the resurrection. The cross and the ransom are present.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** 1 Corinthians 1, any text on the wisdom of God, the cross, or the ransom. It is especially useful after a sermon on God’s hidden purposes in suffering.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** Very little. A song about the Spirit or the resurrection can sit beside it if the service needs that.
- **Diet sentence.** Adds trust in God’s wisdom tied to the cross. It does not add the empty tomb.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop the fourth verse, and do not sing the earlier verses alone.
