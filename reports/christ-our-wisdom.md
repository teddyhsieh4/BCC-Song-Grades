## Thesis

Christ is our wisdom. His purposes are unnumbered and good. He rules with power. The church clings to His commandments, strengthened by His faithful Word. Once in foolishness they scorned Him, but His blood has ransomed them. They adore Him for the beauty of the cross.

## Voice and addressee

Corporate throughout (“we,” “us”). Addressed to Christ as wisdom. It is prayer and confession of who He is and what the blood has done.

---

## How the text moves

It starts with the humbling when His ways are hidden, the unnumbered purposes that are good and glorious, and the request for trust and clearer sight that He is God and we are not. The second verse asks for gladness when understanding fails, naming that He ordains joy and sadness to fulfill His plan, and that He rules with power. The third verse commits to follow though the way is veiled, clinging to His commandments, strengthened by His faithful Word, and confessing that they will never be abandoned. The fourth verse adores Him for the beauty of the cross, names the former foolish scorn, and the blood that has ransomed, and ends with the endless mercies of His humble heart to save.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“Christ our wisdom, we adore You / For the beauty of the cross / Once in foolishness we scorned You / But Your blood has ransomed us.”

Dropping that stanza leaves a strong song of trust in the wisdom of God. Keeping it makes the song preach the cross and the ransom. Both are required for the full text.

## Persons of the Trinity named

Christ is named throughout. God is named as the one He is. The Spirit is not named. The blood and the cross are Christ’s.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. The beauty of the cross, the former scorn, and the blood that has ransomed are required. A vague theist can sing the earlier verses about purposes and trust. The fourth verse forces the gospel.

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“Christ our wisdom” is 1 Corinthians 1:24 and 1:30. “Your blood has ransomed us” is 1 Peter 1:18–19 and Mark 10:45. “Strengthened by Your faithful word” is the ordinary sense of the sufficiency of Scripture. “You are God and we are not” is the ordinary sense of the Creator-creature distinction. The lines stay inside the texts.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

Christ is named. The cross is named. The blood is named. The ransom is named. The former scorn is named. The song does not stop at titles or atmosphere. It moves from the wisdom of God to the blood that ransomed.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 5

The lyric declares who Christ is and what the blood has done. The requests for trust and clearer sight are responses to those facts. There are no high-claim private vows that float free of the gospel. The voice is corporate.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 5

Christ has purposes unnumbered, each one good and glorious. He ordains joy and sadness to fulfill His perfect plan. He rules with power over every raging flood. He has commandments and a faithful Word. He is the beauty of the cross. His blood has ransomed. He has a humble heart to save. The attributes are specific and tied to the wisdom, the Word, and the finished work.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The language is plain. The claims are dense. The congregation can learn the wisdom of Christ and the ransom of the blood by singing these lines. The corporate voice fits the gathering. Singability is high, and the teaching is the reason for the 5.

---

## Tone

Reverent and steady. It does not ask the room to confer on Christ anything He does not already possess. The intensity stays inside the purposes, the Word, and the blood.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names the Spirit or the resurrection. Those are small gaps. The main gospel elements of the cross and the ransom are present.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** 1 Corinthians 1, any text on the wisdom of God, the cross, or the ransom. Especially useful after a sermon that has named both the hidden purposes and the blood.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** Very little. A Spirit-focused song or a fuller resurrection confession can sit beside it if the service needs that voice.
- **Diet sentence.** It adds a dense confession of Christ as wisdom whose blood has ransomed, and of the church that clings to His Word. It does not add a full narrative of the empty tomb.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop the fourth verse. Do not let the earlier verses stand alone without the cross and the ransom.
