## Thesis

Christ is the sure and steady Anchor in the fury of the storm, when the winds of doubt blow, when temptation claims the battle, through the floods of unbelief, and as we face the wave of death. The singer will hold fast to the Anchor. It shall never be removed. Though the singer justly stands accused, the Anchor goes deeper still. The soul is told to lift its eyes to Calvary, where His love is forever proved. We will cross that great horizon, clouds behind and life secure.

---

## Overall recommendation

Excellent / Prefer

Feature it. Keep the lines about the just accusation and the call to look to Calvary, because they are what make the Anchor hold. The church has sung it 7 times across at least four years of the 2023–2026 window.

## Voice and addressee

Mostly first-person singular, speaking to the singer’s own soul, with first-person plural in the last verse and tag. It mixes confession and command (“O my soul, now / Lift your eyes to Calvary”), and the whole church can sing it.

---

## How the text moves

Each verse names a storm: doubt, temptation, unbelief, and finally death. Each time the answer is the Anchor, and in verse 3 the answer is Calvary itself. The song ends with the church crossing the horizon of death into glory.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“Though I justly stand accused” and “Hopeless somehow, O my soul, now / Lift your eyes to Calvary / This my ballast of assurance / See His love forever proved.” Without them, the song tells the singer to hold on without saying why the Anchor holds.

## Persons of the Trinity named

Christ is named as the Anchor and the shore of our salvation. The Father and the Spirit are not named. The work of the Son is explicit.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. A vague theist could not sing “Though I justly stand accused” followed by “Lift your eyes to Calvary” without changing them.

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“Christ the sure and steady Anchor” and “It shall never be removed” are Hebrews 6:19. “Though I justly stand accused” is Romans 3:23 and Romans 8:33. “Lift your eyes to Calvary / See His love forever proved” is Romans 5:8 and Galatians 2:20. “As we face the wave of death” and “trials give way to glory” are 1 Corinthians 15:55 and Romans 8:18.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

Christ is the Anchor and the shore of our salvation. The song names Calvary and admits that the singer “justly” stands accused, so the cross answers real guilt. Substitution is implied there and never spelled out.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

The song declares what Christ is, and the response is to hold fast to Him. The score is 4 because the lyric stays with the singer in the storm more than with the cross itself. The leader does not need to qualify any line.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 4

Christ is steady, ever faithful, and ever true, and His love is proved at Calvary. Justice appears in the accusation the singer deserves. The score is 4 because faithfulness and love carry the song and other attributes are not named.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The storm imagery is easy to follow, and the refrain (“I will hold fast to the Anchor / It shall never be removed”) is short. It teaches a congregation where to look in doubt, temptation, and death: to the cross.

---

## Tone

The tone is reverent and honest. It admits doubt, temptation, and fear of death, and answers each with Christ.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names the Spirit. The main gospel elements of guilt and Calvary are present.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Hebrews 6, Romans 5, Romans 8, 1 Corinthians 15, or any text on hope in suffering and death. It is especially useful after a sermon that has already named the cross and the empty tomb.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The Spirit, if the service has not named Him.
- **Diet sentence.** Adds assurance for believers in doubt, temptation, and death, tied to Calvary. It adds little on the Spirit.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop the lines about the just accusation and Calvary, and do not sing the refrain alone.
