## Thesis

Christ is the sure and steady Anchor in the fury of the storm, when the winds of doubt blow, when temptation claims the battle, through the floods of unbelief, and as we face the wave of death. The singer will hold fast to the Anchor. It shall never be removed. Though the singer justly stands accused, the Anchor goes deeper still. The soul is told to lift its eyes to Calvary, where His love is forever proved. We will cross that great horizon, clouds behind and life secure.

## Voice and addressee

Mostly first-person singular addressing the soul and the congregation, with first-person plural in the final verse and tag. It is declaration and command. The congregation can own the confession that Christ is the Anchor, the just accusation, the lift of the eyes to Calvary, and the hold-fast that shall never be removed.

---

## How the text moves

It opens by naming Christ the sure and steady Anchor in the fury of the storm, when the winds of doubt blow through the singer and the sails have all been torn, in the suffering and the sorrow when sinking hopes are few. The refrain is the hold-fast: “I will hold fast to the Anchor / It shall never be removed.” Verse 2 names the Anchor while the tempest rages, when temptation claims the battle and it seems the night has won, and that the Anchor goes deeper still though the singer justly stands accused. Verse 3 names the Anchor through the floods of unbelief, commands the soul to lift its eyes to Calvary, and names that love as the ballast of assurance forever proved. Verse 4 names the Anchor as we face the wave of death, when trials give way to glory, as we draw our final breath, and that we will cross that great horizon, clouds behind and life secure. The tag names Christ the shore of our salvation, ever faithful, ever true.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“Though I justly stand accused” and “Hopeless somehow, O my soul, now / Lift your eyes to Calvary / This my ballast of assurance / See His love forever proved.”

Dropping those lines leaves the hold-fast to the Anchor without the named just accusation and the lift of the eyes to Calvary where His love is forever proved. The rest of the lyric still names the Anchor in the storm, but the specific gospel act that shows why the Anchor holds is in those lines.

## Persons of the Trinity named

Christ is named as the Anchor and the shore of our salvation. The Father and the Spirit are not named. The work of the Son is explicit.

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. The just accusation, the lift of the eyes to Calvary where His love is forever proved, and the Anchor that shall never be removed cannot be sung by a vague theist without correction. The hold-fast is tied to that finished work.

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“Christ the sure and steady Anchor” is the ordinary sense of Hebrews 6:19. “Though I justly stand accused” is the ordinary sense of Romans 3:23 and Romans 8:33. “Lift your eyes to Calvary / See His love forever proved” is the ordinary sense of Romans 5:8 and Galatians 2:20. “It shall never be removed” is the ordinary sense of Hebrews 6:19. “As we face the wave of death” and “trials give way to glory” is the ordinary sense of 1 Corinthians 15:55 and Romans 8:18. The lines stay inside ordinary biblical sense. The score is 5 because the lyric names the ordinary sense of those texts.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

Christ is named as the Anchor and the shore of our salvation. Substitution is implied in the just accusation and the lift of the eyes to Calvary where His love is forever proved. The cross is named. The song does not stop at the Anchor. It preaches the finished work that makes the hold-fast possible.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

The text is declaration of what Christ is and of the ordinary response of holding fast. The first-person singular is testimony grounded in the just accusation and the lift of the eyes to Calvary. The singer does not make high-claim present-tense vows without grounding. The score is 4 because the lyric stays focused on the Anchor in the storm rather than a fuller narrative of the finished work.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 4

Christ is the sure and steady Anchor, the one whose love is forever proved at Calvary, the shore of our salvation, ever faithful, ever true. The attributes are specific and tied to the Anchor that holds in the storm and the love proved at the cross. Holiness and justice appear as the just accusation and the love that answers it. The score is 4 because the lyric stays focused on the Anchor and the proved love rather than a broader list of attributes.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The language is plain. The lyric teaches that Christ is the sure and steady Anchor, that the singer justly stands accused, that the soul must lift its eyes to Calvary where His love is forever proved, and that the hold-fast shall never be removed. The congregation can learn the ordinary sense of Hebrews 6:19 and Romans 5:8 by singing these lines. The singular and plural voices are declaration the whole church can own. Singability is high, and the teaching is the reason for the 5. Keep the lines about the just accusation and the lift of the eyes to Calvary so the hold-fast stays attached to the gospel.

---

## Tone

Reverent and honest. It does not ask the room to confer on Christ what He has not already done. The intensity stays inside the Anchor that holds in the storm, the just accusation, the lift of the eyes to Calvary, and the hold-fast that shall never be removed.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names the Spirit. That is a small gap. The main gospel elements of the just accusation and the lift of the eyes to Calvary where His love is forever proved are present.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Hebrews 6, Romans 5, Romans 8, 1 Corinthians 15, any text on the Anchor of the soul, the just accusation, or the hope that holds in suffering and death. Especially useful after a sermon that has named the cross and the empty tomb.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The Spirit if the service has not already supplied it.
- **Diet sentence.** It adds a dense confession that Christ is the sure and steady Anchor, that the singer justly stands accused, that the soul must lift its eyes to Calvary where His love is forever proved, and that the hold-fast shall never be removed. It does not add a full narrative of the Spirit’s ongoing work.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop the lines about the just accusation and the lift of the eyes to Calvary. Do not let the hold-fast stand alone without the named love proved at the cross.
