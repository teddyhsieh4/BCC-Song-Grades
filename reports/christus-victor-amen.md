## Thesis

The Most High, King of the ages and Great I AM, has redeemed His people by the blood and led them through mighty waters. He is strength, song, and sure salvation. The Son of Man, dwelling among us, lived, died, and rose as the spotless Lamb. The King of the nations will stand as Christ the Victor. The church answers with blessing, honor, glory, and power to the Lamb upon the throne, and joins every tribe and tongue in the Amen.

---

## Overall recommendation

**Excellent / Prefer**

Feature it. Sing all three verses. Verse 1 names the blood that redeemed and the leading through the waters. Verse 2 names the Son of Man sent for sinners, the blood, the spotless Lamb, and the living, dying, and rising. Without those lines the song is true ascription to the King of the ages that has not named the cross or the resurrection.

The church has sung it for at least two years (6 logs in the 2023–2026 snapshot). It is already known.

## Voice and addressee

The verses address the Most High in the second person. The chorus is the congregation’s ascription to the Lamb upon the throne. The voice is corporate throughout.

---

## How the text moves

Verse 1 names the Most High as King of the ages and Great I AM, the blood that redeemed, and the leading through mighty waters. He is strength, song, and sure salvation. The chorus ascribes blessing, honor, glory, and power to the Lamb for the battle won. Verse 2 names the Most High dwelling among us as Son of Man sent for sinners, the blood, the spotless Lamb, and the living, dying, and rising. The chorus expands to every tribe and tongue joining the angels in the triumph of the Son. Verse 3 names the King of the nations, robed in praise and crowned with splendor, who will stand as Christ the Victor. The bridge is the Amen. The tag sings the victory of the Lamb.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

Do not drop verse 1. “By the blood You have redeemed us, / Led us through mighty waters” is the redemption and the exodus pattern. Without it the church has praised the King of the ages and has not named the blood.

Do not drop verse 2. “O Most High, dwelling among us, / Son of man sent for sinners. / By Your blood You have redeemed us; / Spotless Lamb, mighty Savior / Who lived, who died, who rose victorious” is the incarnation, the sending for sinners, the blood, and the resurrection. Without it the ascription to the Lamb has not named the work that placed Him on the throne.

No line is false.

## Persons of the Trinity named

The Most High is addressed as King of the ages, Great I AM, and King of the nations. The Son is named as Son of Man, spotless Lamb, mighty Savior, and Christ the Victor. The Spirit is unnamed. The work is the Son’s.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Not the whole song. Verse 1 names the blood that redeemed. Verse 2 names the Son of Man sent for sinners, the blood, the spotless Lamb, and the rising. A vague theist could not sing those lines as written.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

**Unbiblical.** None.

**Non-biblical.** None.

**Thin.** Burial is not named between death and rising. The Spirit is unnamed. The lyric does not need those details to make its claim.

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“O Most High, King of the ages, / Great I AM, God of wonders, / By the blood You have redeemed us, / Led us through mighty waters” is Exodus 15:1–2, Isaiah 43:16, and 1 Peter 1:18–19. The exodus pattern and the blood are the ordinary sense of those texts.

“O Most High, dwelling among us, / Son of man sent for sinners. / By Your blood You have redeemed us; / Spotless Lamb, mighty Savior / Who lived, who died, who rose victorious” is John 1:14, Mark 10:45, 1 Peter 1:19, and 1 Corinthians 15:3–4. The sequence is the gospel sequence.

“O Most High, King of the nations, / … When You stand, Christ the Victor / Who was and is and is forever” is Revelation 11:15, Revelation 19:11–16, and Revelation 1:8. The return as Victor is the ordinary hope of those texts.

“Now to the Lamb upon the throne / Be blessing, honor, glory, power / For the battle You have won” is Revelation 5:12–13. The ascription is the text’s ascription.

The lines use the ordinary sense of those texts.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

Christ is named as the Most High dwelling among us, the Son of Man, the spotless Lamb, the mighty Savior, and Christ the Victor. The blood is named twice. The living, dying, and rising are named. The battle is won. The Lamb is upon the throne. The work is not left as a title.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 5

The main claim is objective. The Most High redeemed by the blood. The Son of Man lived, died, and rose. The King will stand as Victor. The chorus is the congregation’s ascription in response. There is no personal vow that must be meant before the song may be sung.

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The verses are clear. The chorus is short. The Amen is simple. The words are ordinary. The church has already sung it. The people learn the shape of the Most High’s work from the blood and the waters, through the Son of Man’s living, dying, and rising, to the standing of Christ the Victor. They do not need a weekly explanation of any line.

If time is short, do not drop verse 1 or verse 2.

---

## Tone

The tone is glad and sober. “Hallelujah! Amen!” answers the battle won and the triumph of the Son. There is no intensity beyond the text.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names the Spirit, the burial, or present intercession. It never says “in our place” in those words, though the blood that redeemed and the Son of Man sent for sinners state the same fact.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Exodus 15, Revelation 5, 1 Corinthians 15, or any text on the victory of the Lamb and the return of the King. It works as a response after the sermon has opened the passion or the triumph.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The Spirit when the day’s text requires the third Person. Explicit burial language if the sermon has required it and no other song supplies it.
- **Diet sentence.** It adds the blood that redeemed, the Son of Man who lived, died, and rose, the Lamb upon the throne, and Christ the Victor. It does not add the Spirit’s naming.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop verse 1 or verse 2. Do not treat the Amen bridge as a standalone tag after a thinner song. The verses supply the reason for the ascription.
