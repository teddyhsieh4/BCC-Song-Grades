## Thesis

The Father has blessed His people in Christ with every spiritual blessing. He chose them to be pure and blameless, adopted them through the Son, redeemed them by sacrifice, made Christ the head of all, and sealed them with the Spirit until the final redemption. The church answers with praise to the God who saves.

---

## Overall recommendation

**Excellent / Prefer**

Feature it. Sing all three verses. Verse 1 names the Father’s election and adoption. Verse 2 names the washing of sins, redemption through sacrifice, and Christ’s headship. Verse 3 names the Spirit’s seal and the praise of the Three-in-One. Any verse dropped leaves a true line of Ephesians 1 incomplete.

The church has sung it for at least four years (6 logs in the 2023–2026 snapshot). It is already known.

## Voice and addressee

The verses call the congregation to praise God. “Come praise and glorify our God.” The chorus turns and addresses Him: “To the praise of Your glory.” The voice is corporate throughout. There is no solo “I.”

---

## How the text moves

Verse 1 begins with the Father of our Lord and the blessings poured in Christ. It names election to purity and adoption through the Son. Verse 2 moves to the grace given in Christ, the washing of sins, redemption through sacrifice, and the mystery that Christ is the head of all. Verse 3 names belief in the Word, the Spirit’s seal, the guarantee until redemption is done, and endless praise to the Three-in-One. The chorus returns the same ascription after each verse.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

Do not drop verse 2. “In Him our sins are washed away / Redeemed through sacrifice” is the gospel content. Without it the church has praised election and adoption and has not named the washing or the sacrifice.

Do not drop verse 3 if the Spirit or the Trinity is the day’s text. “Through our faith we have a seal / The Spirit of the Lord” and “To God, the Three in One” complete the Ephesians 1 movement.

No line is false.

## Persons of the Trinity named

The Father is named as the Father of our Lord. The Son is named as Christ, the Son, and the head of all. The Spirit is named as the Spirit of the Lord who seals. The final line praises “God, the Three in One.”

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Not the whole song. Verse 1 names the Father of our Lord and adoption through His Son. Verse 2 names sins washed and redemption through sacrifice. Verse 3 names the Spirit and the Three-in-One. A vague theist could not sing the verses as written.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

**Unbiblical.** None.

**Non-biblical.** None.

**Thin.** The cross is named as “sacrifice” rather than as blood or Calvary. Wrath, burial, and resurrection are not named. The lyric follows Ephesians 1:3–14 and does not need those details to make its claim.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“Come praise and glorify our God / The Father of our Lord / In Christ He has in heav’nly realms / His blessings on us poured” is Ephesians 1:3. “For pure and blameless in His sight / He destined us to be” is Ephesians 1:4. “And now we’ve been adopted through / His Son eternally” is Ephesians 1:5.

“In Him our sins are washed away / Redeemed through sacrifice” is Ephesians 1:7. “In Him God has made known to us / The myst’ry of His will / That Christ should be the head of all / His purpose to fulfill” is Ephesians 1:9–10.

“For we’ve believed the Word / And through our faith we have a seal / The Spirit of the Lord” is Ephesians 1:13. “The Spirit guarantees our hope / Until redemption’s done” is Ephesians 1:14. “Until we join in endless praise / To God, the Three in One” gathers the whole passage into trinitarian praise.

The lines use the ordinary sense of Ephesians 1. Election, adoption, redemption, headship, and sealing are stated as the text states them.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

Christ is the One in whom every blessing is poured, through whom adoption comes, in whom sins are washed and redemption is accomplished by sacrifice, and who is made head of all. The finished work is named as redemption through sacrifice. The Name is not used without the work.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 5

The hymn declares what God has done in Christ and calls the congregation to praise. There is no first-person vow, no private feeling, and no romance. The “we” of “we’ve believed” and “we join in endless praise” is the church confessing what is true of those who are in Christ.

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The verses are compact. The chorus is clear. The words follow a single chapter the church already knows how to hear. Keys on file include A and A#. The people learn the shape of Ephesians 1 by singing it. They do not need a weekly explanation of any line.

If time is short, do not drop verse 2.

---

## Tone

The tone is public and glad. “Come praise and glorify” is a call, not a mood. The ascriptions in the chorus follow the works named in the verses. The final line is ordinary trinitarian praise.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names the cross as blood or Calvary, never says wrath, burial, or resurrection, and never names repentance as an act. It never says “in our place” in those words, though “redeemed through sacrifice” states the purchase.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Ephesians 1, or any text on election, adoption, redemption, or the sealing of the Spirit. It works as a call to worship that already teaches the doctrine, or as a response after the sermon has opened the same chapter.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The events of the cross and resurrection when the day’s text is the passion or 1 Corinthians 15. Explicit substitution language if the sermon has required it and no other song supplies it.
- **Diet sentence.** It adds the whole movement of Ephesians 1 in the people’s mouth: election, adoption, redemption, headship, and the Spirit’s seal, ending in trinitarian praise. It does not add the narrative of the cross or the resurrection.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop verse 2. Do not treat the chorus as a generic “God who saves” tag after a thinner song. The verses supply the content of the praise.
