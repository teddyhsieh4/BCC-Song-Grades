## Thesis

Jesus sought the singer when he was a stranger, wandering from the fold of God. To rescue him from danger, Jesus interposed His precious blood. The singer is prone to wander, yet asks that grace bind the wandering heart and seal it for the courts above.

---

## Overall recommendation

Excellent / Prefer

Feature it, and keep the full text. The second stanza is the one that must stay: Jesus sought the singer as a stranger and “Interposed His precious blood.” The church has sung it 9 times across at least four years of the 2023–2026 window.

## Voice and addressee

First-person singular, addressing God and naming Jesus. It is testimony of what Jesus did and a prayer that grace would hold a wandering heart, and the whole congregation can own it.

---

## How the text moves

The first stanza asks God to tune the heart to sing His grace. The second remembers how far His help has brought the singer, and how Jesus rescued him by His blood. The third confesses the heart’s tendency to wander and asks God to seal it for heaven.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“Jesus sought me when a stranger, / Wandering from the fold of God; / He, to rescue me from danger, / Interposed His precious blood.”

The rest of the hymn rests on that stanza. Without it, the hymn is praise and a prayer for help with no rescue named.

## Persons of the Trinity named

Jesus is named. God is addressed. The Spirit is not named. The blood and the seeking are Jesus’.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. A vague theist could not sing “Jesus sought me when a stranger” or “Interposed His precious blood” without changing them.

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“Jesus sought me when a stranger, / Wandering from the fold of God” is Luke 15 and Romans 5:6–8. “He, to rescue me from danger, / Interposed His precious blood” is the substitution of 1 Peter 1:18–19 and Hebrews 9. “Here I raise my Ebenezer” is 1 Samuel 7:12. “Prone to wander” is the remaining sin and the need for grace.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

The second stanza says Jesus sought the singer while he was lost and put His own blood between the singer and danger. That is substitution in the hymn’s own picture, and it is tied to Jesus by name.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

The second stanza declares what Jesus has done. The first and third stanzas are the singer’s requests and his confession that he is “prone to wander.” Those rest on the blood, but they are why the score is 4.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 4

God is the fount of every blessing, the source of mercy that never stops, and the one whose help brings His people safely home. Jesus is the seeker who gave His blood. Holiness and justice appear mainly as the danger the blood rescues from, which is why this is a 4.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The congregation already knows this hymn well. It teaches the seeking Savior and the believer’s ongoing struggle with sin in words a congregation can say honestly. The singular voice is testimony the whole church can own.

---

## Tone

The tone is reverent and honest. “Prone to wander, Lord, I feel it” admits sin plainly, and the hymn answers it with grace.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names the Spirit or the resurrection. When the sermon needs the resurrection, pair it with In Christ Alone, whose third verse names it.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Luke 15, Romans 5, 1 Peter 1, 1 Samuel 7, or any text on the seeking Savior, the blood that rescues, or the believer’s ongoing need of grace.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The resurrection, and the Spirit, if the service has not named them.
- **Diet sentence.** Adds an honest confession of a wandering heart, answered by the blood. It does not add the empty tomb.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop the second stanza, and do not sing the first and third alone.
