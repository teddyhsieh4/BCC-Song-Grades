## Thesis

Jesus sought the singer when he was a stranger, wandering from the fold of God. To rescue him from danger, Jesus interposed His precious blood. The singer is prone to wander, yet asks that grace bind the wandering heart and seal it for the courts above.

---

## Overall recommendation

Excellent / Prefer

Feature it. The second stanza names the seeking Savior, the stranger status, the fold of God, and the interposing of the precious blood. That line is required. Keep the full text.

## Voice and addressee

First-person singular addressing God and naming Jesus. It is testimony of what Jesus has done and a prayer that grace would hold the wandering heart. The congregation can own the confession.

---

## How the text moves

It starts with the call for the fount of every blessing to tune the heart to sing grace, with streams of mercy calling for praise. It names the mount of redeeming love. The second stanza raises the Ebenezer, recalls that Jesus sought the singer when a stranger wandering from the fold, and that He interposed His precious blood. The third stanza confesses the debt to grace, the proneness to wander, and the request that the heart be taken and sealed for the courts above.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“Jesus sought me when a stranger, / Wandering from the fold of God; / He, to rescue me from danger, / Interposed His precious blood.”

Dropping that stanza loses the explicit seeking, the stranger status, and the interposing of the blood. The rest of the hymn rests on that claim.

## Persons of the Trinity named

Jesus is named. God is addressed. The Spirit is not named. The blood and the seeking are Jesus’.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. The seeking of Jesus when a stranger, the interposing of the precious blood, and the mount of redeeming love are required. A vague theist cannot sing these lines without correction.

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“Jesus sought me when a stranger, / Wandering from the fold of God” is Luke 15 and Romans 5:6–8. “He, to rescue me from danger, / Interposed His precious blood” is the substitution of 1 Peter 1:18–19 and Hebrews 9. “Here I raise my Ebenezer” is 1 Samuel 7:12. “Prone to wander” is the ordinary sense of the remaining sin and the need for grace. The lines stay inside ordinary biblical sense.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

Jesus is named. The seeking is named. The stranger status is named. The precious blood is named. The interposing is named. The song does not stop at titles or atmosphere. It preaches the finished work that sought and rescued.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

The second stanza declares what Jesus has done. The first and third stanzas include the singer’s request and the confession of proneness to wander. Those are grounded in the blood and the seeking, which is why the score is 4 rather than 5. The vows rest on the interposed blood.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 4

God is the fount of every blessing, the source of streams of mercy never ceasing, the one of redeeming love, the one whose help has brought the singer thus far, and the one whose good pleasure brings safely home. Jesus is the seeker who interposed the precious blood. The attributes are specific and tied to the mercy and the blood. Holiness and justice appear mainly as the danger from which the blood rescues.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The language is plain. The claims are dense. The congregation can learn the seeking Savior and the interposed blood by singing the second stanza. The singular voice is testimony the whole church can own. Singability is high, and the teaching is the reason for the 5.

---

## Tone

Reverent and honest. It does not ask the room to confer on Jesus anything He has not already accomplished. The intensity stays inside the interposed blood and the confession of wandering.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names the Spirit or the resurrection. Those are small gaps. The main gospel elements of seeking and blood are present.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Luke 15, Romans 5, 1 Peter 1, any text on the seeking Savior or the blood that rescues. Especially useful after a sermon that has named the stranger status and the interposed blood.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The resurrection and a fuller naming of the Spirit if the service has not already supplied them.
- **Diet sentence.** It adds a dense confession that Jesus sought the stranger and interposed His precious blood. It does not add a full narrative of the empty tomb.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop the second stanza. Do not let the first and third stanzas stand alone without the interposed blood.
