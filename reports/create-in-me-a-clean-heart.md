## Thesis

The singer asks God to create a clean heart and to renew a right spirit, prays not to be cast out or to lose the Holy Spirit, and asks for the joy of salvation and a restored soul.

---

## Overall recommendation

Acceptable with notes

Use it when the sermon is Psalm 51, and not as a general opener. The church has logged it once in this window, in 2024. The lines that have to stay are “Create in me a clean heart” and “Restore to me the joy of Thy salvation.” “Take not Thy Holy Spirit from me” is David's sentence, and it needs the sermon with it. Jesus is not named. The blood is not named. Pair a song that says the cross. Do not teach from this chorus that a Christian can lose the Spirit.

## Voice and addressee

First person singular, speaking to God. “Create in me,” “Cast me not away,” “Take not Thy Holy Spirit from me,” “Restore to me.” The addressee is “O Lord my God” and “O Lord.” The church sings David's prayer in the singular. That fits a psalm. It is still one voice, even when the room sings it together.

---

## How the text moves

It opens with the ask that gives the song its title: a clean heart, and a right spirit renewed. The verse asks not to be cast away from God's presence and not to have the Holy Spirit taken. It asks for the joy of salvation restored, and it repeats the right spirit. The bridge asks the Lord to renew the singer's spirit and restore the singer's soul. The prayer does not move on to a confession of a particular sin, or to a sacrifice, or to the Son. It stays on the work the singer asks God to do in him.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“Create in me a clean heart O Lord my God / And renew a right spirit within me” is the line the grade depends on, with “Restore to me the joy of Thy salvation.” “Take not Thy Holy Spirit from me” is Scripture in this song, and it is the line that will mislead the room if it is sung with no explanation. Cutting it would leave a simpler prayer for a clean heart and for joy, and it would also hide the sentence the sermon has to handle. No line denies a text. The bridge, “restore my soul,” can be dropped without changing the psalm the chorus is quoting. The Spirit line cannot be dropped silently, and it cannot be featured alone.

## Persons of the Trinity named

“Thy Holy Spirit” names the Spirit, and the prayer fears the Spirit's removal. “O Lord my God” is the Lord of the psalm. The Father and the Son are unnamed. Nothing in the lyric says that the clean heart is the Son's work, or that the Spirit seals the believer.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

A person who prays to God for a clean heart can sing the chorus without saying the name of Jesus. “Take not Thy Holy Spirit from me” and “the joy of Thy salvation” assume the God who gives his Spirit and who saves, which a vague theist does not mean by those words. They still do not say Jesus, the cross, or the resurrection. Someone who denies the Spirit can be stopped by the verse. Someone who simply wants to feel clean can sing the title line unchanged.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none. The fear of losing the Spirit is David's sentence in Psalm 51:11. It is not a New Covenant promise, and singing it is not the same thing as teaching that Christ withdraws his Spirit from his people. The line needs the sermon. It does not contradict the psalm.
- **Non-biblical:** none.
- **Thin:** “Lord renew my spirit and restore my soul.” “Restore my soul” echoes Psalm 23:3, “He restores my soul,” turned from a statement about the shepherd into a request. The bridge adds little the verse has not already asked.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 4

The chorus and the verse follow Psalm 51:10-12. The ESV reads, “Create in me a clean heart, O God, and renew a right spirit within me. Cast me not away from your presence, and take not your Holy Spirit from me. Restore to me the joy of your salvation, and uphold me with a willing spirit.” The song says “O Lord my God” where the psalm says “O God,” keeps “Thy,” repeats “a right spirit,” and does not sing “a willing spirit.” Those are small differences of address and of one phrase. The prayer is still David's.

Psalm 51:11 belongs with 1 Samuel 16:14, “the Spirit of the LORD departed from Saul.” David has seen an anointed king lose the Spirit, and he prays not to be that man. Ephesians 1:13-14 says those who believed the gospel were “sealed with the promised Holy Spirit, who is the guarantee of our inheritance.” The song quotes David faithfully. It does not say the later promise. A church that sings the line on a Sunday has to say both, or the room will hear a threat the New Covenant does not make.

The song also begins at verse 10. Psalm 51:1-4, the mercy, the confession, “against you, you only, have I sinned,” is not in the lyric. “Create” is still the right verb. The clean heart is God's work. Ezekiel 36:26 is the same work promised to the people: a new heart, and the heart of stone removed. The chorus agrees with that. It does not say the blood. Psalm 51:7, “Purge me with hyssop,” is earlier in the psalm and is not in this setting.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 2

The Son is not named. The cross is not named. The Spirit is named, and the salvation is named as a joy to be restored, with no account of how the sinner was saved. A clean heart and a right spirit are the fruit of grace. In this lyric they are asked for without the Savior. The score is for that silence. The psalm itself is not the problem.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 3

The singer is asking, and the ask is about his own heart, his own spirit, his own joy, his own soul. The verbs he uses for God are create, renew, cast not away, take not, restore. The subject of the prayer is God's work, which keeps the song from being a report of feelings. “The joy of Thy salvation” is the one phrase that names an experience, and it names it as something God restores. There is no vow that the singer will do better. The score stays at 3 because the whole text is one person's plea, and the plea is for God to act.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 3

He is the Lord who can create a clean heart and renew a right spirit. He is the God whose presence can be left, whose Spirit is his to give or, in David's fear, to take, and whose salvation has a joy he can restore. Holiness is in the clean heart the singer does not have and cannot produce. Mercy is in the restoration. Wrath, justice, and love are not named. The portrait is the God of Psalm 51:10-12 and no wider.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 3

One use, in 2024, means it is not a staple here. The chorus is short, and anyone who knows the psalm already knows the first two lines. What a congregation can learn is that a clean heart is created by God, and that the joy of salvation is his to restore. What they can learn wrongly, if no one explains the verse, is that the Holy Spirit may be taken from a Christian the way the Spirit departed from Saul. “Thy” will not confuse this room. The Spirit line will, unless the sermon has already distinguished David's anointed fear from the seal in Ephesians 1. The chorus is usable. The verse is usable only with that distinction said out loud.

---

## Tone

The tone is penitential and plain. The line that sets it is “Create in me a clean heart O Lord my God,” and the verse does not brighten it. “Take not Thy Holy Spirit from me” is the heaviest sentence. It is David's plea. Bannister did not add a threat of his own. The bridge is the same plea in fewer words. Nothing in the song is casual.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names Jesus, never says “I have sinned,” and never says that the clean heart was bought by his blood.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Psalm 51, after verses 1-4 have been preached, and after the difference between Saul and the New Covenant seal has been said. Ezekiel 36 can sit under the chorus. The song does not serve a general call to worship.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The blood, and the name of Christ. Psalm 51:7 is in the psalm and not in the song. Pair it with *Rock of Ages* (logged 6 times) or *Jesus, Your Mercy* (logged 14 times).
- **Diet sentence.** Once in this window, it adds David's prayer for a clean heart and for the joy of salvation. It never adds the cross, and it never says the Spirit's seal.
- **What not to do.** Do not preach from “Take not Thy Holy Spirit from me” that Christ withdraws his Spirit from his people. Do not loop that line. Do not drop it and pretend the song was only the chorus, unless the chart in front of the band really is only the chorus. Know which text is being sung.

## Why this overall label

The song puts Psalm 51:10-12 in the congregation's mouth, including the prayer for a clean heart and for the joy of salvation. It does not name Jesus or the blood, and “Take not Thy Holy Spirit from me” is David's fear, which the New Covenant does not hand to the church as a threat. With the sermon saying both, and with the cross somewhere else in the hour, the chorus can be used. It should not be asked to carry a Sunday.
