## Thesis

Strength comes as the people wait on the Lord, who reigns forever, does not grow weary, defends the weak, and lifts those who wait on him.

---

## Overall recommendation

Weak

Use sparingly, and only after Scripture has already named the gospel. The chorus draws true lines from Isaiah 40 about the everlasting God who does not faint and who gives strength to the weary. The verse and pre-chorus stay on waiting and reigning. Christ is not named. The cross, substitution, and resurrection are absent. A non-Christian can sing the whole song without tension. That is why it stays Weak even though the Isaiah fragments are accurate.

## Voice and addressee

First-person plural (“we wait,” “you lift us”). The pre-chorus and chorus address God (“Our God,” “You are”). The voice is corporate and directed to God.

---

## How the text moves

It begins with waiting and the promise that strength will rise. The pre-chorus names the reigning God and strong Deliverer. The chorus expands the everlasting God who does not faint, defends the weak, and lifts on wings like eagles. The movement stays inside Isaiah 40 imagery. It does not advance to the cross or the resurrection.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

The chorus lines drawn from Isaiah 40 (“You do not faint, You won’t grow weary / You’re the defender of the weak / You lift us up on wings like eagles”). Drop them and the song is only the repeated waiting verse. Even with them, the gospel remains unsaid.

## Persons of the Trinity named

God is addressed as the everlasting God, hope, and strong Deliverer. The Son and the Spirit are not named.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Yes. The lines name an everlasting God who does not grow weary, defends the weak, and gives strength to those who wait. A vague theist or moralist can sing the whole song without correction. The name of Christ is not required for any line to work.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** the repeated “strength will rise as we wait” functions as mood more than as a claim that can be checked beyond the general promise.
- **Thin:** “You are the everlasting God” and “our strong Deliverer” are true and underfed. The Isaiah fragments are accurate but not unfolded into the whole counsel.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 3

“You do not faint, You won’t grow weary” and “You lift us up on wings like eagles” follow the ordinary sense of Isaiah 40:28–31. “Defender of the weak” and “comfort those in need” are consistent with the same chapter. The lines are true. They are also thin: the song stacks the Isaiah promise without naming the Servant who fulfills it or the gospel that makes the waiting people heirs of the promise.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 2

Christ is not named. The cross, substitution, and resurrection are absent. “Strong Deliverer” is a title without the finished work. The song stays in the range of generic theistic praise drawn from one Old Testament text.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

The lines declare what God is and what He does for those who wait. First-person plural is corporate. The repeated verse is simple and experiential in tone, but the chorus stays with objective claims about God. Desire language is limited to waiting.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 3

God is everlasting, does not faint or grow weary, reigns forever, defends the weak, comforts those in need, and gives strength. The attributes are drawn from Isaiah 40. Holiness, justice, and mercy in relation to sin are not named. “Great / good / love” language is avoided, but the description remains incomplete for this church’s diet.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 3

The diction is plain and the form is short. The congregation has already sung it in multiple years. Singability is high. The text does not teach the gospel or the person of Christ. It is useful only as a brief response after Scripture has already done the work.

---

## Tone

Reverent in the chorus. The verse is more casual and repetitive. No line asks the room to confer on God what He already possesses.

---

## What the congregation never says

The name of Christ, the cross, substitution, resurrection, sin, or repentance. The Spirit is not named. These are the reasons the rest of the hour must supply what the lyric leaves out.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Isaiah 40 (comfort, the incomparable God, strength for the weary) after the text has already been read and explained. Do not use it as the main teaching song.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The person and work of Christ, the cross, and the resurrection. Any song that names only the everlasting God without the gospel leaves the same gap.
- **Diet sentence.** Adds a short, accurate fragment of Isaiah 40 on God’s inexhaustible strength. It never adds the gospel or the name of Christ.
- **What not to do.** Do not feature it. Do not place it as the opener or the closer when the sermon has not already named the cross. Do not tag it with a vague bridge that keeps the song inside generic strength.
