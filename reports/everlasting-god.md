## Thesis

Strength comes as the people wait on the Lord, who reigns forever, does not grow weary, defends the weak, and lifts those who wait on him.

---

## Overall recommendation

Weak

Do not feature it. If it is used, put it after Scripture has already named the gospel. The chorus draws true lines from Isaiah 40 about the God who does not grow weary and gives strength to the weak. But Christ is never named, the cross and resurrection are absent, and a non-Christian could sing the whole song without changing a word. The church has sung it 8 times across at least four years of the 2023–2026 window.

## Voice and addressee

First-person plural (“we wait,” “you lift us”). The pre-chorus and chorus address God (“Our God,” “You are”). The voice is corporate.

---

## How the text moves

The verse waits for strength, the pre-chorus names God as reigning Deliverer, and the chorus praises Him in the words of Isaiah 40. The song stays inside that one chapter and never reaches the cross or the resurrection.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

The chorus lines from Isaiah 40 (“You do not faint, You won’t grow weary / You’re the defender of the weak / You lift us up on wings like eagles”). Without them, the song is only the repeated waiting verse. Even with them, the gospel is never stated.

## Persons of the Trinity named

God is addressed as the everlasting God, hope, and strong Deliverer. The Son and the Spirit are not named.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Yes. A vague theist or moralist could sing every line. The name of Christ is not needed for any line to make sense.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** the repeated “strength will rise as we wait” works as mood more than as a claim that can be checked beyond the general promise.
- **Thin:** “You are the everlasting God” and “our strong Deliverer” are true but thin. The Isaiah lines are accurate but never connected to the rest of Scripture.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 3

“You do not faint, You won’t grow weary” and “You lift us up on wings like eagles” are Isaiah 40:28–31. “Defender of the weak” and “comfort those in need” fit the same chapter. The lines are true and thin: the song repeats Isaiah’s promise without naming the Servant who fulfills it or the gospel that makes the waiting people heirs of it.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 2

Christ is not named, and the cross, substitution, and resurrection are absent. “Strong Deliverer” is a title with no work attached. The song is general praise drawn from one Old Testament text.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

The chorus declares what God is and does for those who wait, and the “we” is corporate. The verse is simple and leans on the experience of waiting. The only desire language is the waiting itself.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 3

God is everlasting, never weary, reigning, and the defender and comforter of the weak. Holiness, justice, and mercy toward sinners are not named, so the picture is accurate but incomplete for this church.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 3

The song is short and easy to sing. It does not teach the gospel or the person of Christ, so it is useful only as a brief response after the Scripture reading.

---

## Tone

The chorus is reverent. The verse is more casual and repetitive.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names Christ, the cross, substitution, the resurrection, sin, repentance, or the Spirit. The rest of the hour has to supply all of it.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Isaiah 40, after the text has been read and explained. Do not use it as a main teaching song.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The person and work of Christ, the cross, and the resurrection.
- **Diet sentence.** Adds a short, accurate piece of Isaiah 40 on God’s tireless strength. It never adds the gospel or the name of Christ.
- **What not to do.** Do not feature it, and do not use it to open or close a service whose sermon has not named the cross.
