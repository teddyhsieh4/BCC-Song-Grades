## Thesis

God has no rival. He is without beginning, the maker of the skies, truth, wisdom, sinless, unable to be tempted, and unable to die, and the church answers that he is holy.

---

## Overall recommendation

Acceptable with notes

Use it when the sermon is on the holiness and incomparability of God, and put a song with the cross next to it. The church has logged it once in this window, in 2024. Keep both verses. The chorus only repeats “You are holy.” The verses are worth singing. They never name the Son or the cross, so this cannot be the gospel song of the morning.

## Voice and addressee

The church speaks to God (“O God,” “You,” “Lord”). The singer’s inner life is not the subject. There is no “I.”

---

## How the text moves

Verse 1 says there is none like God and lists eternity, creation, truth, and wisdom. The chorus answers with holiness, four times. Verse 2 returns to “none like You” and lists sinlessness, truthfulness, freedom from temptation, and immortality. The chorus does not add a new claim after that.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

The verses carry the confession. “Only You are never sinning / Only You have never lied / Only You cannot be tempted / Only You can never die” is the stanza that would be a loss. The chorus, “For You are holy,” is true and, sung alone, teaches only the one word. No line is false. Dropping verse 2 would leave creation and wisdom and would hide the moral claims. If the chorus is all that is sung, the grade would not hold.

## Persons of the Trinity named

The song says “O God” and “Lord.” Father, Son, and Spirit are unnamed, and Jesus is unnamed. Holiness here is the holiness of God, in the register of Isaiah 6, with no sentence about the Holy One’s Son.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

A member of another religion who confesses one holy God could sing a great deal of this text. “None like You,” “no beginning,” “make the skies,” and “You are holy” do not require the name of Jesus. The lines that would press a singer who thinks God can be untrue or changeable are “Only You have never lied” and “Only You can never die.” Even those lines do not say that God has spoken in the Son.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** none.
- **Thin:** the chorus, which says “You are holy” four times and does not say what that holiness has done. “Only You are truth unending” is a true confession said in a short phrase.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 4

“O God, there’s none like You / In all the earth” follows Psalm 86:8, “There is none like you among the gods, O Lord.” “O God, who can compare / To You” is the question of Isaiah 40:25, “To whom then will you compare me?” “Only You have no beginning” says what Psalm 90:2 says of God, “from everlasting to everlasting.” “Only You could make the skies” is creation, Genesis 1:6-8. “Only You are always wise” is the confession of Romans 16:27, “the only wise God.”

“Only You are never sinning” confesses the God of 1 John 1:5: “God is light, and in him is no darkness at all.” “Only You have never lied” is Titus 1:2, “God, who never lies.” “Only You cannot be tempted” shortens James 1:13, “God cannot be tempted with evil.” Hebrews 4:15 says the Son “in every respect has been tempted as we are, yet without sin.” The song’s line is James 1:13, said of God. “Only You can never die” is 1 Timothy 6:16, “who alone has immortality,” and 1 Timothy 1:17, the “immortal” God. “For You are holy” is the seraphim’s word in Isaiah 6:3. The verses are accurate. They list the claims and do not stay with one passage long enough to explain it.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 2

The song exalts God and never mentions the Son. There is no incarnation, no cross, no resurrection, and no priestly work. “Lord” is a title with no sentence about the Lord Jesus. A congregation can finish both verses and the chorus without having said what God has done to save. That keeps this category at 2.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

Every line is about God. “There’s none like You,” “Only You have no beginning,” “Only You are never sinning.” There is no vow and no report of the singer’s feelings. The chorus, “For You are holy,” is the same kind of line, said four times. The song declares God. It does not open any of those claims into the gospel.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 4

The song names incomparability, eternity, creation, truth, wisdom, sinlessness, truthfulness, freedom from temptation, immortality, and holiness. Justice, mercy, and wrath are unnamed. The weight is on holiness and on God’s difference from everyone else. These are specific words, and they stop before the way that holy God justifies the ungodly.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 4

The lines are short and the contrasts are easy to sing. One hearing will not exhaust the verses, and the room can learn that God has no rival and does not sin, lie, or die. The church has sung it once in this window, in 2024. What the people do not learn is the cross. The chorus is the part a band can loop without teaching the verses.

---

## Tone

The tone is reverent. The line that sets it is “For You are holy, Lord / You are holy,” after a verse that has already said God does not sin and cannot be tempted.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names the Son, the Spirit, sin, the cross, the resurrection, or mercy.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Isaiah 6, Isaiah 40, Psalm 86, James 1:13, or a sermon on the attributes of God. Read the text first so “holy” is not only the chorus.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The Son and the cross. Pair it with *Come Behold the Wondrous Mystery* or *In Christ Alone* when the service still needs the incarnation and the death of Christ.
- **Diet sentence.** In a year it can add a direct confession that God is incomparable and holy. One log means it has not shaped the diet. It never adds the gospel.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop the verses and tag “You are holy.” Do not make this the only song about God in a service that has not yet named Christ.

## Why this overall label

*Holy, Holy, Holy* (Heber) is Strong, with Christ-Exalting at 2 and the other four scores at 5. It works Isaiah 6 and Revelation 4 through. This song’s scores are 4s. The verses list attributes from several texts. Christ is unnamed, and so is the way a holy God receives sinners. Use it beside the gospel, with both verses kept.
