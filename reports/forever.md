## Thesis

The church gives thanks to the Lord, God and King, because he is good and his love endures forever. He showed a mighty hand and an outstretched arm. A life has been reborn. From sunrise to sunset his love endures, and by his grace the church will carry on. The chorus calls him faithful, strong, and with us, forever.

---

## Overall recommendation

Weak

Do not feature it. It appears once in this window, in 2024. “His love endures forever” and “With a mighty hand and an outstretched arm” are the lines that have to stay if it is sung at all. “For the life that's been reborn” gestures at new life and never says how. Jesus is not named. The cross is not named. If it is used, keep it short, after Scripture, and let another song say the death and the resurrection.

---

## Alternative song suggestions

The lyric is reaching for the steadfast love of Psalm 136, for the exodus arm, and for a life made new. Three of these the church already sings. One is not in the catalog.

**Already sung**

**His Mercy Is More** (Matt Boswell / Matt Papa, modern hymn, CCLI 7065053). Logged 14 times across at least four years (2023, 2024, 2025, 2026). The mercy that endures is sung against the record of sin, and the cross is in the song.

**Bless the Lord, O My Soul (Psalm 103)** (Zach Jones, Sovereign Grace, CCLI 4764203). Logged 7 times across at least four years (2023, 2024, 2025, 2026). The song blesses the Lord for his benefits, in the path of Psalm 103, where forgiveness and steadfast love are among the things the soul is told not to forget.

**And Can It Be** (Charles Wesley, hymn, CCLI 25280). Logged 2 times, in 2024 and 2025. The sinner is named, the blood is named, and the chains fall. The new life is told there as a rescue.

**Not yet logged**

**Christ Is Mine Forevermore** (Jonny Robinson / Rich Thompson, CityAlight, CCLI 7036096). Not in the catalog. “Christ has paid for every failing,” and the life is his forever.

## Voice and addressee

Most of the song speaks about God to the congregation, in the plural where a person appears at all: “Give thanks,” “we will carry on.” “His love,” “He is good,” “Forever God is faithful.” The chorus does not turn to “You.” The church is telling itself to give thanks, and then declaring what God is. There is no private “I.”

---

## How the text moves

It opens with thanks to the Lord, God and King, and with the line the verses repeat: his love endures forever. He is good, and he is above all things. The next verse names the exodus arm, a mighty hand and an outstretched arm, and then “the life that's been reborn,” still under the same refrain. The chorus steps aside from the psalm's sentence and says God is faithful, strong, and with us, forever. The last verse runs from the rising of the sun to its setting, keeps the refrain, and adds that by the grace of God the church will carry on. The refrain does not change. The new claims are the reborn life, the three words of the chorus, and the resolve to carry on.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“His love endures forever” is the line the grade depends on, with “With a mighty hand and an outstretched arm.” “For the life that's been reborn” is the line that sounds like the gospel and does not say it. If the band keeps “Forever God is faithful / Forever God is strong / Forever God is with us” and drops the thanks and the arm, the song is three attributes and a resolve. “By the grace of God we will carry on” should not be the line left ringing. No line is false. Without the psalm's refrain, the chorus would be a short praise song with less Scripture in it.

## Persons of the Trinity named

“The Lord,” “our God and King,” and “God” are the names. Father, Son, and Spirit are unnamed. “The life that's been reborn” names a new birth. It does not say that Jesus rose, and it does not say who gives that birth.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Yes. Thanks to God the King, a love that endures, a mighty hand, a God who is faithful, strong, and with his people: a worshiper of one God can sing all of that. “The life that's been reborn” will sound Christian to a Christian and vague to anyone else. It does not name Jesus, and it does not say the cross. Nothing in the song forces the name.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** “He is above all things,” as a general height with no act attached. “By the grace of God we will carry on” is a resolve. Grace is named. The line does not say what the church is carrying or what the grace did.
- **Thin:** “For the life that's been reborn.” New birth is a real mercy. The line does not say the resurrection, the blood, or the name. “Forever God is with us” is presence in three words.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 3

“Give thanks to the Lord” and “For He is good” and “His love endures forever” follow Psalm 136:1, “Give thanks to the LORD, for he is good, for his steadfast love endures forever.” The song's “love” is the congregational word for that steadfast love. “Our God and King” adds a title the psalm's first line does not need, and the title is true. “With a mighty hand and an outstretched arm” is the exodus wording of Deuteronomy 5:15, “with a mighty hand and an outstretched arm.” Psalm 136:12 says “a strong hand and an outstretched arm.” The song is on the Deuteronomy phrase, inside a psalm that sings the same deliverance.

“From the rising to the setting sun” takes Psalm 113:3, “From the rising of the sun to its setting the name of the LORD is to be praised,” and sets the Psalm 136 refrain under it. The join is fair. The psalm's line is about the name being praised. “For the life that's been reborn” reaches toward 1 Peter 1:3, “he has caused us to be born again to a living hope through the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead,” and stops at “reborn.” “He is above all things” is true and unspecific. “By the grace of God we will carry on” is the church's own sentence.

What the song keeps from Psalm 136 is the refrain, the goodness, and one exodus line. The psalm's creation, the Red Sea, the wilderness, and the kings are not here. The lines that are here are mostly accurate. They are a refrain and a few additions, which is why the score stays at 3.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 2

Jesus is not named. The cross is not named. “The life that's been reborn” is the one gesture toward the gospel, and it has no grave and no blood. “Our God and King” is a title with the works of the King left in the exodus arm and the enduring love. The song gives thanks for a love that endures. It does not say the Son.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

The song tells the congregation to give thanks and then says what is true of God: good, above all things, a mighty hand, love that endures, faithful, strong, with us. “We will carry on” is the one resolve, and it is corporate. There is no “I feel,” no vow that the singer's heart belongs to God forever, and no request for a nearer feeling. The reborn life is stated as a fact. The score is 4 because the resolve is still the church talking about its own continuance, in a song that is otherwise thanks and declaration.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 3

Good, King, a mighty hand and an outstretched arm, love that endures, faithful, strong, and with his people. “His love endures forever” is the line the verses repeat, so love carries the weight, and in Psalm 136 that love is steadfast love shown in acts. The chorus adds faithfulness, strength, and presence. “Above all things” is height without a scene. Holiness, justice, and wrath are unnamed. Psalm 136 itself sings God striking down kings. This song does not. The portrait is goodness, power, enduring love, and nearness.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 3

One use, in 2024, means the room has barely sung it in this window. The refrain is easy, and a congregation can learn to say that the Lord's love endures and that he delivered with a mighty hand. They can also learn “reborn” without the resurrection, and “we will carry on” as the application. The refrain is the part worth keeping. It will not teach the cross. “Forever God is with us” is clear and small. The song is useful as a short thanks after a psalm has already been read. It is weak as the song that is supposed to carry the hour.

---

## Tone

The tone is steady thanks. The line that sets it is “His love endures forever,” sung under every claim. “Forever God is faithful / Forever God is strong / Forever God is with us” is the same steadiness in the chorus. “We will carry on” is the most determined line, and it is still plain. Nothing in the song is playful or desperate.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names Jesus, never says that his love endured the cross, and never says that the new birth is through his resurrection.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Psalm 136 or the exodus, after the reading has supplied the acts this lyric skips. Deuteronomy 5:15 can sit under the arm. It does not serve a sermon on the cross.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The Son, the cross, and the resurrection that 1 Peter 1:3 ties to the new birth. Pair it with *His Mercy Is More* or *And Can It Be* so “reborn” is not left unexplained.
- **Diet sentence.** Once in this window, it adds a congregational refrain of enduring love and one line of the exodus. It never adds the gospel.
- **What not to do.** Do not loop “we will carry on” or “the life that's been reborn” without the refrain and the arm. Do not let “Forever God is with us” become the whole chorus.

## Why this overall label

The refrain tells the truth of Psalm 136, that the Lord's love endures forever, and one verse remembers the exodus arm. The same song calls a life reborn and tells the church it will carry on, and it never names Jesus, the cross, or the resurrection. Once in 2024 is enough. It should stay a short thanks, after the Scripture has already spoken.
