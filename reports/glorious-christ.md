## Thesis

The radiance of the Father before the dawn of time left the air of heaven to breathe the dust of earth. He came to be forsaken and died to take our curse. He is seated now in heaven, enthroned at God’s right hand, and has shattered death. There is no greater sacrifice than when He laid down His life.

---

## Overall recommendation

Excellent / Prefer

Feature it, and keep every section. The second verse (the curse) and the bridge (the right hand and the defeat of death) must stay. The song runs from Christ before creation to His return. The church has sung it 9 times across at least four years of the 2023–2026 window.

## Voice and addressee

Addressed directly to Christ (“You”), and corporate in the chorus (“we join the song of angels”). The whole song is declaration of who He is and what He has done.

---

## How the text moves

The song follows Christ’s whole story. Verse 1 is the eternal Son and Creator. Verse 2 is the incarnation and the cross, and the bridge is His seat at God’s right hand, His victory over death, and His return.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“You came to be forsaken / And died to take our curse” and “You’re seated now in heaven / Enthroned at God’s right hand / You’ve shattered death.” The chorus line “No greater sacrifice / Than when You laid down Your life” must stay with them. Without these, the song praises the eternal Son without the cross or the empty tomb.

## Persons of the Trinity named

Christ is named. The Father is named as the one of whom He is the radiance. The Spirit is not named. The lyric stays within the person and work of the glorious Christ.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. A vague theist could not sing “And died to take our curse” or “Enthroned at God’s right hand” without changing them.

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“The radiance of the Father” is Hebrews 1:3. “You spoke and all creation came to be” is Colossians 1:16 and John 1:3. “You left the air of heaven / To breathe the dust of earth” is Philippians 2:6–8 and John 1:14. “Died to take our curse” is Galatians 3:13. “Enthroned at God’s right hand” is Hebrews 1:3 and Acts 2:33. “You’ve shattered death” is 1 Corinthians 15 and Hebrews 2:14.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

The song moves from the eternal Son to His death for our curse, His session, and His victory over death. The curse is named in the words of Galatians 3:13.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 5

The lyric declares who Christ is and what He has done. “The greatest of all delights” is the response, and it comes after those facts. There are no private vows.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 5

Christ is the radiance of the Father, the Creator, the Son who came down, the one forsaken for our curse, and the King seated at God’s right hand who will come again. His power and His love are both named, and both are shown in what He did.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The song gives a congregation the whole story of Christ in a few minutes: pre-existence, incarnation, cross, session, and return. The chorus is easy to join after one hearing.

---

## Tone

The tone is reverent and full of wonder. The wonder is about the person of Christ and the curse He took.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names the Spirit. The main gospel elements are present.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Hebrews 1, Colossians 1, Galatians 3:13, or any text on the incarnation, the cross, or Christ’s session. It is especially useful after a sermon that moves from the glory of the Son to the curse He bore.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** Very little. A song about the Spirit can sit beside it if the service needs that.
- **Diet sentence.** Adds the whole story of Christ, with the curse and the right hand both named. It adds little on the Spirit.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop the second verse or the bridge, and do not sing the chorus alone.
