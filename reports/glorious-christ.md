## Thesis

The radiance of the Father before the dawn of time left the air of heaven to breathe the dust of earth. He came to be forsaken and died to take our curse. He is seated now in heaven, enthroned at God’s right hand, and has shattered death. There is no greater sacrifice than when He laid down His life.

## Voice and addressee

Addressed directly to Christ (“You”). Corporate in the chorus (“we join the song of angels”). It is declaration of who He is and what He has done.

---

## How the text moves

It starts with the radiance of the Father before the dawn of time, the speaking of creation, and the design of molecules and planets so that we could see. The chorus confesses the glorious Christ, the unequaled power, the love beyond all heights, and the laying down of life. The second verse names the leaving of heaven, the breathing of the dust of earth, the dwelling among the outcast and the poor, the coming to be forsaken, and the dying to take our curse. The bridge names the session at the right hand, the shattering of death, and the coming again when all will be made right.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“You came to be forsaken / And died to take our curse” and “You’re seated now in heaven / Enthroned at God’s right hand / You’ve shattered death.”

Dropping either loses the explicit taking of the curse or the session and the defeat of death. The chorus’s “No greater sacrifice / Than when You laid down Your life” is also required.

## Persons of the Trinity named

Christ is named. The Father is named as the one of whom He is the radiance. The Spirit is not named. The lyric stays within the person and work of the glorious Christ.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. The radiance of the Father, the leaving of heaven, the dying to take our curse, and the shattering of death are required. A vague theist cannot sing these lines without correction.

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“The radiance of the Father” is Hebrews 1:3. “You spoke and all creation came to be” is Colossians 1:16 and John 1:3. “You left the air of heaven / To breathe the dust of earth” is Philippians 2:6–8 and John 1:14. “Died to take our curse” is Galatians 3:13. “Enthroned at God’s right hand” is Hebrews 1:3 and Acts 2:33. “You’ve shattered death” is 1 Corinthians 15 and Hebrews 2:14. The lines stay inside ordinary biblical sense.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

Christ is named. The radiance is named. The leaving of heaven is named. The taking of the curse is named. The laying down of life is named. The session is named. The shattering of death is named. The song does not stop at titles or atmosphere. It preaches the finished work.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 5

The lyric declares who Christ is and what He has done. “The greatest of all delights” is response to those facts. There are no private vows or reports of the singer’s inner weather. The voice is corporate declaration.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 5

Christ is the radiance of the Father before the dawn of time, the speaker of creation, the one who left heaven to breathe the dust of earth, the one who came to be forsaken and died to take our curse, the one of unequaled power and love beyond all heights, the one who laid down His life, the one seated at the right hand, the one who shattered death, and the one who is coming again. The attributes are specific and tied to the person and finished work.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The language is plain. The claims are dense. The congregation can learn the pre-existence, the incarnation, the taking of the curse, the session, and the defeat of death by singing these lines. Singability is high, and the teaching is the reason for the 5.

---

## Tone

Reverent and declarative. It does not ask the room to confer on Christ anything He has not already accomplished. The intensity stays inside the radiance, the curse, and the right hand.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names the Spirit. That is a small gap. The main gospel elements are present.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Hebrews 1, Colossians 1, Galatians 3:13, any text on the incarnation, the cross, or the session. Especially useful after a sermon that has moved from the glory of the Son to the taking of the curse.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** Very little. A Spirit-focused song can sit beside it if the service needs that voice.
- **Diet sentence.** It adds a dense confession of the glorious Christ who left heaven, died to take our curse, and is seated at the right hand having shattered death. It does not add much on the Spirit.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop the second verse or the bridge. Do not let the chorus stand alone without the taking of the curse and the session.
