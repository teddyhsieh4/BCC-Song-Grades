## Thesis

Jesus came from the praise of heaven into a world whose sin was black, born of a virgin, the Word made flesh. Living, he loved the singer. Dying, he saved the singer. Buried, he carried the singer's sins far away. Rising, he justified the singer freely, and the verdict stands. He is coming again. On the way the hymn walks through Calvary, the grave, the ascension, and the trumpet.

---

## Overall recommendation

Excellent / Prefer

Feature it when the service needs the gospel from the birth to the return, and sing the chorus every time. The church has logged it 3 times across at least three years (2023, 2024, 2025). “Living, He loved me / Dying, He saved me / Buried, He carried my sins far away / Rising, He justified freely forever” is the stanza that has to stay, with the Calvary verse. “Dwelt among men, my example is He” is the example Jesus names in John 13:15, and it belongs in the verse with the virgin birth and the Word made flesh. “My Savior Jesus is mine” is testimony after the trumpet. The bridge's coming belongs with 1 Thessalonians 4:14.

## Voice and addressee

The stanzas tell what Jesus did, and they tell it to the congregation with “me” and “my” as the one he loved, saved, and redeemed. “My example is He,” “my sins,” “my Redeemer,” “my Lord,” “My Savior Jesus is mine.” The bridge turns for a moment toward address: “Your glories will shine,” on the Word to Worship printing, inside a stanza that is otherwise about his coming. The chorus is testimony. The events are public. The pronouns take them personally, which is what a hymn of this kind is for.

---

## How the text moves

It starts in heaven's praise and in sin “as black as could be,” then the virgin birth, the years among men, and the Word made flesh whose glory was revealed. The chorus answers with the whole course: living, dying, buried, rising, justified, and coming. The second stanza goes up Calvary, the nails, the anguish, the rejection, the sins he bore, and the hands that healed now taking the nails. The third stanza is the grave that could not keep him, the stone, the rising, the conquest of death, and the ascension: “my Lord evermore.” The bridge is the trumpet, the shining sky, and “My Savior Jesus is mine.” The chorus does not change the claim. It repeats the course after each scene. Chapman's garden stanza, the burial vigil, is not in this 2009 text. The chorus still says “Buried.”

---

## Lines you cannot cut

The chorus is the block the grade depends on. “Dying, He saved me,” “Buried, He carried my sins far away,” and “Rising, He justified freely forever” have to be sung. A band that shortens the chorus to “O glorious day” loses those verbs. The Calvary stanza has to stay with them: “Bearing our sins, my Redeemer is He” and “took the nails from me.” “Dwelt among men, my example is He” is John 13:15 inside the incarnation stanza. If verse 1 is all the room hears, the chorus is what they miss. No line denies the gospel. Dropping the chorus and keeping “my example is He” would waste the song. Dropping “Rising, He justified” would leave death and burial without the verdict Paul ties to the resurrection.

## Persons of the Trinity named

Jesus is named, and he is Redeemer, Lord, and Savior. The Word made flesh is the Son. The Father is not given that title. The Spirit is unnamed. “Heaven was filled with His praises” before he came is the Son's glory with the Father left unspoken. The song is a hymn of the Son's course from heaven to the trumpet.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. “Jesus came forth to be born of a virgin,” “The Word became flesh,” “Dying, He saved me,” and “took the nails from me” cannot be moved to another lord. “Rising, He justified freely forever” and “My Savior Jesus is mine” close the same door. “My example is He,” sung alone from verse 1, is the line a moralist could keep. The rest of the verse and the chorus will not let him keep it.

- **Unbiblical:** none.

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“Jesus came forth to be born of a virgin” is the birth the Gospels tell. “The Word became flesh / And the light shined among us / His glory revealed” follows John 1:14, “And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we have seen his glory,” and John 1:9, the true light coming into the world. “Dwelt among men, my example is He” agrees with John 13:15, “For I have given you an example, that you also should do just as I have done to you.” First Peter 2:21 sets the same example in his suffering. This stanza sets the word on the years he dwelt among men, in the verse that also says the virgin birth and the Word made flesh.

“Despised and rejected” is Isaiah 53:3. “Bearing our sins” is Isaiah 53:12 and 1 Peter 2:24, “He himself bore our sins in his body on the tree.” “Took the nails from me” is the same exchange in the song's picture: the nails belong to the singer, and they are in Jesus' hands. “Buried, He carried my sins far away” joins the burial to the removal of sins. Psalm 103:12 says the transgressions are removed “as far as the east is from the west.” The burial is the chorus's word for where that carrying happened. “Rising, He justified freely forever” agrees with Romans 4:25, “raised for our justification,” and with Romans 3:24, “justified by his grace as a gift.” “Freely” is the gift. “Forever” says the verdict stands. “Death could not hold Him” agrees with Acts 2:24, “it was not possible for him to be held by it.” “Now is ascended, my Lord evermore” is Acts 1 and Ephesians 1:20-21. “The trumpet will sound for His coming” is 1 Corinthians 15:52.

“Wonderful day, my beloved one bringing” is compressed. 1 Thessalonians 4:14 says, “For since we believe that Jesus died and rose again, even so, through Jesus, God will bring with him those who have fallen asleep.” The chorus has already said the dying, the rising, and the coming. The bridge reaches that bringing. “My Savior Jesus is mine” is the testimony that follows the trumpet. The course from the virgin birth to the trumpet is told in lines that can be tied to those texts.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

The person is Jesus, the Word made flesh, the Redeemer, the Lord. The work runs the course: virgin birth, life, death on the tree, sins borne, burial, resurrection, justification, ascension, and the coming at the trumpet. “Took the nails from me” and “carried my sins far away” are the exchange, said in the singer's own case. Wrath is not the word used. Anguish, rejection, nails, and sins carried are the suffering the song does name. The chorus is the gospel in one breath, and the stanzas walk the same path more slowly.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

The events are the content: heaven, the virgin, the Word, Calvary, the grave, the ascension, the trumpet. “Me” and “my” mark the one for whom those events count: loved me, saved me, my sins, the nails from me, my Redeemer, my Lord. That is testimony of public facts. “My Savior Jesus is mine” is the most possessive line, and it stands at the end of the coming, after the work has been told. “My example is He” is the line most easily turned into a private project of imitation. The score stays at 4 because the pronouns are everywhere and the facts are what the pronouns are attached to.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 4

The Son loves, saves, bears sins, is despised and rejected, rises, justifies, ascends as Lord, and comes in glory. “The hand that healed nations” remembers the ministry of mercy, then stretches that hand on the tree. Holiness is not an attribute-word in the lyric. Justice is present as sins borne and nails taken, without the word wrath. Faithfulness is present as the promise that he is coming. The incarnation glory, “His glory revealed,” is John 1:14. The portrait is the Son's course. The Father and the Spirit are not described.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 4

Three logs across at least three years means the church knows the song and does not sing it every month. The chorus is the part a congregation can learn in a morning, and it teaches the course of the gospel in paired verbs: living, dying, buried, rising, coming. “Justified freely forever” is a doctrine in four words, and it is worth singing slowly enough to hear. “My example is He” is easy to understand and easy to overhear as the point of the birth. “My beloved one bringing” is the line that may need to be glanced at on a page. The Calvary stanza and the chorus are what the room should be able to say back. The song is long. The length is the story. Cutting it down to verse 1 would keep the weakest line and lose the nails.

---

## Tone

The tone is narrative and wondering. The line that sets it is the chorus, “Living, He loved me / Dying, He saved me,” under the name “O glorious day.” Calvary is heavier: anguish, despised and rejected, the nails. The bridge warms into “My Savior Jesus is mine.” The wonder stays tied to the events.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never says the word wrath, never names the Father or the Spirit, and never says that the example in verse 1 is the reason he came.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** The birth, Isaiah 53, the resurrection and justification in Romans 4:25, the ascension, or the trumpet in 1 Corinthians 15. The chorus can serve any of those if the matching stanza is actually sung.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The word wrath, when the sermon is the curse borne. Pair it with *In Christ Alone* (logged 8 times) or *It Was Finished Upon That Cross* (logged 11 times) on that morning. The song already carries birth, death, burial, resurrection, and return.
- **Diet sentence.** Three times in at least three years, it adds the Son's course from the virgin birth to the trumpet, with justification in the chorus. It never says the word wrath.
- **What not to do.** Do not end on “my example is He.” Do not drop the chorus to save time. Do not replace “took the nails from me” with a tag that only says “glorious day.”

## Identification notes

One setting of an older hymn. The catalog names Mark Hall, Michael Bleecker, and John Wilbanks, and gives 2009 and CCLI 5638022. Word to Worship, PraiseCharts, and the song's copyright listings name John Wilbur Chapman, Mark Hall, and Michael Bleecker, and they confirm 5638022 and 2009. “Wilbanks” does not appear on those credits. Chapman wrote the hymn “One Day” in 1910. Hall and Bleecker are the Casting Crowns credit for this 2009 setting, which adds “The Word became flesh” and “took the nails from me,” and which does not include Chapman's garden stanza. The header uses Chapman, Hall, and Bleecker. The year on the header is 2009, the year of this setting. The setlist keys include A. No separate arrangement year is on file.

Word to Worship and PraiseCharts both print “took the nails from me.” Word to Worship prints “Wonderful day, my beloved one bringing / My Savior Jesus is mine,” and its bridge also echoes “We long for Your coming” and “Your glories will shine.” PraiseCharts prints “my beloved ones bringing” and does not print those echoes. Chapman's older last line is often printed “Glorious Savior, this Jesus is mine.” This grade follows the Word to Worship wording for the bridge. The singular “beloved one” is the harder grammar. “Beloved ones” would mean the people brought on that day. Either printing still ends at “My Savior Jesus is mine.”

## Why this overall label

The chorus tells the church that Jesus loved the singer living, saved the singer dying, carried the singer's sins in the burial, justified the singer rising, and is coming. The stanzas walk the virgin birth, Calvary, the grave, and the trumpet. “My example is He” is the example Jesus gave in John 13:15. The coming that brings his people is 1 Thessalonians 4:14. With the chorus and the Calvary stanza sung, this is a text to feature.
