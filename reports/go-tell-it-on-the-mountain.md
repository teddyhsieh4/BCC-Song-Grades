# Go Tell It on the Mountain

**Source:** African American spiritual, refrain older than the stanzas. The three nativity stanzas in the form associated with John Wesley Work Jr., as printed in *The Presbyterian Hymnal* (1990) and *Christian Worship* (1993), read 26 Nov 2026 on Hymnary. Those books tie the stanzas to Luke 2:8-20. A common publication date for Work's stanzas is 1907. No single CCLI number is used in the catalog, and none is put on this report.

## Thesis

The church is told to go and say, over the hills and everywhere, that Jesus Christ is born. Shepherds watched in the night and saw a holy light. They feared when the angel chorus hailed the Savior's birth. In a lowly manger the humble Christ was born, and God sent salvation on that morning.

---

## Overall recommendation

Acceptable with notes

Sing it at Christmas, and sing the three nativity stanzas with the refrain. The church has logged it once in this window, in 2023. “That Jesus Christ is born” and “God sent us salvation / That blessed Christmas morn” have to stay. The song announces the birth and calls it salvation. It does not say the cross or the resurrection. On a Christmas morning it can open the news. It should not be the only Christ-song in the hour.

## Voice and addressee

The refrain is a command: go tell it. The “you” is the church, sent to say the news. The stanzas are narrative, third person, about the shepherds, the angels, and the child. “Our Savior's birth” and “God sent us salvation” are the only first-person words, and they are plural. Nobody addresses God as “You.” The song faces the congregation and then the world beyond it, with a birth to announce.

---

## How the text moves

The refrain states the errand before any scene: tell it on the mountain, over the hills and everywhere, that Jesus Christ is born. The first stanza is the night watch and the holy light. The second is the fear, and the angel chorus that hailed the Savior's birth. The third comes down to the manger, the humble Christ, and the salvation God sent on that morning. The refrain returns the same sentence. The news does not grow past the birth. By the last stanza the birth has been called salvation, and the church is still being sent to say so.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“Go, tell it on the mountain / That Jesus Christ is born” is the line the grade depends on, with “hailed our Savior's birth” and “God sent us salvation / That blessed Christmas morn.” The shepherds and the light are the scene Luke gives, and they should stay so the birth is not a bare slogan. A stanza some books add, about a seeker looking night and day until the Lord showed the way, is a different text. This grade does not use it. If that stanza is on a chart, it changes the song from the nativity to a private search, and it should not replace the manger. No line in the three stanzas is false. Without “salvation” and “Savior,” the refrain would be an announcement of a birth with the angel's title left out.

## Persons of the Trinity named

Jesus Christ is named, and he is the Savior and the humble Christ in the manger. God is the one who sent salvation. The Father is not given that title. The Spirit is unnamed. The angel chorus hails the birth and is not said to be the Spirit's work. The song is about the Son's birth and the God who sent him.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Someone who denies that Jesus is the Christ cannot sing the refrain. “That Jesus Christ is born,” “our Savior's birth,” and “the humble Christ” confess who the child is. A hearer who only wants a teacher's birthday can try the shepherds and the manger until “God sent us salvation.” The cross is still unsaid. The announcement blocks a vague theist. It leaves the reason the child saves for another text.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none in the three nativity stanzas and the refrain.
- **Non-biblical:** “O'er silent flocks by night.” Luke's shepherds are “keeping watch over their flock by night.” “Silent” is the song's detail. “That blessed Christmas morn” names the morning in the church's calendar. Luke says “this day.”
- **Thin:** “The humble Christ.” The manger is Luke 2:7. Humility is the right word for that birth, and Philippians 2:7-8 will later say he humbled himself to death. The song stops at the manger. The seeker stanza, if a chart has it, is thinner than these three, and it is not part of this grade.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 4

The refrain and the stanzas follow Luke 2:8-14. Shepherds were out in the field, keeping watch by night. An angel of the Lord stood before them, and the glory of the Lord shone around them, and they were filled with fear. The angel said, “For unto you is born this day in the city of David a Savior, who is Christ the Lord.” The song's “holy light,” the fear and trembling, and “the angel chorus that hailed our Savior's birth” are that night. “Jesus Christ is born” is the angel's Christ, named with the name the church confesses. “Down in a lowly manger” is Luke 2:7, the child laid in a manger because there was no place in the inn. “The humble Christ” fits that scene.

“God sent us salvation / That blessed Christmas morn” is the song's summary of the angel's word, that the birth is the arrival of a Savior. The summary is true. It does not say how the Savior will deal with sin. “Silent” and “Christmas morn” are the song's own details around a narrative that otherwise stays with Luke. The three stanzas are accurate to the announcement. They are the announcement, which is why the score is 4 and not a full account of the gospel.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 3

Jesus Christ is named, born, humble, and hailed as Savior, and the birth is called the salvation God sent. That is the incarnation and the angel's title, which is more than a Christmas scene. The finished work is not here. There is no cross, no blood, no burial, and no resurrection, because the song's whole text is the night of the birth and the command to tell it. A Christmas lyric can be faithful to Luke 2 and still leave the cross for the rest of the church's singing. The score stays at 3 because “salvation” is announced and not unfolded.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

The refrain sends the church to proclaim an event. The stanzas narrate the shepherds, the light, the fear, the angels, and the manger. “Our Savior” and “sent us salvation” are the church included in the angel's “unto you,” which is still a fact about the birth. There is no “I feel,” and no vow. The seeker stanza some books add would change this score. The three stanzas do not. The song declares what happened and tells the church to say it.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 3

God sends salvation. The child is Christ, Savior, and humble. The light over the shepherds is called holy. That is as far as the attributes go. Love, justice, wrath, and faithfulness are not named. The humility is the manner of the birth. The holiness is the light of the announcement. The news is that he sent a Savior into a manger. The character of God in this song is in that sending.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 4

One log, in 2023, means Christmas is the only hearing in this window. The refrain is short, and the command is plain: go tell that Jesus Christ is born. A congregation learns that the birth is news to be said in public, that shepherds heard it from angels, and that God sent salvation in that child. They do not learn the cross. “Savior” and “salvation” are the words that keep the carol from being only a manger picture, and they are the words a cut stanza-set might lose if someone sings the refrain alone. The diction is ordinary. The teaching is the announcement, which is the right teaching for the night Luke 2 is read, and it is not enough teaching for the whole morning.

---

## Tone

The tone is urgent and glad. The line that sets it is “Go, tell it on the mountain, over the hills and everywhere / Go, tell it on the mountain, that Jesus Christ is born.” The shepherds' fear is in the second stanza, and the refrain does not erase it. It sends the church out with the news anyway. Nothing in the three stanzas is cute. The manger is called lowly, and the child is called the Christ.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never says that the child will die for sin and rise, never names a sinner, and never says how the salvation sent at Christmas was accomplished.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Luke 2, on a Christmas morning or the Sunday the birth is preached. The refrain belongs with the angel's announcement. The manger stanza belongs with Luke 2:7.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The cross and the resurrection. Pair it with *Come Behold the Wondrous Mystery* (logged 7 times), which goes on from the birth, or with *In Christ Alone* (logged 8 times), so the Savior who is born is also the Savior who dies and rises.
- **Diet sentence.** Once in this window, it adds a command to announce the birth, and it calls that birth the salvation God sent. It never adds the cross.
- **What not to do.** Do not sing the refrain only. Do not replace the manger stanza with a seeker stanza. Do not make this the only song about Christ on the morning it is used.

## Identification notes

One traditional song. The catalog names an African American spiritual, leaves the year as “unknown / pub. 1907,” and leaves CCLI empty, which matches the note in the rubric that Go Tell It has no CCLI. The header year 1907 is the publication of the stanzas in common use. The refrain is older. Hymnary's Presbyterian and Lutheran printings credit the stanzas to John Wesley Work Jr. and keep the refrain as the spiritual. This grade follows those three stanzas: the watching shepherds and the holy light; the fear and the angel chorus hailing the Savior's birth; the lowly manger, the humble Christ, and the salvation God sent.

Edition-specific numbers exist and were not adopted. A hymn site prints 5342545. One denominational list prints 7040771. A modern arrangement prints 7197607. They are not one settled number for the spiritual, and the catalog rightly has none. Some books add a stanza beginning “When I was a seeker.” That stanza is not in the three scored here. If the church chart includes it, the leader should know this grade did not. No arrangement year is on file. The setlist variant does not print a key.

---

## Why this overall label

The refrain sends the church to say that Jesus Christ is born, and the stanzas tell the shepherds, the angels, the manger, and the salvation God sent that morning. The narrative stays with Luke 2. The cross and the resurrection are outside the song, so a Christmas service still needs another text that says them. With the three stanzas sung, the carol can carry the announcement. It should not be asked to carry the whole gospel.
