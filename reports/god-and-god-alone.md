## Thesis

There is one church, one faith, one cross, one grace, and one name that saves, and the praise belongs to God alone. He washes sin, raises from the grave, sits on the throne, and will receive every knee. The church answers by lifting his name above every other song.

---

## Overall recommendation

Strong

Use it when the service is on the uniqueness of God and the name of Jesus, and sing verse 2 with the chorus. The church has logged it 5 times across at least three years (2023, 2024, 2025). “One cross, one grace, one name that saves” and “Who else can wash our sin away” and “Who else can raise us from the grave” have to stay. “We lift You higher” and “Your name be louder” are the church’s praise. They should not be the only lines the band repeats. Jesus is named. The wrath borne at the cross is not.

## Voice and addressee

The verses and chorus are corporate. “We lift You,” “our sin,” “raise us.” God is addressed as “You,” and verse 2 turns the praise to Jesus by name: “Jesus, all praise to You belongs.” The bridge asks what could separate “us” and says every knee will bow. The church is speaking to God about his uniqueness and his saving work.

---

## How the text moves

It starts with one church, one faith, one anthem, then one cross, one grace, and one name that saves. The chorus says the church lifts him higher, asks that his name be louder than any other song, and says he is seated on the throne forever. Verse 2 asks who else washes sin and who else raises from the grave, and answers with Jesus. The bridge asks what could separate the church from this love, and answers that every knee will bow. The chorus does not add a new doctrine. The saving claims are in the two verses.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“One cross, one grace, one name that saves” and “Who else can wash our sin away” and “Who else can raise us from the grave” are the lines the grade depends on, with “Jesus, all praise to You belongs.” “You are forever seated on Your throne” keeps the praise from being only the church’s volume. If the band loops “We lift You higher” and “Your name be louder” and drops the cross, the washing, and the grave, the room is left with a louder song and a higher lift. No line is false. The verses are what teach.

## Persons of the Trinity named

Jesus is named in verse 2 as the one who receives the praise after the washing and the grave. “God and God alone” and “You” carry the rest. The Father and the Spirit are unnamed. The one God of the title is, by verse 2, the Jesus who saves.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. “One cross, one grace, one name that saves,” “wash our sin away,” “raise us from the grave,” and “Jesus, all praise to You belongs” cannot be moved to another god. A worshiper who wants a generic deity can try “We lift You higher” and “seated on Your throne” by themselves. Those lines are not the song once verse 1 and verse 2 are sung.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** “Your name be louder, louder than any other song.” That is the church asking for volume. It does not state a doctrine.
- **Thin:** “We lift You higher, higher.” Praise can magnify the Lord. The line does not say what he has done. “This amazing love” in the bridge names love and then asks what could separate the church from it. The cross that shows the love is in verse 1.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 4

“One name that saves” agrees with Acts 4:12, that there is no other name under heaven by which we must be saved. “Who else can wash our sin away” agrees with Revelation 1:5, “who loves us and has freed us from our sins by his blood,” in shorter words. The song says wash and does not say blood. “Who else can raise us from the grave” is the hope that God raises his people. “You are forever seated on Your throne” agrees with Hebrews 1:3, the Son seated at the right hand of the Majesty on high, though the song does not say “at the right hand.” “Every knee will bow down” is Philippians 2:10. “What could separate us from this amazing love” follows Romans 8:39, that nothing in all creation “will be able to separate us from the love of God in Christ Jesus our Lord.” The song says “this amazing love” and “our God” and does not say “in Christ Jesus our Lord” in the bridge. Verse 2 has already named Jesus.

“We lift You higher” and “Your name be louder” are the church’s praise. Psalm 34:3 says, “Oh, magnify the LORD with me.” These lines do not add a fact about God. The saving lines can be tied to texts. The chorus spends its time on height and volume.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 4

The song names the cross and the saving name. “One cross, one grace, one name that saves” and “Jesus, all praise to You belongs” put that name on Jesus. “Who else can wash our sin away” says sin is washed. The song never states substitution. The blood and a death in the sinner’s place are absent from the lyric. “Who else can raise us from the grave” raises the church. The verses confess one cross and one saving name, and they leave the exchange unsaid.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

The verses declare what is true of God: one cross, one grace, one name, the washing of sin, the raising from the grave, the throne, every knee. “We lift You higher” and “Your name be louder” are the church’s action and the church’s request about its own sound. They are corporate. They are still about how the church praises. The score stays at 4 because the chorus, which will be repeated, is that praise, and the doctrines sit in the verses.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 4

He is God alone, the one who saves, who washes sin, who raises from the grave, who is seated forever, and before whom every knee bows. Love is “this amazing love,” and the song has already set that love next to the cross and the grace. Holiness and wrath are not named. The uniqueness is the title. Justice is present as sin that must be washed, without the word wrath.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 4

Five logs across at least three years means the room has sung it and does not live on it. The lines are short. A congregation can learn that there is one saving name, one cross, one grace, and that Jesus washes sin and raises from the grave, and that he is on the throne. “Louder” and “higher” will not teach that if they are all that is repeated. Verse 2 is the stanza to keep in front of the people. The song is clear enough to use often when those lines are actually sung.

---

## Tone

The tone is declarative and large. The line that sets it is “We lift You higher, higher, God and God alone,” after the cross and the saving name. The verses are plainer than the chorus. Nothing in the song is playful. The bowing of every knee is the weight under the volume.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never says that the cross bore wrath, never says “blood,” and never names the Spirit.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Acts 4:12, Philippians 2, Romans 8:31-39, or the uniqueness of God, with verse 2 left in.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The wrath borne and the blood. Pair it with *Hallelujah, What a Savior* (logged 6 times) or *It Was Finished Upon That Cross* (logged 11 times) when the sermon is the cost of the cross.
- **Diet sentence.** Five times in at least three years, it adds one cross, one saving name, a washing of sin, and the raising of the church from the grave. It never says the word wrath.
- **What not to do.** Do not loop “higher” and “louder” and drop “one cross” and “wash our sin away.” Do not sing this as the Phil McHugh song. That is a different text and CCLI 12386.

## Why this overall label

The song tells the church that one cross, one grace, and one name save, that Jesus washes sin and raises from the grave, and that he is seated on the throne while every knee bows. The chorus also asks the church to lift him higher and to make his name louder than any other song. With the two verses sung, this is a text the church can use regularly.
