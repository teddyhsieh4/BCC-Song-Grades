## Thesis

The congregation will not fear the battle or the night. They will walk the valley with the Lord by their side. The Father’s love is a strong and mighty fortress. Who can stand against them if God is for them? Even when the singer stumbles, falls, or turns back, the Lord’s love is sure. He will not abandon or forsake. Neither height nor depth can separate. Hell and death will not defeat. He who gave His Son to free us holds the singer in His love.

---

## Overall recommendation

Strong

Use it regularly. The bridge names Romans 8: the giving of the Son to free us, the love that holds, and the impossibility of separation. Keep the bridge. The rest of the lyric is a clear confession that God is for His people and that His love is sure even in stumbling and turning back. The gospel is present in the bridge.

## Voice and addressee

Mostly first-person plural addressing the congregation, with first-person singular in verse 2 and the bridge. It is declaration and testimony. The congregation can own the confession that God is for them, that the Father’s love is a fortress, and that He who gave His Son holds them in His love.

---

## How the text moves

It opens with the corporate confession: “We won’t fear the battle, we won’t fear the night / We will walk the valley with You by our side / You will go before us, You will lead the way / We have found a refuge, only You can save.” The chorus declares “Sing with joy now: our God is for us / The Father’s love is a strong and mighty fortress / Raise your voice now, no love is greater / Who can stand against us if our God is for us.” Verse 2 is the personal testimony: “Even when I stumble, even when I fall / Even when I turn back, still Your love is sure / You will not abandon, You will not forsake / You will cheer me onward with never-ending grace.” The bridge names the ordinary sense of Romans 8: “Neither height nor depth can separate us / Hell and death will not defeat us / He who gave His son to free us / Holds me in His love.”

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“He who gave His son to free us / Holds me in His love” and “Neither height nor depth can separate us.”

Dropping those lines leaves the confession that God is for us and that His love is a fortress without the named giving of the Son and the impossibility of separation. The chorus still declares that God is for us, but the specific gospel act that shows why God is for us is in the bridge.

## Persons of the Trinity named

The Father is named. The Son is named as the one given to free us. The Spirit is not named. The work of the Father in giving the Son is explicit.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. The giving of the Son to free us, the love that holds, and the impossibility of separation cannot be sung by a vague theist without correction. The confession that God is for us is tied to the Son who was given.

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“Our God is for us” and “Who can stand against us if our God is for us” is the ordinary sense of Romans 8:31. “The Father’s love is a strong and mighty fortress” is the ordinary sense of Psalm 18:2 and Proverbs 18:10. “You will not abandon, You will not forsake” is the ordinary sense of Deuteronomy 31:6 and Hebrews 13:5. “Neither height nor depth can separate us / Hell and death will not defeat us / He who gave His son to free us / Holds me in His love” is the ordinary sense of Romans 8:38–39 and Romans 8:32. The lines stay inside ordinary biblical sense. The score is 5 because the bridge names the ordinary sense of those texts.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 4

The Son is named as the one given to free us. The giving of the Son is the ordinary sense of Romans 8:32. The impossibility of separation is named. The score is 4 rather than 5 because the rest of the lyric does not name the cross, the substitution, or the resurrection in further detail. The gospel is present in the bridge, which is why the score is 4 rather than 3.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

The chorus and verse 1 declare what God is and what He will do. Verse 2 is testimony of the sure love even in stumbling and turning back. The bridge is the declaration of Romans 8. Those claims are grounded in the giving of the Son and the impossibility of separation, which is why the score is 4 rather than 5. The singer does not make high-claim present-tense vows without grounding. The testimony rests on what God has already done in giving the Son.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 4

God is for His people. The Father’s love is a strong and mighty fortress. He will not abandon or forsake. He gave His Son to free us and holds the singer in His love. The attributes are specific and tied to the fortress, the sure love, and the giving of the Son. Holiness and justice appear as the giving of the Son to free us. The score is 4 because the lyric stays focused on the love that is for us and the impossibility of separation rather than a broader list of attributes.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The language is plain. The lyric teaches that God is for His people, that the Father’s love is a fortress, that He will not forsake, and that the giving of the Son makes separation impossible. The congregation can learn the ordinary sense of Romans 8 by singing the bridge. The plural and singular voices are testimony the whole church can own. Singability is high, and the teaching is the reason for the 5. Keep the bridge so the confession that God is for us stays attached to the Son who was given.

---

## Tone

Reverent and honest. It does not ask the room to confer on God what He has not already done. The intensity stays inside the fortress, the sure love, the giving of the Son, and the impossibility of separation.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names the Spirit. That is a small gap. The main gospel elements of the giving of the Son to free us and the impossibility of separation are present in the bridge.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Romans 8, Psalm 18, Deuteronomy 31, Hebrews 13, any text on God being for His people, the fortress of His love, or the impossibility of separation. Especially useful after a sermon that has named the giving of the Son.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** A fuller naming of the cross and the empty tomb if the service has not already supplied them. The bridge names the giving of the Son; the rest of the hour can name the cross and the resurrection in further detail.
- **Diet sentence.** It adds a clear confession that God is for His people, that the Father’s love is a fortress, that He will not forsake even in stumbling, and that the giving of the Son makes separation impossible. It does not add a full narrative of the cross or the empty tomb.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop the bridge. Do not let the chorus stand alone without the named giving of the Son.
