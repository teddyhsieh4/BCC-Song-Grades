## Thesis

The singer was an orphan lost at the fall, running from the Father’s call, with no righteousness and no right to the throne. The Father worked His will, loved still, and in love before the foundation predestined the adoption. The Son left home, knew the cost, set His face, and paid the debt. By His blood there is redemption; He died and rose so the singer might be a new creation. The Spirit moved in darkness and a heart of stone, awakening the sleeping spirit so that the light of Christ shone. The singer is a child of God, born again, heaven’s citizen, by grace and grace alone.

---

## Overall recommendation

**Excellent / Prefer**

Feature it. Sing all three stanzas and the choruses. The first names the Father’s predestining love and the adoption. The second names the Son’s leaving home, the cost, the blood, the death, and the rising. The third names the Spirit’s work in the darkened heart. Without those lines the song is true confession of need that has not named the Persons or the work.

The church has sung it for at least four years (6 logs in the 2023–2026 snapshot). It is already known.

## Voice and addressee

The song is first-person singular throughout. “I was an orphan.” “You predestined to adopt me.” It addresses the Father, then the Son, then the Spirit. The claims can be sung by the congregation together.

---

## How the text moves

The first stanza names the orphan lost at the fall, the running, the absence of righteousness, and the Father’s working will and continuing love. The chorus names the predestining love before the foundation and the adoption as a child of God by grace alone. The second stanza names the Son’s leaving home, the known cost, the set face, the failed atonement of human work, and the paid debt. The chorus names the blood, the death that the singer might reap what was sown, and the rising that makes a new creation. The third stanza names the darkness, the stone heart, and the Spirit’s making to see and moving within. The chorus names the awakening touch, the light of Christ, and the calling into the unshakable kingdom. The tag states standing, running, slaying sin, and reaching the end by grace alone.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

Do not drop the second stanza and its chorus. “You left your home to seek out the lost; / You knew the great and terrible cost / But Jesus, your face was set. / … Nothing I did could ever atone, / But Jesus, you paid my debt. / By Your blood I have redemption and salvation / Lord you died that I might reap what you have sown, / And you rose that I might be a new creation” is the incarnation’s cost, the insufficiency of works, the blood, the death, and the resurrection. Without it the church has confessed the Father’s love and the Spirit’s work and has not named the cross or the rising.

Do not drop the first chorus. “And in love before you laid the world’s foundation / You predestined to adopt me as your own” is the predestining love. Without it the adoption is named as a present fact that has not named the eternal ground.

No line is false.

## Persons of the Trinity named

The Father is addressed as the One who worked His will, loved still, and predestined the adoption. The Son is addressed as Jesus who left home, paid the debt, and rose. The Spirit is addressed as the One who made to see and moved in the stone heart. All three Persons are named in their ordinary work.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Not the whole song. The second stanza names the leaving home, the paid debt, the blood, the death, and the rising. The first chorus names the predestining adoption. A vague theist could not sing those lines as written.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

**Unbiblical.** None.

**Non-biblical.** None.

**Thin.** Burial is not named between death and rising. Present intercession is not named. The lyric does not need those details to make its claim.

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“I was an orphan, lost at the fall; / Running away when I’d hear you call, / But Father, you worked your will. / I had no righteousness of my own / I had no right to draw near your throne, / But Father, you loved me still” is Genesis 3, Romans 3:10, and Ephesians 2:4–5. The lostness and the continuing love are the ordinary sense of those texts.

“And in love before you laid the world’s foundation / You predestined to adopt me as your own / … I’m a child of God by grace and grace alone” is Ephesians 1:4–5 and Galatians 4:4–5. The predestination and the adoption are the ordinary sense of those texts.

“You left your home to seek out the lost; / You knew the great and terrible cost / But Jesus, your face was set. / … Nothing I did could ever atone, / But Jesus, you paid my debt. / By Your blood I have redemption and salvation / Lord you died that I might reap what you have sown, / And you rose that I might be a new creation” is Luke 9:51, Romans 3:25, 1 Peter 1:18–19, and 2 Corinthians 5:17. The sequence is the gospel sequence.

“I was in darkness all of my life / … But Spirit, you made me see. / … Head full of rocks a heart made of stone / But Spirit, you moved in me. / And at your touch my sleeping spirit was awakened; / On my darkened heart the light of Christ has shone” is 2 Corinthians 4:6 and Ezekiel 36:26. The Spirit’s work is the ordinary sense of those texts.

The lines use the ordinary sense of those texts.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

Christ is named as the One who left home, knew the cost, set His face, paid the debt, shed the blood, died, and rose. The work is stated as redemption, salvation, and new creation. It is not left as a title.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

The main claim is objective. The Father predestined. The Son paid the debt and rose. The Spirit moved and awakened. The first-person lines are the singer’s confession of the benefit. They are not a vow the singer must mean before he may sing. They are the testimony of the grace received.

The score stays at 4 because the voice is the singer’s testimony rather than pure declaration about God alone.

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The stanzas are clear. The refrain “by grace and grace alone” is short. The words are ordinary. The church has already sung it. The people learn the shape of the gospel from the Father’s predestining love, through the Son’s paid debt and rising, to the Spirit’s awakening. They do not need a weekly explanation of any line. Keys on file include E.

If time is short, do not drop the second stanza.

---

## Tone

The tone is glad and sober. “By grace and grace alone” answers the work just named. There is no intensity beyond the text.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names the burial or present intercession. It never says “wrath” in that word, though “the great and terrible cost” and “you paid my debt” state the same fact.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Ephesians 1–2, Romans 3–5, 2 Corinthians 5, or any text on election, adoption, the cross, or the new birth. It works as a response after the sermon has opened the doctrines of grace.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** Explicit burial language if the sermon has required it and no other song supplies it. The return of Christ if the day’s text is the second coming.
- **Diet sentence.** It adds the Father’s predestining adoption, the Son’s paid debt and rising, and the Spirit’s awakening of the stone heart. It does not add the burial narrative.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop the second stanza. Do not treat the “grace alone” tag as a standalone refrain after a thinner song. The stanzas supply the reason for the claim.
