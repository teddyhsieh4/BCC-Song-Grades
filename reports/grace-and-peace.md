## Thesis

Grace and peace have come to lawbreakers and thieves, people under a judgment of death. The matchless King paid the blood price. The slaughtered Lamb brings atonement, so the vilest sinner’s heart can be cleansed. The singer, loved by God and called as a saint, is satisfied in the riches of Christ.

---

## Overall recommendation

Excellent / Prefer

Feature it. The church has logged it 3 times across at least three years (2023, 2025, 2026), so the room is still learning a text that already teaches. Sing all three stanzas. “You have said that our judgment is death” and “Paid the blood price for me” and “Slaughtered lamb, what atonement You bring” have to stay with “Loved by God and called as a saint.” The song puts Romans in the congregation’s mouth: the sentence of death, the blood, the cleansing of the worst sinner, and the call of a saint.

## Voice and addressee

The stanzas address God as “You.” “Our judgment,” “for me,” “the vilest sinner’s heart,” “my heart.” The singer stands with lawbreakers and then says the grace has come to the singer. Jesus is not given the name “Jesus” until the riches of Christ in the third stanza. The King and the Lamb are addressed as the one who paid. The church is confessing to God what he has done for sinners, in a singular “me” a whole room can sing.

---

## How the text moves

It opens on the greeting of grace and peace and asks how it can belong to lawbreakers, thieves, the worthless, and the least. God has said their judgment is death, for all eternity, without hope or rest. The mystery is that his grace has come. The second stanza names the matchless King who paid the blood price, the slaughtered Lamb and his atonement, and the vilest heart cleansed and freed. The third stanza turns the same greeting toward grateful song that does not cease, the love of God, the call of a saint, and a heart satisfied in the riches of Christ. The tag calls that grace an amazing love. The claim moves from the sentence of death, to the blood, to the saint’s satisfaction.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“You have said that our judgment is death” and “The matchless King of all / Paid the blood price for me” and “Slaughtered lamb, what atonement You bring” are the lines the grade depends on. “The vilest sinner’s heart / Can be cleansed, can be free” and “Loved by God and called as a saint” belong with them. If the band keeps “Your grace has come to me” and drops the judgment, the blood, and the Lamb, the greeting is left without the sinners it was written for. No line is false. The second stanza is the one a short cut is most likely to lose, and it is the atonement.

## Persons of the Trinity named

Christ is named in “the riches of Christ.” The matchless King and the slaughtered Lamb are the one who paid and atones, addressed as “You.” “Loved by God” names God as the one who loves and calls. The Father and the Spirit are not given those titles. The grace and peace of the first line are the apostolic greeting, sung here without the phrase “from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.”

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. “Paid the blood price for me,” “Slaughtered lamb, what atonement You bring,” and “In the riches of Christ” cannot be moved to another savior. A moralist who thinks well of a clean record cannot sing “lawbreakers and thieves” and “the vilest sinner’s heart” as the moralist’s own company without accepting the sentence. “Your grace has come to me,” sung alone, is smaller than the song.

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“Grace and peace” is the greeting of Romans 1:7, “Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.” “For lawbreakers and thieves” and “the worthless, the least” put that greeting on sinners. “You have said that our judgment is death / For all eternity” agrees with Romans 6:23, “For the wages of sin is death,” and with the eternity of that sentence apart from Christ. “The matchless King of all / Paid the blood price for me” is a price paid by the King for the singer. Hebrews 9:12 says Christ entered the holy places “by means of his own blood, thus securing an eternal redemption.” “Slaughtered lamb, what atonement You bring” agrees with Revelation 5:9, the Lamb who was slain and by whose blood people are ransomed, and with the atonement that cleanses. “The vilest sinner’s heart / Can be cleansed” is the reach of that blood, said of the worst heart, which this church may sing without shrinking the line. “Loved by God and called as a saint” agrees with Romans 1:7, those “called to be saints,” and with the love that calls. “In the riches of Christ” is the wealth of Ephesians 1. The three stanzas follow the book the album names.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

The King pays the blood price. The Lamb is slaughtered and brings atonement. The sinner’s heart is cleansed and freed. The singer is loved, called a saint, and satisfied in Christ’s riches. Judgment of death is named before the blood, so the grace is not a mood. The resurrection and the word “cross” are not here. The blood price and the slain Lamb are the death. That is the gospel in the stanzas.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

The stanzas declare the judgment, the blood, the Lamb, the atonement, the cleansing, and the call. “That Your grace has come to me,” “Paid the blood price for me,” and “My heart is satisfied” put the singer in the good that God has done. “Oh, what an amazing love I see” is the singer’s sight of that love. The “me” is testimony of a public fact, the price and the call. The score stays at 4 because the tag lingers on what the singer sees.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 5

He speaks a judgment of death and then gives grace and peace to the people under that sentence. He is the matchless King. He pays a blood price. He is the slaughtered Lamb whose atonement cleanses the vilest heart. He loves and calls saints. Christ is the wealth that satisfies. Justice and mercy are both in the lyric, in that order: death, then blood. Holiness is in the cleansing of a heart that was vile. Love is the tag, after the price has been named.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

Three logs across at least three years means the song is in the rotation and is not yet worn smooth. The words are plain. A congregation that sings the three stanzas learns that grace and peace are for lawbreakers, that the judgment was death, that the King paid in blood, that the Lamb atones, and that the called saint is satisfied in Christ. “Slaughtered lamb” is strong and clear. “Vilest” should be sung as written. The second stanza is the one that teaches the atonement. The song is short enough to feature and dense enough to carry a Sunday that is in Romans.

---

## Tone

The tone is astonished and plain. The line that sets it is “Grace and peace, oh how can this be,” asked of lawbreakers who are under a sentence of death. The second stanza is heavier: blood price, slaughtered Lamb, vilest heart. The third stanza is grateful and does not lighten the blood. The wonder stays tied to the judgment and the price.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never says that Christ rose, never says the word cross, and never names the Spirit who applies the call.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Romans 1:7, Romans 6:23, Ephesians 2, Hebrews 9, or the cleansing of sinners. All three stanzas belong. The second belongs with the blood.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The resurrection. Pair it with *Christ Our Hope in Life and Death* (logged 8 times) or *Yet Not I but Through Christ in Me* (logged 10 times) so the blood price is not left without the empty grave.
- **Diet sentence.** Three times in at least three years, it adds the sentence of death, the blood price, the atonement of the Lamb, and the call of a saint. It never adds the resurrection.
- **What not to do.** Do not sing only the tag, “Your grace has come to me.” Do not drop “our judgment is death” or “slaughtered lamb.”
