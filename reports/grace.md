## Thesis

Grace leads this sinner home from death to life forever and sings the song of righteousness by blood and not by merit. It reaches to every tribe and nation and has called the singer’s heart into the joy of salvation. By grace the singer is redeemed and restored, and freely walks into the arms of Christ. The prince of life, without a stain, was traded for this sinner.

---

## Overall recommendation

Excellent / Prefer

Feature it. The lyric names the sinner, the move from death to life, righteousness by blood and not by merit, the prince of life traded for the sinner, and the free walk into the arms of Christ. Keep those lines. The whole text is dense and Scripture-shaped.

## Voice and addressee

Mostly second-person addressing grace (and, by implication, the Lord), with first-person singular in the chorus and bridge. It is testimony of what grace has done. The congregation can own the confession of the sinner led home and the free walk into the arms of Christ.

---

## How the text moves

It opens by addressing grace that leads this sinner home from death to life forever and sings the song of righteousness by blood and not by merit. Verse 2 names grace that reaches to every tribe and nation and has called the singer’s heart into the joy of salvation. The chorus declares “By grace I am redeemed / By grace I am restored / And now I freely walk / Into the arms of Christ my Lord.” Verse 3 names the grace that cannot be explained by earthly wisdom: “The prince of life, without a stain / Was traded for this sinner.” The bridge asks that praise rise and overflow, because grace will see the singer welcomed home to walk beside the Saviour.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“The prince of life, without a stain / Was traded for this sinner” and “By blood and not by merit.”

Dropping those lines leaves the declarations of being led home, redeemed, and restored without the named exchange of the sinless prince of life for the sinner, and without the clear statement that righteousness is by blood and not by merit. The chorus still names redemption and restoration, but the specific gospel trade and the exclusion of merit are in those lines.

## Persons of the Trinity named

Christ is named as Lord and as the prince of life. Grace is addressed. The Father and the Spirit are not named. The work of the Son is explicit.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. The sinner led home from death to life, righteousness by blood and not by merit, the prince of life traded for the sinner, and the free walk into the arms of Christ cannot be sung by a vague theist without correction. The grace that is named is the grace of that finished exchange.

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“Leads this sinner home / From death to life forever / And sings the song of righteousness / By blood and not by merit” is the ordinary sense of Ephesians 2:1–8 and Romans 3:21–26. “The prince of life, without a stain / Was traded for this sinner” is the ordinary sense of Acts 3:15, 1 Peter 1:19, and 2 Corinthians 5:21. “By grace I am redeemed / By grace I am restored” is Ephesians 1:7 and Colossians 1:13–14. The lines stay inside ordinary biblical sense. The reach to every tribe and nation is the ordinary sense of Revelation 5:9 and Matthew 28:19.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

Christ is named as Lord and as the prince of life. Substitution is stated: the sinless prince of life was traded for this sinner. Righteousness is by blood and not by merit. The singer is redeemed and restored and freely walks into the arms of Christ. The song does not stop at the word “grace.” It preaches the exchange that makes the grace specific.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

The verses declare what grace has done. The chorus is testimony of the result: redeemed, restored, freely walking into the arms of Christ. Those claims are grounded in the blood and the trade of the prince of life, which is why the score is 4 rather than 5. The singer does not make high-claim present-tense vows without grounding. The testimony rests on what grace has already done through the finished work.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 4

Grace is the grace that leads the sinner home, sings righteousness by blood, reaches to every tribe, calls the heart into the joy of salvation, and trades the sinless prince of life for the sinner. Christ is Lord. The attributes are specific and tied to the blood and the trade. Holiness appears as the prince of life without a stain. The score is 4 because the lyric stays focused on the work of grace rather than a broader list of attributes.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The language is plain. The lyric teaches the sinner led home from death to life, righteousness by blood and not by merit, the trade of the prince of life, and the free walk into the arms of Christ. The congregation can learn the finished work by singing those lines. The first-person lines are testimony the whole church can own. Singability is high, and the teaching is the reason for the 5. Keep the lines about blood and the trade so the word “grace” stays attached to the gospel.

---

## Tone

Reverent and honest. It does not ask the room to confer on grace what the finished work has not already done. The intensity stays inside the blood, the exclusion of merit, and the trade of the prince of life.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names the Spirit or the present intercession. Those are small gaps. The main gospel elements of the blood, the exclusion of merit, the trade of the prince of life, and the move from death to life are present.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Ephesians 2, Romans 3, 2 Corinthians 5:21, Acts 3:15, any text on grace, justification by blood, or the exchange. Especially useful after a sermon that has named the sinner and the prince of life traded for him.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The Spirit and a fuller naming of the resurrection if the service has not already supplied them.
- **Diet sentence.** It adds a clear confession that grace leads the sinner home from death to life by blood and not by merit, that the prince of life was traded for the sinner, and that the singer freely walks into the arms of Christ. It does not add a full narrative of the Spirit’s ongoing work.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop the lines about blood and not by merit, or the trade of the prince of life. Do not let the word “grace” stand alone without those named acts.
