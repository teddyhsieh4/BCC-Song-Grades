## Thesis

Grace leads this sinner home from death to life forever and sings the song of righteousness by blood and not by merit. It reaches to every tribe and nation and has called the singer’s heart into the joy of salvation. By grace the singer is redeemed and restored, and freely walks into the arms of Christ. The prince of life, without a stain, was traded for this sinner.

---

## Overall recommendation

Excellent / Prefer

Feature it. Keep the lines “By blood and not by merit” and “The prince of life, without a stain / Was traded for this sinner,” because they turn the word “grace” into the gospel. The church has sung it 7 times across at least four years of the 2023–2026 window.

## Voice and addressee

The verses speak to grace itself, and through it to the Lord. The chorus and bridge are first-person testimony. The whole congregation can sing it as its own story.

---

## How the text moves

Verse 1 says grace leads the sinner home by blood. Verse 2 says it reaches every nation and called the singer, and verse 3 names how: the sinless prince of life was traded for him. The chorus answers each time: redeemed, restored, and walking into the arms of Christ.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“The prince of life, without a stain / Was traded for this sinner” and “By blood and not by merit.” Without them, the chorus still says redeemed and restored, but the song never says what grace cost or that merit has no part in it.

## Persons of the Trinity named

Christ is named as Lord and as the prince of life. Grace is addressed. The Father and the Spirit are not named. The work of the Son is explicit.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. A vague theist could not sing “The prince of life, without a stain / Was traded for this sinner” without changing it.

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“Leads this sinner home / From death to life forever / And sings the song of righteousness / By blood and not by merit” is Ephesians 2:1–8 and Romans 3:21–26. “The prince of life, without a stain / Was traded for this sinner” is Acts 3:15, 1 Peter 1:19, and 2 Corinthians 5:21. “By grace I am redeemed / By grace I am restored” is Ephesians 1:7 and Colossians 1:13–14. The reach to every tribe and nation is Revelation 5:9 and Matthew 28:19.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

Christ is Lord and the prince of life. Substitution is stated as a trade: the sinless one for the sinner. Righteousness comes “by blood and not by merit,” which is justification in four words.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

The verses declare what grace has done, and the chorus gives the result as testimony. The score is 4 because the chorus centers the singer (“I am redeemed”), though it rests on the blood and the exchange.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 4

God’s grace leads sinners home, reaches every tribe, and calls the heart to joy. Its cost is the sinless prince of life given for the sinner. The score is 4 because grace is the one attribute the song develops.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The chorus is short and joyful, and the verses are clear. A congregation that sings it learns that grace means substitution and excludes merit.

---

## Tone

The tone is reverent and glad. The gladness comes from being welcomed home, and verse 3 keeps it tied to the cost.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names the Spirit or Christ’s present intercession, and the resurrection is not stated. When the sermon needs the resurrection, pair it with Christ Our Hope in Life and Death.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Ephesians 2, Romans 3, 2 Corinthians 5:21, Acts 3:15, or any text on grace, justification, or the great exchange.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The Spirit and a fuller statement of the resurrection, if the service has not supplied them.
- **Diet sentence.** Adds grace defined as the sinless one traded for the sinner, by blood and not by merit. It adds little on the Spirit.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop the lines about blood and merit or the trade of the prince of life, and do not let the word “grace” stand alone.
