## Thesis

The Lord is great and worthy of praise in the city of our God, the holy place, the joy of the whole earth. In him the singers have the victory, and he aids them against the enemy, so they bow. They want to lift his name, thank him for works in their lives, and trust his unfailing love, because he alone is God eternal in earth and heaven.

---

## Overall recommendation

Weak

Do not feature it. The church has logged it 5 times across at least four years (2023, 2024, 2025, 2026). “Great is the Lord and most worthy of praise / In the city of our God, the holy place” is the line that carries Psalm 48, and no line is false. Christ is never named. A worshiper who does not confess Jesus can sing the whole song unchanged. If it is used at all, keep it short, after Psalm 48 has been read, and let another song name the Son.

## Voice and addressee

The verse speaks about the Lord and then says “we have the victory” and “we bow down.” The chorus addresses him: “Lord, we want to lift Your name,” “thank You,” “we trust in Your unfailing love.” The church is declaring his greatness and then telling him what it wants to do.

---

## How the text moves

It opens with the greatness and the praise, the city of our God, the holy place, and the joy of the whole earth. It then says the Lord is the one in whom the singers have the victory, that he aids them against the enemy, and that they bow. The chorus does not return to the city. It says they want to lift his name, thank him for works in their lives, and trust his unfailing love, and it ends on the claim that he alone is God eternal through earth and heaven. The psalm’s city is the start. The chorus is the singers’ thanks and trust.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“Great is the Lord and most worthy of praise / In the city of our God, the holy place / The joy of the whole earth” is the line the grade depends on, with “For You alone are God eternal.” “He aids us against the enemy” and “the works You’ve done in our lives” are the lines that leave the psalm for the singers’ own story. If the band keeps the chorus and drops the city, the song is a thank-you for personal works and a trust in unfailing love. No line is false. The opening is what ties the song to Psalm 48.

## Persons of the Trinity named

“The Lord” and “God eternal” are the names. Father, Son, and Spirit are unnamed. Nothing in the lyric says the victory is the Son’s or that the city is the people of Christ.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Yes. The city of our God, the holy place, the joy of the earth, the victory, the enemy, the bowing, the unfailing love, and the eternal God can be sung by a worshiper of the God of Israel who does not confess Jesus. No line would force the name. “The works You’ve done in our lives” can be sung by any devout person about the singer’s own story.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** none.
- **Thin:** “In whom we have the victory” and “He aids us against the enemy.” Psalm 48 tells God’s defense of Zion. The song makes the victory and the enemy personal and does not say who the enemy is. “The works You’ve done in our lives” is thanks for unnamed works.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 3

“Great is the Lord and most worthy of praise / In the city of our God, the holy place / The joy of the whole earth” follows Psalm 48:1-2. The ESV reads, “Great is the LORD and greatly to be praised in the city of our God,” and calls Mount Zion “the joy of all the earth.” The song’s “most worthy of praise” and “the joy of the whole earth” are that text in a common English form. “You alone are God eternal / Throughout earth and heaven above” is a true confession of the one eternal God.

“In whom we have the victory” and “He aids us against the enemy” take the psalm’s theme of God’s defense and aim it at the singers. “We want to thank You / For the works You’ve done in our lives” leaves the city and thanks God for private benefits. The opening can be tied to Psalm 48. The rest of the song does not stay there. That is why the score stays at 3.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 2

Jesus is not named. The cross, the blood, the resurrection, and the name of Christ are absent. “The victory” has no gospel under it. “You alone are God eternal” is true of God and, in this lyric, is not tied to the Son. A worshiper of one God can receive the whole song unchanged.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 3

The opening declares the Lord, his city, and his praise. “We bow down on our knees” is the church’s response to that greatness. The chorus is desire and thanks: “we want to lift Your name,” “we want to thank You,” “we trust in Your unfailing love,” “the works You’ve done in our lives.” Half the song is about the Lord in his city. Half is about what the singers want and what he has done for them. The chorus is the half a band will repeat.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 3

He is great, worthy of praise, the God of the holy city, the joy of the earth, the giver of victory, the aid against the enemy, the one whose love does not fail, and the only eternal God in earth and heaven. “Unfailing love” is the attribute the chorus stays on. Holiness is the “holy place,” the city, and not a direct word about his character. Justice and wrath are unnamed. The list is real, and it is brief.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 3

Five logs across at least four years means the chorus is known here. The words are easy. A congregation can learn the opening of Psalm 48 and can say that God alone is eternal. They also learn to thank him for “works in our lives” and to trust an unfailing love, without ever hearing the name of Jesus. The opening is the part worth keeping, and only after the psalm has been read. The chorus will not teach the gospel.

---

## Tone

The tone is bright and grateful. The line that sets it is “Lord, we want to lift Your name on high,” after the bowing. The city and the holy place are steadier than “the works You’ve done in our lives.” Nothing in the song is harsh. The praise is warm and general.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names Jesus, never says that the victory is his cross and resurrection, and never says why the city of God is the joy of the earth.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Psalm 48, and only after the psalm has been read, with the opening left intact. It does not serve a sermon on the cross.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The Son. Pair it with *Behold Our God* or *Only a Holy God* so greatness is not left without the God those songs name more fully, and with a song that names the cross if the morning is the gospel.
- **Diet sentence.** Five times in at least four years, it adds two lines of Psalm 48 and a confession that God alone is eternal. It never adds Christ.
- **What not to do.** Do not treat this as the Michael W. Smith song (CCLI 1149). Do not loop the chorus and drop the city. Do not let it be the only song about God in the hour.
