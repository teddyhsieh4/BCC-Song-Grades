## Thesis

The singer asks for a heart that is a home for God and a life that resembles the Lord, and then confesses Jesus as the way, the truth, and the life, whose blood saved the singer.

---

## Overall recommendation

Acceptable with notes

Sing it only with the lines that name Jesus, the shed blood, and the price. It appears once in this window, in 2025. “My hands to stretch forth in healing others” asks every singer to confess a healing ministry the New Testament does not give the congregation as a whole. “A heart where You can feel at home” is a soft picture of the God who dwells with his people. Keep “And by Your shed blood, the Great Redeemer / You saved my soul, You paid the price.” Without those lines the song is a consecration prayer with a healing claim.

## Voice and addressee

First person singular, addressed to the Lord (“Oh Lord,” “Thee,” “You”). The middle of the song describes the singer’s tongue, hands, and feet. The confession at the end is still spoken to Christ.

---

## How the text moves

It opens with a longing that the heart be a habitation. It then asks to be like the Lord in speech, in healing, and in carrying the news. The last section changes the claim: Jesus is the way, the truth, and the life, and by his blood he saved the singer and paid the price. The ending, “You saved my soul, now have my life,” returns to consecration after that confession.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“And by Your shed blood, the Great Redeemer / You saved my soul, You paid the price” is the stanza the grade depends on. If a band keeps the longing and the healing line and drops the blood, the song no longer says how anyone is saved. “My hands to stretch forth in healing others” is the line that would hurt the song if it were sung as the point. No one line is false by itself. The blood lines have to stay.

## Persons of the Trinity named

“Lord Jesus Christ” is named, with the titles King of kings, Lord of lords, and Prince of Peace. The Father and the Spirit are unnamed. The opening “Oh Lord” is the same addressee as the Jesus of the last section. The song never says how the Spirit dwells in the church, which is the setting of Ephesians 2:22.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

The opening could be sung by someone who wants God to feel at home in the heart and who wants to be like him. “You’re the Way / You’re the Truth and You’re the Life” and “Lord Jesus Christ” stop that use. “By Your shed blood” and “You saved my soul, You paid the price” stop it again. If those lines are dropped, “habitation,” “healing,” and “Good News” can be sung without saying who the news is about.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none. The healing line overreaches. It does not deny a text.
- **Non-biblical:** “A heart where You can feel at home.” Scripture does not speak of God feeling at home in a heart.
- **Thin:** “Oh Lord, my heart longs to be / A habitation fit for Thee.” Ephesians 2:22 says the church is “a dwelling place for God by the Spirit.” The song moves that dwelling into a private longing and does not mention the Spirit. “I want to be like You” states the desire and does not say the cross that makes a people like Christ.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 3

“A habitation fit for Thee” reaches for Ephesians 2:22, “a dwelling place for God by the Spirit,” and for 1 Corinthians 6:19, “your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit.” The first verse says “feel at home” and never mentions the Spirit or the body as the Spirit’s temple. “You’re the Way / You’re the Truth and You’re the Life” is John 14:6, “I am the way, and the truth, and the life.” The song stops before “No one comes to the Father except through me.” “King of kings, Lord of lords” is Revelation 19:16. “Prince of Peace” is Isaiah 9:6. “By Your shed blood” and “You paid the price” stand near 1 Peter 1:18-19 and 1 Corinthians 6:20, “you were bought with a price.” “You saved my soul” is the singer’s summary of that purchase.

“My hands to stretch forth in healing others” has no verse that assigns healing hands to every person who sings. The Lord heals. The lyric puts the healing in the singer’s hands as a thing the singer offers God. That line is the main reason for the 3.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 3

Jesus is named as the way, the truth, and the life, and as King, Lord, Prince of Peace, and Redeemer. “Shed blood,” “saved my soul,” and “paid the price” point to a redemption. The word cross is not in the lyric, and the lines do not say that he died for sins. The resurrection is not there. Titles with an implied price, and with the saving act left unstated, are a 3.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 3

The song begins as desire. “Oh Lord, my heart longs to be” and “I want to be like You” are the singer’s ask. The hands, tongue, and feet are the singer’s offering. The turn to “You are God” and “You saved my soul” declares Christ. “Now have my life” is a consecration after the blood has been named. There is no “I will” vow of moral performance. The first half is longing. The last section is testimony. Both are in the song, so the score stays at 3.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 3

The titles are way, truth, life, King of kings, Lord of lords, Prince of Peace, and Redeemer. Those are real names. Holiness, justice, and wrath are unnamed. “Love can flourish” describes the heart the singer wants. The blood is the one act attached to the titles.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 3

The church has sung it once in this window, in 2025. The words are plain until “habitation,” which Ephesians 2 can explain if the reading is in front of the song. What the room can learn is John 14:6 and a short confession of the blood. What it can also learn, if the band features it, is that every singer’s hands are for healing. That second lesson is the reason this is not a song to feature. The singular “my heart” means the church sings one person’s consecration together.

---

## Tone

The tone is earnest and devotional. The line that sets the opening is “A heart where You can feel at home.” The line that sets the end is “You saved my soul, You paid the price.” The healing line is the intense one, and it is sung in the same gentle list as speech and good news.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names sin, the cross as a cross, the resurrection, the Spirit, or repentance.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** John 14:6, 1 Peter 1:18-19, or Ephesians 2:19-22, after the reading has said “dwelling place” and “by the Spirit.”
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The resurrection, and a clearer account of the death. Pair it with *In Christ Alone* when the blood line needs the cross and the empty tomb beside it.
- **Diet sentence.** Once in 2025, it has not shaped the year. It can add a short confession that Jesus is the way and that his blood paid the price. It also adds a healing claim the church should not adopt as a lyric.
- **What not to do.** Do not feature “My hands to stretch forth in healing others.” Do not drop the shed-blood lines and keep the longing. Do not explain “feel at home” as if it were Ephesians 2:22 without the Spirit and the church.

## Why this overall label

The song does confess Jesus as the way, the truth, and the life, and it says his blood saved the singer and paid the price. The cross itself is only implied. It also asks God to feel at home and offers the singer’s hands for healing. Those lines are why it stays a careful, occasional song, with the blood lines kept.
