## Thesis

The Son of God came as the Man of Sorrows to reclaim ruined sinners. He stood condemned in the sinner's place, sealed the sinner's pardon with His blood, made full atonement as the spotless Lamb, cried "It is finished," and now reigns in heaven. When He returns to bring His ransomed home, the church will sing the same praise again. The hymn moves from the incarnation to the cross, the ascension, and the second coming, and every stanza ends "Hallelujah! What a Savior!"

---

## Overall recommendation

Excellent / Prefer

Feature it, and sing it more often. The church has logged it 6 times, all in 2025 and 2026, so it is newer to this room than most of the repertoire. Five short stanzas cover the incarnation, substitution, atonement, the finished work, the ascension, and the return of Christ. Each is said in plain words that a congregation can learn in one hearing. "In my place condemned He stood" states substitution in six words.

## Voice and addressee

The hymn speaks about Christ in the third person, to the congregation. The singer appears in the second stanza ("In my place condemned He stood," "my pardon"), and the third and fifth stanzas are in the plural ("Guilty, vile, and helpless we," "this song we'll sing"). The refrain is addressed to no one in particular. It is praise said aloud before God and the church.

---

## How the text moves

The hymn follows the course of Christ's work in order. Stanza 1 is the incarnation and its purpose. Stanzas 2 and 3 are the cross, first as the singer's substitute and then as the church's atonement. Stanza 4 moves from the cross to "It is finished" and to heaven. Stanza 5 looks ahead to His return. The refrain stays the same, and each stanza gives it a new reason.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

Stanzas 2 and 3 are the center: "In my place condemned He stood; / Sealed my pardon with His blood" and "Guilty, vile, and helpless we; / Spotless Lamb of God was He." Those four lines are substitution and atonement. Stanza 5 is also worth keeping, since it gives the hymn its hope and is the only stanza about the future. The band does not always sing every stanza. If a stanza must go, stanza 1 or stanza 4 costs the least, since stanza 2 already names the Son's coming and stanza 5 already names His reign. Keep 2, 3, and 5.

The resurrection is not sung as an event. The hymn goes from "It is finished" to "Now in heaven exalted high," which assumes it. The second coming, which the table does not track, is the whole of stanza 5: "When He comes, our glorious King, / All His ransomed home to bring."

## Persons of the Trinity named

The Son is named as "the Son of God," "Lamb of God," "Man of Sorrows," and "King." The Father is implied in "Son of God" and "Lamb of God." The Spirit is not named.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. Every stanza is about the Son of God dying in the sinner's place. A Jew or Muslim would stop at the first stanza's "Son of God." No one could sing "In my place condemned He stood" without confessing a substitutionary death.

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

The hymn can be read line by line against Scripture. "Man of Sorrows" is Isaiah 53:3. "Ruined sinners to reclaim" is Luke 19:10. "Bearing shame and scoffing rude" is Hebrews 12:2 and Matthew 27:39–44. "In my place condemned He stood" is Isaiah 53:5–6 and 2 Corinthians 5:21. "Sealed my pardon with His blood" is Ephesians 1:7. "Spotless Lamb of God" is John 1:29 and 1 Peter 1:19. "Full atonement" is Hebrews 10:12–14, one sacrifice for all time. "It is finished" is John 19:30, and "Lifted up was He to die" is John 12:32–33. "Now in heaven exalted high" is Philippians 2:9. "When He comes ... All His ransomed home to bring" is John 14:3 and 1 Thessalonians 4:16–17.

Each stanza holds three lines of doctrine. The words are short, and they are exact.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

Christ is the subject of every stanza, and the gospel is told in order from His coming to His return. Sin is named in strong terms ("Guilty, vile, and helpless"). Substitution is stated outright. The cross, the blood, the finished work, and the exaltation are all here. The only gaps are the burial and an explicit resurrection line, and the move from the cross to heaven makes the resurrection plain.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 5

The hymn tells what Christ did, and the singer appears only as the one for whom He did it. "Can it be?" in the third stanza is the singer's wonder, and it is wonder at "full atonement," which is a fact. The refrain is praise for the Savior. The singer makes no vows.

---

## Description of God's Character: 4

| Attribute | Line |
|---|---|
| Justice | "condemned," "Full atonement" |
| Holiness | "Spotless Lamb of God" |
| Mercy, saving love | "Ruined sinners to reclaim," "Sealed my pardon" |
| Humility of the Son | "Man of Sorrows," "Bearing shame" |
| Exaltation, kingship | "Now in heaven exalted high," "our glorious King" |
| Faithfulness to His people | "All His ransomed home to bring" |

Justice and mercy meet at the cross in this hymn. A holy Lamb is condemned so guilty sinners can be pardoned. That is the character of God shown in the gospel. The score is 4 because the hymn shows God's character through Christ's work and does not name the Father or attributes such as sovereignty and wisdom in their own right.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The stanzas are short, in plain words, and every one ends the same way, so a congregation can join by the second stanza. The plural voice in stanzas 3 and 5 makes it a confession of the whole church. In under three minutes the congregation sings the whole gospel. People can carry "In my place condemned He stood" home and repeat it to one another, which is what Colossians 3:16 asks for.

---

## Tone

The tone moves from sorrow to triumph. The first three stanzas are quiet and astonished. The fourth turns at "It is finished," and the fifth ends in hope. The refrain is the same words each time, and the meaning grows as the story moves.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never sings the burial or names the resurrection as an event, never names the Father or the Spirit, and never speaks of repentance or faith directly.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** After a sermon on Isaiah 53, John 19, Hebrews 10, 2 Corinthians 5:21, or 1 Peter 2:21–25. It fits Good Friday and the Lord's Supper well. Stanza 5 also makes it a fit for sermons on Christ's return.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** An explicit resurrection song when the sermon is on the empty tomb, such as *Christ Our Hope in Life and Death*.
- **Diet sentence.** It adds a full, plain account of substitution and the finished work in a hymn the room can learn quickly. It deserves a regular place in the rotation.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop stanza 2 or 3 to save time.

## Identification notes

This is Philip Bliss's hymn, sometimes indexed by its first line, "Man of Sorrows, what a name." It is a different song from the 2013 "Man of Sorrows" by Hillsong, which this church does not use. When a set list says "Man of Sorrows," check which one is meant.

The band sings two arrangements: Ascend the Hill's, and one by Kara Young and Maggie Boswell. Both keep Bliss's stanzas, and the band sings them with "helpless we" in stanza 3. The Ascend the Hill recording adds a chorus of repeated "Hallelujah" between stanzas, which the band does not sing. This report grades Bliss's stanzas, so the grade is the same for both arrangements.

The catalog row lists CCLI 29515. No CCLI number for either arrangement could be confirmed from published sources. PraiseCharts carries a different arrangement, Tommy Walker's (2004, Integrity's Hosanna! Music, CCLI 5065729), which the band does not use. Hymndex lists the plain hymn as CCLI 5243833. The catalog number stays as it is until the band's charts show which number to report.

## Why this overall label

In five short stanzas, the hymn tells the whole gospel from the incarnation to the return of Christ, and it states substitution in plain words. It is easy to learn and hard to forget. It belongs in the Prefer lane.
