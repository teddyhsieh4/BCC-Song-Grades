## Thesis

The Son of God is holy and worthy. The Word made flesh came for rebels, the Lamb died at Calvary to cancel our debt, and that same Lamb rose to reign. The church answers by lifting high the name of Jesus.

---

## Overall recommendation

**Excellent / Prefer**

Feature it. Sing all three verses and the bridge. Verse 3 is the Calvary-and-resurrection stanza. Verse 2 is the incarnation-and-sinners stanza. If either is dropped, the remaining song is a true worthiness refrain with less gospel in it.

The church has already sung it for at least four years (14 logs in the 2023–2026 snapshot). This is a regular-use text, not a filler.

## Voice and addressee

Verses speak about the Son in the third person, then answer “Worthy! He is worthy!” The chorus turns and addresses Him: “Be to Your Name,” “We lift high the Name of Jesus.” The voice is corporate throughout. There is no solo “I.”

---

## How the text moves

Three verses, one chorus, one bridge.

1. **Who He is.** Holy Son of God. Majesty no one can stand before. Beauty beyond thought. Worthy.
2. **What He became and whom He came for.** Word made flesh. King who bore pain and poverty. He claims the rebel and the wretch.
3. **What He did and where He is now.** Lamb who died. Debt canceled at Calvary. Raised to reign.

The chorus ascribes glory, honor, power, and praise to His name and says no one can rival Him. The bridge takes the Isaiah / Revelation “holy, holy” line and adds “none beside You.” The hymn begins with the Son’s holiness and ends with Calvary, the empty grave, and the Name.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

**Do not drop verse 3.** “Holy, holy is the Lamb who died / canceling our debt at Calvary / rising from the grave to reign on high” is the finished work in three lines. Without it the church has praised a majestic Son who came for rebels, and has not named the death, the debt, or the resurrection.

**Do not drop verse 2** if verse 3 is already at risk. “Word made flesh” and “claim the rebel and the wretch” are the incarnation and the people He came to save.

No single line is false.

## Persons of the Trinity named

The Son is named as Son of God, Word made flesh, King, Lamb, Jesus. The Father and the Spirit are unnamed. “Lord Almighty” in the bridge is the Son being addressed with the Isaiah 6 title, which Scripture also gives to Christ. The hymn is not anti-Trinitarian. It is simply a hymn to the Son.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Not the whole song. Verse 2 names the Word made flesh and the claiming of rebels. Verse 3 names the Lamb, Calvary, and the resurrection. The chorus names Jesus.

Verse 1 plus the first half of the chorus (“all glory and honor… be to Your Name”) could be sung by a moral monotheist if “Jesus” were never reached. That is a reason to keep the later verses attached to the hook.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

**Unbiblical.** Nothing contradicts Scripture. “None can stand before His majesty” is Psalm 76:7 and Malachi 3:2, not a denial that we now come by the blood.

**Non-biblical.** “Beautiful beyond our highest thought” is praise language, not a claim that needs a verse number.

**Thin.** Wrath is unnamed. “In our place” is implied by debt and by the Lamb, not stated. Burial is skipped between death and rising. Those are omissions inside an otherwise dense text.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

**Verse 1.** “Holy, holy is the Son of God” is the holiness of the Son (John 6:69; Revelation 4–5). “None can stand before His majesty” is Psalm 76:7, Nahum 1:6, and Malachi 3:2. The line is about God as He is, not about the worshiper’s feelings.

**Verse 2.** “Word made flesh” is John 1:14, quoted in sense. “King who bore our pain and poverty” is Isaiah 53:4 and 2 Corinthians 8:9: He was rich and became poor. “Come to claim the rebel and the wretch” is Luke 19:10 and Romans 5:6–8. The objects of His coming are named as sinners, not as people who needed a lift.

**Verse 3.** “Lamb who died” is John 1:29 and Revelation 5:6–9. “Canceling our debt at Calvary” is Colossians 2:13–14. “Rising from the grave to reign on high” is 1 Corinthians 15:3–4 and Acts 2:33. The sequence is the gospel sequence.

**Chorus and bridge.** “No one could rival Your glory and fame” is Isaiah 42:8 and 48:11. “We lift high the Name of Jesus” is Philippians 2:9–11. “Holy, holy, Lord Almighty / none beside You” is Isaiah 6:3, Revelation 4:8, and Isaiah 45:5–6.

The lyric reads these texts in their ordinary sense. The one compression is “canceling our debt” without naming whose justice required the debt. That is why Christ-exalting stays a 4. Accuracy itself stays a 5.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 4

The hymn is about Jesus from the first line. Incarnation, the coming for rebels, the Lamb, Calvary, debt, resurrection, and reign are all in the people’s mouth. The Name is lifted after those works have been named.

What keeps this category at 4 is the missing word of substitution and wrath. “Canceling our debt” is the right metaphor (Colossians 2). A taught room will hear “for us.” The lyric never says He stood in our place or bore wrath. Titles are here, and the work is here. The meaning of the work for the people singing is one step short of explicit.

Keep verse 3. If it goes, this category falls.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 5

The hymn declares who Christ is and what He did. The congregation answers with ascription. “We lift high the Name of Jesus” is corporate praise, not a report of inner weather. There is no first-person vow that needs a disclaimer, and no romance.

## Description of God’s Character: 5

| Lyric | Attribute | Text |
|---|---|---|
| holy, holy | holiness | Isa 6:3; Rev 4:8 |
| Son of God | deity of the Son | Jn 20:31 |
| majesty no one can stand before | holiness and fear of the Lord | Ps 76:7; Mal 3:2 |
| Word made flesh | incarnation | Jn 1:14 |
| bore pain and poverty | humility and mercy | 2 Cor 8:9; Isa 53:4 |
| claims the rebel and the wretch | saving grace | Lk 19:10; Rom 5:6–8 |
| Lamb who died / debt at Calvary | atoning mercy | Jn 1:29; Col 2:14 |
| rising to reign | power and kingship | Acts 2:33; Rev 5 |
| none beside You | uniqueness | Isa 45:5–6 |
| glory, honor, power, praise | worth | Rev 5:12–13 |

Love as a word is never used. Mercy and grace are shown in the acts. Holiness leads every verse. That is a balanced portrait of the Son.

The Spirit and the Father are unnamed. The character score remains a 5 because what is said of the Son is specific and weighted toward holiness and worth, not toward mood.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

Short verses, a clear hook, words a congregation can mean together. The church already knows it (at least four years in the snapshot). Keys on file include E and Eb.

The hymn is compact enough that cutting is rarely needed. If time is tight, do not cut verse 3. Drop a bridge repeat before you drop a verse.

No disclaimer is required. The people are confessing Christ’s worth and work, not promising a feeling.

---

## Tone

Reverent, public, and glad. “Worthy!” is an answer to named holiness, not a volume cue. “Beautiful beyond our highest thought” is the one warmer line. It comes after the majesty lines. It does not replace them.

The church is not asked to make Him worthy. Verse 1 already said He is.

---

## What the congregation never says

They never say “I am a sinner” in the first person (they do name “the rebel and the wretch”).  
They never say wrath, “in our place,” or justification.  
They never name the Father or the Spirit.  
They never name repentance or a present High Priest.  
They never name the burial.

## Setlist notes

**When it serves the sermon.** John 1. John 1:29. Revelation 4–5. Philippians 2. Colossians 2. 1 Corinthians 15. Isaiah 6. A call to worship that tells the room who is being addressed. A response after the sermon has named sin more personally than verse 2 does.

**Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** A personal confession of sin. Wrath and substitution in plain words when the text is Isaiah 53 or Romans 3. The Spirit, when the day’s text is John 14–16.

**What it adds to the year’s diet.** Holiness of the Son, worthiness language that is tied to Calvary, incarnation, debt canceled. It will not supply repentance language or the Father and the Spirit.

**What not to do.** Do not loop the chorus and skip verse 3. Do not treat this as interchangeable with Andrew Peterson’s “Is He Worthy.” That is a different song.
