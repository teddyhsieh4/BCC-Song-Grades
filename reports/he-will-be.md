## Thesis

God has been and will be faithful to His word, gracious to the one who doubts and wanders, and worthy of praise as the one who redeems those He loves.

---

## Overall recommendation

Strong

Use it regularly on the faithfulness of God or the Exodus pattern of redemption. Keep the third verse and the bridge, because they are where redemption is named. It is Strong and not higher because Christ and the cross are never named. The church has sung it 8 times across at least two years of the 2023–2026 window.

## Voice and addressee

First-person singular in the choruses and bridge (“I cling,” “my hope,” “my feet”), and more general in the verses. The choruses address God. Every believer can sing the claims as true of themselves.

---

## How the text moves

Each verse names a way God has been faithful: to His word, in grace to the doubter, and in redeeming His people through the sea. Each chorus answers with “He has been / He will be.” The bridge gathers His titles as provider, protector, sustainer, and redeemer.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

The third verse (“His mighty hand has parted raging seas / For those He loves, He has redeemed”) and the bridge (“He is my … merciful redeemer”). Without them, the song is about faithfulness and grace in general, with no act of redemption.

## Persons of the Trinity named

God is named as faithful, gracious, worthy, the Lord who reigns, and the merciful redeemer. The Father is named as the one who gives what is not deserved. The Son and the Spirit are not named.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Mostly not. A vague theist could sing the first two verses and the choruses. The redemption lines in verse 3 and the bridge would give them pause.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** none that affect the claim.
- **Thin:** “God is gracious when I doubt” is true but thin until verse 3 and the bridge name redemption. The repeated “He has been / He will be” is clear but never reaches the work of Christ.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 4

“God is faithful to His word” and “His promises will never fail” are the faithfulness of God (Numbers 23:19, 2 Timothy 2:13). “His mighty hand has parted raging seas / For those He loves, He has redeemed” follows the Exodus pattern and the language of redemption. “What I don’t deserve the Father gives to me” states grace. The lines are accurate, but they say less than a full statement of substitution or of the person of Christ.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 3

Redemption is stated (“He has redeemed,” “merciful redeemer”), but through the Exodus picture. Christ, the cross, the blood, and the resurrection are not named, which is why this is a 3.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

The verses declare what God is and has done. The choruses and bridge speak in the first person, and what they speak of is clinging to truth and hoping in the Lord. The claims stay objective.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 4

God is faithful, gracious, worthy, holy, reigning in majesty, provider, covenant protector, sustainer, and merciful redeemer. Justice and mercy at the cross are implied in the undeserved gift and the redemption but not stated.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The repeated “He has been / He will be” makes it easy to learn, and the church has sung it often since 2025. It teaches God’s faithfulness and covenant care well.

---

## Tone

The tone is reverent and steady, with the confidence of someone who has seen God keep His word.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names Christ, the cross, the blood, the resurrection, or the Spirit, and substitution is not stated. When the sermon needs the finished work, pair it with a song that states it, such as In Christ Alone.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Texts on the faithfulness of God, the Exodus pattern of redemption, or grace to the wandering. Do not use it as the only gospel song.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The person and work of Christ, the cross, and the resurrection.
- **Diet sentence.** Adds God’s faithfulness and covenant redemption. It does not add the name of Christ or the cross.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop the third verse or the bridge, and do not make it the only teaching song on a Sunday when the sermon has not named the cross.
