## Thesis

God has been and will be faithful to His word, gracious to the one who doubts and wanders, and worthy of praise as the one who redeems those He loves.

---

## Overall recommendation

Strong

Use it regularly. The three verses move from faithfulness to the word, through grace that draws near in doubt and wilderness, to the worthy God whose hand has redeemed. The bridge names Him provider, covenant protector, sustainer, and merciful redeemer. The text is solid enough for regular use on texts about the faithfulness of God or the Exodus pattern of redemption. It has already been logged in at least two years of the current window.

## Voice and addressee

First-person singular in the choruses and bridge (“I cling,” “my hope,” “my feet”). The verses are more general. The choruses address God. The voice is personal but the claims are shareable.

---

## How the text moves

It begins with God’s faithfulness to His word even when there seems no way. The second verse turns to grace that draws near when the singer doubts and wanders. The third verse names the worthy God whose mighty hand has redeemed those He loves. The bridge expands the titles: provider, covenant protector, sustainer, merciful redeemer. Each chorus returns the same pattern of “He has been / He will be.”

---

## Lines you cannot cut

The third verse (“His mighty hand has parted raging seas / For those He loves, He has redeemed”) and the bridge (“He is my … merciful redeemer”). Drop either and the song stays on general faithfulness and grace without the act of redemption. The first two verses alone still name the faithful and gracious God but leave the redeemed people unsaid.

## Persons of the Trinity named

God is named as faithful, gracious, worthy, the Lord who reigns, and the merciful redeemer. The Father is named as the one who gives what is not deserved. The Son and the Spirit are not named.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Mostly no. The third verse requires the God who has redeemed those He loves by His mighty hand. The bridge requires the merciful redeemer. A vague theist can sing the first two verses and the faithfulness/grace choruses, but the redemption lines create tension.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** none that affect the claim.
- **Thin:** “God is gracious when I doubt” is true and underfed until the third verse and bridge name the redemption. The repeated “He has been / He will be” pattern is clear but does not unfold the work of Christ.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 4

“God is faithful to His word” and “His promises will never fail” follow the ordinary sense of the faithfulness of God (Numbers 23:19, 2 Timothy 2:13). “His mighty hand has parted raging seas / For those He loves, He has redeemed” follows the Exodus pattern and the language of redemption. “What I don’t deserve the Father gives to me” states grace. The lines are accurate. They are not as dense as a full statement of substitution or the person of Christ.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 3

Christ is not named. Redemption is stated (“He has redeemed,” “merciful redeemer”). The cross, blood, and resurrection are absent. The focus stays on the faithful, gracious, redeeming God. Titles without the finished work of the Son keep this in the 3 range.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

The verses declare what God is and has done. The choruses and bridge use first-person singular for clinging, hope, and feet. The claims remain objective: faithfulness to the word, grace given, redemption accomplished. Desire language is limited to clinging to truth and hope in the Lord.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 4

God is faithful, gracious, worthy, holy, the Lord who reigns in majesty, provider, covenant protector, sustainer, and merciful redeemer. The attributes are specific. Justice and mercy in relation to the cross are implied in the undeserved gift and the redemption rather than expanded.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The diction is plain. The argument moves from faithfulness to the word, through grace in doubt, to redemption and the titles of the redeemer. The congregation has already sung it in the current window. The repeated pattern makes it easy to learn and useful for teaching the character of God.

---

## Tone

Reverent. The language stays with faithfulness, grace, redemption, and the Lord who reigns. It does not ask the room to confer on God what He already possesses.

---

## What the congregation never says

The name of Christ, the cross, the blood, or the resurrection. The Spirit is not named. Explicit substitution is absent. Pair the song with texts or songs that name the Son and the finished work when the sermon of the day requires them.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Texts on the faithfulness of God, the Exodus pattern of redemption, or the grace that draws near to the wandering. Do not use it as the sole gospel song.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The person and work of Christ, the cross, and the resurrection. Any song that names redemption without the Son leaves the same gap.
- **Diet sentence.** Adds a clear confession of the faithful, gracious, redeeming God who has acted for those He loves. It does not add the name of Christ or the finished work of the cross.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop the third verse or the bridge. Do not place it as the only teaching song when the sermon has not already named the cross.

## Why this overall label

The lyric states the faithfulness of God to His word, the grace that draws near in doubt, and the redemption of those He loves by His mighty hand. The claims can be tied to the ordinary sense of Scripture. Christ is not named and the finished work of the cross is not unfolded. That is why it is Strong.
