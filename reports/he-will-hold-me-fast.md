## Thesis

Christ keeps those He has bought. When faith fails and love grows cold, He holds the singer. Those He saves are His delight. He will not lose them, because He bled, satisfied justice, and rose. The same Christ will bring them to sight at His return.

---

## Overall recommendation

**Excellent / Prefer**

Feature it. Sing all three verses. Verse 3 names the cross, the satisfaction of justice, the resurrection, and the return. Without it the song is a true promise of keeping that does not state the cost or the finished work.

The church has sung it for at least four years (6 logs in the 2023–2026 snapshot). It is already known.

## Voice and addressee

The verses are first-person singular. “When I fear my faith will fail.” “For my love is often cold.” “For my life He bled and died.” The chorus states the promise in the third person and names the Savior’s love. The whole text is confession of dependence on Christ’s keeping. It can be sung by the congregation together.

---

## How the text moves

Verse 1 names the singer’s weak faith and cold love and answers with the necessity that Christ hold him. Verse 2 moves to the people Christ has saved. They are His delight. He will not let their souls be lost. They were bought at a cost. Verse 3 states the cost and the outcome. He bled and died. Justice is satisfied. The singer is raised with Him to endless life until faith becomes sight at His coming. The chorus returns the same promise after each verse.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

Do not drop verse 3. “For my life He bled and died / Justice has been satisfied / Raised with Him to endless life / ‘Til our faith is turned to sight / When He comes at last” is the gospel content. Without it the church has confessed that Christ keeps weak believers and has not named the blood, the satisfaction of justice, the resurrection, or the return.

Verse 2’s “Bought by Him at such a cost” and “He’ll not let my soul be lost” are also needed. They connect the keeping to the purchase.

No line is false.

## Persons of the Trinity named

Christ and the Savior are named throughout. The Father and the Spirit are unnamed. The keeping is the work of Christ.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Not the whole song. Verse 2 names those He saves and the purchase. Verse 3 names the blood, the satisfaction of justice, the resurrection with Him, and His coming. A vague theist could sing verse 1 and the chorus if “Christ” and “Savior” were ignored. The later verses stop that.

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

**Unbiblical.** None.

**Non-biblical.** None.

**Thin.** Incarnation, burial, repentance, and present intercession are not named. The lyric does not need them to make its claim. The claim is the keeping of the purchased people until the return.

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“When I fear my faith will fail / Christ will hold me fast” and “For my love is often cold / He must hold me fast” state the necessity of Christ’s keeping. John 10:28–29 and Jude 24 give the same claim. The singer’s cold love is named as the reason he cannot keep himself.

“Those He saves are His delight / Precious in His holy sight” is Zephaniah 3:17 and Ephesians 1:6. “He’ll not let my soul be lost / His promises shall last” is John 6:39 and John 10:28. “Bought by Him at such a cost” is 1 Corinthians 6:20 and 1 Peter 1:18–19.

“For my life He bled and died / Justice has been satisfied” is the substitution of Isaiah 53:5–6, Romans 3:25–26, and 1 Peter 2:24. “Raised with Him to endless life” is Romans 6:5 and 1 Corinthians 15:20–22. “‘Til our faith is turned to sight / When He comes at last” is 1 John 3:2 and 1 Thessalonians 4:16–17.

The lines use the ordinary sense of those texts. The promise of keeping is grounded in the purchase and the resurrection.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

Christ is named in every verse. The cross is stated as “bled and died” for the singer’s life. Justice is satisfied. The singer is raised with Him. The return is named. The keeping is the work of the One who bought and rose. Titles without the finished work do not appear.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

The main claim is objective. Christ holds. He will not lose those He bought. Justice is satisfied. The first-person lines name the singer’s fear and cold love as the reason he needs to be held. That is honest dependence, not a vow the singer must mean before he may sing. The chorus states the Savior’s love as fact.

The score stays at 4 because the opening is the singer’s fear rather than a declaration about God alone. The lyric never leaves the singer’s weakness as the last word.

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The verses are short. The chorus is clear. The words are ordinary. The church has already sung it for at least four years. Keys on file include F and G.

The people learn that Christ keeps them because He bought them and satisfied justice, and that He will bring them to sight. They do not need a weekly disclaimer. The first-person lines are confession of need, not a claim the singer must prove.

If time is short, do not drop verse 3.

---

## Tone

The tone is sober and confident. “When I fear my faith will fail” and “my love is often cold” name weakness without drama. The answer is the same in every verse. Christ holds. The final lines look to the return without intensity for its own sake.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names the Father or the Spirit, the incarnation, burial, repentance as an act, or present intercession. It never says “in our place” in those words, though “for my life He bled and died” and “justice has been satisfied” state the same fact.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** John 10, Romans 8:28–39, 1 Peter 1, Jude, or texts on perseverance and the return. It works after a sermon that has named the weakness of faith and the certainty of Christ’s keeping.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The Father and the Spirit when the day’s text requires them. Explicit repentance language if the sermon has called for it and no other song supplies it. Pair with a song that names the Spirit’s sealing when the text is Ephesians 1.
- **Diet sentence.** It adds the keeping of the saints grounded in the cross, the satisfaction of justice, the resurrection, and the return. It does not add trinitarian naming or the incarnation.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop verse 3. Do not treat the chorus as a standalone assurance tag after a different gospel song. The verses supply the reason for the promise.
