## Thesis

Here is love, vast as the ocean. The Prince of Life, the Ransom, shed His precious blood. On the mount of crucifixion, fountains opened deep and wide. Heaven’s peace and perfect justice kissed a guilty world in love.

## Voice and addressee

Mostly declarative of the love and the ransom. Some later stanzas move to first-person response. The opening is corporate-usable confession of what the blood accomplished.

---

## How the text moves

It starts with the vastness of the love and the loving-kindness as a flood, grounded in the Prince of Life who is the Ransom and who shed His precious blood. It moves to the mount of crucifixion where fountains opened deep and wide, the floodgates of God’s mercy, and the meeting of heaven’s peace and perfect justice with a guilty world. Later stanzas (where present) turn to the response of the singer and the directing work of the Spirit through the Word. The first two verses carry the gospel claims.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“When the Prince of Life, our Ransom, / Shed for us His precious blood” and “And heav’n’s peace and perfect justice / Kissed a guilty world in love.”

Dropping either loses the explicit ransom, the blood, the crucifixion, or the meeting of peace and justice over guilt. Those lines are required.

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“The Prince of Life, our Ransom, / Shed for us His precious blood” is Mark 10:45, 1 Peter 1:18–19, and Acts 3:15. “On the mount of crucifixion” is the ordinary sense of the cross. “Heav’n’s peace and perfect justice / Kissed a guilty world in love” is Romans 3:25–26 and the propitiation that satisfies justice while giving peace. The lines stay inside ordinary biblical sense.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

The Prince of Life is named. The Ransom is named. The precious blood is named. The mount of crucifixion is named. The guilty world is named. The meeting of peace and perfect justice is named. The song does not stop at titles or atmosphere. It preaches the finished work.

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

The first two verses declare what the love and the ransom accomplished. Later stanzas (where used) include the singer’s response and trust. The governing claims are objective, which is why the score is 4 rather than 5. The vows rest on the blood and the crucifixion.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 5

God’s love is vast as the ocean. His loving-kindness is as the flood. The Prince of Life is the Ransom who shed His precious blood. On the mount of crucifixion, mercy flowed in a vast and gracious tide. Grace and love poured like mighty rivers. Heaven’s peace and perfect justice kissed a guilty world in love. The attributes are specific and tied to the cross rather than listed generically.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The language is plain. The claims are dense. The congregation can learn the ransom, the blood, and the meeting of peace and justice by singing the first two verses. Singability is high, and the teaching is the reason for the 5.

---

## Tone

Reverent and declarative. It does not ask the room to confer on the Prince anything He has not already accomplished. The intensity stays inside the ocean of love and the mount of crucifixion.

---

## What the congregation never says

In the first two verses the congregation does not name the Spirit or the resurrection. Those confessions have to come from later stanzas or the rest of the service. The ransom and the justice are present.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Any text on the cross, ransom, propitiation, or the love of God shown in the blood. Especially useful after a sermon on Romans 3 or 1 Peter 1.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The resurrection and a fuller naming of the Spirit if the arrangement does not include the later stanzas.
- **Diet sentence.** It adds a dense confession that the Prince of Life is the Ransom who shed His blood, and that peace and perfect justice met over a guilty world. It does not add a full narrative of the empty tomb.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop the first two verses. Do not let later response stanzas stand alone without the ransom and the crucifixion.
