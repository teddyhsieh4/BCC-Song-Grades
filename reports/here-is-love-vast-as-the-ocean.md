## Thesis

Here is love, vast as the ocean. The Prince of Life, the Ransom, shed His precious blood. On the mount of crucifixion, fountains opened deep and wide. Heaven’s peace and perfect justice kissed a guilty world in love.

---

## Overall recommendation

Excellent / Prefer

Feature it, and keep the first two verses. They name the Prince of Life as our Ransom, His blood shed on the mount of crucifixion, and heaven’s peace and perfect justice meeting a guilty world. Later stanzas vary by arrangement, but the opening two carry the gospel. The church has sung it 9 times across at least four years of the 2023–2026 window.

## Voice and addressee

Mostly declaration about the love of God and the Ransom. Some later stanzas turn to first-person response. The opening verses are a confession the whole congregation can make together.

---

## How the text moves

Verse 1 pictures God’s love as an ocean and a flood, and grounds it in the Prince of Life who shed His blood as our Ransom. Verse 2 stands at the mount of crucifixion, where mercy opens like fountains and peace and justice meet. Later stanzas, where an arrangement includes them, turn to the singer’s response and the Spirit’s work through the Word.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“When the Prince of Life, our Ransom, / Shed for us His precious blood” and “And heav’n’s peace and perfect justice / Kissed a guilty world in love.”

Cutting either loses the ransom and the blood, or the meeting of peace and justice over a guilty world. Both must stay.

## Persons of the Trinity named

God and the Prince of Life are named, and the ransom and the blood are the Prince’s. The Spirit is named only in later stanzas, where an arrangement includes them.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. A vague theist cannot sing a Ransom who shed His blood on the mount of crucifixion, or justice and peace meeting over a guilty world, without changing the words.

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“The Prince of Life, our Ransom, / Shed for us His precious blood” is Mark 10:45, 1 Peter 1:18–19, and Acts 3:15. “On the mount of crucifixion” places it at the cross. “Heav’n’s peace and perfect justice / Kissed a guilty world in love” is Romans 3:25–26: the propitiation that satisfies justice and brings peace.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

The Prince of Life is named as our Ransom. His blood, the crucifixion, the guilt of the world, and the meeting of peace and justice are all named. The song preaches the finished work in its first two verses.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

The first two verses declare what the love of God and the Ransom accomplished. Later stanzas, where used, add the singer’s response and trust, which is why the score is 4 and not 5. That response rests on the blood and the cross.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 5

God’s love is vast as the ocean and His kindness a flood. The Prince of Life is the Ransom who shed His blood. At the cross mercy flowed like a tide, and peace and perfect justice met over a guilty world. Every attribute is tied to the cross.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The words are plain and the claims are dense. A congregation that sings the first two verses learns the ransom, the blood, and how God is both just and the one who gives peace. That teaching is why it earns a 5.

---

## Tone

Reverent and full of wonder at the size of God’s love.

---

## What the congregation never says

The first two verses never name the resurrection or the Spirit. When the arrangement drops the later stanzas, pair it with a song that names the empty tomb, such as Christ Our Hope in Life and Death.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Any text on the cross, ransom, propitiation, or God’s love shown in the blood. It fits especially well after a sermon on Romans 3 or 1 Peter 1.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The resurrection, and the Spirit if the arrangement leaves out the later stanzas.
- **Diet sentence.** Adds a dense confession that the Prince of Life is the Ransom who shed His blood, and that peace and perfect justice met over a guilty world. It does not add the empty tomb.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop the first two verses, and do not sing later response stanzas without them.
