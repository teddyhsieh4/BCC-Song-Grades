## Thesis

The triune God is holy, almighty, and merciful, and His church joins heaven in praising Him. The saints and angels fall down before the eternal God "which wert, and art, and evermore shalt be." Sinful man cannot see His glory, and He alone is holy, perfect in power, love, and purity. All His works will praise His name. The hymn is about who God is: holy, eternal, and three in one.

---

## Overall recommendation

Strong

Use it regularly. The church has logged it 6 times across at least four years of the 2023–2026 window. It teaches the doctrine of God clearly. It sets the congregation beside the seraphim of Isaiah 6 and the elders of Revelation 4, it confesses the Trinity in plain words, and it ties God's holiness to the sinfulness of the people singing. It is not a gospel hymn. Christ is never named, and the cross is not in it. Place it where the set's other songs preach the cross.

## Voice and addressee

Every stanza is addressed to God as "Thee" and "Thou." The congregation speaks as "our" in the first stanza ("our song shall rise to Thee"). After that, the singers join a larger choir: the saints, the cherubim and seraphim, and finally all God's works "in earth, and sky, and sea."

---

## How the text moves

Stanza 1 is the church's morning praise to the triune God. Stanza 2 lifts the view to heaven's throne room, where saints cast down their crowns and angels fall before Him. Stanza 3 turns to God's hiddenness from sinful eyes and His unique holiness. Stanza 4 repeats the first stanza's opening and ending, now with all creation joining in. The hymn starts with the church and ends with the whole creation.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

Stanza 3 is the one most often dropped in other churches, and it does the most. The band usually sings all four. "Though the eye of sinful man Thy glory may not see" names sin and the distance it puts between God and man. "Only Thou art holy; there is none beside Thee" is the hymn's clearest confession. The last line of stanzas 1 and 4, "God in three Persons, blessed Trinity," should never be cut, because it is what makes the hymn Christian. No line is false.

---

## Persons of the Trinity named

The Trinity is confessed as a doctrine: "God in three Persons, blessed Trinity." The threefold "holy" has long been read by the church as pointing to the Trinity, and Heber wrote the hymn for Trinity Sunday. No single Person is named. The Son and the Spirit are present only in "three Persons."

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Not all of it. A Jew could sing most of the hymn, since stanzas 2 and 3 are close to Isaiah 6. "God in three Persons, blessed Trinity" stops him, and it stops a Muslim or a Unitarian too. That line comes twice. Still, the hymn never names Jesus, so its Christian content rests on the doctrine of the Trinity alone.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** none. "Early in the morning" is Psalm 5:3, and "the glassy sea" is Revelation 4:6.
- **Thin:** the gospel. The hymn is dense on God's being and gives no account of how sinful man can stand before Him.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

The hymn is built from Isaiah 6 and Revelation 4. "Holy, holy, holy! Lord God Almighty!" is Revelation 4:8, and the threefold "holy" is Isaiah 6:3. "Casting down their golden crowns around the glassy sea" is Revelation 4:6 and 4:10. "Cherubim and seraphim falling down before Thee" is Isaiah 6:2 and Revelation 4:8–9. "Which wert, and art, and evermore shalt be" is Revelation 4:8. "Though the darkness hide Thee" fits Psalm 97:2 and 1 Timothy 6:16, and "the eye of sinful man Thy glory may not see" is Exodus 33:20. "Only Thou art holy" is Revelation 15:4, and "All Thy works shall praise Thy name" is Psalm 145:10.

Every stanza is Scripture set in order, and "God in three Persons" states the church's confession exactly.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 2

Christ is not named, and the cross is not in the hymn. The Son is present only as one of the "three Persons." The hymn names sin and shows that sinful man cannot see God, then leaves the question of how a sinner can draw near. The hymn was written to teach the doctrine of God, and it does that well. But this category asks about the person and work of Christ, and a song that leaves them out entirely is a serious omission under the rubric. That is the 2.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 5

The hymn speaks only of who God is and what heaven does before Him. The singers do nothing but sing. No feelings, vows, or experiences are reported.

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The hymn teaches the congregation the Trinity, the holiness of God, and the worship of heaven in four stanzas. The older words ("wert," "Thee") are familiar from hymnals, and the tune is one of the best known in the church. It gives the people language they can use when they read Isaiah 6 or Revelation 4 themselves.

---

## Tone

The tone is reverent and full. The repeated "holy" sets the pace, and the hymn builds from the church's morning song to the praise of all creation. Nothing in it is casual.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names Jesus, the Father, or the Spirit as Persons, never sings the incarnation, the cross, the resurrection, or forgiveness, and never says how sinful man may come near the holy God.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** After a sermon on Isaiah 6, Revelation 4, Exodus 33–34, or any text on the holiness of God or the Trinity. It also works as an opening call to worship, before the gospel is sung.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The gospel. Follow it with a song that answers stanza 3's problem, such as *Hallelujah, What a Savior* or *Before the Throne of God Above*. Then the people who sang that sinful eyes cannot see God also sing how they have been brought near.
- **Diet sentence.** It adds a full doctrine of God and of the Trinity. It adds no gospel, so it needs a partner.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop stanza 3. Do not use it as the only song in a service where the gospel needs to be sung.

---

## Identification notes

This is Heber's hymn sung to NICAEA. The setlists mark it "hymn" to keep it apart from the Vineyard "Holy Holy Holy" (Chisum / Sadler, `holy-holy-holy-vineyard`), which is a different song.

The catalog row lists CCLI 1156 for the hymn. PraiseCharts carries several arrangements under their own numbers: the PraiseCharts traditional arrangement (CCLI 4665010), the John Wasson arrangement (CCLI 4670036), Don Moen's (CCLI 7111465), and the Gettys' live arrangement (CCLI 7097107). The band sings the plain hymn, usually all four stanzas, so the catalog number 1156 stands. This report grades Heber's four stanzas.

---

## Why this overall label

The hymn gives the church a full doctrine of God, drawn from Isaiah 6 and Revelation 4, and it confesses the Trinity by name. It never names Christ or the cross, so it needs a gospel song beside it. That keeps it at Strong.
