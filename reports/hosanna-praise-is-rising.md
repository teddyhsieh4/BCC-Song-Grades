## Thesis

Praise is rising and eyes are turning to the Lord. Hope is stirring and hearts are yearning. When the people see Him they find strength to face the day, and in His presence their fears are washed away. He is the God who saves them, worthy of all their praises. They welcome Him to have His way among them. Hearts are returning, and in His kingdom broken lives are made new.

---

## Overall recommendation

**Weak**

Do not feature it. At most a brief filler after Scripture has already done the work. The lyric names the God who saves and welcomes the Lord Jesus. It does not name sin, the cross, the blood, the resurrection, or the finished work. A non-Christian could sing most of the lines without tension.

The church has sung it for at least four years (6 logs in the 2023–2026 snapshot). That does not raise the grade.

## Voice and addressee

The song is first-person plural throughout. “We turn to You.” “We long for You.” “We welcome You here, Lord Jesus.” It addresses the Lord. The voice is corporate in intent but dominated by the people’s turning, yearning, and welcoming.

---

## How the text moves

Verse 1 names praise rising, eyes turning, hope stirring, and hearts yearning. The pre-chorus states that when the people see Him they find strength and their fears are washed away in His presence. The chorus names Him as the God who saves, worthy of praises, and welcomes Him to have His way. Verse 2 names hearts returning and broken lives made new in His kingdom. The pre-chorus and chorus return.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

There is no line that states the gospel in ordinary words. “You are the God Who saves us” and “We welcome You here, Lord Jesus” are the strongest claims. Without them the song is true theistic praise of presence and renewal that has not named Jesus or the saving. Even with them the lyric remains thin.

No line is false. The thinness is the problem.

## Persons of the Trinity named

The Lord Jesus is named as the One welcomed. The Father and the Spirit are unnamed. The work is left as “the God Who saves us.”

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Yes, most of it. A vague theist could sing the praise rising, the hearts yearning, the fears washed away in presence, the God who saves, and the lives made new. The name “Lord Jesus” and the welcome of Him are the lines that create tension. They are not enough to raise the whole song.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

**Unbiblical.** None.

**Non-biblical.** None.

**Thin.** Sin, the cross, the blood, the burial, the resurrection, and the Spirit are unnamed. “The God Who saves us” and “broken lives are made new” are true statements that are not unfolded. The lyric is impressionistic praise of presence and welcome.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 3

“Praise is rising, eyes are turning to You, we turn to You / Hope is stirring, hearts are yearning for You, we long for You” is Psalm 63:1 and Psalm 42:1 in general terms. The yearning is true. It is not tied to a text in its ordinary sense.

“When we see You, we find strength to face the day / In Your Presence all our fears are washed away” is Psalm 16:11 and Isaiah 41:10 in general terms. The strength and the washing of fears are true. They are not the ordinary sense of a particular text.

“You are the God Who saves us, worthy of all our praises” is Psalm 68:20 and Revelation 5:12 in general terms. The saving and the worthiness are true. They are not unfolded.

“In Your Kingdom broken lives are made new, You make us new” is Revelation 21:5 in general terms. The making new is true. It is not the ordinary sense of that text.

The lines are true fragments. They do not unfold Scripture.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 2

Christ is named as Lord Jesus who is welcomed. “The God Who saves us” is stated. The cross, the blood, the resurrection, and the mediation are not named. Naming Christ and calling Him the God who saves without the finished work is a 2.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 2

The main claim is the people’s rising praise, turning eyes, stirring hope, yearning hearts, returning hearts, and welcoming. The objective statements (“You are the God Who saves us,” “broken lives are made new”) are brief and not developed. The lyric is dominated by personal experience of presence and longing.

Saving power, worthiness, presence, and renewing power are named in general terms. Holiness, justice, and the finished work are not shown in the acts. The account is thin.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 3

The chorus is singable. The words are clear. The people do not learn the shape of the gospel by singing it. They learn that Jesus is welcome, that He saves, and that presence washes fears. That is true. It is not dense enough for regular use here. Keys on file include F and F#.

---

## Tone

The tone is warm and longing. “Hope is stirring, hearts are yearning” and “We welcome You here, Lord Jesus” name desire without naming the cost or the finished work.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names sin, the cross, the blood, the burial, the resurrection, the Spirit, or substitution. It never says “in our place.” It never names the Father as distinct from the Lord Jesus.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Palm Sunday or a text on the welcome of the King, after the sermon has already named the finished work. It does not carry the gospel by itself.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** Everything the lyric omits: sin, the cross, the blood, the resurrection. Pair with a song that states substitution before or after this one.
- **Diet sentence.** It adds a welcome of the Lord Jesus and a general confession that He saves and makes new. It does not add the gospel narrative.
- **What not to do.** Do not feature it. Do not use it as the only gospel song in the set. Do not treat the chorus as sufficient after a thinner song.
