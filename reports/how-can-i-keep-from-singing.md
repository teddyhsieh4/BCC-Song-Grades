## Thesis

An endless song echoes in the singer’s soul. Storms come, and the singer holds a rock. The singer cannot keep from singing praise, because the King’s love is amazing and the singer is loved by the King. In the darkest night the Savior lives. The singer will sing in trouble, in winning and losing, until the last breath, with angels and saints around the throne.

---

## Overall recommendation

Weak

Do not feature it. The church has logged it 5 times across at least four years (2023, 2024, 2025, 2026). “I know my Savior lives” is the line that has to stay if the song is sung at all, and it still does not say the cross. The chorus is a singer who cannot keep from singing, loved by the King. Jesus is not named. The death that makes the love amazing is not named. Keep it off the list, or sing it only after another song has already said the cross, and keep it short.

---

## Alternative song suggestions

The lyric is reaching for a song that continues through storms, because the singer is loved by the King and the Savior lives. Three of these the church already sings. One is not in the catalog.

**Already sung**

**He Will Hold Me Fast** (Ada Habershon / Matt Merker, modern hymn, CCLI 7016161). Logged 6 times across at least four years (2023, 2024, 2025, 2026). The singer is held through fear and failure because Christ loves and will not let go, and the cross is in view.

**Yet Not I but Through Christ in Me** (Jonny Robinson / Rich Thompson / Michael Farren, CityAlight, CCLI 7121852). Logged 10 times across at least four years (2023, 2024, 2025, 2026). The song in the night and at the end is Christ’s, and the cross and the resurrection are named.

**Christ Our Hope in Life and Death** (Keith Getty / Matt Boswell / Jordan Kauflin / Matt Merker / Matt Papa, Getty, CCLI 7147502). Logged 8 times across at least four years (2023, 2024, 2025, 2026). Hope in life and in death rests on Christ’s finished work.

**Not yet logged**

**Abide with Me** (Henry Francis Lyte / William Henry Monk, hymn, CCLI 43190). Not in the catalog. The singer asks the Lord to abide through the failing of helpers and through death, and a later stanza says, “Hold Thou Thy cross before my closing eyes.”

## Voice and addressee

First person singular. “Echoes in my soul,” “I am holding on,” “I know I am loved,” “I will lift my eyes,” “I can sing.” God is “You” and “Your praise,” “Your name,” “the King,” and “my Savior.” The church would be singing one person’s resolve to keep singing.

---

## How the text moves

It starts inside the singer: an endless song in the soul, music ringing, storms, and a rock the singer clings to. The chorus asks how the singer can keep from singing praise and from shouting the name, because the love is amazing and the King loves the singer, and that love makes the heart want to sing. The next verse lifts the eyes in the darkest night because the Savior lives, and the singer will walk with God and sing the songs God gives. The bridge lists the times the singer can sing: trouble, winning, losing a step, being picked up, being heard in prayer, the last breath, and the throne with angels and saints. The chorus returns to the heart that wants to sing. The Savior who lives is one line. The song does not go on to a death.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“I know my Savior lives” is the line the grade depends on. It is the only line that names a saving person and a life beyond the dark. “How amazing is Your love” and “I know I am loved by the King” are the lines the chorus repeats, and they do not say how the love came. “To the rock I cling” is a picture of safety. If the band keeps the chorus and drops “my Savior lives,” the song is an endless song in the soul and a heart that wants to sing. No line is false. The Savior line is easy to lose because it is not the title.

## Persons of the Trinity named

“My Savior,” “the King,” “Lord,” and “You” are the names. Father, Son, and Spirit are unnamed. The Savior is not given the name Jesus. The throne of angels and saints is the end of the singer’s song, and the one on the throne is not described.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Most of it, yes, if “Savior” and “Your name” are left vague. “I know my Savior lives” and “shouting Your name” require a savior the singer trusts. They do not require the cross, and they do not say which name. A worshiper who believes in a living king who loves that worshiper can sing the chorus without correction. The line that raises the cost is “my Savior lives,” and even that line does not say the Savior died.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** “There is an endless song / Echoes in my soul / I hear the music ring.” The song in the soul is the singer’s inner music. “It makes my heart want to sing” reports the feeling the love produces.
- **Thin:** “To the rock I cling.” Safety is a rock, and the rock is not named. “How amazing is Your love” and “I know I am loved by the King” are true as far as they go. They do not say what the King did.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 3

“I will lift my eyes / In the darkest night” stands near Psalm 121:1, “I lift up my eyes to the hills.” The song lifts the eyes because “I know my Savior lives.” That confession stands near Job 19:25, “For I know that my Redeemer lives.” The song says Savior and does not say Redeemer, and it does not go on to Job’s “at the last he will stand upon the earth.” “I’ll sing with the angels / And the saints around the throne” is a true hope of the last song. “You hear me, Lord / When I call to You in prayer” is the plain claim that God hears.

“There is an endless song / Echoes in my soul” cannot be tied to a verse. “To the rock I cling” is a picture of holding on. “How can I ever say enough / How amazing is Your love” is wonder without a text attached. The Savior who lives is the one doctrinal line. The song is mostly the singer’s music and the singer’s holding on.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 2

The Savior lives, and the King loves the singer. Those are titles and a love. The cross, the blood, sin, and the burial are absent. “My Savior lives” gestures at the resurrection and does not say he died or rose. A love that is amazing, with no work named, stays a thin gospel. The score is 2 because the finished work is not in the lyric.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 2

“I know my Savior lives” and “You hear me, Lord” declare something about God. The rest is the singer’s inner life: a song echoing in the soul, holding on through storms, a heart that wants to sing, eyes lifted, singing when the singer wins or loses a step, singing because God picks the singer up. “I know I am loved by the King / And it makes my heart want to sing” is the chorus, and it is about the heart. The vow to sing until the last breath is the singer’s resolve.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 3

He is the King whose love is amazing, the Savior who lives, the rock the singer clings to, the one who sees the singer through, who picks the singer up, who hears prayer, and who will have angels and saints around his throne. Love is the word the chorus repeats. Faithfulness is implied in the hearing and the holding, and it is not named. Holiness, justice, and wrath are unnamed. The portrait is love, life, and nearness in trouble.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 3

Five logs across at least four years means the chorus is known. The question “How can I keep from singing” is easy to remember. What a congregation learns is that a loved person wants to sing, that a Savior lives, and that singing can continue through trouble until the throne. They do not learn the cross. “I know my Savior lives” is the line that teaches, and it is one line in a long list of the singer’s own singing. The song is clear. The teaching is thin.

---

## Tone

The tone is earnest and bright. The line that sets it is “I know I am loved by the King / And it makes my heart want to sing.” The storms and the darkest night are named, and they turn at once into another reason to sing. The last breath and the throne are hopeful. The song does not sit long in the trouble.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names Jesus, never says that the Savior died for sin, and never says what the amazing love cost.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** A brief response after a sermon on perseverance or on Job 19:25, and only if “my Savior lives” is sung and the cross has already been named. It does not serve a sermon on the death of Christ.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The cross and the name of Jesus. Pair it with *He Will Hold Me Fast* or *Yet Not I but Through Christ in Me* so the singing in the night has a death and a resurrection under it.
- **Diet sentence.** Five times in at least four years, it adds a resolve to keep singing because the King loves and the Savior lives. It never adds the cross.
- **What not to do.** Do not loop “it makes my heart want to sing” and drop “my Savior lives.” Do not sing this as the Robert Lowry hymn. The published lyric is a new text. Do not sing it as Jeremy Camp’s “Give Me Jesus,” which is the song that carries the catalog’s old CCLI number.

## Identification notes

One new song, not a stanza-for-stanza arrangement of Robert Lowry’s “How Can I Keep from Singing.” Integrity says the Tomlin, Cash, and Redman text is inspired by that hymn. The catalog note calls 2006 the arrangement year of the traditional text. This grade treats 2006 as the year the new lyric was written. Word to Worship, Integrity, and an Easy Song listing agree on the three writers and on CCLI 4822372. The catalog prints 4874344. That number, on Word to Worship and PraiseCharts, is Jeremy Camp’s “Give Me Jesus,” a different song. The header uses 4822372. Setlist keys include F-sharp and E. No separate arrangement year is on file.

---

## Why this overall label

The song says the singer is loved by the King and knows the Savior lives, and it fills the rest of its lines with an endless song in the soul and a heart that wants to sing. The cross is absent. Five logs across at least four years do not make the lyric thicker. It should not be featured.
