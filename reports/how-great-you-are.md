## Thesis

God is great as the Father of creation, whose splendor fills the earth and whose works shout His worth. He is great in the Son, the Word made flesh, who was crushed for our rebellion, died our death, and rose from the grave. He will be great when the King returns to claim His blood-washed bride. The church answers each stanza with the same chorus: "How great You are."

## Voice and addressee

The stanzas speak about God in the third person, to the congregation, in the plural ("Our God is great," "our rebellion," "Our King will come"). The chorus turns to address God directly in the singular: "My soul must sing to You an offering." So the church declares the truth together, and each singer answers it personally.

---

## How the text moves

The hymn moves through three acts of God in order. Stanza 1 is creation and providence. Stanza 2 is redemption in Christ. Stanza 3 is His return. The chorus repeats after each, and each time it answers a larger act of God.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

Stanza 2 is the heart of the song: "The light of life was crushed for our rebellion, / He died our death and rose up from the grave." Without it the song is praise to a Creator who will someday come. Stanza 3's "To claim His blood-washed bride" ties the return of Christ back to the cross, so it should stay as well. No line is false.

## Persons of the Trinity named

The Father is named in stanza 1 ("The Father of creation"). The Son is named as "The Word made flesh," "The light of life," and "Our King." The Spirit is not named.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

A theist could sing the first stanza, which is Psalm 19 in modern words. The second stanza stops him at once: "The Word made flesh" confesses the incarnation, and "He died our death and rose up from the grave" confesses substitution and resurrection. Only a Christian can sing the song through.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** "The galaxies can't help but shout His worth" is a modern picture of Psalm 19:1. It fits the psalm.
- **Thin:** none. The chorus is short, but it answers stanzas that carry the doctrine.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

Each stanza can be traced to its texts. "His splendor fills the earth" is Isaiah 6:3, and "The galaxies can't help but shout His worth" is Psalm 19:1–4, the heavens declaring the glory of God. The thunder singing His praise recalls Psalm 29. "How great You are" is Psalm 145:3. "The Word made flesh" is John 1:14, and "The light of life" is John 1:4. "Crushed for our rebellion" is Isaiah 53:5, "crushed for our iniquities." "He died our death" is 2 Corinthians 5:21 and 1 Peter 3:18. "Our King will come with trumpet blasts resounding" is 1 Thessalonians 4:16, and "in an instant faith will turn to sight" is 1 Corinthians 15:52 and 2 Corinthians 5:7. "His blood-washed bride" is Revelation 7:14 and Revelation 19:7.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

Two of the three stanzas are about Christ, and the middle one tells the whole gospel in order. Sin is named as rebellion. Christ's death is stated as a substitution, "He died our death," and the resurrection is sung as an event. The third stanza adds the second coming and calls the church a bride washed in His blood. The lyric is short, and the Christ content in it is dense.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

The stanzas are all declaration: what God made, what Christ did, and what He will do. The chorus is the singer's response, "My soul must sing to You an offering." That response is tied to what the stanzas have just said. It is a 4 because the chorus is in the singular and takes up a good share of the singing time.

---

## Description of God's Character: 4

| Attribute | Line |
|---|---|
| Creator, Father | "The Father of creation" |
| Glory, splendor | "His splendor fills the earth" |
| Power | "He came with pow'r to save" |
| Faithfulness to His promise | "God's promise to the fallen" |
| Justice, holiness | "crushed for our rebellion" |
| Kingship | "Our King will come" |
| Greatness | "How great You are" |

The attributes are tied to acts: creation, the cross, the return. That is how Scripture often shows God's character. Mercy and love are shown in the cross and not named as words, and attributes such as wisdom and eternity are absent.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The stanzas are corporate, plain, and tied to Scripture, and they follow a shape many in the congregation already know from *How Great Thou Art*. In three stanzas the people sing creation, the cross, the resurrection, and the return. The chorus is easy to learn. This is a song that teaches and admonishes (Colossians 3:16) while it gives the room a full voice of praise.

---

## Tone

The tone is grand and joyful. Stanza 1 is loud with thunder and galaxies. Stanza 2 is quieter and more weighty. Stanza 3 rises to the trumpet. The chorus is the same each time, and it gains weight as the stanzas move forward.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names the Spirit, never speaks of repentance or faith as its own response (beyond "faith will turn to sight"), and never sings Christ's present intercession.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** After a sermon on Psalm 19, Psalm 145, Isaiah 53, John 1, 1 Corinthians 15, or 1 Thessalonians 4. Its three stanzas also make it a good closing song when the sermon has covered creation and redemption.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** Very few. A song that names the Spirit, or one that gives voice to confession and repentance, would round out the set.
- **Diet sentence.** It adds creation, cross, and return in one song, with substitution stated plainly. Use it often.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop stanza 2. Do not mix its stanzas with *How Great Thou Art* in a medley, since the two texts have different words and different strengths.

## Identification notes

This is the Sovereign Grace song, inspired by Stuart Hine's *How Great Thou Art* (1949) but a separate text and tune. The catalog keeps the two apart (`how-great-thou-art`). Every setlist variant on file is a capitalization or key note. CCLI 6271677 matches PraiseCharts.
