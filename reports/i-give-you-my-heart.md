## Thesis

The singer’s desire is to honor the Lord with all his heart, to worship Him, to give Him praise, and that all he adores is in the Lord. The singer gives the Lord his heart and soul, lives for Him alone, and asks the Lord to have His way in every breath and every moment the singer is awake. There is no named sin, cross, substitution, or resurrection.

---

## Overall recommendation

Avoid

Do not use. The lyric is a first-person vow of surrender and desire without gospel grounding. It is also a Hillsong association. The church does not use Hillsong songs. Even without the association, the high-claim present-tense vows without the finished work would keep the overall label at Weak or Avoid.

## Voice and addressee

Mostly first-person singular addressing the Lord. It is desire and vow. The congregation is asked to own a high-claim present-tense surrender without the named finished work that makes the surrender possible.

---

## How the text moves

It opens with the desire: “This is my desire, to honour You / Lord, with all my heart I worship You / All I have within me, I give You praise / All that I adore is in You.” The chorus is the vow: “Lord, I give You my heart / I give You my soul / And I live for You alone / Every breath that I take / Every moment I’m awake / Lord have Your way in me.” There is no further movement into the finished work of Christ.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

None. The lyric does not name the finished work that would make any line essential. Dropping any line leaves the same vow of desire and surrender without gospel grounding.

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Yes. A vague theist who wants to give a higher power his heart and soul and live for that power alone can sing the lyric without tension. There is no named sin, cross, or resurrection that would correct the claim.

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 2

“This is my desire, to honour You / Lord, with all my heart I worship You” is true as a desire. “Lord, I give You my heart / I give You my soul / And I live for You alone” is a vow that Psalms model as honest thirst only when it is grounded in the finished work. The lyric does not name the ordinary sense of any gospel text. The score is 2 because the vow stands without the named cross, substitution, or resurrection that would make it possible.

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 2

The text is desire and vow. The first-person singular is a high-claim present-tense surrender without grounding in the finished work. The score is 2 because the lyric is dominated by the personal vow rather than objective truth about God and the gospel.

## Description of God’s Character: 2

The Lord is the one the singer desires to honor and the one who is asked to have His way. There is no named attribute of holiness, justice, mercy, or steadfast love tied to a text. The score is 2 because the lyric does not describe the character of God beyond the request that He have His way.

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 2

The language is plain. The lyric teaches that the singer’s desire is to honor the Lord, to give Him heart and soul, and to live for Him alone. It does not teach the ordinary sense of any gospel text. The singular voice is a high-claim vow that requires a weekly disclaimer that the singer means it. Singability is high, but the teaching is the reason for the 2. The church does not feature a vow of surrender that is not grounded in the finished work.

## Tone

Earnest and personal. It asks the room to confer on the Lord the surrender of heart, soul, and every breath without naming the finished work that makes the surrender possible. The intensity stays inside the desire and the vow.
