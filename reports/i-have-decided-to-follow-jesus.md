## Thesis

The singer has decided to follow Jesus and will not turn back. Stanza 2, the text this church sings, is “Though none go with me, still I will follow.” The world is behind and the cross is before, and still there is no turning back.

---

## Overall recommendation

Weak

Do not feature it. The church has logged it 3 times, in 2023 and 2026. “I have decided to follow Jesus” and “The world behind me, the cross before me” are the whole song, and they are a vow. The cross is the road in front of the singer. The song never says that Jesus died for sinners or that he rose. If it is used at all, sing all three stanzas, after a song that has already preached the cross, and do not let the decision be the gospel of the morning.

---

## Alternative song suggestions

The lyric is reaching for a life that follows Jesus, leaves the world, and keeps the cross in front. Three of these the church already sings. One is not in the catalog.

**Already sung**

**Jesus, I My Cross Have Taken** (Henry F. Lyte, hymn, CCLI 3874967). Logged 6 times across at least four years (2023, 2024, 2025, 2026). The singer takes up the cross and leaves all else, and the hymn goes on with the Savior who keeps the singer.

**Come Thou Fount of Every Blessing** (Robert Robinson, hymn, CCLI 3199992). Logged 9 times across at least four years (2023, 2024, 2025, 2026). The singer is prone to wander and asks to be bound to the God who sought the singer.

**In Christ Alone** (Keith Getty / Stuart Townend, Getty, CCLI 3350395). Logged 8 times across at least four years (2023, 2024, 2025, 2026). Following Christ is standing in him, and the song tells the cross, the wrath he bore, and the resurrection.

**Not yet logged**

**Christ Is Mine Forevermore** (Jonny Robinson / Rich Thompson, CityAlight, CCLI 7036096). Not in the catalog. The life is Christ’s because he paid for every failing, and the heart’s treasure is Christ himself.

## Voice and addressee

First person singular. The singer announces a decision to the room: “I have decided,” “I still will follow.” Jesus is spoken about. The song does not address him. The church would be repeating one person’s vow to the room.

---

## How the text moves

It states the decision three times and closes each stanza with “no turning back.” The second stanza is “Though none go with me, still I will follow.” The third stanza names what is left and what is ahead: the world behind, the cross before, and still no turning back. The refrain does not change. Nothing after the decision tells who Jesus is or what the cross did. The song moves from a choice, to loneliness, to a direction.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

All three stanzas are the song. “I have decided to follow Jesus” is the vow. “Though none go with me, still I will follow” is the second stanza. “The world behind me, the cross before me” is the only line that names the cross, and it names the cross as what stands in front of the singer. Dropping that stanza would leave a decision to follow and a promise not to turn, with no cross at all. No line is false. No line states the atonement. Cutting any stanza shortens a vow. It does not supply the gospel the vow lacks.

## Persons of the Trinity named

Jesus is named as the one the singer has decided to follow. The Father and the Spirit are unnamed. The cross is not said to be his death. The song tells the room the singer’s choice and does not go on to speak to Jesus.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. “I have decided to follow Jesus” cannot be sung by a worshiper who will not follow Jesus. That line stops the interchange. It does not, by itself, tell why Jesus is Lord or what the cross did. A person can decide to follow Jesus as a teacher and sing every stanza without ever confessing a death for sin.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none. A disciple’s resolve not to turn back is a right desire. The song makes that resolve the whole confession.
- **Non-biblical:** none.
- **Thin:** the entire lyric. “I have decided” is the singer’s choice. “The cross before me” does not say that Christ died, rose, or saves. “Though none go with me, still I will follow” is courage. It is not the gospel.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 3

“I have decided to follow Jesus” and “no turning back” are a disciple’s resolve. Luke 9:23 says, “If anyone would come after me, let him deny himself and take up his cross daily and follow me.” The song has the following and a cross in front. It does not have the denial of self, the daily cross, or the Jesus who is speaking. “The world behind me, the cross before me” can sit next to that call as a picture of leaving the world and facing the cross. The picture does not say what happened on the cross.

The decision is a true line about the singer’s act. The church is asked to sing that act as its confession. Scripture calls people to follow Jesus. The saving news is the death and resurrection, which this lyric never tells. The score stays at 3 because the resolve is true and the song never says the news that gives the resolve its ground.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 2

Jesus is named as the one to follow. The cross is named as what is in front. There is no death for sinners, no blood, no burial, no resurrection, and no statement of who he is beyond the name. Lordship here is the singer’s promise to follow. The finished work is absent. A vow to follow, with the cross as a direction, is a 2 on this category.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 2

Every stanza is the singer’s vow. “I have decided.” “I still will follow.” “No turning back.” Nothing in the song declares what Jesus has done. The cross is located in front of the singer, which is still about the singer’s road. There is no line that would remain if the “I” were removed. The song is the decision.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 2

Jesus is the one who is followed. The cross is ahead of the singer. No attribute is named: no holiness, no mercy, no justice, no love, no power, no faithfulness. The character of God is not described. The song describes the singer’s choice.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 3

Three logs, in 2023 and 2026, means it is occasional here. The words are few and memorable. A congregation can learn to say that following Jesus is a road with no turning back, and that the cross is ahead and the world is behind. They cannot learn from this lyric why they follow or what the cross accomplished. “I have decided” is easy to hear as the moment of salvation. That hearing is the danger of the song in this room. The three stanzas are clear. They teach a vow.

---

## Tone

The tone is resolute. The line that sets it is “no turning back, no turning back,” after “I have decided to follow Jesus.” The second stanza is lonelier. The third is the same resolve with the cross in view. Nothing is playful. The firmness is the singer’s, and the song gives it no other ground.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never says that Jesus died for sinners and rose, never names a sin, and never says that the decision to follow is the fruit of grace.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Luke 9:23 or a call to perseverance, after the cross and the resurrection have already been sung or preached. All three stanzas belong. The third is the one that mentions the cross.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The death and resurrection of Jesus. Pair it with *In Christ Alone* or *Jesus, I My Cross Have Taken* so “the cross before me” is not the only cross in the service.
- **Diet sentence.** Three times in at least two years, it adds a short vow to follow Jesus without turning back. It never adds the gospel.
- **What not to do.** Do not use it as the invitation that explains salvation. Do not drop “the cross before me” and leave only the decision. Do not add a chorus that makes the decision louder and the cross smaller.

## Identification notes

One folk song, three stanzas. The catalog attributes it to Sadhu Sundar Singh and calls the origin unknown, mid-twentieth-century folk. Hymnary now attributes the text to Simon K. Marak, a Garo pastor and teacher, and notes the older attribution to Singh. William J. Reynolds published an arrangement in the Assembly Songbook (1959) after finding the song in an undated Australian collection. The earliest Hymnary listing of a North American printing is 1950. This grade uses the three stanzas this church sings, in this order: “I have decided to follow Jesus,” “Though none go with me, still I will follow,” and “The world behind me, the cross before me.” Singh is not treated as the confirmed author. The header names Marak. The tune is the Assam folk melody. The era is unknown and traditional.

Hymnary and a worship multitrack listing print CCLI 24494 for the traditional text. The catalog prints 16509. This check could not attach 16509 to these stanzas. Later choruses that add “You gave Yourself for me” are different songs, with their own numbers, and are not this grade. The setlist key is C. No arrangement year is on file.

---

## Why this overall label

The song is a clear vow to follow Jesus, to keep following if no one else does, and to leave the world with the cross in front. No line is false. The vow is the whole confession. The cross is a direction. The lyric never says Christ died for sinners. Three uses in this window do not change that. It should not be the song that tells the church the gospel.
