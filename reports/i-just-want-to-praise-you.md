## Thesis

The singer wants to praise God, to lift hands and say “I love You,” and to call God everything. The singer exalts God’s holy name on high, and then repeats that exaltation.

---

## Overall recommendation

Weak

Do not feature it. The church has logged it twice, in 2025 and 2026. “I exalt Your holy name on high” is the line that has to stay if the song is used at all. The rest is the singer’s desire to praise and to say “I love You.” Jesus is not named. A worshiper who does not confess Jesus can sing every line unchanged. If it is used, keep it to a few bars after a psalm of praise has already been read, and do not let it open the service.

---

## Alternative song suggestions

The lyric is reaching for praise, love toward God, and the exalting of his holy name. Three of these the church already sings. One is not in the catalog.

**Already sung**

**Holy, Holy, Holy** (Reginald Heber, hymn, CCLI 1156). Logged 6 times across at least four years (2023, 2024, 2025, 2026). The holy name is the thrice-holy Lord, and the church sings who he is.

## Voice and addressee

First person singular, speaking to God. “I just want to praise You,” “Lift my hands and say I love You,” “You are everything to me,” “I exalt Your holy name.” The church would be borrowing one person’s affection and calling it the song.

---

## How the text moves

It states the desire to praise, the lifted hands, the words “I love You,” and the claim that God is everything to the singer. It then exalts the holy name on high. The same lines return, and the exaltation is sung three times. There is no second claim. The song begins with the singer’s want and ends on the name, without ever saying who bears the name or what he has done.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“I exalt Your holy name on high” is the line the grade depends on. It is the only line that says something about God beyond the singer’s love. “I just want to praise You” and “Lift my hands and say I love You” and “You are everything to me” are the desire. If those lines are all that is sung, the holy name disappears and the song is affection. No line is false. The name is easy to lose because the title is the desire to praise.

## Persons of the Trinity named

“You” and “Your holy name” are the whole address. Father, Son, and Spirit are unnamed. Nothing says the holy name is the name of Jesus.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Yes. Praise, lifted hands, “I love You,” “You are everything to me,” and “I exalt Your holy name” can be sung to any god a worshiper calls holy. No line would force the name of Jesus, a cross, or a resurrection. The song gives no claim that would correct the singer.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** “Lift my hands and say I love You.” Lifted hands are a biblical posture. The line’s weight is the singer’s “I love You,” which reports affection and does not state a text.
- **Thin:** the whole chorus. “You are everything to me” is the singer’s estimate. “I exalt Your holy name on high” says the name is holy and lifts it. It does not say who he is.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 3

“I exalt Your holy name on high” is praise of God’s name, and the holiness of his name is a biblical confession. Psalm 30:1, which some listings attach to the song, says, “I will extol you, O LORD, for you have drawn me up.” The song says “I just want to praise You” and does not say that the Lord has drawn the singer up, or why. “You are everything to me” is the singer’s word for God’s sufficiency, said without a work of God attached. The lines are not false. They do not teach a text. The score stays at 3 because the praise is real and empty of any particular act of God.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 2

Jesus is not named. The cross, the blood, sin, and the resurrection are absent. “Your holy name” could be any holy name the singer loves. The song exalts a name and never says the name, and it never says a finished work. That is a 2.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 2

“I exalt Your holy name” aims at God. Everything around it is the singer’s want: “I just want to praise You,” “Lift my hands,” “I love You,” “You are everything to me.” The title is the desire. The holy name is the object of the desire, and the desire is what the song repeats. A congregation singing this is reporting its affection.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 2

The name is holy. “You are everything to me” says God is the singer’s all, which is a word about the singer’s need as much as about God. Love in the song is the singer’s “I love You.” The lyric never says that God is love, and it never says that God has loved the singer. Justice, mercy, wrath, faithfulness, and power are unnamed. The portrait is a holy name and the singer’s devotion.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 3

Two uses, in 2025 and 2026, means it is not a staple. The words take a few seconds. A congregation learns that praise and a holy name are good, and that the singer loves God and lifts empty hands. They do not learn who God is or what he has done. The line is clear. The clarity is the thinness. It can follow a psalm. It cannot lead one.

---

## Tone

The tone is affectionate and small. The line that sets it is “Lift my hands and say I love You.” “I exalt Your holy name on high” is the same softness aimed at the name. Nothing is harsh, and nothing is heavy. The song is a fond word to God.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names Jesus, never says that God has loved sinners, and never says why the holy name is to be exalted.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** A few bars after a psalm of praise has been read, if “Your holy name” is sung and not replaced by the love line alone. It does not serve a doctrinal sermon.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The name of Christ and any work of God. Pair it with *Holy, Holy, Holy* or *Come Praise and Glorify* so the holy name has a person and a saving act.
- **Diet sentence.** Twice in two years, it adds a short desire to praise and a holy name. It never adds the gospel.
- **What not to do.** Do not open with it. Do not loop “I love You” and drop “Your holy name.” Do not treat lifted hands as the lesson.
