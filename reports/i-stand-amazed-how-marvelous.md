## Thesis

The singer stands amazed in the presence of Jesus the Nazarene and wonders how He could love a sinner, condemned and unclean. In the garden He prayed “Not My will, but Thine,” with no tears for His own griefs but sweat drops of blood for the singer’s. He took the sins and sorrows, made them His own, bore the burden to Calvary, and suffered and died alone. When the ransomed see His face in glory, the joy through the ages will be to sing of His love.

**Excellent / Prefer**

Feature it. Sing all the stanzas. The opening names the sinner, condemned and unclean. The garden stanza names the prayer and the blood drops for the singer. The Calvary stanza names the sins made His own and the dying alone. Without those lines the refrain is true praise of the Savior’s love that has not named the cost or the place.

## Voice and addressee

The hymn is first-person singular throughout. “I stand amazed.” “A sinner, condemned, unclean.” It speaks of Jesus in the third person. The claims can be sung by the congregation together.

---

## How the text moves

The opening stanza names the amazement in the presence of Jesus the Nazarene and the wonder at His love for a sinner, condemned and unclean. The refrain answers with the marvel of the Savior’s love. The garden stanza names the prayer “Not My will, but Thine” and the sweat drops of blood for the singer. The angel stanza names the comfort in the sorrows He bore that night. The Calvary stanza names the sins and sorrows taken, made His own, and the burden borne to Calvary where He suffered and died alone. The final stanza looks to the ransomed in glory seeing His face and singing of His love through the ages. The refrain returns after each.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

Do not drop the garden stanza. “For me it was in the garden / He prayed: ‘Not My will, but Thine.’ / He had no tears for His own griefs, / But sweat drops of blood for mine” is the cost in the garden. Without it the church has praised the Savior’s love and has not named the blood drops or the prayer.

Do not drop the Calvary stanza. “He took my sins and my sorrows, / He made them His very own; / He bore the burden to Calvary, / And suffered and died alone” is the substitution and the dying. Without it the love has not named the place or the burden.

No line is false.

## Persons of the Trinity named

Jesus is named as the Nazarene and the Savior. The Father and the Spirit are unnamed. The work is the Son’s.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Not the whole song. The opening names the sinner, condemned and unclean. The garden stanza names the blood drops for mine. The Calvary stanza names the sins made His own and the dying alone. A vague theist could not sing those lines as written.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

**Unbiblical.** None.

**Non-biblical.** None.

**Thin.** Burial and resurrection are not named in those words. The lyric does not need them to make its claim. The claim is the Savior’s love that took the sins and bore them to Calvary.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“I stand amazed in the presence / Of Jesus the Nazarene, / And wonder how He could love me, / A sinner, condemned, unclean” is Luke 5:26 and Romans 5:8. The amazement at the love for the unclean is the ordinary sense of those texts.

“For me it was in the garden / He prayed: ‘Not My will, but Thine.’ / He had no tears for His own griefs, / But sweat drops of blood for mine” is Luke 22:42–44. The prayer and the blood drops are the ordinary sense of those verses.

“He took my sins and my sorrows, / He made them His very own; / He bore the burden to Calvary, / And suffered and died alone” is Isaiah 53:4–6 and 1 Peter 2:24. The taking of the sins and the bearing to Calvary are the ordinary sense of those texts.

“When with the ransomed in glory / His face I at last shall see, / ’Twill be my joy through the ages / To sing of His love for me” is Revelation 5:9 and 1 John 3:2. The seeing of the face is the ordinary hope of those texts.

The lines use the ordinary sense of those texts.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

Christ is named from the first line as Jesus the Nazarene. The garden prayer and the blood drops are named. The sins are made His own. The burden is borne to Calvary. He suffered and died alone. The work is not left as a title.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

The main claim is objective. Jesus prayed in the garden, took the sins, bore the burden to Calvary, and died. The first-person lines are the singer’s amazement at the love for the unclean and the joy of singing it in glory. They are not a vow the singer must mean before he may sing. They are the confession of the benefit.

The score stays at 4 because the voice is the singer’s amazement rather than pure declaration about God alone.

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The stanzas are clear. The refrain is short. The words are ordinary. The church has already sung it. The people learn the shape of the Savior’s love from the unclean sinner, through the garden and the Calvary burden, to the joy in glory. They do not need a weekly explanation of any line. Keys on file include F.

If time is short, do not drop the garden stanza or the Calvary stanza.

---

## Tone

The tone is glad and sober. “How marvelous! How wonderful!” answers the work just named. There is no intensity beyond the text.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names the Father or the Spirit, the burial, or the resurrection in those words. It never says “wrath” in that word, though “condemned” and “died alone” state the same fact.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Luke 22, Isaiah 53, 1 Peter 2, or any text on the garden and the cross. It works as a response after the sermon has opened the passion.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The Father and the Spirit when the day’s text requires them. Explicit resurrection language if the sermon has required it and no other song supplies it.
- **Diet sentence.** It adds the unclean sinner, the garden prayer and blood drops, the sins made His own, and the burden borne to Calvary. It does not add trinitarian naming or the resurrection narrative.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop the garden stanza or the Calvary stanza. Do not treat the refrain as a standalone tag after a thinner song. The stanzas supply the reason for the marvel.
