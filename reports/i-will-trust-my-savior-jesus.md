## Thesis

The singer will trust the Savior Jesus in doubt and weakness because His way is best and leads to rest, and because the cross shows the gift is complete.

---

## Overall recommendation

Acceptable with notes

Use it sparingly, and only when the rest of the set has already named the gospel clearly. The bridge names the cross, the One who died for me, and the complete gift. The verses and chorus are personal vows and prayers to trust. The cross line keeps it above Weak, and the weight of personal vows keeps it below Strong. The church has sung it 8 times across at least four years of the 2023–2026 window.

## Voice and addressee

First-person singular throughout (“I will trust,” “my heart,” “died for me”). The chorus addresses Jesus. The voice is personal, and the subject is the singer’s trust.

---

## How the text moves

The verses vow trust in doubt, in weakness, and on the way home. The bridge gives the reason, the cross and the complete gift. The song then returns to the chorus prayer for more trust.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

The bridge (“Oh on that cross, how it was seen / I can go now ever trusting in the One who died for me / What could I bring for Your gift is complete”). Without it, the song is only a repeated vow to trust. Even with it, the vow is the frame of the whole song.

## Persons of the Trinity named

Jesus is named as Saviour and the One who died for me. The Father and the Spirit are not named.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Not the whole song. The bridge needs “the One who died for me” and the complete gift. The verses and chorus could be sung by someone who means the vow but does not know the finished work.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** none that affect the claim.
- **Thin:** “the shield of Jesus is the safest place of all” is true but thin until the bridge names the cross. The repeated “I will trust” does not say what the cross accomplished beyond the complete gift.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 3

“The One who died for me” and “Your gift is complete” are the gospel (Romans 5:8 and the finished work). “His way is best” and the path that leads to rest fit the faithfulness of Christ. The lines are true and thin: the song names substitution, propitiation, and the resurrection less clearly than it names the singer’s vow.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 3

Jesus is named as Saviour and as the One who died for me, and the bridge names the cross and the complete gift. Substitution is implied. The blood, imputed righteousness, and the empty tomb are absent, and most of the song is about the response of trust.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 2

Most of the song is first-person vow and prayer: “I will trust my Saviour Jesus,” “Help me trust You more and more,” “May my heart be ever Yours.” Those large present-tense promises are not well grounded in the rest of the song, which is why this is a 2. The bridge supplies objective claims about the cross, and then the song returns to the vow.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 3

Jesus is Saviour, shield, the one whose way is best, and the One who died for me. Holiness, justice, and sovereignty are not named, and “Saviour” and “shield” stay general until the bridge.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 3

The words are easy to sing, and the bridge teaches the cross and the complete gift. The rest of the song teaches the response more than the finished work, so it is useful only after Scripture or another song has named the gospel clearly.

---

## Tone

The bridge is reverent. The verses and chorus are more intimate, centered on the singer’s trust.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names the Spirit, the resurrection, or substitution beyond “died for me,” and it never speaks as “we.” When the sermon needs propitiation, pair it with Jesus Thank You, whose chorus states it.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** After a clear gospel text on the cross or the finished work, when the room is ready to respond in trust. Do not use it as the main teaching song.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The Spirit, if the sermon needs Him named, and a fuller statement of propitiation and of the church as a body on a Romans 3 or 1 Corinthians 12 Sunday.
- **Diet sentence.** Adds a personal response of trust that names the cross. It does not add a corporate confession of the finished work.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop the bridge, do not use it to open or close the service, and do not add a tag that repeats the vow.
