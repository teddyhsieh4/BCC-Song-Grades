## Thesis

Jesus, Lord of heaven, gave grace the singer did not deserve, and the promise of his word. The singer stands in wonder at the sacrifice Jesus made and at the debt he freely paid. The rest of the song says that love is deeper than the ocean, higher than the heavens, past the stars, and without bounds, and that this love is all the church ever needs.

---

## Overall recommendation

Acceptable with notes

Use it sparingly, and do not let the chorus become the whole song. The church has logged it 3 times across at least three years (2023, 2024, 2026). “The sacrifice You made” and “My debt You freely paid” have to stay. They are one verse. The chorus measures love by ocean, heaven, and stars, and never says the cross. Sing the debt lines every time, and let another song name the blood and the resurrection.

## Voice and addressee

First person at the start, speaking to Jesus. “I do not deserve,” “I stand in wonder,” “my debt.” The chorus turns to “we”: “Your love is all we ever need.” Jesus is named and addressed as Lord of heaven. The church sings one person’s wonder and then a shared claim about the size of his love.

---

## How the text moves

It opens with Jesus as Lord of heaven and with grace the singer did not deserve, and with the promise of his word. The singer then stands in wonder at a sacrifice and at a debt freely paid. The chorus leaves the debt and measures the love: deeper than the ocean, higher than the heavens, beyond the stars. A refrain says the love has no bounds. A later section stacks deep, wide, great, and high, and says the love is all the church ever needs. The sacrifice and the debt are one verse. The song spends the rest of its time on the size of the love.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“Lord, I stand in wonder / Of the sacrifice You made / With mercy beyond measure / My debt You freely paid” is the stanza the grade depends on. “Jesus, Lord of heaven” and “the grace that You have given” belong with it. “Your love is deeper than any ocean” and “Your love is all we ever need” are the lines the chorus will repeat. If those are all that remain, the debt has disappeared and the song is the size of love. No line is false. The debt lines are the ones a long chorus will bury.

## Persons of the Trinity named

Jesus is named, and he is Lord of heaven, the one who gave grace, made the sacrifice, and paid the debt. The Father and the Spirit are unnamed. “The promise of Your word” is his word. The love in the chorus is his love.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. “Jesus, Lord of heaven,” “the sacrifice You made,” and “My debt You freely paid” cannot be moved to another lord. The chorus, “Your love is deeper than any ocean” and “Your love is all we ever need,” could be sung about a generic love if the name were changed. With the name and the debt left in, the song stops that change.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** “Reaches beyond the stars in the sky.” The stars are a measure of distance. They do not state a doctrine.
- **Thin:** “Your love is deep, Your love is wide / Your love is great, Your love is high” and “Your love has no bounds” and “Your love is all we ever need.” The size is repeated. The debt has already been paid in one couplet, and the chorus does not return to it.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 3

“I do not deserve / The grace that You have given” agrees with the shape of Ephesians 2:8, salvation by grace, and with the singer’s lack of desert. “The promise of Your word” is a true good. “The sacrifice You made” and “My debt You freely paid” say that Jesus offered a sacrifice and paid what the singer owed. Colossians 2:14 speaks of the record of debt cancelled by the cross. The song says debt and sacrifice and does not say the cross or the record.

“Your love is deeper than any ocean / Higher than the heavens / Reaches beyond the stars” stands near Ephesians 3:18, the breadth and length and height and depth of Christ’s love. The song uses ocean, heaven, and stars for that size. The size is the chorus. The debt is one verse. The lines are not false. They stack a true payment and then a long measure of love, which is why the score stays at 3.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 3

Jesus is Lord of heaven. He gives grace, keeps his word, makes a sacrifice, and freely pays the singer’s debt. That is more than a title. The cross, the blood, the burial, and the resurrection are not named. Mercy is “beyond measure,” which fits the payment and then becomes the size of the love. The finished work is one stanza. The song’s length is the unbounded love.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 3

“The sacrifice You made” and “My debt You freely paid” declare what Jesus did. “I do not deserve” and “I stand in wonder” are the singer’s place in that gift. The chorus is about his love, not about the singer’s feelings, and it is still a list of heights and depths with “all we ever need” as the church’s conclusion. The debt is objective. The wonder and the measuring take more of the song.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 3

Jesus is Lord of heaven. He is gracious to the undeserving. He keeps his word. He makes a sacrifice, shows mercy, and pays a debt freely. His love is deep, wide, great, high, and without bounds. Love is the attribute the chorus repeats until it is the song. Holiness, justice, and wrath are unnamed. The payment implies a real debt. The portrait after that line is the size of love.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 3

Three logs across at least three years means some of the room knows it. The chorus is the part people will remember, and it teaches that Christ’s love is large. The verse teaches that the singer did not deserve grace and that Jesus paid a debt. A congregation can learn the debt if the verse is sung. They will learn only the size of love if the chorus is looped. “Beyond the stars” needs no explanation. The debt line does the teaching, and it is brief.

---

## Tone

The tone is wondering and warm. The line that sets it is “Your love is deeper than any ocean,” after the debt has been paid. “I stand in wonder of the sacrifice You made” is quieter and more specific. The stacked “deep, wide, great, high” keeps the warmth going. The song is tender, and the tenderness sits on a real payment for one verse.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never says the word cross, never says that Jesus rose, and never names the Father or the Spirit.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Ephesians 2 or Ephesians 3, if the debt lines are sung and the chorus is not the only thing the room hears. It does not serve a sermon that needs the cross described.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The cross in plain words, and the resurrection. Pair it with *Hallelujah, What a Savior* (logged 6 times) or *In Christ Alone* (logged 8 times).
- **Diet sentence.** Three times in at least three years, it adds a Lord of heaven who paid a debt the singer did not deserve. It never adds the empty tomb, and the chorus never returns to the debt.
- **What not to do.** Do not loop “Your love has no bounds” and drop “My debt You freely paid.” Do not treat the ocean and the stars as the doctrine.
