## Thesis

Jesus is thanked because His blood has washed away sin, the Father’s wrath is completely satisfied, and the former enemy is now seated at His table.

---

## Overall recommendation

Excellent / Prefer

Feature it freely on the cross, propitiation, or the reconciliation of enemies. Keep the chorus every time, because it is where the satisfied wrath and the washed sin are stated. The church has sung it 7 times across at least four years of the 2023–2026 window.

## Voice and addressee

First-person singular in the verses and bridge (“I cannot comprehend,” “reserved for me,” “I’ve been brought near,” “I want to live for You”). The chorus addresses Jesus directly. Every believer in Christ can sing these lines as true of themselves.

---

## How the text moves

Verse 1 opens with the mystery of the cross: the Holy One crushed His Son, who drank the cup reserved for the singer. The chorus answers with thanks, and verse 2 adds the sacrifice that brought the singer near and made an enemy a friend. The bridge ends in response: “Lover of my soul, I want to live for You.”

---

## Lines you cannot cut

The chorus (“Your blood has washed away my sin / Jesus, thank You / The Father’s wrath completely satisfied / Jesus, thank You / Once Your enemy, now seated at Your table”). Without it, the song thanks Jesus without saying what for. Keep verse 1 with it, since that verse names the crushed Son and the bitter cup.

## Persons of the Trinity named

Jesus is addressed throughout. The Father is named as the one whose wrath is completely satisfied and as the perfect Holy One who crushed His Son. The Spirit is not named.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. A vague theist could not sing “The Father’s wrath completely satisfied” or “Once Your enemy, now seated at Your table” without changing them.

---

- **Unbiblical:** none.

- **Thin:** the lyric stops at the cross and the table and never reaches the empty tomb.

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“You, the perfect Holy One, crushed Your Son / Who drank the bitter cup reserved for me” is Isaiah 53:10, Luke 22:42, and Matthew 26:39: the Father’s purpose at the cross and the cup of wrath. “Your blood has washed away my sin” and “The Father’s wrath completely satisfied” are propitiation and cleansing (Romans 3:25, 1 John 1:7, Hebrews 9:14). “Once Your enemy, now seated at Your table” is reconciliation (Romans 5:10, Ephesians 2:13–16).

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

Christ is the one who drank the cup and whose blood washes sin, and the chorus thanks Him for the wrath satisfied. Substitution and propitiation are stated in plain words, which is what this category asks for.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

The verses and chorus declare what Christ has done. The bridge (“Lover of my soul, I want to live for You”) is the one place the song turns from declaration to desire. It comes after the finished work has been sung, but it is the reason this score is a 4.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 5

The Father is the perfect Holy One who crushed His Son and whose wrath is satisfied. Jesus’ mercy and kindness know no end. Holiness, justice, mercy, and grace are all named.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The chorus is short, and “Jesus, thank You” repeats, so a room learns it quickly. Singing it teaches propitiation and reconciliation in words a congregation can say together.

---

## Tone

The tone is reverent. The feeling in the song is gratitude for the cross and the cup.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names the empty tomb or the Spirit. When the sermon needs the resurrection, pair it with a song that names it, such as Christ Our Hope in Life and Death.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** The cross, propitiation, or reconciliation (Romans 5, Ephesians 2).
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The resurrection, if the sermon needs the empty tomb.
- **Diet sentence.** Adds a dense confession of propitiation and reconciliation. The resurrection has to come from another song.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop the chorus, and do not follow it with a vague chorus of general thanks.
