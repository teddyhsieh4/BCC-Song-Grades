Jesus is thanked because His blood has washed away sin, the Father’s wrath is completely satisfied, and the former enemy is now seated at His table.

## Voice and addressee

First-person singular in the verses and bridge (“I cannot comprehend,” “reserved for me,” “I’ve been brought near,” “I want to live for You”). The chorus addresses Jesus directly. The claims are confessional and shareable. The congregation can sing the lines as true of every believer in Christ.

---

## How the text moves

It opens with the mystery of the cross and the agonies of Calvary: the perfect Holy One crushed His Son, who drank the bitter cup reserved for the singer. The chorus states that the blood has washed away sin, the Father’s wrath is completely satisfied, and the former enemy is now seated at the table. The second verse names the perfect sacrifice that has brought the singer near, turned the enemy into a friend, and poured out the riches of glorious grace without end. The bridge turns to desire: “Lover of my soul, I want to live for You.” The claim advances from the cross and the cup, through the satisfied wrath and the washed sin, to the reconciled place at the table and the response of living for Him.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

The chorus (“Your blood has washed away my sin / Jesus, thank You / The Father’s wrath completely satisfied / Jesus, thank You / Once Your enemy, now seated at Your table”). Drop the washed sin, the satisfied wrath, or the move from enemy to table and the thanksgiving loses its ground. The first verse that names the crushed Son and the bitter cup should stay with it.

## Persons of the Trinity named

Jesus is addressed throughout. The Father is named as the one whose wrath is completely satisfied and as the perfect Holy One who crushed His Son. The Spirit is not named.

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. The cross, Calvary, the crushed Son, the bitter cup, the blood that washes sin, the Father’s wrath completely satisfied, and the move from enemy to table require the God of the gospel. A vague theist cannot sing those lines without correction.

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“You, the perfect Holy One, crushed Your Son / Who drank the bitter cup reserved for me” follows the ordinary sense of the Father’s purpose at the cross and the cup of wrath (Isaiah 53:10, Luke 22:42, Matthew 26:39). “Your blood has washed away my sin” and “The Father’s wrath completely satisfied” follow the ordinary sense of propitiation and cleansing (Romans 3:25, 1 John 1:7, Hebrews 9:14). “Once Your enemy, now seated at Your table” follows the ordinary sense of reconciliation (Romans 5:10, Ephesians 2:13–16). The ordinary sense of the texts is in view at each claim.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

Christ is the one whose blood washes sin, who drank the bitter cup, and who is thanked for the satisfied wrath and the reconciled place at the table. The cross, Calvary, the perfect sacrifice, and the move from enemy to friend are explicit. Substitution and propitiation are stated.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

The lines declare what Christ has done: the blood, the satisfied wrath, the crushed Son, the bitter cup, and the reconciled table. First-person singular is confessional and shareable. The bridge (“Lover of my soul, I want to live for You”) is desire language. It rests on the finished work rather than on inner weather, yet it is the one line that moves from declaration to response.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 5

The Father is the perfect Holy One who crushed His Son and whose wrath is completely satisfied. Jesus is the one whose blood washes sin, whose sacrifice brings near, and whose mercy and kindness know no end. Holiness, justice (wrath satisfied, bitter cup), mercy, and grace are named. The attributes are specific and balanced.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The diction is plain. The argument is complete in the cross, the blood, the satisfied wrath, and the reconciled table. The congregation has already sung it in multiple years. The confessional voice and the clear chorus make it useful for teaching substitution and propitiation.

---

## Tone

Reverent. The language stays with the mystery of the cross, the agonies of Calvary, the bitter cup, the satisfied wrath, and the table. It does not ask the room to confer on God what He already possesses.

---

## What the congregation never says

The empty tomb and the explicit naming of the Spirit are absent. Pair the song with texts or songs that name the resurrection when the sermon of the day requires them.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** The cross, propitiation, reconciliation (Romans 5, Ephesians 2), or any text on the blood that washes sin and the wrath that is satisfied.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** Explicit naming of the resurrection if the sermon requires the empty tomb.
- **Diet sentence.** Adds a dense confession that the blood has washed away sin, the Father’s wrath is completely satisfied, and the former enemy is now seated at the table. It does not add a song that names the empty tomb.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop the chorus. Do not tag it with a chorus that flattens the satisfied wrath and the washed sin into generic thanks.
