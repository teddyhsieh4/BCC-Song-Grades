## Thesis

Jesus’ name is the sweetest, he hears the singer and picks the singer up after each fall, he died and took away the singer’s shame, and he is the coming King.

---

## Overall recommendation

Acceptable with notes

Use it sparingly, and only with verse 2. It shows up once in this window, in 2026. Verse 1 is affection: the sweetest name, a God who hears, a God who picks the singer up. Verse 2 has the line the song needs, “You died and took away my shame,” and the title “the First, the Last, the same.” Verse 3 calls him the coming King and then asks for “the love that You can bring.” The refrain is still “sweetest.” The cross and the resurrection have to come from the rest of the service. If verse 2 is not going to be sung, choose another song.

## Voice and addressee

Mostly “I” speaking to Jesus (“You”). Verse 3 shifts to “we” for the coming King and the love the church needs. The refrain is praise of the name, addressed to him.

---

## How the text moves

Verse 1 is personal help: he hears, and he picks the singer up. Verse 2 adds praise, the title First and Last, and the death that took shame away. Verse 3 looks to the coming King and asks for his love. The refrain returns each time to “the sweetest name.” The death line does not change the refrain.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“O Jesus, You died and took away my shame” is the line the grade depends on. “You’re still the First, the Last, the same” is the other line with a text under it. If a band sings only verse 1 and the refrain, the church is left with “sweetest,” “hear me,” and “pick me up.” No line is false. Dropping verse 2 would leave a song too thin for this label. Verse 2 has to stay.

## Persons of the Trinity named

Jesus is named in every verse. The Father and the Spirit are unnamed. “The First, the Last, the same” is said to Jesus.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Verse 1 could be sung by someone who likes the name Jesus as comfort: he hears, and he helps when the singer falls. “You died and took away my shame” and “the soon and coming King” cannot be sung that way. If verse 2 and verse 3 are dropped, the refrain “You’re the sweetest name of all” works as affection for whatever name the singer already loves.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** “the sweetest name of all,” as a comparison of flavor. The name is above every name in Philippians 2:9. “Sweetest” is the song’s own word for that love of the name.
- **Thin:** “You always hear me when I call” and “You pick me up each time I fall.” God hears prayer. The lines do not say from what the singer falls, or what the death has to do with the fall. “We need the love that You can bring” asks for love and does not say what that love did at the cross.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 3

“You’re still the First, the Last, the same” puts Revelation 22:13, “I am the Alpha and the Omega, the first and the last,” with Hebrews 13:8, “Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever.” “You died and took away my shame” is a true summary and not a text. Isaiah 53 and the cross are the place shame is borne. The line does not quote them. “The soon and coming King” agrees with Revelation 22:20, “Surely I am coming soon,” and does not use that sentence.

“You always hear me when I call” is in the neighborhood of Psalm 34:17 and Psalm 145:18, and it promises hearing with no condition and no mention of asking according to his will. “You pick me up each time I fall” is pastoral comfort. No verse promises it in those words. The song is accurate in verse 2’s titles and in the fact that Jesus died. The rest is light.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 3

Jesus is addressed by name, called the first and the last, and looked for as King. “You died and took away my shame” is the one gospel line. There is no cross, no blood, no burial, and no resurrection. Shame is removed. The singer is not told how. A death that takes shame away is more than a title, and it is less than the account Scripture gives. That is a 3.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 2

“You’re the sweetest name of all” is the singer’s love of the name. “You always hear me” and “You pick me up each time I fall” are what Jesus does for the singer. “How I love to praise Your name” reports that love. Verse 3’s “we” still asks for “the love that You can bring.” The objective lines are “the First, the Last, the same,” the death, and the coming King. They sit inside a refrain about sweetness. The weight is on the singer’s experience of Jesus.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 3

Jesus is the first and the last, the same, and the coming King, and he hears, lifts, dies for shame, and loves. Holiness, justice, and wrath are unnamed. “Sweetest” is not an attribute. The real titles are in verse 2 and the first line of verse 3. They are not developed.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 3

One log, in 2026, means the room is not carrying this as a known text. The refrain is easy. What the church learns from verse 1 is that Jesus hears and helps. What it learns from verse 2, if the verse is sung, is that he died and that shame is gone, plus the first-and-last title. The mechanism is missing, so the death line has to sit under a sermon that supplies the cross. Verse 1 is the one a band will keep. That is the wrong verse to keep alone.

---

## Tone

The tone is affectionate. The line that sets it is “Jesus, You’re the sweetest name of all.” “You pick me up each time I fall” is tender. The death line is quieter than the refrain and easy to sing past.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never says sin, the cross, the blood, the resurrection, repentance, or why the shame was his to take.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Revelation 22:13, Hebrews 13:8, or a sermon that has already opened Isaiah 53 or the cross.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The cross and the resurrection. Pair it with *All I Have Is Christ* or *Before the Throne of God Above* so the name is tied to the blood.
- **Diet sentence.** Once in 2026, it adds a short love of the name of Jesus and one death line. It does not add the cross or the empty tomb to the year.
- **What not to do.** Do not sing verse 1 twice and skip verse 2. Do not build a tag on “sweetest.” If the death line is not going to be sung, choose another song.

## Why this overall label

Verse 2 confesses that Jesus died and took away shame, and that he is the first and the last. The refrain the church will remember is the sweet name, with hearing and help. That is enough for an occasional use when verse 2 stays and the sermon has already said why he died.
