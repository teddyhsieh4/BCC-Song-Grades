## Thesis

All that was once held dear and thought gain is counted loss and spent compared to knowing Jesus. There is no greater thing. He is all, the best, joy, and righteousness. The heart’s desire is to know Him more, to be found in Him and known as His, and to possess by faith the gift of righteousness that could not be earned. The prayer is to know the power of His risen life and to know Him in His sufferings, to become like Him in His death, so as to live with Him and never die.

---

## Overall recommendation

**Strong**

Use it regularly. Sing all three stanzas. Stanza 1 names the counting of all as loss compared to knowing Jesus. Stanza 2 names the desire to know Him more and the all-surpassing gift of righteousness possessed by faith. Stanza 3 names the power of the risen life and the knowing Him in sufferings. Without those lines the chorus is true affection for Jesus that has not named the exchange or the resurrection.

The church has sung it for at least four years (6 logs in the 2023–2026 snapshot). It is already known.

## Voice and addressee

The song is first-person singular throughout. “All I once held dear.” “Knowing you, Jesus.” It addresses Jesus. The claims can be sung by the congregation together when the people mean the exchange.

---

## How the text moves

Stanza 1 names the counting of all that was held dear and thought gain as loss and spent compared to knowing Jesus. The chorus answers that knowing Him is the greatest thing, that He is all, the best, joy, and righteousness, and that the singer loves Him. Stanza 2 names the desire to know Him more, to be found in Him and known as His, and to possess by faith the gift of righteousness that could not be earned. Stanza 3 names the prayer to know the power of the risen life and to know Him in His sufferings, to become like Him in His death, so as to live with Him and never die. The chorus returns.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

Do not drop stanza 1. “All I once held dear, built my life upon / All this world reveres, and wars to own / All I once thought gain I have counted loss / Spent and worthless now, compared to this” is the exchange of Philippians 3. Without it the chorus is true affection that has not named the counting of all as loss.

Do not drop stanza 3. “Oh, to know the power of your risen life / And to know You in Your sufferings / To become like you in your death, my Lord / So with you to live and never die” is the power of the resurrection and the fellowship of sufferings. Without it the knowing of Jesus has not named the rising or the death.

No line is false.

## Persons of the Trinity named

Jesus is addressed as the One to be known, the all, the joy, and the righteousness. The Father and the Spirit are unnamed. The work is the Son’s.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Not the whole song. Stanza 1 names the counting of all as loss compared to knowing Jesus. Stanza 2 names the gift of righteousness possessed by faith. Stanza 3 names the risen life and the sufferings. A vague theist could not sing those lines as written.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

**Unbiblical.** None.

**Non-biblical.** None.

**Thin.** Substitution, the blood, and the burial are not named. The lyric states the counting of all as loss, the gift of righteousness by faith, and the power of the risen life. It assumes the finished work rather than stating the cross at length.

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 4

“All I once held dear, built my life upon / All this world reveres, and wars to own / All I once thought gain I have counted loss / Spent and worthless now, compared to this” is Philippians 3:7–8. The exchange is the ordinary sense of those verses.

“Now my heart’s desire is to know you more / To be found in you and known as yours / To possess by faith what I could not earn / All-surpassing gift of righteousness” is Philippians 3:9–10. The righteousness by faith is the ordinary sense of those verses.

“Oh, to know the power of your risen life / And to know You in Your sufferings / To become like you in your death, my Lord / So with you to live and never die” is Philippians 3:10–11. The power of the resurrection and the fellowship of sufferings are the ordinary sense of those verses.

The lines use the ordinary sense of Philippians 3. The score stays at 4 because the finished work that grounds the knowing is assumed rather than stated at length.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 4

Christ is named as Jesus, the all, the joy, and the righteousness. The risen life is named. The sufferings and the death are named as the place of knowing Him. The cross is not stated as substitution or the blood. The work is not left as a title. It is not stated at the length of a full passion hymn.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 3

The main claim is the singer’s counting of all as loss and the desire to know Jesus more. That is the confession of Philippians 3. The chorus is affection: “You’re my all, you’re the best / You’re my joy, my righteousness / And I love you, Lord.” The first-person lines are the singer’s testimony. They are not a high-claim vow that must be meant before the song may be sung, but they are the singer’s desire.

The score stays at 3 because the voice is the singer’s desire and affection rather than pure declaration about God alone.

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 4

The stanzas are clear. The chorus is short. The words are ordinary. The church has already sung it. The people learn the exchange of Philippians 3 and the desire to know Christ in the power of the resurrection and the fellowship of sufferings. The affection in the chorus is the line a leader may need to frame. Keys on file include C.

If time is short, keep stanza 1 and stanza 3.

---

## Tone

The tone is sober and resolved. “All I once thought gain I have counted loss” and “to become like you in your death” name the cost without drama. The answer is knowing Jesus.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names the Father or the Spirit, the blood, the burial, or substitution in those words. It never says “in our place.” It never names the cross beyond the sufferings and the death.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Philippians 3, or any text on counting all as loss and knowing Christ. It works after the sermon has already named the finished work that makes the knowing possible.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The cross as atonement and the blood when the day’s text is the passion. Pair with a song that states substitution before or after this one.
- **Diet sentence.** It adds the counting of all as loss compared to knowing Jesus, the gift of righteousness by faith, and the power of the risen life. It does not add the cross narrative at length.
- **What not to do.** Do not use it as the only gospel song in the set. Do not drop stanza 1 or stanza 3. Frame the desire when the room needs the reminder that the knowing rests on the exchange.
