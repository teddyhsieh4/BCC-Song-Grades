## Thesis

The church asks not for itself but for God’s renown. Because the cross has saved, it prays that Jesus Christ be known wherever it is, that the kingdom come and the will be done so that everyone might know His Name, and that by grace it will preach the gospel till its dying breath.

---

## Overall recommendation

Strong

Use it regularly as a corporate prayer for the kingdom and the advance of the gospel. The first verse is the one that must stay: the cross has saved, so the prayer is for renown rather than for self. The second verse supplies the courage to speak and the preaching of the gospel. Pair it with a denser Christological or substitution song if the service has not already named the finished work more fully.

## Voice and addressee

Corporate throughout (“our hearts,” “we ask,” “us”). It is prayer addressed to God, based on the Lord’s Prayer and the Valley of Vision prayer “God’s Cause.”

---

## How the text moves

It starts with God’s glorious cause engaging the hearts of the church, the desire that Jesus Christ be known wherever they are, and the refusal to ask for themselves. The ground of the prayer is that the cross has saved. The chorus asks that the kingdom come and the will be done so that everyone might know the Name, that the song be heard everywhere, till the sovereign work is done. The second verse asks for strength and courage to speak, for wondrous deeds through the weak, and for grace to preach the gospel till the dying breath.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“We ask not for ourselves, but for Your renown / The cross has saved us so we pray / Your kingdom come.”

Without that line the prayer loses its gospel ground and its God-centered aim. The second verse’s “By grace we’ll preach Your gospel” is also required if the song is to name the mission.

## Persons of the Trinity named

God is addressed. Jesus Christ is named. The Spirit is not named. The prayer stays within the God whose cause, Name, and kingdom are in view, and the Jesus Christ who is to be known.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

A theist who prays for a higher purpose could sing parts of the chorus. The lines that require the cross that has saved and the name of Jesus Christ keep the whole song from being usable by a vague theist.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** none.
- **Thin:** the mechanism of the cross. “The cross has saved us” is true and necessary, but the lyric does not unfold substitution, blood, or resurrection. The prayer is for the advance of what the cross has already accomplished.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 4

“Let Your kingdom come / Let Your will be done” is Matthew 6:10. “The cross has saved us” is the ordinary sense of 1 Corinthians 1:18 and Galatians 6:14. “By grace we’ll preach Your gospel” is the mission of Matthew 28 and the grace of Ephesians 2. The lines stay inside the texts. The score is 4 rather than 5 because the cross is named without being unfolded.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 4

Jesus Christ is named. The cross is named as the ground of the prayer. The gospel is named as what is to be preached. The song does not stop at titles. It does not preach the finished work in detail, which is why the score is 4 rather than 5.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 5

The lyric is corporate prayer. It declares the cause of God, the fact that the cross has saved, and the desire that the Name be known. There are no private vows or reports of the singer’s inner weather. “Engages our hearts” is the engagement of the church with the cause, not the substance of the song.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 4

God has a glorious cause. His renown is the aim of the prayer. His Name is to be known. His will is to be done. His sovereign work on earth will be done. He gives strength. He performs wondrous deeds through the weak. The attributes are specific and tied to the kingdom and the gospel rather than listed generically. Holiness and justice are not in view beyond the cross that has saved.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The language is plain. The prayer is corporate. The congregation can ask together for the kingdom, the will, the Name, and the advance of the gospel. Singability is high, and the teaching is real, so the score is 5.

---

## Tone

Reverent and steady. It does not ask the room to confer on God anything He does not already possess. The intensity stays inside the prayer for the kingdom and the Name.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never unfolds the cross (blood, substitution, resurrection) or names the Spirit. Those confessions have to come from the rest of the service. The cross is named as the ground of the prayer.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Matthew 6:10, any text on the kingdom, the advance of the gospel, or the mission of the church. Useful as a sending or response after a sermon that has already named the finished work.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The details of the cross and the name of the Spirit if the service has not already supplied them.
- **Diet sentence.** It adds a clear corporate prayer that the kingdom come because the cross has saved, and that the gospel be preached. It does not add a full narrative of the finished work.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop the line that the cross has saved. Do not let the chorus stand alone without the renown and the gospel ground.

## Why this overall label

The lyric is a corporate prayer grounded in the cross that has saved, aimed at the renown of God and the knowledge of Jesus Christ, and committed to preaching the gospel. That is enough for regular use. The compact naming of the cross keeps it at Strong rather than Excellent / Prefer. Keep the first verse and supply a fuller gospel confession elsewhere if the service needs it.
