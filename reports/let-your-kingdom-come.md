## Thesis

The church asks not for itself but for God’s renown. Because the cross has saved, it prays that Jesus Christ be known wherever it is, that the kingdom come and the will be done so that everyone might know His Name, and that by grace it will preach the gospel till its dying breath.

---

## Overall recommendation

Strong

Use it regularly as the church’s prayer for the kingdom and the spread of the gospel. Keep the first verse, which grounds the prayer in the cross and aims it at God’s renown rather than the church’s own. The second verse asks for courage to speak and grace to preach. When the service has not named the finished work in full, pair it with a song that does. The church has sung it 9 times across at least four years of the 2023–2026 window.

## Voice and addressee

Corporate throughout (“our hearts,” “we ask,” “us”), and addressed to God as prayer. It draws on the Lord’s Prayer and the Valley of Vision prayer “God’s Cause.”

---

## How the text moves

The first verse asks that Jesus Christ be known wherever the church is, on the ground that the cross has saved. The chorus prays the Lord’s Prayer outward: kingdom come, will done, until all know His Name. The second verse asks for courage, for God’s power through weak people, and for grace to preach the gospel to the end.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“We ask not for ourselves, but for Your renown / The cross has saved us so we pray / Your kingdom come.”

Without this line the prayer loses both its gospel ground and its God-centered aim. “By grace we’ll preach Your gospel” in the second verse must stay as well, or the mission is never named.

## Persons of the Trinity named

God is addressed and Jesus Christ is named as the one to be known. The Spirit is not named.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Partly. Someone praying for a higher purpose could sing parts of the chorus. The cross that has saved and the name of Jesus Christ are what a vague theist would have to change.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** none.
- **Thin:** what happened at the cross. “The cross has saved us” is true and necessary, but the lyric does not name substitution, the blood, or the resurrection. The prayer asks for the spread of what the cross has already won.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 4

“Let Your kingdom come / Let Your will be done” is Matthew 6:10. “The cross has saved us” is 1 Corinthians 1:18 and Galatians 6:14. “By grace we’ll preach Your gospel” joins the commission of Matthew 28 to the grace of Ephesians 2. The score is 4 and not 5 because the cross is named but never explained.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 4

Jesus Christ is named, the cross is the ground of the prayer, and the gospel is what the church asks grace to preach. The finished work itself is not described, which is why this is a 4.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 5

The lyric is corporate prayer. It declares God’s cause, the saving cross, and the desire that His Name be known. “Engages our hearts” describes the church taking up God’s cause, and no line reports private feeling.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 4

God has a glorious cause, and His renown, His Name, and His will are the aim of the prayer. He gives strength, does wondrous deeds through the weak, and will finish His sovereign work on earth. Holiness and justice are not in view beyond the cross, so this is a 4.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The words are plain and the prayer is corporate. A congregation that sings it learns to pray the Lord’s Prayer for the spread of the gospel, which is why it earns a 5.

---

## Tone

Reverent and eager, the tone of a church asking to be used.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names the blood, substitution, the resurrection, or the Spirit. When the service needs them, pair it with Christ Our Hope in Life and Death.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Matthew 6:10, or any text on the kingdom, the spread of the gospel, or the church’s mission. It works well as a sending song after a sermon that has named the finished work.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** What the cross accomplished, and the Spirit, if the service has not already named them.
- **Diet sentence.** Adds a corporate prayer that the kingdom come because the cross has saved, and that the gospel be preached. It does not add an account of the finished work.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop the line that the cross has saved, and do not let the chorus stand without it.
