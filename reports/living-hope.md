## Thesis

A chasm stood between the sinner and God. The God of ages stepped down, wore the sinner’s sin, went to the cross, and rose. Jesus Christ is the living hope of the people He set free.

---

## Overall recommendation

**Avoid**

Do not use it. The lyric is not the reason. Brian Johnson is a co-writer, Bethel Music Publishing owns half the song, and this church’s rule is already written: a strong association with Bethel is automatic Avoid. The ministries on that short list are never to be used.

The church has sung it for at least four years (12 logs in the 2023–2026 snapshot). That history is why the row is in the catalog. It is not a reason to keep the title in the rotation. Replace it with a resurrection-and-hope text that does not carry this credit.

The scores below grade the words on their own, so the difference between the text and the ban is visible. The overall label does not follow those scores. The Bethel credit is why the label is Avoid.

## Voice and addressee

First person singular addressing the Lord, then naming Jesus in the third person as “my living hope.” The chorus is praise to “the One who set me free.” The congregation sings a personal rescue story in unison. There is no “we.”

---

## How the text moves

Three verses and a chorus.

1. **The distance and the cry.** Chasm, unclimbable mountain, desperation, a name spoken into the night, loving-kindness tearing through, the work finished, Jesus Christ my living hope.
2. **The descent and the cross.** Mercy, grace, God of ages stepped down from glory, wore my sin, bore my shame, the cross has spoken, I am forgiven, the King calls me His own.
3. **The morning.** Buried body began to breathe. The Roaring Lion declared the grave has no claim. “Jesus, Yours is the victory.”

The song starts with the sinner’s inability and ends with the resurrection. The chorus stays in the middle: set free, death lost its grip, chains broken, salvation in His name. The argument does move.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

On the words alone, verse 2 and verse 3 are the stanzas the grade would depend on: incarnation-as-descent, “wear my sin,” the cross, forgiveness, the buried body breathing.

The association question does not have a stanza you can cut. The credit applies to the whole title.

**The lines that need a careful ear** are “spoke Your name into the night” and “wear my sin.” See the accuracy section.

## Persons of the Trinity named

Jesus Christ is named repeatedly. “God of ages” is the One who stepped down, which the lyric treats as the Son. The Father and the Spirit are unnamed.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Not the whole song. Verse 2 names the descent, sin worn, shame borne, the cross, forgiveness. Verse 3 names a buried body that began to breathe. The chorus names salvation in Jesus’ name.

Verse 1 is softer. A desperate theist could sing a chasm, a mountain, and a name spoken into the night. That is one more reason the later verses matter for the words. It does not change the Bethel credit.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

**Unbiblical.** Nothing in the lyric contradicts Scripture.

**Non-biblical.** “Tore through the shadows of my soul” is mood language. “Spoke Your name into the night” can be prayer (Psalm 50:15). It can also sound like a technique: say the Name and the chasm closes. The surrounding lines (“the work is finished”) pull it back toward the finished work. Isolated, it is a soft invitation hook.

**Thin.** Wrath is unnamed. “Wear my sin” is a clothing image for what 2 Corinthians 5:21 and Isaiah 53 say with “bore” and “made to be sin.” The image is close. It is also a little prettier than those texts.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 4

**Verse 1.** “How great the chasm that lay between us” is Luke 16:26 as a picture of the distance sin makes, and Isaiah 59:2. “How high the mountain I could not climb” is inability (Psalm 49:7–9; Ephesians 2:1). “In desperation I turned to heaven / and spoke Your name into the night” is prayer, and it is the weakest sentence in the verse. Romans 10:13 is “call on the name of the Lord.” The lyric’s night-and-name picture is one step toward the seeker hook. “The work is finished, the end is written” is John 19:30 and Revelation 21:6. “Jesus Christ, my living hope” is 1 Peter 1:3.

**Verse 2.** “The God of ages stepped down from glory” is Philippians 2:6–7 and Micah 5:2. “To wear my sin and bear my shame” is Isaiah 53:4–6 and 2 Corinthians 5:21, in a softer verb. “The cross has spoken, I am forgiven” is Ephesians 1:7 and Colossians 1:14. “The King of kings calls me His own” is John 10:3 and Revelation 19:16.

**Verse 3.** “Your buried body began to breathe” is 1 Corinthians 15:4 and Luke 24:5–7. “Out of the silence, the Roaring Lion / declared the grave has no claim on me” is Revelation 5:5 next to 1 Corinthians 15:54–57 and Romans 6:9. “Jesus, Yours is the victory” is 1 Corinthians 15:57.

The main claims are sound. The category is a 4 because the verb is “wear,” where Isaiah 53 and 2 Corinthians 5:21 usually say bear or make-to-be-sin, and because “spoke Your name into the night” is impressionistic next to the rest.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 4

On the words alone this is a gospel narrative: inability, descent, sin, shame, cross, forgiveness, burial, resurrection, hope. Jesus is named as the hope, not only as a title.

On the words alone, substitution is implied, wrath is unnamed, and the chorus uses freedom-and-chains language while the cross language is already in verse 2. “You have broken every chain / there’s salvation in Your name” is true (Acts 4:12; Acts 12 imagery) and thinner than verse 2.

None of that reaches the association rule. The Avoid is the credit line.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 3

Verses 2 and 3 declare works of God. Verse 1 and the chorus report the singer’s rescue as experience: desperation, shadows of my soul, set me free, death has lost its grip on me, I’m Yours forever.

That mix is why the category is a 3. The “I” is a conversion “I,” which Scripture uses. It is also the voice that makes the song feel like a testimony single. “Beautiful Savior, I’m Yours forever” is belonging language, not romance, and it still sits on the singer’s status more than on the Lord’s character.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 3

Named: living hope, loving-kindness, mercy, boundless grace, God of ages, King of kings, Savior, Roaring Lion, the One who forgives and raises the dead.

That list is real. Holiness, justice, and wrath are missing. Love appears as loving-kindness in verse 1 and is not defined there by the cross. The cross arrives in the next verse, which helps. The portrait is still weighted toward rescue-kindness and victory. For a resurrection hymn that is understandable. For this church’s character score it stays a 3.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 4

The words are clear and already known (at least four years in the snapshot). Keys on file are often B. As a piece of congregational writing the lyric would be usable. The category grades the text’s usefulness. It cannot override a ban on the title.

If the song were ever sung, verse 2 and verse 3 would have to stay, and the chain-breaking chorus would have to be kept from eating the hour. That advice is now moot. The title should come out of the rotation.

---

## Tone

Earnest, rising, built for a last chorus. Not sensual. Not cute. The risk in the words is ungrounded triumph: “broken every chain” sung past the point where verse 2’s cross is still in view. That is a tone problem in many rooms. It is not the reason for Avoid.

---

## What the congregation never says

They never say wrath, “in my place,” or justification.  
They never name the Father or the Spirit.  
They never say “we.”  
They never name repentance as a word.

Those gaps would have been pairing notes. They are not the ban.

---

## Setlist notes

**When it serves the sermon.** It should not be scheduled.

**Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The whole title is the gap. Use a different resurrection-and-hope text from the catalog when the sermon is 1 Peter 1, 1 Corinthians 15, or the empty tomb.

**What it adds to the year’s diet.** It should add nothing going forward. What it has added historically is a popular Easter chorus with a Bethel credit.

**What not to do.** Do not keep it because the words are better than the source. Do not keep it as an instrumental. Do not treat a Wickham-only recording as a different song. The writers do not change with the artist on the track.
