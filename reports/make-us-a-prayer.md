## Thesis

The singer asks to be made a prayer, humbled so that God will draw near, and set free to stand as a house of prayer for the land. The second stanza says the same things in the plural.

---

## Overall recommendation

Weak

Do not feature it. One log, in 2024, is the record in this window. “House of prayer” is a biblical phrase for God’s house. Here it is the singer, and then the people, crying out for “this land.” Christ is never named. Sin is never named. “Humble my heart so that You may draw nigh” makes God’s nearness wait on the singer’s humility. If it is used at all, use the plural stanza, after Isaiah 56 or Matthew 21 has been read, and let another song say the cross.

---

## Voice and addressee

The first stanza is “me,” speaking to God (“You”). The second stanza is “us,” the same prayer in the plural. Nobody is named. The church is asking to be made into the prayer.

---

## How the text moves

It starts with one person: make me a prayer, humble my heart, cause me to stand for this land, a house of prayer. It then repeats the prayer for the congregation. The plural stanza does not add a doctrine. It widens the same ask from “me” to “us.”

---

## Lines you cannot cut

There is no gospel line to protect and no line that is false on its own. “To stand as a house of prayer” is the phrase with a text under it. If that line is dropped, the song is only “make us a prayer,” humility, and “this land.” Keeping the house-of-prayer line does not bring Christ into the song. No single line would change the label.

## Persons of the Trinity named

No person is named. “You” is the one who draws nigh and who can set the singer free. Father, Son, and Spirit are all unspoken.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Yes. A worshiper who prays to God and wants a humbler heart and a better land can sing every line. Nothing in the text requires Jesus. “Set free” does not say set free from what. “House of prayer” can be heard as a sincere religious life.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** none.
- **Thin:** “Make me a prayer / Let my prayer be a cry.” The cry is not given a content. “Cause me to stand / And cry out for this land” does not say what should be asked for the land. “One who’s set free” does not say what the freedom is from. “Humble my heart so that You may draw nigh” uses the order of James 4:8 and leaves out the cleansing and the double-mindedness in that verse.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 3

“To stand as a house of prayer” takes the phrase of Isaiah 56:7, “my house shall be called a house of prayer for all peoples,” which Jesus quotes in Matthew 21:13 while he drives the sellers out of the temple. In those texts the house is God’s house. In the song the house is the singer, and then the people, standing and crying for “this land.” The phrase is biblical. The referent has moved.

“Humble my heart so that You may draw nigh” is near James 4:8, “Draw near to God, and he will draw near to you.” James 4:8-10 also says “Cleanse your hands, you sinners,” and “Humble yourselves before the Lord, and he will exalt you.” The song keeps the drawing near and the humility. It does not call the singer a sinner, and it puts God’s nearness after the heart has been humbled, as the result the singer can secure by the prayer. That is a 3. The words are recognizable. The temple saying and the James passage are both shortened.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 2

Christ is not in the song. There is no incarnation, no cross, no resurrection, and no mediator. “Set free” could be a gospel word. Here it has no object and no Savior. The prayer never says in whose name the people stand. The song never gives Christ a name, and it never gives him a work.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 2

The song is a request about the singers. “Make me a prayer,” “Humble my heart,” “Cause me to stand,” “Cause me to be.” The plural stanza changes the pronouns and not the kind of claim. God is asked to act on the heart so that the people can cry out. There is no declaration of what God has done. The first-person lines are the whole text.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 2

God is the one who can draw near, who can humble a heart, and who can set a people free. None of those acts is described. Holiness, justice, mercy, wrath, and faithfulness are unnamed. “This land” is the object of the cry. The song says almost nothing about who God is.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 3

The second stanza gives the church “us,” which the first stanza does not. The words are short. One use, in 2024, means it is not a known text. What the room learns is that they should want to be a praying people for the land. They do not learn who hears them, or the gospel that gives them access. “This land” is undefined for a congregation in Irvine. It can mean the nation, the city, or a feeling about the culture. The plural stanza is the one to sing if the song is used. It still does not teach Christ.

---

## Tone

The tone is earnest petition. The line that sets it is “Let my prayer be a cry” and “Humble my heart so that You may draw nigh.” It is solemn. It is not playful, and it is not addressed to Christ.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names the Father, the Son, the Spirit, sin, the cross, the resurrection, or the content of the prayer for the land.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Isaiah 56:7 or Matthew 21:12-13, after the reading has said whose house the phrase belongs to. James 4, if the sermon has already said “you sinners.”
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** Christ and the cross. Pair it with *O Great God* when the humility needs sin and grace, and with *Let Your Kingdom Come* when “this land” needs the kingdom prayer.
- **Diet sentence.** Once in 2024, it adds a short consecration to prayer. It never adds the name of Jesus or the gospel.
- **What not to do.** Do not treat “house of prayer” as a slogan for the band. Do not drop the plural stanza and leave the church singing only “make me.” Do not explain “draw nigh” as if humility were the ground of access.

## Why this overall label

“House of prayer” is a real phrase, and a prayer for humility is a right thing to ask. The song never says who God is, never names Christ, and never says what the people are praying for, except “this land.” That is too thin to feature.
