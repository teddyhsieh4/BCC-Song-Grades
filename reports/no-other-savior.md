## Thesis

Jesus is Lord of every age, author of faith, the first and the last, Prince of Peace, and high priest at God’s right hand. He was sacrificed, breathed his last for his people, died to save, overcame the grave, and will reign forever, and there is no other Savior.

---

## Overall recommendation

Strong

Use it regularly when the sermon is on the person and work of Christ, and keep the second stanza. Five logs, in 2023, 2024, and 2026, means the room has already met it. “Sacrificed to death / For us, Your final breath / You died, the world to save / To overcome the grave” is the stanza that has to stay. “You died, the world to save” is broad. The line before it says the death was “for us.” Sin and wrath are not named. The song can still teach the cross, the grave, the priesthood, and the reign.

## Voice and addressee

The church speaks to Christ (“Your,” “You”). “How great You are” is praise addressed to him. The chorus states the conclusion to him and about him: there is no other Savior. There is no private “I.”

---

## How the text moves

It begins with titles of dignity: Lord of ages, author of faith, first and last, crowned Prince of Peace, throned at the right hand with the world at his command. The chorus says he is the Lamb and that there is no other Savior, and that every knee bows. The second stanza comes down to mercy, meekness, and weakness, and then to the sacrifice, the last breath for us, the death that saves, and the grave overcome. The bridge stays on the reign. The second stanza is what keeps the titles from being only titles.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“Sacrificed to death / For us, Your final breath / You died, the world to save / To overcome the grave” is the stanza the grade depends on. The chorus, “Jesus, Lamb of God / There is no other Savior,” is true and, without that stanza, does not say what the Lamb did. “Generous and meek / Protector of the weak” can be dropped and the cross remains. No line is false. Losing the sacrifice stanza would drop the song into a list of titles and a hail. That stanza has to stay.

## Persons of the Trinity named

Jesus is named, and he is the Lamb, the high priest, and the one throned at God’s right hand. “God” in that line is the Father’s place in Psalm 110:1 and Mark 16:19, though the song does not say “Father.” The Spirit is unnamed.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. “Jesus, Lamb of God,” “no other Savior,” “Sacrificed to death,” and “overcame the grave” cannot be transferred. The opening titles are already Christian. A singer who will not confess the death and the grave cannot honestly sing the second stanza, which is where the song says what the Savior did.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** none.
- **Thin:** “Generous and meek / Protector of the weak” and “Lover of the least.” These are true of Jesus and are said without a scene. “How great You are” adds size and not a doctrine. “At your renown” adds a word Philippians 2:10 does not have.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 4

“Author of our faith” is Hebrews 12:2. The ESV says “the founder and perfecter of our faith.” The song’s “Author” is the older wording of that office. “The First, the Last, the same” joins Revelation 22:13, “the first and the last,” with Hebrews 13:8, “the same yesterday and today and forever.” “Glorious Prince of Peace” is Isaiah 9:6. “Throned at God’s right hand” is Mark 16:19, “sat down at the right hand of God,” and Psalm 110:1. “The world at Your command” says what that session means for his rule.

“Merciful High Priest” is Hebrews 4:14-16. “Sacrificed to death / For us, Your final breath” says a death in the place of “us.” “You died, the world to save” is broad in the way John 3:16 and 1 John 2:2 speak of the world. It does not say that every person is saved, and it does not name the sin. The previous line’s “for us” is the substitution. “To overcome the grave” says the resurrection as victory over death. “Every knee bows down” is Philippians 2:10. “At your renown” is the song’s addition. “You will reign forever” agrees with the reign those texts look toward. The second stanza is Scripture-shaped. Several opening lines are titles stacked in a list.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 4

Christ is Lamb, Savior, high priest, and the one who was sacrificed, who died, who overcame the grave, and who reigns. The finished work is in the second stanza. Sin is not named, wrath is not named, and the cross is not the word the song uses. “For us, Your final breath” and “overcame the grave” are enough to teach the death and the resurrection in short lines. They are not a full telling. That is a 4.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

The song declares who Christ is and what he did. “How great You are” is praise, and it is attached to “Lamb of God” and “no other Savior.” There is no “I” and no vow about the singer’s love or effort. “Every knee bows down” is the world’s future confession. The meekness and the care for the weak are still claims about him. The song stays on Christ.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 4

The list is long: Lord of every age, author of faith, first and last, majesty, Prince of Peace, high priest, mercy, meekness, care for the least and the weak, the Lamb, the Savior, the one who reigns. Holiness and wrath are unnamed. Justice is present as the world under his command and every knee bowing. Mercy is named on the high priest, which is the right office for it. The gentler words, generous and meek, sit beside the sacrifice rather than replacing it, as long as the second stanza is sung.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 4

Five times across three years means many in the room have sung the chorus. The chorus is memorable. The second stanza is where they learn the death, the “for us,” and the grave. “Author of our faith” may need the reading of Hebrews 12:2 so “author” is not heard as a vague origin. “The world to save” should be left as the song says it, with “for us” still in the verse, and with the sermon naming the sin if the sermon’s text does. The F variants in the catalog are keys. “Saviour” on one variant is the same song.

---

## Tone

The tone is formal praise that comes down to a death. The line that sets the opening is “Crowned in majesty.” The line that sets the work is “For us, Your final breath.” The chorus returns to “How great You are” after that death. The greatness is tied to the Lamb when the stanza has been sung.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names sin, wrath, repentance, or the cross by that word, and it never says that he was buried.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Hebrews 4, Hebrews 12:2, Philippians 2:9-11, Isaiah 9:6, or the death and resurrection together. The second stanza belongs with the cross and the grave. The first stanza belongs with the session.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** Sin named as sin, and the wrath borne. Pair it with *Jesus, Your Mercy* when the sacrifice needs guilt and mercy in plainer words.
- **Diet sentence.** Five uses in three years adds a modern text that actually says the sacrifice, the grave, the priesthood, and the reign. It does not add a confession of sin or of wrath.
- **What not to do.** Do not live on the chorus and skip “Sacrificed to death.” Do not treat “the world to save” as a statement that every person is saved. Do not cut “for us” when the world line is kept.

## Why this overall label

The second stanza says he was sacrificed, that the last breath was for us, that he died to save, and that he overcame the grave, after the song has already seated him at God’s right hand as high priest. Sin and wrath are unnamed, and several lines are titles. The church can use this often if that sacrifice stanza is sung.
