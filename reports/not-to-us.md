## Thesis

The cross is before the singer and the world is behind, and the glory is for God’s name. The church does not receive that glory. Hearts open before the throne. Holy fire is asked onto the offering. Earth, waves, the universe, and the children are then said to be dancing and shouting, all for him.

---

## Overall recommendation

Weak

Do not feature it. It appears once in this window, in 2025. “Not to us, but to Your name be the glory” is the line that carries Psalm 115, and it has to stay if the song is sung at all. “The cross before me, the world behind” names the cross as the road in front of the singer and does not say what the cross did. “Send Your holy fire on this offering” and “Your children dancing” should not be the lines left ringing. Jesus is not named.

---

## Alternative song suggestions

The lyric is reaching for the glory of God’s name, a cross in front of the singer, and a life that is all for the Lord. Three of these the church already sings. One is not in the catalog.

**Already sung**

**All Glory Be to Christ** (Dustin Kensrue, modern hymn, CCLI 7004543). Logged 9 times across at least four years (2023, 2024, 2025, 2026). The glory is given to Christ, and the song stays with his kingdom and his worth.

**When I Survey the Wondrous Cross** (Isaac Watts, hymn, CCLI 27893). Logged once, in 2024. The cross is surveyed as the death on which the Prince of glory died, and the singer’s boast is that cross.

**Jesus, I My Cross Have Taken** (Henry F. Lyte, hymn, CCLI 3874967). Logged 6 times across at least four years (2023, 2024, 2025, 2026). The singer takes up the cross and leaves all to follow, and the hymn goes on to the Savior’s care.

**Not yet logged**

**Christ Is Mine Forevermore** (Jonny Robinson / Rich Thompson, CityAlight, CCLI 7036096). Not in the catalog. The life belongs to Christ, who has paid for every failing, and the heart’s treasure is Christ himself.

## Voice and addressee

The first lines are the singer: “the cross before me,” “It’s not for me, it’s all for You.” The refrain and the second verse move to “us” and “our hearts,” still addressing God as “You.” “Your children” are the church. The song speaks to God and keeps saying the praise is for him.

---

## How the text moves

It starts with the cross in front and the world behind, no turning back, a banner raised, and the claim that this is for God. Heavens shake, hands clap, and again it is all for him. The refrain gives the glory to his name. Verse 2 opens hearts before the throne and asks for holy fire on the offering so worship will burn for the world to see. The bridge then sets the earth shouting, the waves crashing, the sun raging, the universe singing, and the children dancing, all for him. The refrain does not change. The cross of the first line never becomes a death. The song moves from a vow, to the name, to fire and dancing.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“Not to us, but to Your name be the glory” is the line the grade depends on. It is Psalm 115:1 in the church’s mouth, and it is the line that has to stay if anything is sung. “The cross before me, the world behind” is the other line that mentions the cross, and it is a direction, the road in front of the singer. “Send Your holy fire on this offering” and “Your children dancing, dancing, dancing” are the lines that take over if the refrain is dropped. No line denies Psalm 115:1. Without the refrain, the song is a banner, a fire, and a dance.

## Persons of the Trinity named

“You,” “Your name,” and the throne are the address. Father, Son, and Spirit are unnamed. “Holy fire” does not say the Spirit. The cross is not said to be the Son’s death.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

The refrain can. “Not to us, but to Your name be the glory” can be sung by any worshiper who wants the honor to go to that worshiper’s god. “The cross before me, the world behind” stops a worshiper who will not have the cross in front. Jesus is still not named, and the cross is not said to be a death for sin. Someone who admires sacrifice as a path can sing the first lines without confessing the atonement.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** “Send Your holy fire on this offering / Let our worship burn for the world to see.” The line asks for fire on a human offering and for worship that burns as a display. “Your children dancing, dancing, dancing / It’s all for You” sets dancing as the act. The creation lines, earth shouting and waves crashing, are decoration around that claim.
- **Thin:** “The cross before me, the world behind / No turning back.” The cross is a direction. The line does not say who died or for whom.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 3

“Not to us, but to Your name be the glory” agrees with Psalm 115:1, “Not to us, O LORD, not to us, but to your name give glory, for the sake of your steadfast love and your faithfulness.” The song keeps the gift of glory to the name and does not sing the steadfast love and faithfulness that the psalm gives as the reason.

“The cross before me, the world behind” uses the picture of a road. Luke 9:23 says, “If anyone would come after me, let him deny himself and take up his cross daily and follow me.” The song has the cross in front and the world behind, and it does not have Jesus speaking, or the daily cross, or the death he died. “Our hearts unfold before Your throne” is approach to the throne without a priest. “Send Your holy fire on this offering” cannot be tied to a verse in its ordinary sense. The refrain is the one line that is the text. The rest is vow, fire, and motion.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 2

Jesus is not named. The cross is in front of the singer and is not the atoning death. There is no blood, no sin, no burial, and no resurrection. The glory of the name is real and is not said to be the name of the Son. The song gives God the honor and leaves the gospel out.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 2

“Not to us, but to Your name be the glory” declares where the honor goes. That line is about God. Around it the song is the singers’ offering: the cross before me, no turning back, our hearts unfold, holy fire on this offering, my all for you, children dancing. “It’s all for You” is said again and again. The refrain is the objective center. The song spends more time on the offering and the movement.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 3

His name receives the glory. He has a throne before which hearts open. The heavens, the earth, the waves, and the sun are set in motion for him. Those are signs of his greatness. “Holy fire” is asked for and is not an attribute explained. Love, justice, wrath, mercy, and faithfulness are unnamed, though Psalm 115:1 itself names steadfast love and faithfulness as the reason for the glory. The song does not sing that reason. The portrait is the name, the throne, and the world’s noise.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 3

One use, in 2025, means the room has barely sung it in this window. The refrain is clear and worth knowing: the glory is for God’s name. A congregation that learns only the refrain has learned Psalm 115:1 without the psalm’s reason. A congregation that learns the whole song also learns to ask for holy fire and to treat dancing as the offering, and never hears the name of Jesus. The refrain can sit after the psalm is read. The rest of the lyric should not carry the hour.

---

## Tone

The tone is urgent and physical. The line that sets it is “Send Your holy fire on this offering,” and then “Your children dancing, dancing, dancing / It’s all for You.” The refrain is plainer and heavier than the bridge. The banner and the fire push. The glory line holds still.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names Jesus, never says that the cross was his death for sin, and never says that the glory of the name is for the sake of steadfast love and faithfulness.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Psalm 115:1, if the refrain is actually sung and the psalm’s reason has been read. It does not serve a sermon on the atonement.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The name of Christ and the death of the cross. Pair it with *When I Survey the Wondrous Cross* or *All Glory Be to Christ* so the glory and the cross are not left as a road and a dance.
- **Diet sentence.** Once in this window, it adds Psalm 115:1 in a short refrain. It never adds the gospel.
- **What not to do.** Do not loop “holy fire” or “dancing.” Do not drop “Not to us, but to Your name.” Do not treat “the cross before me” as a statement of the atonement.

## Why this overall label

The refrain tells the truth of Psalm 115:1, that the glory belongs to God’s name. The rest of the song puts a cross in front of the singer as a road, asks for holy fire, and sets the children dancing, and it never names Jesus or the atonement. Once in 2025 is enough. It should stay a short refrain after the psalm, if it is used at all.
