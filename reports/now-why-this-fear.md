## Thesis

Fear and unbelief are answered by the finished work: the Father put His spotless Son to grief, the debt is canceled at the cross, complete atonement is made, and no wrath remains.

---

Excellent / Prefer

## Voice and addressee

Mixed. The first verse asks “Now why this fear and unbelief?” and “Condemn me for that debt of sin.” The chorus addresses Jesus. The third verse speaks to the soul (“Be still my soul”). The claims are confessional and shareable. The congregation can sing the lines as true of every believer in Christ.

---

## How the text moves

It opens by questioning fear and unbelief: the Father has put His spotless Son to grief, and the righteous Judge will not condemn for a debt now canceled at the cross. The chorus places all trust in the blood by which Jesus has rescued through great love. The second verse states complete atonement, the debt fully paid by death, no wrath remaining, and shelter by saving grace and sprinkled blood. The bridge repeats the sweet sound of saving grace and “Christ died for me.” The third verse calls the soul to know the peace bought by the merits of the great High Priest, to rely on His precious blood, and not to fear banishment since Jesus sets free. The claim advances from the canceled debt at the cross, through complete atonement and the absence of remaining wrath, to the present liberty of the High Priest.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

The first verse (“Has not the Father put to grief / His spotless Son for us? / And will the righteous Judge of men / Condemn me for that debt of sin / Now canceled at the cross?”) and the second (“Complete atonement You have made / And by Your death have fully paid / The debt Your people owed / No wrath remains for us to face”). Drop the canceled debt, the complete atonement, or the absence of remaining wrath and the answer to fear loses its ground. The High Priest lines in the third verse should stay with them.

## Persons of the Trinity named

The Father is named as the one who put His spotless Son to grief and as the righteous Judge. Jesus is addressed as the one whose blood is trusted, who has rescued, who died, and who is the great High Priest. The Spirit is not named.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. The spotless Son put to grief, the debt canceled at the cross, complete atonement, no remaining wrath, and the great High Priest require the God of the gospel. A vague theist cannot sing those lines without correction.

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“Has not the Father put to grief / His spotless Son for us?” and “the debt of sin / Now canceled at the cross” follow the ordinary sense of the Father’s purpose at the cross and the cancellation of the debt (Isaiah 53:10, Colossians 2:14). “Complete atonement You have made / And by Your death have fully paid / The debt Your people owed / No wrath remains for us to face” follows the ordinary sense of propitiation and the finished work (Romans 3:25, 1 John 2:2, Romans 8:1). “The merits of your great high priest / Have bought your liberty” follows the ordinary sense of the present work of the High Priest (Hebrews 4:14–16, Hebrews 7:25). The ordinary sense of the texts is in view at each claim.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

Christ is the spotless Son put to grief, the one whose blood is trusted, the one who has made complete atonement, fully paid the debt, and left no wrath remaining. He is the great High Priest whose merits have bought liberty. The cross, the canceled debt, and the absence of remaining wrath are explicit. Substitution and propitiation are stated.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

The lines declare what the Father and the Son have done: the spotless Son put to grief, the debt canceled, complete atonement, no wrath remaining, and the High Priest who has bought liberty. First-person and address-to-the-soul language is confessional and shareable. “All my trust is in Your blood” is trust language that rests on the finished work rather than on inner weather.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 5

The Father is the righteous Judge who put His spotless Son to grief and will not condemn for a canceled debt. Jesus is the one who has made complete atonement, fully paid the debt, left no wrath remaining, and is the great High Priest whose merits have bought liberty. Holiness (spotless Son), justice (righteous Judge, wrath, canceled debt), mercy, and grace are named. The attributes are specific and balanced.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The diction is plain. The argument moves from the canceled debt at the cross, through complete atonement and the absence of remaining wrath, to the present liberty of the High Priest. The congregation has already sung it in multiple years. The confessional voice and the clear answer to fear make it useful for teaching assurance grounded in the finished work.

---

## Tone

Reverent. The language stays with the spotless Son put to grief, the canceled debt, the complete atonement, and the High Priest. It does not ask the room to confer on God what He already possesses.

---

## What the congregation never says

The empty tomb and the explicit naming of the Spirit are absent. Pair the song with texts or songs that name the resurrection when the sermon of the day requires them.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** The cross, propitiation, assurance (Romans 8:1), the High Priest (Hebrews), or any text that answers fear with the finished work.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** Explicit naming of the resurrection if the sermon requires the empty tomb.
- **Diet sentence.** Adds a dense answer to fear that names the canceled debt, the complete atonement, the absence of remaining wrath, and the High Priest who has bought liberty. It does not add a song that names the empty tomb.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop the first or second verse. Do not tag it with a chorus that flattens the canceled debt and the absence of remaining wrath into generic trust.
