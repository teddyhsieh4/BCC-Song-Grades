## Thesis

Fear and unbelief are answered by the finished work: the Father put His spotless Son to grief, the debt is canceled at the cross, complete atonement is made, and no wrath remains.

---

## Overall recommendation

Excellent / Prefer

Feature it freely on the cross, propitiation, assurance, or the High Priest. Sing all three verses: the first two state the atonement, and the third turns it into assurance for a fearful soul. The church has sung it 7 times across at least four years of the 2023–2026 window.

## Voice and addressee

The voice shifts. Verse 1 asks “Now why this fear and unbelief?” and “Condemn me for that debt of sin.” The chorus addresses Jesus, and verse 3 speaks to the soul (“Be still my soul”). Every believer in Christ can sing these lines as true of themselves.

---

## How the text moves

The hymn opens by questioning fear and answers it with the cross. Verse 2 states that the atonement is complete and no wrath remains. Verse 3 tells the soul to be still and rest in the High Priest who has bought its liberty, so the hymn ends in present assurance.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

The first verse (“Has not the Father put to grief / His spotless Son for us? / And will the righteous Judge of men / Condemn me for that debt of sin / Now canceled at the cross?”) and the second (“Complete atonement You have made / And by Your death have fully paid / The debt Your people owed / No wrath remains for us to face”). Without them, the hymn tells the soul not to fear without giving the reason. The High Priest lines in the third verse should stay with them.

## Persons of the Trinity named

The Father is named as the one who put His spotless Son to grief and as the righteous Judge. Jesus is addressed as the one whose blood is trusted, who has rescued, who died, and who is the great High Priest. The Spirit is not named.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. A vague theist could not sing “No wrath remains for us to face” or a spotless Son put to grief for us without changing the words.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** none that affect the claim.
- **Thin:** the resurrection is not named, though the High Priest of verse 3 is the risen Christ.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“Has not the Father put to grief / His spotless Son for us?” is Isaiah 53:10, and “the debt of sin / Now canceled at the cross” is Colossians 2:14. “Complete atonement You have made / And by Your death have fully paid / The debt Your people owed / No wrath remains for us to face” is propitiation and the finished work (Romans 3:25, 1 John 2:2, Romans 8:1). “The merits of your great high priest / Have bought your liberty” is the present work of the High Priest (Hebrews 4:14–16, Hebrews 7:25).

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

Christ is the spotless Son put to grief, the one whose blood is trusted, and the great High Priest. Substitution and propitiation are stated in plain words, and the hymn also names His present intercession, which few songs in the catalog do.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

The lines declare what the Father and the Son have done. The first person and the address to the soul are how the hymn applies that to a fearful believer. “All my trust is in Your blood” is trust that rests on the finished work. The score is 4 because so much of the hymn is spoken to the singer’s own soul.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 5

The Father is the righteous Judge who will not condemn for a canceled debt. Jesus has made complete atonement and is the High Priest whose merits buy liberty. Holiness, justice, mercy, and grace are all named.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The argument is easy to follow: a question about fear, then the answer from the cross, then rest. It teaches assurance from the finished work, which is something a fearful believer can take home.

---

## Tone

The tone is reverent and steady. Verse 3’s “Be still my soul” sets it: calm that comes from the paid debt.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names the empty tomb or the Spirit. When the sermon needs the resurrection, pair it with Behold Him, whose second verse names it.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** The cross, propitiation, assurance (Romans 8:1), the High Priest in Hebrews, or any text that answers fear with the finished work.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The resurrection, if the sermon needs the empty tomb.
- **Diet sentence.** Adds an answer to fear from the paid debt and a song that names the High Priest’s present work. For the empty tomb, the set needs another song.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop the first or second verse, and do not follow it with a chorus of generic trust that drops the paid debt.
