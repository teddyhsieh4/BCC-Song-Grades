## Thesis

A thousand tongues are not enough for the praise of the Redeemer whose name charms fear, breaks the power of canceled sin, and whose blood makes the foulest clean.

---

## Overall recommendation

Excellent / Prefer

Feature it freely on the name of Jesus, the power of His blood, or the triumphs of grace. Keep the third stanza (the name that charms our fears) and the fourth (the blood that breaks the power of canceled sin). The church has sung it 7 times across at least three years of the 2023–2026 window.

## Voice and addressee

First-person singular in the opening (“O for a thousand tongues to sing / My great Redeemer’s praise”) and in the address to God (“My gracious Master and my God”). Every believer in Christ can sing these lines as true of themselves.

---

## How the text moves

The hymn opens wishing for a thousand tongues to praise the Redeemer and asks God’s help to spread His name. The third stanza praises the name of Jesus, and the fourth tells what His blood does for sinners.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

The fourth stanza (“He breaks the power of canceled sin / He sets the prisoner free / His blood can make the foulest clean / His blood availed for me”) and the third (“Jesus! the name that charms our fears / That bids our sorrows cease / ’Tis music in the sinner’s ears / ’Tis life, and health, and peace”). Without the fourth, the praise has no gospel content.

## Persons of the Trinity named

Jesus is named as the Redeemer, the name that charms fears, the one whose blood makes clean, and the gracious Master. God is named as God and King. The Spirit is not named in the standard congregational stanzas.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. A vague theist could not sing “His blood can make the foulest clean / His blood availed for me” without changing it.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** the wish for a thousand tongues is ordinary poetry for praise. It does not change the claim.
- **Thin:** the short selection most churches sing does not name the empty tomb or the cross itself. Fuller Wesley texts include the voice that gives new life to the dead.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“Jesus! the name that charms our fears / That bids our sorrows cease / ’Tis music in the sinner’s ears / ’Tis life, and health, and peace” is the name of Jesus and the peace it brings (Acts 4:12, Romans 5:1, Philippians 2:9–10). “He breaks the power of canceled sin / He sets the prisoner free / His blood can make the foulest clean / His blood availed for me” is the blood that cleanses and the cancellation of the debt (Colossians 2:14, 1 John 1:7, Romans 6:14, Hebrews 9:14).

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

Christ is the Redeemer, the name that brings peace, and the one whose blood breaks the power of canceled sin and makes the foulest clean. “Canceled sin” states that the debt is gone, and “breaks the power” states that its hold is broken too.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

The hymn declares what the Redeemer’s name and blood accomplish. The first-person desire (“O for a thousand tongues”) and claim (“His blood availed for me”) rest on that work. The score is 4 because the hymn opens with the singer’s desire.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 5

God is Redeemer, King, and gracious Master. His name brings life, health, and peace, and His blood frees and cleanses. Mercy, power, and grace are all named.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

This is a classic hymn the congregation knows, with short, memorable lines. It teaches both the forgiveness of sin and freedom from its power.

---

## Tone

The tone is reverent and joyful. The joy is about the name of Jesus and what His blood has done.

---

## What the congregation never says

In the usual short selection, the congregation never names the empty tomb or calls to repentance. When the sermon needs the resurrection, pair it with Behold Him, whose second verse names it.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** The name of Jesus, the power of the blood, the triumphs of grace, or any text on freedom from sin.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The cross itself or the empty tomb, if the sermon needs them.
- **Diet sentence.** Adds a classic hymn on the name and the blood, with both forgiveness and freedom from sin’s power. In its short form it does not add the cross itself.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop the third or fourth stanza, and do not follow it with a chorus of general praise that drops canceled sin and the blood.
