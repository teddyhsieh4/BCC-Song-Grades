## Thesis

A thousand tongues are not enough for the praise of the Redeemer whose name charms fear, breaks the power of canceled sin, and whose blood makes the foulest clean.

---

## Overall recommendation

Excellent / Prefer

Feature it freely on the name of Jesus, the power of the blood, or the triumphs of grace. The third stanza names the name that charms fears and is life, health, and peace. The fourth names the blood that breaks the power of canceled sin, sets the prisoner free, and makes the foulest clean. It has already been logged in multiple years of the current window.

## Voice and addressee

First-person singular in the opening (“O for a thousand tongues to sing / My great Redeemer’s praise”) and in the address to God (“My gracious Master and my God”). The claims about the name and the blood are confessional and shareable. The congregation can sing the lines as true of every believer in Christ.

---

## How the text moves

It opens with the desire for a thousand tongues to sing the Redeemer’s praise, the glories of God and King, and the triumphs of His grace. The second stanza asks the gracious Master and God to assist in proclaiming the honors of His name through all the earth. The third stanza names Jesus as the name that charms fears, bids sorrows cease, is music in the sinner’s ears, and is life, health, and peace. The fourth stanza states that He breaks the power of canceled sin, sets the prisoner free, and that His blood can make the foulest clean and availed for the singer. Later stanzas (often included) name the voice that gives new life to the dead and the call to hear, see, and leap. The claim advances from the desire to praise, through the name that charms fear, to the blood that breaks the power of canceled sin and makes clean.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

The fourth stanza (“He breaks the power of canceled sin / He sets the prisoner free / His blood can make the foulest clean / His blood availed for me”) and the third (“Jesus! the name that charms our fears / That bids our sorrows cease / ’Tis music in the sinner’s ears / ’Tis life, and health, and peace”). Drop the canceled sin, the blood that makes clean, or the name that is life and peace and the praise loses its ground.

## Persons of the Trinity named

Jesus is named as the Redeemer, the name that charms fears, the one whose blood makes clean, and the gracious Master. God is named as God and King. The Spirit is not named in the standard congregational stanzas.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. The Redeemer’s praise, the name that is life and peace to the sinner, the blood that breaks the power of canceled sin and makes the foulest clean require the God of the gospel. A vague theist cannot sing those lines without correction.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** the desire for a thousand tongues is ordinary poetic desire for praise. It does not alter the claim.
- **Thin:** the empty tomb is not named in the standard short selection (fuller Wesley texts include the voice that gives new life to the dead). The blood, the canceled sin, and the clean foulest are stated; the means of the cross is left for other songs when the short text is used.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“Jesus! the name that charms our fears / That bids our sorrows cease / ’Tis music in the sinner’s ears / ’Tis life, and health, and peace” follows the ordinary sense of the name of Jesus and the peace it brings (Acts 4:12, Romans 5:1, Philippians 2:9–10). “He breaks the power of canceled sin / He sets the prisoner free / His blood can make the foulest clean / His blood availed for me” follows the ordinary sense of the blood that cleanses and the cancellation of the debt (Colossians 2:14, 1 John 1:7, Romans 6:14, Hebrews 9:14). The ordinary sense of the texts is in view at each claim.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

Christ is the Redeemer whose praise requires a thousand tongues, the name that charms fear and is life and peace, and the one whose blood breaks the power of canceled sin, sets the prisoner free, and makes the foulest clean. The blood and the canceled sin are explicit. The finished work is stated in the power broken and the clean foulest.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

The lines declare what the Redeemer has done and what His name and blood accomplish. First-person desire (“O for a thousand tongues”) and the personal claim (“His blood availed for me”) are confessional and shareable. They rest on the finished work rather than on inner weather.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 5

God is the Redeemer, the God and King whose glories and triumphs of grace are sung, the gracious Master whose name charms fear and is life, health, and peace, and the one whose blood breaks the power of canceled sin and makes the foulest clean. Mercy, power, and the triumph of grace are named. The attributes are specific.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The diction is plain. The argument moves from the desire to praise, through the name that charms fear, to the blood that breaks the power of canceled sin. The congregation has already sung it in multiple years. The confessional voice and the clear stanzas make it useful for teaching the name of Jesus and the power of the blood.

---

## Tone

Reverent. The language stays with the Redeemer’s praise, the name that charms fear, and the blood that makes clean. It does not ask the room to confer on God what He already possesses.

---

## What the congregation never says

The empty tomb is not named in the standard short selection. Explicit call to repentance is absent. Pair the song with texts or songs that name the means of the canceled sin when the sermon of the day requires them.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** The name of Jesus, the power of the blood, the triumphs of grace, or any text on the Redeemer who breaks the power of canceled sin.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** Explicit naming of the cross or the empty tomb if the sermon requires the means or the resurrection.
- **Diet sentence.** Adds a dense classic that names the Redeemer whose praise requires a thousand tongues, the name that is life and peace, and the blood that breaks the power of canceled sin and makes the foulest clean. It does not add a song that walks the substitutionary means of that blood when the short text is used.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop the third or fourth stanzas. Do not tag it with a chorus that flattens the canceled sin and the clean foulest into generic praise.
