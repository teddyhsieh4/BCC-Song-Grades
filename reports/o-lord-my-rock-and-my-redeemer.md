## Thesis

The Lord is the singer’s Rock and Redeemer, the greatest treasure of the longing soul, the strong defender, and the gracious Savior. Guilt and the cross were laid on His shoulders. In the singer’s place He suffered, bled, and died. He rose, the grave and death are conquered, and the bonds of sin and shame are broken.

---

## Overall recommendation

Excellent / Prefer

Feature it, and keep verse 3: it is where substitution and the resurrection are stated. Verses 1 and 2 present the Lord as treasure, defender, and refuge, and verse 3 shows why He is called Redeemer. The church has sung it 8 times across at least four years of the 2023–2026 window.

## Voice and addressee

First-person singular, addressing the Lord as Rock and Redeemer. It is testimony and prayer, and the whole congregation can own its confession of a ruined life redeemed.

---

## How the text moves

Verses 1 and 2 praise the Lord as the soul’s treasure and as defender in trouble. Verse 3 tells what He did for the ruined sinner, from the cross to the empty grave. The song closes by asking that all the singer’s days bring glory to His name.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“My guilt and cross laid on Your shoulders / In my place You suffered bled and died / You rose the grave and death are conquered / You broke my bonds of sin and shame.”

Without verse 3, “Rock and Redeemer” stay titles, and the song never says what the Redeemer did.

## Persons of the Trinity named

The Lord is named as Rock and Redeemer, gracious Savior. The Father and the Spirit are not named. The work of the Son is explicit.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. A vague theist could not sing “In my place You suffered bled and died” or “You rose the grave and death are conquered” without changing them.

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“O Lord, my Rock and my Redeemer” is Psalm 19:14. “My guilt and cross laid on Your shoulders / In my place You suffered bled and died” is Isaiah 53:4–6 and 1 Peter 2:24. “You rose the grave and death are conquered” is 1 Corinthians 15:55–57 and Hebrews 2:14. “You broke my bonds of sin and shame” is Romans 6:6–7 and Colossians 2:13–15. The images of rock, shield, and refuge in verses 1 and 2 are used as the Psalms use them.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

Verse 3 states substitution outright (“In my place You suffered bled and died”) and names the resurrection and freedom from sin. That is what turns “Rock” into “Redeemer” in this song.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

Verse 3 declares what the Lord has done. Verses 1 and 2 speak of Him through the singer’s experience: treasure, defender, song, hope, and joy. That experience rests on verse 3, but it is why the score is 4. There are no vows that need a qualifier.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 5

The Lord is Rock, Redeemer, treasure, defender, refuge, and gracious Savior. His grace is too deep to fathom, His love exceeds the heavens, His truth is perfect wisdom, and His faithfulness is a refuge. Holiness and justice appear in the guilt laid on His shoulders.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The singular voice is one the whole church can own, and verse 3 teaches substitution in plain words. Keep verse 3 so the congregation learns why He is called Redeemer.

---

## Tone

The tone is reverent and honest. Verse 2 names weariness, sorrow, and trial without dwelling on them, and verse 3 answers them with the cross.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names the Spirit or Christ’s present intercession. The main gospel elements are present.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Psalm 19:14, Isaiah 53, 1 Peter 2, 1 Corinthians 15, or any text on the Rock, the Redeemer, substitution, or the resurrection.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The Spirit and Christ’s present intercession, if the service has not already named them.
- **Diet sentence.** Adds a personal confession of substitution and resurrection under the old titles Rock and Redeemer. It adds little on the Spirit.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop verse 3, and do not let the titles stand alone without it.
