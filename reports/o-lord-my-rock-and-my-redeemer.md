## Thesis

The Lord is the singer’s Rock and Redeemer, the greatest treasure of the longing soul, the strong defender, and the gracious Savior. Guilt and the cross were laid on His shoulders. In the singer’s place He suffered, bled, and died. He rose, the grave and death are conquered, and the bonds of sin and shame are broken.

## Voice and addressee

First-person singular addressing the Lord as Rock and Redeemer. It is testimony and prayer. The congregation can own the confession of the ruined life and the request that all days bring glory to His name.

---

## How the text moves

It opens by addressing the Lord as Rock and Redeemer, the greatest treasure of the longing soul, and declares that true delight is found in Him alone. Grace is a well too deep to fathom, love exceeds the heavens’ reach, and truth is a fount of perfect wisdom. Verse 2 names Him as strong defender of the weary heart, sword and shield against the deceiver, song when enemies surround, hope when tides of sorrow rise, and joy when trials abound. Verse 3 names Him as gracious Savior of the ruined life: “My guilt and cross laid on Your shoulders / In my place You suffered bled and died / You rose the grave and death are conquered / You broke my bonds of sin and shame.” It closes with the request that all days bring glory to His name.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“My guilt and cross laid on Your shoulders / In my place You suffered bled and died / You rose the grave and death are conquered / You broke my bonds of sin and shame.”

Dropping verse 3 leaves the titles of Rock and Redeemer without the named substitution, the death in the singer’s place, the resurrection, and the broken bonds. Verses 1 and 2 still declare the treasure and the defender, but the gospel act that shows the Redeemer is in those lines.

## Persons of the Trinity named

The Lord is named as Rock and Redeemer, gracious Savior. The Father and the Spirit are not named. The work of the Son is explicit.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. The guilt and cross laid on His shoulders, the suffering and death in the singer’s place, the resurrection, and the broken bonds of sin and shame cannot be sung by a vague theist without correction. The titles of Rock and Redeemer are tied to that finished work in verse 3.

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“My guilt and cross laid on Your shoulders / In my place You suffered bled and died” is the ordinary sense of Isaiah 53:4–6 and 1 Peter 2:24. “You rose the grave and death are conquered” is 1 Corinthians 15:55–57 and Hebrews 2:14. “You broke my bonds of sin and shame” is Romans 6:6–7 and Colossians 2:13–15. “O Lord, my Rock and my Redeemer” is Psalm 19:14. The lines stay inside ordinary biblical sense. Verses 1 and 2 use biblical images of rock, shield, and refuge without leaving the ordinary sense of those texts.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

The Lord is named as gracious Savior of the ruined life. Substitution is stated: “In my place You suffered bled and died.” The resurrection is named. The bonds of sin and shame are broken. The song does not stop at titles. It preaches the finished work that makes the Rock a Redeemer.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

Verse 3 declares what the Lord has done. Verses 1 and 2 declare attributes and the singer’s experience of Him as treasure, defender, song, hope, and joy. Those claims are grounded in the finished work of verse 3, which is why the score is 4 rather than 5. The singer does not make high-claim present-tense vows without grounding. The prayer rests on what the Redeemer has already done.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 5

The Lord is Rock, Redeemer, greatest treasure, strong defender, sword, shield, song, hope, joy, refuge, and gracious Savior. Grace is a well too deep to fathom. Love exceeds the heavens’ reach. Truth is a fount of perfect wisdom. Faithfulness is a refuge. The attributes are specific and tied to the substitution, the death, and the resurrection. Holiness and justice appear as the guilt and cross laid on His shoulders.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The language is plain. Verse 3 teaches the ruined life, the substitution, the death, the resurrection, and the broken bonds. The congregation can learn the finished work by singing those lines. The singular voice is testimony and prayer the whole church can own. Singability is high, and the teaching is the reason for the 5. Keep verse 3 so the titles of Rock and Redeemer stay attached to the cross and the empty tomb.

---

## Tone

Reverent and honest. It does not ask the room to confer on the Lord what He has not already done. The intensity stays inside the substitution, the resurrection, and the broken bonds.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names the Spirit or the present intercession. Those are small gaps. The main gospel elements of the substitution, the death, the resurrection, and the broken bonds are present.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Psalm 19:14, Isaiah 53, 1 Peter 2, 1 Corinthians 15, any text on the Rock, the Redeemer, the substitution, or the resurrection. Especially useful after a sermon that has named the guilt and cross laid on His shoulders.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The Spirit and a fuller naming of present intercession if the service has not already supplied them.
- **Diet sentence.** It adds a dense confession that the Lord is Rock and Redeemer who suffered and died in the singer’s place, rose, and broke the bonds of sin and shame. It does not add a full narrative of the Spirit’s ongoing work.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop verse 3. Do not let the titles of Rock and Redeemer stand alone without the named substitution and resurrection.
