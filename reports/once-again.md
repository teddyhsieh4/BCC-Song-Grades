## Thesis

Jesus Christ became nothing and was poured out to death. The singer thinks upon the sacrifice and the gift of life, and is in that place once again. He looks upon the cross where Christ died, is humbled by the mercy, and is broken inside. He thanks and pours out his life. Christ is now exalted to the highest place as King of the heavens, where one day the singer will bow. For now he marvels at the saving grace and is full of praise. He thanks for the cross, calling Christ “my Friend.”

---

## Overall recommendation

**Acceptable with notes**

Use it sparingly, when the rest of the set supplies what the song lacks. The lyric names the sacrifice, the pouring out to death, and the cross where Christ died. It does not name sin, substitution, the blood, or the resurrection. The voice is the singer’s personal looking, humbling, breaking, thanking, and pouring out. The “my Friend” line is the language the room will need to frame.

The church has sung it for at least four years (6 logs in the 2023–2026 snapshot). That does not raise the grade.

## Voice and addressee

The song is first-person singular throughout. “I think upon Your sacrifice.” “I’m humbled by Your mercy.” “I pour out my life.” It addresses Jesus Christ. The voice is the singer’s personal response.

---

## How the text moves

Verse 1 names Jesus Christ becoming nothing and poured out to death, the wonder at the gift of life, and the return to that place. The chorus looks upon the cross where He died, names the humbling by mercy and the breaking inside, and thanks while pouring out the life. Verse 2 names the present exaltation as King of the heavens, the future bowing, and the present marvel at saving grace. The bridge thanks for the cross and calls Christ “my Friend.”

---

## Lines you cannot cut

Do not drop verse 1. “Jesus Christ, I think upon Your sacrifice, / You became nothing, poured out to death” is the emptying and the death. Without it the chorus is true looking at a cross that has not named the becoming nothing or the pouring out.

The “my Friend” line in the bridge is the one to watch. It is not false. It is the language that can flatten the distance between the King and the singer.

No line is false. The thinness and the personal voice are the limits.

## Persons of the Trinity named

Jesus Christ is addressed as the One who became nothing, was poured out, died on the cross, and is exalted as King. The Father and the Spirit are unnamed. The work is the Son’s.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Not the whole song. Verse 1 names the becoming nothing and the pouring out to death. The chorus names the cross where He died. A vague theist could not sing those lines as written. The “my Friend” line and the personal humbling and breaking are the lines a non-Christian could more easily sing.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

**Unbiblical.** None.

**Non-biblical.** None.

**Thin.** Sin, the blood, substitution in ordinary words, the burial, and the resurrection are not named. The lyric names the emptying, the pouring out to death, and the cross. It assumes the finished work rather than stating it at length. The personal breaking and the “my Friend” line keep the voice close to the singer.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 3

“Jesus Christ, I think upon Your sacrifice, / You became nothing, poured out to death” is Philippians 2:7–8. The emptying and the death are the ordinary sense of those verses.

“Once again I look upon / The cross where You died, / I’m humbled by Your mercy / And I’m broken inside” is 1 Corinthians 1:18 and Romans 12:1 in general terms. The looking at the cross and the humbling are true. They are not tied to a text in its ordinary sense beyond the cross itself.

“Now You are exalted to the highest place, / King of the heavens, where one day I’ll bow” is Philippians 2:9–11. The exaltation is the ordinary sense of those verses.

The lines use the ordinary sense of the emptying and the exaltation. The score stays at 3 because the gospel that grounds the looking is named rather than unfolded, and the personal response is the main movement.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 3

Christ is named as Jesus Christ who became nothing, was poured out to death, died on the cross, and is exalted as King. The cross is named. The blood, substitution, and the resurrection are not stated. Naming the cross and the pouring out without the fuller work is a 3.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 2

The main claim is the singer’s thinking upon the sacrifice, being in that place once again, looking upon the cross, being humbled and broken inside, thanking, and pouring out the life. The objective statements (became nothing, poured out to death, exalted) are brief. The lyric is dominated by the personal response.

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 3

The verses are clear. The chorus is short. The words are ordinary. The people learn that Christ became nothing, was poured out to death, and is exalted. They also learn the singer’s personal humbling, breaking, and pouring out. The “my Friend” line is the one a leader may need to frame. Keys on file include D and E.

---

## Tone

The tone is sober and personal. “I’m humbled by Your mercy and I’m broken inside” and “Thank You for the cross, my Friend” name the response without naming the cost in ordinary substitutionary words.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names sin, the blood, the burial, the resurrection, or substitution in those words. It never says “in our place.” It never names the Father or the Spirit.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Philippians 2, or any text on the emptying and the exaltation, after the sermon has already named the finished work. It does not carry the gospel by itself.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** Sin, the blood, substitution, and the resurrection. Pair with a song that states those before or after this one.
- **Diet sentence.** It adds the becoming nothing, the pouring out to death, the looking at the cross, and the present exaltation. It does not add the gospel narrative at length.
- **What not to do.** Do not feature it. Do not use it as the only gospel song in the set. Frame the “my Friend” line and the personal breaking when the room needs the reminder that the response rests on the cross.
