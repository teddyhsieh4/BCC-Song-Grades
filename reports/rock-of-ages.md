## Thesis

Christ is the Rock struck for sinners, and the singer hides in Him. The water and blood from His side are a double cure for sin, saving from wrath and making pure. No work, zeal, or tears can satisfy God's law or atone for sin. The sinner brings nothing, clings to the cross, and asks to be clothed and washed. In death and at the judgment, he has the same hope: the Rock of Ages, cleft for him.

---

## Overall recommendation

Excellent / Prefer

Feature it. The church has logged it 6 times across at least four years of the 2023–2026 window. In four short stanzas the hymn states the sinner's guilt, God's wrath, the law's demand, the failure of every human effort, the sufficiency of Christ's cross, and the hope of standing at the judgment in Him. "Nothing in my hand I bring, / Simply to Thy cross I cling" is grace alone in two lines. The doctrine of the Reformation is in plain words a child can sing.

## Voice and addressee

The hymn is a prayer of one sinner to Christ, in the first person singular throughout ("let me hide myself in Thee," "Wash me, Savior, or I die"). There is no "we." Each singer confesses for himself what the whole congregation is confessing.

---

## How the text moves

Stanza 1 asks to hide in Christ and names what His blood does: it saves from wrath and makes pure. Stanza 2 shuts every other door: works, zeal, and tears cannot atone. Stanza 3 is the sinner coming empty-handed, naked, helpless, and foul, to the cross. Stanza 4 carries that same plea through death to the judgment seat and ends where it began. The hymn moves from the cross to the last day, and the prayer does not change.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

Stanza 2, "Not the labors of my hands / Can fulfill Thy law's demands," is the stanza most often skipped and the one that makes the hymn Protestant. It rules out every ground of salvation but Christ. Stanza 3's "Nothing in my hand I bring, / Simply to Thy cross I cling" is the hymn's center. Stanza 4 sets the whole prayer before "Thy judgment throne," and without it the hymn loses its view of the last day. No line is false.

## Persons of the Trinity named

The Son is addressed throughout as "Rock of Ages," "Savior," and Judge. The Father and the Spirit are not named. The Father is implied in "Thy law's demands" and "wrath," but the hymn addresses Christ as the one whose law and wrath they are.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. Anyone who trusts his own merit would stop at stanza 2 or at "Nothing in my hand I bring." A non-Christian could not sing "Simply to Thy cross I cling." The hymn excludes every hope but Christ's cross.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** "Rock of Ages" is Isaiah 26:4, where the Hebrew is literally "the rock of ages." "Cleft for me" joins the rock Moses struck (Exodus 17:6; 1 Corinthians 10:4) to the cleft where God hid Moses (Exodus 33:22). The images are Scripture joined by the poet.
- **Thin:** none.

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

The hymn can be read line by line against Scripture. "Rock of Ages, cleft for me" is Isaiah 26:4 and 1 Corinthians 10:4, the rock that was Christ. "Let the water and the blood, / From Thy wounded side which flowed" is John 19:34 and 1 John 5:6. "Save from wrath" is Romans 5:9, and "make me pure" is 1 John 1:7. "Not the labors of my hands / Can fulfill Thy law's demands" is Romans 3:20 and Galatians 2:16. "All for sin could not atone; / Thou must save, and Thou alone" is Acts 4:12. "Naked, come to Thee for dress" is Isaiah 61:10 and Philippians 3:9, the righteousness from God. "Foul, I to the fountain fly" is Zechariah 13:1. "See Thee on Thy judgment throne" is 2 Corinthians 5:10 and Revelation 20:11.

The hymn is exact on justification by grace alone through faith alone in Christ alone, and it says so in words a congregation can remember.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

Every stanza is about Christ and His cross. The singer names his sin, God's wrath, and the law's demands, then turns from his own works to the blood and water from Christ's side. Substitution is stated in "cleft for me." Justification is pictured as Christ clothing the naked sinner. The resurrection is not sung. The hymn centers on the cross and its benefits and states them fully.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

The hymn is personal prayer, but every line of it is doctrine. The singer describes his state (naked, helpless, foul) and asks for what Christ provides. Nothing is about feelings or experience apart from that. It is a 4 because the whole hymn is in the first person singular as a prayer, rather than a declaration of truth to and with the congregation.

---

## Description of God's Character: 4

| Attribute | Line |
|---|---|
| Justice, wrath | "save from wrath," "Thy law's demands" |
| Holiness | "make me pure," the law that cannot be met |
| Grace | "look to Thee for grace" |
| Sufficiency to save | "Thou must save, and Thou alone" |
| Eternity, steadfastness | "Rock of Ages" |
| Judgment | "Thy judgment throne" |

Justice and grace are held together at the cross. The holiness of God's law and the certainty of His judgment give grace its weight. Love, faithfulness, and sovereignty are not named, and the attributes are all seen through Christ's saving work.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The congregation has known this hymn for generations, and the words are short and plain. In four stanzas the people confess their sin, reject their own works, cling to the cross, and look ahead to judgment without fear. A believer can carry "Nothing in my hand I bring" through life and into death. The hymn gives the congregation a great deal to remember in few words.

---

## Tone

The tone is humble and urgent. Stanza 3 is the most desperate, ending "Wash me, Savior, or I die." Stanza 4 is quieter and looks ahead. The hymn never becomes triumphant. Its confidence is the confidence of a sinner who has nowhere else to go.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never sings the incarnation, burial, or resurrection, never names the Father or the Spirit, and never sings in the plural voice.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** After a sermon on Romans 3–5, Galatians 2:15–21, Ephesians 2:8–9, Philippians 3:7–11, Isaiah 26, or Exodus 17 and 33. It fits a Lord's Supper service and a funeral.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The resurrection. Follow it with a song that sings the empty tomb, such as *How Great You Are* or *Christ Our Hope in Life and Death*.
- **Diet sentence.** It adds justification by faith alone in its clearest sung form. Use it often.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop stanza 2. Do not swap in a modern "Rock of Ages" text with different words under the same title without checking it.

---

## Identification notes

This is Toplady's hymn. The band sings it in the Ascend the Hill or Norton Hall Band arrangement, and this report grades Toplady's four stanzas, which both keep. Indelible Grace and others have written new songs titled "Rock of Ages" with different words, and some modern arrangements of Toplady add a chorus or tag (The Worship Initiative, CCLI 7202111; Charlie Hall, CCLI 6367309). Those are different from what the band sings.

The year follows the hymn's publication. The first lines appeared in *The Gospel Magazine* in October 1775, and the full hymn in 1776. The older catalog row listed 1763, the year of the traditional story that Toplady sheltered in a cleft rock at Burrington Combe.

---

## Why this overall label

The hymn states sin, wrath, the failure of works, and salvation by Christ's cross alone, and it sets the sinner's hope before the judgment seat, all in four plain stanzas. It is dense, Scripture-shaped, and easy to sing. It belongs in the Prefer lane.
