## Thesis

The Son of God shaped the stars, was given by the Father, and was broken so that freedom would be in his blood. He is the holy one, the redeemer of the soul, and the king who will come again and lead his people home. The singer answers as one forgiven, and overcome by that love.

---

## Overall recommendation

Acceptable with notes

Use it now and then, once the service has already said the cross in plain words. The church has logged it 4 times across at least four years in this window (2023, 2024, 2025, 2026). “Precious lamb, our freedom's in Your blood” and “Come again and lead Your people home” have to stay. “You alone were broken on the altar of love” is a picture, and it should not be the line the band leaves ringing by itself. The chorus is the singer overcome by love. Sing the verses with it.

## Voice and addressee

The verses address the Son as “You.” “Our freedom” and “Your people” take in the church. The chorus turns to “I”: “I sing to You, forgiven,” “I'm overcome,” “all my praise,” “my hands.” Jesus is named in the chorus. The Father is named as the giver. The Spirit is not addressed.

---

## How the text moves

It opens with the Son as the one who shaped the stars and as the one who dwells in the singer's heart. The second verse calls him the Father's gift, broken on an altar of love, the lamb whose blood is freedom. The chorus answers with forgiveness and with love that overcomes the singer. A later verse calls him strength the darkness cannot bear, Lord of all, whose kindness draws the singer near. The fourth verse calls him the redeemer who will come again and lead his people home. The bridge calls him worthy and beautiful. The return is the last new claim. The chorus does not add a saving act.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“Precious lamb, our freedom's in Your blood” is the line the grade depends on, together with “Come again and lead Your people home.” “You alone were broken on the altar of love” has to stay beside the blood line, because the breaking is as close as the song comes to a death. If the band keeps “I'm overcome / With Your great love for me” and “the dweller of my heart” and drops the lamb and the return, the church is left with beauty, kindness, and a private overwhelm. No line is false by itself. Without the blood line, the titles and the feeling are what remain.

## Persons of the Trinity named

The Son is named, titled, and addressed. The Father is the giver: “the Father's gift to us.” The Spirit is unnamed. “Jesus, oh holy one” is sung to the Son.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Someone who will not say the name of Jesus can still sing the first verse. “Shaper of the stars,” “how beautiful You are,” and “the dweller of my heart” do not require the gospel. That singer has to stop at “Precious lamb, our freedom's in Your blood,” at “Jesus, oh holy one,” and at “redeemer of my soul.” “Come again and lead Your people home” belongs to this king. “I'm overcome / With Your great love for me,” sung by itself, could be said about anyone the singer loves.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** “Broken on the altar of love.” Scripture does not give the cross that name. The breaking and the blood are the claim. The altar is the song's picture. “I'm overcome / With Your great love for me” reports how the singer feels.
- **Thin:** “How beautiful You are,” “the darkness cannot bear,” and “I will lift up my hands and sing.” Kindness that “draws me near” is true and general.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 3

“Son of God, shaper of the stars” agrees with Colossians 1:16, that by him all things were created, in heaven and on earth. “The Father's gift to us” is the giving of the Son. “Precious lamb, our freedom's in Your blood” stands with Ephesians 1:7, where redemption and the forgiveness of trespasses are through his blood, and with 1 Peter 1:19, “the precious blood of Christ, like that of a lamb.” The song says freedom. The verses say redemption and forgiveness. “I sing to You, forgiven” says the guilt is gone and does not say which sin. “Come again and lead Your people home” agrees with John 14:3, “I will come again and will take you to myself.”

“You alone were broken on the altar of love” puts the death in a picture. Hebrews 13:10 says “We have an altar,” and that verse does not call the altar an altar of love. “You alone the darkness cannot bear” uses the darkness of John 1:5, “the darkness has not overcome it,” and speaks of his strength. “You alone, the dweller of my heart” is close to Ephesians 3:17, “that Christ may dwell in your hearts through faith,” and the line does not say “through faith.” The stars, the blood, the forgiveness, and the return can be tied to texts. The altar and the darkness line are looser.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 3

The lamb, the blood, and freedom are the finished work in a short form. “Broken” is the suffering. “Redeemer of my soul” and “Come again and lead Your people home” give the song a future. The word cross is absent. Resurrection is absent. Sin is assumed in “forgiven” and never spoken. A lamb and blood keep the song from being only a list of titles. The work is still one verse, set beside an altar of love and a chorus about being overcome.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 2

The verses declare the Son: shaper of the stars, the Father's gift, the lamb, Lord of all, the redeemer, the one who comes again. The part a band will repeat is “Savior, I'm overcome / With Your great love for me,” with “the dweller of my heart” and “I will lift up my hands and sing.” Those lines are the singer's interior and the singer's praise. “You are worthy of all my praise” still speaks of his worth. The chorus spends its time on the overwhelm.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 3

He is mighty king, holy one, Lord of all, worthy, kind, strong, the shaper of the stars, and the Father's gift. Love is the word the chorus stays on, and in the second verse that love is the breaking and the blood, which is more than a mood. Holiness is the title “holy one.” Justice and wrath are unnamed. The third verse adds kindness that “draws me near.”

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 3

Four logs across at least four years means the song has been in the rotation, and it is not one of the titles the church sings most weeks. The lines are short. A congregation can learn that the Son made the stars, that freedom is in the lamb's blood, and that he will come and lead his people home. “Altar of love” will not teach the cross by itself. “I'm overcome” will not teach it either. When the song is used, the fourth verse is the one that gives the church a future to sing.

---

## Tone

The tone is adoring and warm. The line that sets it is “Savior, I'm overcome / With Your great love for me,” after the lamb and the blood. “How beautiful You are” keeps the same warmth. The blood line is plainer than the altar line.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never says that he was crucified, buried, and raised, never names a sin, and never says that faith is the Spirit's gift.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Colossians 1, the blood, or the return, with the lamb line and the fourth verse left in. John 14:3 and 1 Peter 1:19 sit near those lines.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The cross in plain words, and the resurrection. Pair it with *It Was Finished Upon That Cross* (logged 11 times) or *Hallelujah, What a Savior* (logged 6 times) so the blood is not left inside an altar of love.
- **Diet sentence.** Four times in at least four years, it adds a creator, a lamb, and a return. It never adds the empty tomb.
- **What not to do.** Do not loop the chorus and the bridge and drop verse 2 and verse 4. Do not let “altar of love” replace “freedom's in Your blood.”

## Why this overall label

The song gives the church the Son who made the stars, the lamb whose blood is freedom, and the king who will come again. It also gives them an altar of love and a chorus about being overcome, and it never says he rose. With the blood line and the return left in, and with the cross and the resurrection elsewhere in the hour, the church can sing it on occasion.
