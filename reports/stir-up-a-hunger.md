## Thesis

God can turn dark to light, turn a lost soul around, and make a hard heart tender by his love. The singer then asks for a hunger that nothing else will satisfy, a hunger for more of him.

---

## Overall recommendation

Weak

Do not feature it. It appears once in this window, in 2024. “You can take a soul that was lost and turn it around” and “my heart can turn as hard as a stone” are the lines with a biblical shape, and they never say who the Lord is or what he did to turn a soul. The chorus asks to be made hungry “for more of You.” If it is used at all, keep it short and after a song that has already named Jesus, sin, and the cross.

---

## Voice and addressee

The verse addresses “You” and “Lord.” The deeds are his: he can turn the dark, turn a lost soul, and make a hard heart tender. The chorus is the singer's ask, first person singular, “in my heart,” “satisfy me,” “for more of You.” The church would be borrowing one person's prayer.

---

## How the text moves

It starts with what God can do: dark into light, a lost soul turned, a heart of stone made tender by love. The chorus does not go on from those deeds. It asks for hunger, says nothing else will satisfy, and aims the hunger at “more of You.” There is no later stanza. The refrain does not name a saving act. The song moves from God's power over a soul to the singer's appetite.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“You can take a soul that was lost and turn it around” and “my heart can turn as hard as a stone / But You can make it tender again with Your love” are the lines the grade depends on. They are also easy to rush past, because the title and the chorus are the hunger. If the band sings only “Stir up a hunger in my heart for more of You,” the lost soul and the stone heart disappear, and the song is an appetite with no work of God under it. No line contradicts a text. Cutting the verse would make the song thinner than it already is. Nothing in the chorus would repair that.

## Persons of the Trinity named

The addressee is “You” and, once, “Lord.” Father, Son, and Spirit are unnamed. Nothing in the lyric says which person turns the soul or receives the hunger.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Yes. “Day into night / You can turn the dark into light,” “a soul that was lost,” and “stir up a hunger in my heart for more of You” can be sung by anyone who wants a god to change them and to be their satisfaction. There is no name of Jesus, no cross, and no resurrection to stop them. “Nothing else will do” still does not say who he is.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** “Day into night / You can turn the dark into light,” as a picture of reversal with no text attached. “For more of You” does not say more of what: more nearness, more knowledge, more power.
- **Thin:** “Nothing will satisfy me / Nothing else will do.” The claim is real as far as it goes. It never says why God satisfies, or what he has done.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 3

“You can take a soul that was lost and turn it around” uses the lost-and-found wording the church knows from Luke 15:24, “he was lost, and is found,” and it leaves out the father, the son, and the feast. “My heart can turn as hard as a stone / But You can make it tender again” stands next to Ezekiel 36:26, “I will remove the heart of stone from your flesh and give you a heart of flesh.” The prophecy says God gives the new heart. The song says he can make it tender again with his love. That is the same actor and a softer result. “Stir up a hunger” recalls Matthew 5:6, “Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness, for they shall be satisfied.” The chorus hungers “for more of You,” not for righteousness, and it does not say they shall be satisfied. It says nothing else will do.

The lost soul and the stone heart can be tied to texts. The dark-to-light line and “more of You” cannot be checked against a verse. The song stacks a true rescue and a true new heart, then spends the chorus on an appetite.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 2

Jesus is not named. The cross, the blood, the burial, and the resurrection are absent. “A soul that was lost” and a heart made tender are the fruit of salvation with the Savior left out. “Lord” is a title with no work attached. The hunger has no gospel under it.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 2

The verse declares what God can do to the dark, to a lost soul, and to a hard heart. That part is about him. The chorus, which carries the title, is the singer's interior: “Stir up a hunger in my heart,” “Nothing will satisfy me,” “for more of You.” The song asks to feel a stronger desire. The desire is the subject by the time the chorus arrives.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 3

He can reverse darkness, turn a lost soul, and soften a heart of stone by love. Those are acts, and they are more specific than “great” or “good.” Love is the means of the new heart and the only attribute named. Holiness, justice, wrath, and faithfulness are unnamed. The person who does these things is not named either. The portrait is power and love, aimed at one sinner's heart.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 3

One use, in 2024, means the room has barely sung it in this window. The words are easy. What a congregation would learn is that God can turn a lost person and soften a hard heart, and that the singer wants a hunger for more of him. They would not learn the name of Jesus or the cross. “For more of You” is the line that will be remembered, and it is the line that teaches the least. The verse is the part worth keeping if the song is used at all.

---

## Tone

The tone is earnest desire. The line that sets it is “Stir up a hunger in my heart for more of You,” after the stone heart has been named. The verse is plainer than the chorus. Nothing in the song is playful or romantic. It is a private prayer of longing.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names Father, Son, or Spirit, never says that Christ died and rose, and never says what the hunger is for beyond “more of You.”

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Ezekiel 36 or Luke 15, and only after the sermon has already said who gives the new heart and finds the lost. The verse can sit under that preaching. The chorus cannot carry it.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The name of Jesus and the cross. Pair it with *All I Have Is Christ* or *O Great God* so the turned soul is not left without the blood.
- **Diet sentence.** Once in this window, it adds a prayer for hunger and a line about a stone heart. It never adds the gospel.
- **What not to do.** Do not loop “for more of You” and drop the lost soul and the stone heart. Do not treat the hunger as the point of the service.

## Why this overall label

The verse tells the truth that God turns a lost soul and can replace a heart of stone. The chorus asks to be made hungry for more of an unnamed Lord, and the song never says the name of Jesus or the cross. Once in 2024 is enough of a hearing. It should not be given a Sunday to itself.
