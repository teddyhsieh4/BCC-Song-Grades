## Thesis

The sinner has been forgiven, the rebel made clean, blinded eyes made to see, and orphans adopted who once hated the love and ran from grace. The Father’s love sent His only Son to rescue. He has saved, called blameless, guides, and will sustain. Mercy and grace color all. The Spirit is given so the people can be sure they are sons of God and rest in the hope to come. Though sufferings fill their lives, they are confident they are heirs with Christ and cry “Abba, Father.”

---

## Overall recommendation

**Excellent / Prefer**

Feature it. Sing both verses and the bridge. Verse 1 names the forgiveness, the cleansing, the seeing, and the adoption of those who hated the love. The chorus names the sending of the only Son to rescue and the saving that calls blameless. Verse 2 names the Spirit given for assurance of sonship. The bridge names the heirs with Christ who cry Abba. Without those lines the song is true praise of the Father’s love that has not named the Son, the Spirit, or the cry.

The church has sung it for at least four years (6 logs in the 2023–2026 snapshot). It is already known.

## Voice and addressee

The song is first-person plural throughout. “The Father’s love for us.” “He has saved us.” It addresses the Father. The voice is corporate.

---

## How the text moves

Verse 1 asks how the sinner has been forgiven, the rebel made clean, blinded eyes made to see, and the orphans adopted who hated the love and ran from grace. The chorus answers with the wonder of the Father’s love that sent the only Son to rescue, who has saved, called blameless, guides, and will sustain. Verse 2 names the mercy that floods with kindness, the grace that colors all, the promise not to leave, and the Spirit freely given so the people can be sure they are sons of God and rest in the hope to come. The bridge names the sufferings, the confidence of being heirs with Christ, and the cry “Abba, Father.”

---

## Lines you cannot cut

Do not drop the chorus. “How wonderful the Father’s love / The Father’s love for us / That He would send His only Son / To come and rescue us / He has saved us, called us blameless / Guides us now and will sustain us” is the sending of the Son and the saving that calls blameless. Without it the church has asked how the sinner was forgiven and has not named the Son or the rescue.

Do not drop the bridge. “Though sufferings may fill our lives / We’re confident we’re heirs with Christ / And so we cry, ‘Abba, Father’” is the heirship and the cry. Without it the sonship named in verse 2 has no stated confidence in suffering.

No line is false.

## Persons of the Trinity named

The Father is addressed as the One whose love sent the Son and who gives the Spirit. The Son is named as the only Son sent to rescue. The Spirit is named as freely given so the people can be sure they are sons of God. All three Persons are named in their ordinary work.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Not the whole song. The chorus names the sending of the only Son to rescue and the saving that calls blameless. Verse 2 names the Spirit and the sons of God. A vague theist could not sing those lines as written.

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“How has the sinner been forgiven / How has the rebel been made clean / Or blinded eyes been made to see / How have the orphans been adopted / Who hated Your love and ran from grace / Despised and rejected all Your ways” is Ephesians 2:1–5, Romans 5:10, and John 9. The change from enemy to son is the ordinary sense of those texts.

“How wonderful the Father’s love / The Father’s love for us / That He would send His only Son / To come and rescue us / He has saved us, called us blameless / Guides us now and will sustain us” is John 3:16, Romans 8:32, and Jude 24. The sending and the calling blameless are the ordinary sense of those texts.

“You freely give Your Spirit to us / So we can be sure we’re sons of God / And rest in the hope of what’s to come” is Romans 8:14–16 and Galatians 4:6. The Spirit’s witness is the ordinary sense of those texts.

“Though sufferings may fill our lives / We’re confident we’re heirs with Christ / And so we cry, ‘Abba, Father’” is Romans 8:15–17. The heirship and the cry are the ordinary sense of those texts.

The lines use the ordinary sense of those texts.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 4

Christ is named as the only Son sent to rescue. The saving that calls blameless is named. The cross, the blood, and the resurrection are not stated in those words. The work is not left as a title. It is not stated at the length of a full passion hymn.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 5

The main claim is objective. The Father sent the Son. He has saved and called blameless. The Spirit is given. The people are heirs with Christ. The first-person plural lines are the church’s confession of the benefit. They are not a vow the singer must mean before he may sing. They are the testimony of the love received.

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The verses are clear. The chorus is short. The words are ordinary. The church has already sung it. The people learn the shape of the Father’s love from the forgiveness and adoption of enemies, through the sending of the Son, to the Spirit’s assurance and the cry of Abba in suffering. They do not need a weekly explanation of any line. Keys on file include F and F#.

If time is short, do not drop the chorus or the bridge.

---

## Tone

The tone is glad and sober. “How wonderful the Father’s love” answers the works just named. There is no intensity beyond the text.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names the cross, the blood, the burial, or the resurrection in those words. It never says “in our place.” It never names substitution beyond the rescue and the saving.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Romans 8, Ephesians 1–2, Galatians 4, or any text on adoption, the Spirit’s witness, and the Father’s love. It works as a response after the sermon has already named the finished work that makes the sonship possible.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The cross as atonement and the resurrection when the day’s text is the passion or 1 Corinthians 15. Pair with a song that states substitution before or after this one.
- **Diet sentence.** It adds the Father’s love that sends the Son, the calling blameless, the Spirit’s assurance of sonship, and the cry of Abba in suffering. It does not add the cross narrative at length.
- **What not to do.** Do not use it as the only gospel song in the set. Do not drop the chorus. Frame the rescue when the room needs the reminder that the love rests on the sending of the Son.
