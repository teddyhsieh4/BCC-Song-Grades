## Thesis

The Lord is the singer’s salvation: He has pulled him from the sea by grace, paid his debt, won the victory, and will not leave him in the grave.

---

## Overall recommendation

Excellent / Prefer

Feature it freely on the person and work of Christ, the security of the believer, or texts that name the Lord as strong to save. The chorus states the paid debt and the won victory. The final verse names the empty grave and the call home. The bridge names Father, Son, and Spirit. It has already been logged in at least four years of the current window.

## Voice and addressee

First-person singular in the verses (“me,” “I am safe,” “I will not fear”). The chorus shifts to “our God” and the bridge to “our salvation.” The claims are confessional and shareable. The congregation can sing the lines as true of every believer in Christ.

---

## How the text moves

It opens with grace that reached and pulled the singer from the raging sea onto solid ground. The second verse refuses fear in darkness and looks to the dawn. The chorus asks who is like the Lord, strong to save and faithful in love, and states that the debt is paid and the victory won. The third and fourth verses place hope in the Lord who flowers every promise of His Word and renews the days of waiting, loss, and weakness. The fifth verse looks to the final day: He will not leave the singer in the grave, the singer will rise, and He will call him home. The bridge ascribes glory to Father, Son, and Spirit. The claim advances from rescue by grace through present security to the resurrection and the Trinity.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

The chorus (“Who is like the Lord our God? / Strong to save, faithful in love / My debt is paid and the vict’ry won”) and the fifth verse (“He will not leave me in the grave / But I will rise, He will call me home”). Drop the paid debt and the empty grave and the confession of salvation loses its ground. The bridge that names the three Persons should stay when the service can carry it.

## Persons of the Trinity named

The Lord is named throughout as salvation. The bridge explicitly names God the Father, God the Son, and God the Spirit. Christ is not titled separately outside the bridge, yet the paid debt, the victory, and the empty grave are His work.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. The debt paid, the victory won, the Lord who will not leave the singer in the grave, and the glory to Father, Son, and Spirit require the God of the gospel. A vague theist cannot sing the paid debt or the Trinity without correction.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** the sea, the walls, the winter and spring, and the rising sun are ordinary images for danger, obstacle, and hope. They do not alter the claim.
- **Thin:** the cross itself is not named. The paid debt and the empty grave state the result; the means is left for other songs.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“The grace of God has reached for me / And pulled me from the raging sea” follows the ordinary sense of rescue by grace (Psalm 18:16, Ephesians 2:8–9). “My debt is paid and the vict’ry won” follows the ordinary sense of the finished work (Colossians 2:13–14, 1 Corinthians 15:57). “He will not leave me in the grave / But I will rise” follows the ordinary sense of the resurrection of those in Christ (1 Corinthians 15:20–23, John 11:25–26). “Glory be to God the Father / Glory be to God the Son / Glory be to God the Spirit” follows the ordinary sense of the triune God. The ordinary sense of the texts is in view at each claim.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

The Lord is strong to save and faithful in love. The debt is paid and the victory is won. He will not leave the singer in the grave; the singer will rise and be called home. The bridge ascribes glory to the Son with the Father and the Spirit. The cross is not named, yet the paid debt and the empty grave place the finished work at the center of the confession.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

The lines declare what the Lord has done and will do. First-person singular is confessional and shareable. “I will not fear,” “my hope is hidden,” and “I know His grace will renew these days” rest on the finished work and the Word rather than on inner weather. Desire or vow language is absent.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 5

The Lord is gracious (reached and pulled), strong to save, faithful in love, the one whose Word flowers every promise, the one who renews the days of weakness, and the one who will not leave His people in the grave. The bridge names Father, Son, and Spirit. Holiness, justice (debt paid), mercy, and sovereignty are present. The attributes are specific and balanced.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The diction is plain. The argument moves from rescue to present security to the final day and the Trinity. The congregation has already sung it in multiple years. The confessional voice and the clear chorus make it useful for teaching the finished work and the resurrection hope.

---

## Tone

Reverent. The language stays with grace that pulls from the sea, the paid debt, the victory, and the empty grave. It does not ask the room to confer on God what He already possesses.

---

## What the congregation never says

The cross and the blood are not named. Explicit call to repentance is absent. Pair the song with texts or songs that name the means of the paid debt when the sermon of the day requires them.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** The person and work of Christ, the security of the believer (Romans 8, Psalm 27), the resurrection, or any text on the Lord as strong to save.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** Explicit naming of the cross if the sermon requires the means of the paid debt.
- **Diet sentence.** Adds a dense confession that the Lord has paid the debt, won the victory, and will raise His people from the grave. It does not add a song that walks the substitutionary means of that debt.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop the chorus or the fifth verse. Do not tag it with a chorus that flattens the paid debt and the empty grave into generic hope.
