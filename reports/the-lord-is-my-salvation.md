## Thesis

The Lord is the singer’s salvation: He has pulled him from the sea by grace, paid his debt, won the victory, and will not leave him in the grave.

---

## Overall recommendation

Excellent / Prefer

Feature it freely on the work of Christ, the security of the believer, or texts that call the Lord strong to save. Keep the chorus (the paid debt) and the fifth verse (the empty grave). The bridge gives glory to Father, Son, and Spirit by name. The church has sung it 7 times across at least four years of the 2023–2026 window.

## Voice and addressee

First-person singular in the verses (“me,” “I am safe,” “I will not fear”). The chorus shifts to “our God” and the bridge to “our salvation,” so the personal testimony becomes the church’s confession.

---

## How the text moves

The song starts with rescue: grace pulled the singer from the sea. The middle verses hold on through darkness, waiting, and loss. The fifth verse looks to the last day, when the singer will rise, and the bridge gives glory to the triune God.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

The chorus (“Who is like the Lord our God? / Strong to save, faithful in love / My debt is paid and the vict’ry won”) and the fifth verse (“He will not leave me in the grave / But I will rise, He will call me home”). Without them, the song is rescue imagery with no gospel content. Keep the bridge when the service has room for it.

## Persons of the Trinity named

The Lord is named throughout as salvation. The bridge explicitly names God the Father, God the Son, and God the Spirit. Christ is not titled separately outside the bridge, yet the paid debt, the victory, and the empty grave are His work.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. A vague theist could not sing “Glory be to God the Son” and “God the Spirit” in the bridge, or “My debt is paid” in the chorus, without changing them.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** the sea, the walls, the winter and spring, and the rising sun are ordinary images for danger, obstacle, and hope. They do not change the claim.
- **Thin:** the cross itself is not named. The song states the result (the debt paid) without the means.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“The grace of God has reached for me / And pulled me from the raging sea” is rescue by grace (Psalm 18:16, Ephesians 2:8–9). “My debt is paid and the vict’ry won” is the finished work (Colossians 2:13–14, 1 Corinthians 15:57). “He will not leave me in the grave / But I will rise” is the resurrection of those in Christ (1 Corinthians 15:20–23, John 11:25–26). The bridge (“Glory be to God the Father / Glory be to God the Son / Glory be to God the Spirit”) confesses the triune God.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

The chorus states that the debt is paid, and the fifth verse states that the Lord will raise His people from the grave. The cross is not named, but the finished work and the resurrection are at the center of the song.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

The lines declare what the Lord has done and will do. “I will not fear,” “my hope is hidden,” and “I know His grace will renew these days” rest on that work and on His Word. The score is 4 because most verses are spoken in the singular, about the singer’s own trouble.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 5

The Lord is gracious, strong to save, faithful in love, and true to every promise of His Word. He renews His people in weakness and will not leave them in the grave. The bridge names all three Persons.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The chorus is short and the bridge is simple to sing. A congregation that sings it learns to confess a paid debt, a coming resurrection, and the Trinity in one song.

---

## Tone

The tone is reverent and hopeful. The verses name darkness and loss honestly, and the chorus answers them with the paid debt.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names the cross or the blood, and never calls to repentance. When the sermon needs the cross, pair it with a song that names it, such as The Glory of the Cross.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** The work of Christ, the security of the believer (Romans 8, Psalm 27), the resurrection, or any text on the Lord as strong to save.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The cross, if the sermon needs the means of the paid debt.
- **Diet sentence.** Adds a confession of the paid debt, the resurrection of believers, and the Trinity by name. It does not add a song about how the debt was paid.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop the chorus or the fifth verse, and do not follow it with a vague chorus about hope that drops the debt and the grave.
