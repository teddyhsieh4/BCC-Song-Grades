## Thesis

The love of God is greater than any tongue or pen can tell. It reaches beyond the highest star and to the lowest hell. God gave His Son to win the guilty pair, reconciled the erring child, and pardoned from sin. That love will still endure when earthly kingdoms fall, and it is redeeming grace to Adam’s race.

---

## Overall recommendation

Excellent / Prefer

Feature it. The first stanza names the guilty pair, the Son given to win them, the reconciliation of the erring child, and the pardon from sin. Keep that stanza. The refrain and the third stanza declare the measureless endurance of the love. The second stanza adds the certainty that the love will still endure when men who refuse to pray call on the rocks.

## Voice and addressee

Third-person declaration about the love of God, with the refrain addressing the love itself (“O love of God”). The congregation sings objective claims about what God has done and about the measure of that love. No first-person vows.

---

## How the text moves

It opens by stating that the love is greater far than tongue or pen can tell, that it goes beyond the highest star and reaches to the lowest hell, and that God gave His Son to win the guilty pair, reconciled the erring child, and pardoned from his sin. The refrain calls the love rich, pure, measureless, and strong, and says it shall forevermore endure as the saints’ and angels’ song. The second stanza looks to the end of years and the fall of earthly thrones, when those who refuse to pray call on the rocks, and affirms that God’s love will still endure as redeeming grace to Adam’s race. The third stanza uses the image of ocean ink, parchment skies, stalks as quills, and every man a scribe to say that even then the scroll could not contain the whole of the love.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“The guilty pair, bowed down with care, / God gave His Son to win; / His erring child He reconciled, / And pardoned from his sin.”

Dropping the first stanza leaves the measure of the love without the Son given, the reconciliation, and the pardon. The refrain and the later stanzas still declare the endurance of the love, but the gospel act that shows it is in those four lines.

## Persons of the Trinity named

God is named. His Son is named. The Spirit is not named. The love is God’s, shown by the Son given.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

No. “God gave His Son to win” the guilty pair, the reconciliation of the erring child, and the pardon from sin cannot be sung by a vague theist without correction. The third stanza’s image of the immeasurable love is still tied to that gift of the Son in the first stanza.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** the third stanza’s extended image of ink, parchment, quills, and scroll is poetry that cannot be checked against a single text, though the claim it serves (the love is greater than can be told) is biblical.
- **Thin:** none that matter. The first stanza is specific about the Son given, the reconciliation, and the pardon.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 5

“The guilty pair, bowed down with care, / God gave His Son to win” is John 3:16 and Romans 5:8. “His erring child He reconciled, / And pardoned from his sin” is 2 Corinthians 5:18–19 and Ephesians 1:7. “It goes beyond the highest star, / And reaches to the lowest hell” is the ordinary sense of Romans 8:38–39 and Psalm 139:8. “Redeeming grace to Adam’s race” is Romans 5:15–19. The lines stay inside ordinary biblical sense. The third stanza’s image is older poetry that serves the same claim of immeasurable love (Ephesians 3:18–19).

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 5

The Son is named as the one given to win the guilty pair. Reconciliation and pardon are named. The love is not left as an abstract attribute. It is shown by the Son given and the sin pardoned. The refrain and the later stanzas keep returning to the endurance of that same love.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 5

The lyric declares what the love of God is and what God has done. It does not report the singer’s feelings or make high-claim vows. The refrain addresses the love itself with the attributes of rich, pure, measureless, and strong. The congregation sings objective truth.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 4

The love is greater far, rich, pure, measureless, strong, and sure. It reaches from the highest star to the lowest hell. It endures forevermore. It is redeeming grace. The attributes are specific and tied to the gift of the Son and the pardon. Holiness and justice appear mainly as the lowest hell and the call on the rocks by those who refuse to pray. The score is 4 because the lyric stays focused on the love and the redemption rather than a broader list of attributes.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 5

The language is plain. The first stanza teaches the Son given, the reconciliation, and the pardon. The refrain and the third stanza give the congregation a memorable way to confess that the love cannot be measured. The room already knows the third stanza. Singability is high, and the teaching is the reason for the 5. Keep the first stanza so the measure of the love stays attached to the Son given.

---

## Tone

Reverent. It does not ask the room to confer on God what He has not already done. The intensity stays inside the immeasurable love shown by the Son given and the pardon.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names the resurrection, the Spirit, or the present intercession. Those are small gaps. The main gospel elements of the Son given, the reconciliation, and the pardon are present.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** John 3, Romans 5, Romans 8:38–39, Ephesians 3:18–19, any text on the love of God shown in the gift of the Son or on the immeasurable love. Especially useful after a sermon that has named the guilty pair and the Son given.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The resurrection and a fuller naming of the Spirit if the service has not already supplied them.
- **Diet sentence.** It adds a dense confession that the love of God is greater than can be told and that it is shown by the Son given to win the guilty pair, the reconciliation, and the pardon. It does not add a full narrative of the empty tomb.
- **What not to do.** Do not drop the first stanza. Do not let the third stanza stand alone as atmosphere without the Son given and the pardon.
