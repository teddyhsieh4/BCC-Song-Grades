## Thesis

No one is like the Lord, and no one could take his place. The singer’s heart beats to worship him. The singer lives to seek his face, longs for his presence, and calls serving him a reward. He is the singer’s God and everything.

---

## Overall recommendation

Weak

Do not feature it. The church has logged it 3 times across at least three years (2024, 2025, 2026). “There’s no one like You, my Lord / No one could take Your place” is the line that has to stay if it is sung at all. The rest is a heart that beats to worship, a life spent seeking his face, and a longing for presence. Jesus is not named. A worshiper who does not confess Jesus can sing every line unchanged. Keep it off the list, or sing only a short pass after Scripture has already named the Son.

## Alternative song suggestions

The lyric is reaching for the uniqueness of the Lord, a heart set on worship, and a life that seeks his face. Three of these the church already sings. One is not in the catalog.

**Already sung**

**Only a Holy God** (Michael Farren / Dustin Smith / Jonny Robinson / Rich Thompson, CityAlight, CCLI 7073332). Logged 8 times across at least four years (2023, 2024, 2025, 2026). The song asks who else commands all the hosts of heaven, and holiness is the answer.

**He Is Worthy** (Nathan Stiff / David Zimmer, Sovereign Grace, CCLI 7138112). Logged 14 times across at least four years (2023, 2024, 2025, 2026). The Lord is worthy, and the worth is tied to who he is and what he has done.

**Behold Our God** (Jonathan Baird / Meghan Baird / Ryan Baird / Stephen Altrogge, Sovereign Grace, CCLI 5937510). Logged 10 times across at least four years (2023, 2024, 2025, 2026). There is none like the God who sits enthroned, and the church is told to behold him.

**Not yet logged**

**Christ Is Mine Forevermore** (Jonny Robinson / Rich Thompson, CityAlight, CCLI 7036096). Not in the catalog. The heart’s treasure is Christ, who paid for every failing, and the life is his forever.

## Voice and addressee

First person singular, speaking to the Lord. “My Lord,” “my heart,” “I live,” “I long,” “You are my God,” “You’re everything to me.” The claim “there’s no one like You” is about him. The reasons that follow are the singer’s heartbeat, search, and longing.

---

## How the text moves

It says no one is like the Lord and no one could take his place. The singer’s heart beats to worship him, and the singer lives to seek his face. That pair returns. The chorus says he is the singer’s God and everything. A second verse keeps the same uniqueness and adds a longing for presence, with service as the reward. The chorus does not add a work of God. The song moves from uniqueness to the singer’s devotion and stays there.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“There’s no one like You, my Lord / No one could take Your place” is the line the grade depends on. It is the only claim about God that is more than the singer’s feeling. “My heart beats to worship You,” “I live just to seek Your face,” and “I long for Your presence, Lord” are the devotion. If the band keeps those and drops the uniqueness, the song is a heartbeat and a search. No line is false. The uniqueness line is short, and the longing is what the title will not protect if the first line is skipped. The title is that first line. Keep it, and do not expect it to carry a gospel.

## Persons of the Trinity named

“Lord” and “God” are the names. Father, Son, and Spirit are unnamed. “No one could take Your place” does not say the place is the Son’s. The face the singer seeks is not given a name.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Yes. “There’s no one like You, my Lord,” “I live just to seek Your face,” and “I long for Your presence” can be sung by any worshiper of one God. Nothing in the lyric names Jesus, a cross, or a resurrection. “To serve You is my reward” can be a devout person’s whole religion. The song would not correct him.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** “My heart beats to worship You.” A heartbeat is the singer’s bodily picture of devotion. It does not state a text.
- **Thin:** “No one could take Your place,” “I live just to seek Your face,” “I long for Your presence,” and “You’re everything to me.” Uniqueness, face, presence, and “everything” are true as far as they go. They never say a work of God.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 3

“There’s no one like You, my Lord” agrees with the sense of Exodus 15:11, “Who is like you, O LORD, among the gods?” and with Psalm 86:8, “There is none like you among the gods, O Lord.” The song says no one could take his place, which is that uniqueness in plainer words. “I live just to seek Your face” stands near Psalm 27:8, “Your face, LORD, do I seek.” The song makes the seeking the whole life.

“My heart beats to worship You,” “I long for Your presence,” and “To serve You is my reward” are the singer’s devotion. They are not false. They do not unfold the passages that say why there is none like the Lord. The score stays at 3 because the uniqueness is biblical and the song never says the acts that show it.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 2

Jesus is not named. The cross, the blood, sin, and the resurrection are absent. “My Lord” and “my God” are titles any worshiper can use. The place no one else can take is not said to be the Son’s office or the Son’s cross. The song exalts an unnamed Lord and leaves the gospel out.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 2

“There’s no one like You” declares something about God. The lines that explain it are the singer: a heart that beats to worship, a life lived to seek his face, a longing for presence, service as a personal reward, “You’re everything to me.” The uniqueness is one claim. The devotion is the rest of the song, and it is what the chorus repeats.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 3

He is Lord and God. No one is like him, and no one could take his place. He has a face to seek and a presence to long for. “Everything to me” is the singer’s summary of his sufficiency. Holiness, justice, wrath, love, and faithfulness are unnamed. The uniqueness is real. The attributes that would show why he is unique are not in the lyric.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 3

Three logs across at least three years means it is known to some and not a staple. The words are simple. A congregation can learn to say that no one is like the Lord and that his face is worth seeking. They cannot learn who the Lord is in Christ or what he has done. “My heart beats to worship You” is easy and teaches the least. The uniqueness line is the one worth keeping, and only after Scripture has already named the Son.

---

## Tone

The tone is devoted and soft. The line that sets it is “My heart beats to worship You / I live just to seek Your face.” “I long for Your presence, Lord” keeps the same quiet hunger. “No one could take Your place” is firmer than the heartbeat. The song stays in the longing.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names Jesus, never says that the Lord is the Holy One who saves, and never says a mighty act that would show why no one is like him.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Exodus 15:11 or Psalm 86, after the reading has already said the acts of God, and only as a short response. It does not serve a sermon on the cross.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The Son and his work. Pair it with *Only a Holy God* or *He Is Worthy* so uniqueness is not left as a heartbeat and a face.
- **Diet sentence.** Three times in at least three years, it adds a one-line confession that no one is like the Lord. It never adds the gospel.
- **What not to do.** Do not sing this as the Sovereign Grace song “Jesus, There’s No One Like You,” or as the LeBlanc song (CCLI 815649). Do not loop “my heart beats to worship You” and drop “no one like You.”
