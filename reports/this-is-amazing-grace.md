## Thesis

Jesus is the King of Glory who breaks the power of sin, rules the nations, and makes orphans into sons and daughters. The grace the song praises is that this King took the singer's place, went to the cross, and laid down His life to set the singer free. The bridge calls the slain Lamb and the risen King worthy.

---

## Overall recommendation

Avoid

Do not use it. The reason is the credit line. Bethel Music Publishing holds part of the song, Bethel Music recorded and released it, and the co-writer who led that recording was on Bethel Church's staff when it was written. This church's rule for a strong Bethel association is Avoid, and the rule does not bend for a good lyric. The band sings Phil Wickham's recording. The song's ownership is the same either way. A CCLI number belongs to the song itself, so Bethel keeps its share of the credit and royalties whichever version the band learns from.

The church has logged it 7 times across at least four years of the 2023–2026 window, so the room will miss it. The scores below grade the words on their own, and they are good: the chorus states substitution plainly. That is why the replacements below need to say the same things just as clearly.

---

## Alternative song suggestions

The lyric is reaching for three things: Christ in the sinner's place, the cross as the measure of grace, and the slain and risen Lamb as worthy of praise (Revelation 5:12). Three of these the church already sings. One is not in the catalog.

**Already sung**

- **He Is Worthy** (Nathan Stiff / David Zimmer, Sovereign Grace, CCLI 7138112, graded Excellent / Prefer). Logged 14 times. It confesses the Word made flesh coming for rebels, the Lamb dying at Calvary to cancel the debt, and the same Lamb rising to reign. It covers the bridge's "worthy" theme with the gospel told in full.
- **Jesus, Thank You** (Pat Sczebel, Sovereign Grace, CCLI 4475341, graded Excellent / Prefer). Logged 7 times. It is the same kind of song, a personal thanks to Jesus for the cross. It names what this lyric leaves out: the Father's wrath satisfied and the enemy made a friend.
- **It Was Finished Upon That Cross** (Jonny Robinson / Rich Thompson / Nigel Hendroff, CityAlight, CCLI 7171907, graded Excellent / Prefer). Logged 11 times. It sings the curse broken, the pardon complete, and death defeated by the resurrection, with the singer clothed in Christ's righteousness.

**Not yet logged**

- **Crown Him with Many Crowns** (Matthew Bridges / Godfrey Thring, hymn, CCLI 23938). Not in the catalog. It begins with "the Lamb upon His throne" and praises Him "who died for thee" and who "died and rose on high." It gives the church the King of Glory and the slain Lamb together, the two titles this song's bridge sets side by side. Use the stanzas in common hymnals.

## Voice and addressee

The verses describe the King in the third person ("Who breaks the power of sin and darkness"), with one "us" in the line about awe. The chorus turns to speak to Jesus directly as "You," in the first person singular: "That You would take my place." The bridge is a declaration with no speaker named, addressed to no one, in the words of Revelation 5.

---

## How the text moves

The verses build a list of what the King does: breaks sin's power, shakes the earth, restores order, adopts orphans, rules the nations. The chorus answers with the one deed that makes it grace, the King dying in the singer's place. The bridge adds the resurrection ("the King who conquered the grave"), so the song ends with the cross and the empty tomb side by side.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

The chorus lines "That You would take my place" and "You laid down Your life" are what make this a gospel song. Without them, the verses are a list of a powerful King's acts. The bridge should stay too, since it is the only place the resurrection is sung. If the band loops the first two chorus lines ("This is amazing grace / This is unfailing love") as a tag, the room repeats the words "grace" and "love" without the substitution that defines them. No line is false.

---

## Persons of the Trinity named

The Son is named ("Jesus") and given the titles King of Glory and Lamb. The Father and the Spirit are not named. In Galatians 4:4–6 the Father adopts sons through the Son. The lyric gives the adoption of "orphans" to the King and leaves the Father out.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

A vague theist could sing the verses, which describe a mighty King who shakes the earth and rules with justice. The chorus stops them. "That You would take my place" and "You laid down Your life / That I would be set free" confess a substitutionary death, and the bridge confesses a slain Lamb who rose from the grave. Only a Christian can sing those lines honestly.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** "leaves us breathless in awe and wonder" reports a feeling. It is mild and pointed at God, but no text is behind it.
- **Thin:** the first two chorus lines, "This is amazing grace / This is unfailing love," which name grace and love before the following lines say what they are. They work in context and are thin when sung alone.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 4

Most lines can be tied to Scripture. "Who breaks the power of sin and darkness" is Colossians 1:13, deliverance from the domain of darkness. "The King of Glory" is Psalm 24:7–10. "Holy thunder" recalls Psalm 29, where the voice of the Lord thunders over the waters. Making the orphan "a son and daughter" is adoption (Romans 8:15, Galatians 4:5). Ruling the nations "with truth and justice" is Psalm 96:13, and shining like the sun is Revelation 1:16. "That You would take my place" is 1 Peter 3:18, the righteous for the unrighteous. The bridge is Revelation 5:12 almost word for word, joined to the resurrection.

The score is 4 because two lines are loose. "That You would bear my cross" is a fair picture of Christ bearing what was owed to the singer, but in the Gospels "take up his cross" is the disciple's duty (Luke 9:23), and the lyric reverses the picture without explaining it. "Brings our chaos back into order" draws on creation language, and the lyric leaves it unclear whether the chaos is the world's, the heart's, or the singer's life.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 4

Christ is the subject of every section. The song gets to the center of the gospel: He took the singer's place, went to the cross, laid down His life, and conquered the grave. That is substitution, death, and resurrection in a short popular song. The score is 4 and not 5 because the reason for the substitution is never given. The singer is set "free," but the lyric never says from what guilt or what judgment. Sin appears only as a power that Jesus breaks, so the people never confess that they deserved the place He took.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 4

The lyric mostly declares what Christ is and what He did. The verses are a list of His acts, and the bridge quotes Revelation. The personal lines are tied to the cross: "That You would take my place," then "I sing for / All that You've done for me." The singer's response is praise for a stated deed. The one line about feelings, "breathless in awe and wonder," comes in response to the holy thunder, which is an act of God.

---

## Description of God's Character: 4

| Attribute | Line |
|---|---|
| Power over sin | "Who breaks the power of sin and darkness" |
| Love | "Whose love is mighty," "unfailing love" |
| Holiness, majesty | "holy thunder," "King of Glory" |
| Sovereignty | "the King above all kings," ruling the nations |
| Justice, truth | "with truth and justice" |
| Grace | "This is amazing grace" |
| Glory | shining "like the sun" |
| Worthiness | "Worthy is the Lamb" |

Love and kingship are balanced by justice and holiness, which is the song's strength. Wrath and judgment are not in the lyric, so holiness never becomes a threat that grace answers. Mercy appears only as the general word "grace."

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 4

The congregation knows it well, and the chorus is easy to sing. By singing it, the people learn that grace means Jesus took their place and died, and that the slain Lamb is the risen King. That is real teaching. The chorus is in the "I" voice, though the verse's "us" and the bridge are corporate. The bridge is short and is often repeated many times. Its words are Scripture, so the repetition does less harm than a repeated chorus of feelings would.

---

## Tone

The tone is triumphant. The verses build volume with "holy thunder" and "the King above all kings," and the chorus moves from that power to the personal wonder of "That You would take my place." Nothing is casual or sensual.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never confesses its sin as guilt, never names God's wrath or the judgment Christ bore, never sings His incarnation, burial, or present intercession, never names the Father or the Spirit, and never speaks of repentance, faith, or justification.

---

## Setlist notes

This song is Avoid and should come out of the rotation. These notes are for the leader who plans the replacement.

- **When it serves the sermon.** The texts this song touches are Revelation 5, Colossians 1:13–14, 1 Peter 3:18, and Psalm 24. For those sermons, use *He Is Worthy* or *Crown Him with Many Crowns* in its place.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** If an older set list still includes it, the hour has to supply sin confessed as guilt and the wrath Christ bore. *Jesus, Thank You* covers both.
- **Diet sentence.** It added a short, well-known statement of substitution and resurrection. The replacements above say the same things, and more, without the Bethel credit.
- **What not to do.** Do not keep it "for the lyric." Do not swap in another Bethel Music title with a similar theme. Do not replace it with a song that has the triumph and leaves out the cross.
