## Thesis

God holds all things, does not change, and remains the Holy One, and his love does not fail. The singer answers by giving him a heart forever, calling him rock and song, and wondering that such a God would make time for one person.

---

## Overall recommendation

Weak

Do not feature it. It appears once in this window, in 2024. “You never change, God, You remain / The Holy One” is the line that has to stay if the song is sung at all. “You have my heart / And I am Yours forever” and “Still You make time for me” are the lines that should not be left to carry the service. The Holy One is named. Jesus, sin, and the cross are not. Put it after Scripture and a song that has already preached the cross, or leave it off the list.

---

## Voice and addressee

First person singular, speaking to God. “You have my heart,” “I am Yours,” “I can't understand,” “I hold on to,” “I sing for You.” The chorus still addresses him: “Praise You, God of earth and sky.” The church would be singing one person's vow and one person's wonder.

---

## How the text moves

It starts with a vow. The singer's heart is his, and the singer is his forever. He is strength, the God of grace and power. The pre-chorus sets that God, who holds everything, next to the wonder that he makes time for the singer. The chorus praises the God of earth and sky, calls his love unfailing and beautiful, and says he never changes and remains the Holy One and the singer's unfailing love. The second verse calls him rock and song. The chorus repeats. Nothing after the vow tells a saving act.

---

## Lines you cannot cut

“You never change, God, You remain / The Holy One” is the line the grade depends on. “God of grace and power,” “God of earth and sky,” and “You are my rock” belong with it. “You have my heart / And I am Yours forever” is a vow with no gospel under it in this lyric. “Still You make time for me” is the line that makes the God who holds all things sound busy. If the band loops the vow and the pre-chorus and drops the Holy One, the song is a personal pledge and a compliment. No line is a denial of a doctrine. Without “the Holy One” and “You never change,” little would remain that a sermon could use.

## Persons of the Trinity named

“God,” “the Holy One,” and “You” are the names. Father, Son, and Spirit are unnamed. “The Holy One” is a title Scripture uses for the LORD. The song does not say the Holy One of Israel, and it does not say the Son.

---

## Could another worshiper sing this unchanged?

Yes. A worshiper who believes in one great God can sing the whole text. “God of earth and sky,” “You never change,” “the Holy One,” “You are my rock,” and “Your unfailing love” do not require Jesus. “You have my heart / And I am Yours forever” is a vow any devout person can make to his own god. Nothing in the song would force a correction.

---

## Unbiblical, non-biblical, thin

- **Unbiblical:** none.
- **Non-biblical:** “Still You make time for me / I can't understand.” The God who holds all things is mindful of man, which is Psalm 8:4. “Make time” is a schedule. The line cannot be checked against a verse.
- **Thin:** “How beautiful is Your unfailing love,” “You are my song / And I sing for You,” and “God of grace and power.” Grace and power are named. The song never says what the power did or whom the grace forgave. “The Holy One and my unfailing love” can be sung as a title for God, the love that does not fail the singer. It can also be heard as the singer's own love, said to be unfailing. The first reading fits “You remain.” The second reading is a claim no Christian should make about his own affection.

---

## Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity: 3

“You never change” agrees with Malachi 3:6, “For I the LORD do not change.” “The Holy One” is the title in Isaiah 43:3, “the Holy One of Israel, your Savior.” The song keeps the title and does not keep “your Savior.” “You are my rock” agrees with Psalm 18:2, “The LORD is my rock.” “Everything You hold in Your hand” agrees with Psalm 95:4, “In his hand are the depths of the earth.” “You are my strength” and “God of grace and power” are true and general.

“You have my heart / And I am Yours forever” is a vow. It does not quote a verse. “Still You make time for me” takes the wonder of Psalm 8:4, “what is man that you are mindful of him,” and says it as a gap in a calendar. The chorus is accurate where it says he does not change and he is the Holy One. The vow and the pre-chorus are the reason the score stays at 3.

---

## Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated: 2

Jesus is not named. There is no cross, no blood, no resurrection, and no sin. “The Holy One” and a love that does not fail are true of God and, in this lyric, are not tied to the Son. The song is general praise and a personal vow. The cross is not in it.

---

## Objectivity vs. Subjectivity: 2

The chorus declares the God of earth and sky, the love that does not fail, the God who never changes, and the Holy One. That part is about him. The verses are the singer: “You have my heart,” “I am Yours forever,” “You are my rock / The One I hold on to,” “You are my song / And I sing for You.” “I am Yours forever” is a large promise about all the years ahead, and the song gives it no cross to stand on. “Still You make time for me / I can't understand” is the singer's surprise. The vow and the wonder decide the score.

---

## Description of God’s Character: 3

Grace, power, unfailing love, immutability, holiness, strength, and a rock. “You never change” and “the Holy One” are specific. Love is the title and the word the chorus repeats, so the weight falls there. “God of earth and sky” says he is God of the creation without saying what he has done in it. Justice, wrath, and mercy toward sinners are unnamed. The list is real, and it is a list of praise words around one doctrine the song actually says: he does not change.

---

## Congregational Usefulness and Clarity: 3

One use, in 2024, means it is not known here as a staple. The lines are easy to sing. A congregation can learn that God does not change and that he is the Holy One, and that his love is called unfailing. They also learn to promise “I am Yours forever” and to thank him for making time. The second lesson is the one that needs a pastor's care, because the promise has no gospel under it. “The Holy One” is the phrase worth keeping. It is one line in a song that mostly vows.

---

## Tone

The tone is affectionate wonder. The line that sets it is “Still You make time for me / I can't understand,” between the God who holds everything and the Holy One. “How beautiful is Your unfailing love” keeps the warmth. The Holy One line is sterner than the rest of the chorus, and the song does not stay there.

---

## What the congregation never says

The congregation never names Jesus, never says that sinners are forgiven by his blood, and never says that the love which does not fail was shown at the cross.

---

## Setlist notes

- **When it serves the sermon.** Malachi 3:6 or Isaiah 6, if “You never change” and “the Holy One” are actually sung and not skipped for the vow. It does not serve a sermon on the cross.
- **Gaps the rest of the hour must cover.** The Son and the cross. Pair it with *How Great You Are* or *Only a Holy God* so holiness is not left as a title beside a personal vow.
- **Diet sentence.** Once in this window, it adds an unchanging Holy One and a love called unfailing. It never adds the gospel.
- **What not to do.** Do not loop “You have my heart” or “make time for me.” Do not drop “You never change” and “the Holy One.”

## Why this overall label

The chorus tells the truth that God does not change and that he is the Holy One, and it calls his love unfailing. The verses promise him the singer's heart forever and marvel that he makes time, and the song never names Jesus or the cross. One hearing in 2024 does not make it a song to build a Sunday on.
