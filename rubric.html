How we grade

The rubric

Lyrics come first. Music serves the text. A report asks whether the song helps this church worship in spirit and in truth (John 4:24), lets the word of Christ dwell richly (Col. 3:16), and can be tested against Scripture the way the Bereans tested teaching (Acts 17:11).

This church

Berean Community Church in Irvine is a Southern Baptist congregation of about a thousand people. Preaching is expositional. The church holds the inerrancy and sufficiency of Scripture and reads it with a grammatical-historical hermeneutic. Members are trained to study the text for themselves. The first vision item is God-centered worship. Songs are chosen for doctrinal accuracy. Worship that does not engage the mind cannot rightly engage the heart. Objective meaning comes before personal application.

The theological stream is close to The Master’s Seminary: lordship salvation, the Doctrines of Grace, and the written Word over private impression. Those distinctives are a reading lens for lyrics. Song pages do not treat TMS as a brand. The banned ministries remain Hillsong, Elevation, and Bethel.

Guiding principles

Evaluation stays close to Bob Kauflin’s Worship Matters. A song should help the congregation remember, proclaim, and celebrate God’s worthiness, works, and Word. Affection grows when the lyric first tells the truth about God and the gospel. Prefer language that is God-centered, corporate, clear, and theologically substantial. Vague spirituality, and songs that live mainly in private feeling, sit lower. The Sunday gathering is the church at worship. Congregational usefulness here means the lyric can teach and admonish the body. A hook that works in a seeker room can still be thin for this one.

Red flags (Avoid, or heavy caution)

  • Prosperity gospel or health-and-wealth implications
  • Charismatic excess, or language that treats private revelation as weightier than the written Word
  • Romanticized “Jesus is my boyfriend” intimacy language
  • Minimizing of sin, repentance, or substitutionary atonement
  • A heavily man-centered core
  • Strong association with Hillsong, Elevation, or Bethel (these ministries are never used)
  • Vague spiritual language a non-Christian could sing without tension
  • Easy-believeism, or “Savior only” language that severs faith from repentance and lordship
  • Language that treats the Spirit as a private guide apart from the written Word
  • Invitation hooks that treat the gathered church mainly as an evangelistic crowd

Five scores (1–5 each)

Freedom from heresy is the floor. A lyric can be true and still score a 3 if it is thin.

  1. Theological Accuracy and Biblical Fidelity (highest weight). Does the song stay true to the whole counsel of Scripture? Read the lyric the way this church reads the Bible. A line that only sounds biblical is weaker than a line you can pin to a text in its ordinary sense. Impressionistic or allegorical “Bible-ish” poetry does not earn a 4 or 5.
  2. Christ-Exalting and Gospel-Saturated. Does the lyric hold both the person and the finished work of Christ? Titles without the cross, substitution, resurrection, or mediation usually land at 2 or 3.
  3. Objectivity vs. Subjectivity. Does the song mainly declare truths about God and Christ, or does it stay inside personal feeling?
  4. Description of God’s Character. Are the attributes balanced, or does love and feeling crowd out holiness, justice, and sovereignty?
  5. Congregational Usefulness and Clarity. Can the body sing it and also be taught by it? An easy hook is not enough for a 4 or 5.

What the numbers mean

  • 5 dense, precise, Scripture-shaped; models what this church wants to sing
  • 4 solid and usable; minor gaps, still teaches
  • 3 true but thin, vague, or incomplete; seeker-accessible more than Berean-rigorous
  • 2 serious omission or imbalance (missing gospel, or mushy poetry in place of doctrine)
  • 1 error, man-centered core, or disqualifying confusion

Use the full range. Do not inflate.

Overall recommendation

These five labels are the official titles on song pages. The chip colors match the catalog.

  • Excellent / Prefer Feature freely
    Gold-standard texts. Use them as the regular diet. Getty, CityAlight, Sovereign Grace, and hymns of that weight such as In Christ Alone, Before the Throne, Is He Worthy, and Yet Not I.
  • Strong Use regularly
    Rich enough for ordinary rotation. Small notes only.
  • Acceptable with notes Spare use only
    Orthodox and usable when the rest of the set supplies what this text lacks.
  • Weak Do not feature
    Popular, generically true, low rigor. At most a brief filler after Scripture has already done the work. How Great Is Our God (Tomlin / Cash / Reeves, CCLI 4348399) is the calibration example of Weak.
  • Avoid Do not use
    Red-flag theology or banned-ministry association.

If a song works mainly because it is easy, emotional, and broadly theistic, keep it at Weak or low Acceptable.

Alternative song suggestions

This section belongs only on a Weak or Avoid report. Leave it off Excellent / Prefer, Strong, and Acceptable with notes.

Name three or four songs that cover the theme the weak or banned title was reaching for. Mix titles the church has already logged with titles that are not in the catalog, so the section can also introduce a stronger text. New titles still stay inside usable families: hymn, modern hymn, Getty, CityAlight, Sovereign Grace, and other writers who are not Hillsong, Elevation, or Bethel. A matching theme does not make a banned-ministry song usable. Mark each suggestion as already sung or not yet logged.

For each suggestion, name the title and say what that lyric actually confesses. A hymn or a modern hymn may be the right replacement. The rubric does not prefer one era over another.

Worked example: the draft of Build My Life (CCLI 7070345) is Weak. It reaches for a unique Savior and a firm foundation, and never names the cross. Already sung: The Solid Rock and In Christ Alone. Not yet logged: Christ Is Mine Forevermore and The Church’s One Foundation. Living Hope (CCLI 7106807) is Avoid on the Bethel credit. A resurrection-hope Sunday would mix songs already sung, such as Christ Our Hope in Life and Death and Yet Not I, with a usable text the catalog does not have. Not Christ Is Risen.

How a report is written

Every song gets a written analysis, not only a score. Quote lyrics. Connect them to Scripture when the link is real. Include setlist notes: sermon placement, pairings, thematic strength. Say the claim in ordinary words. Do not explain the grade with an unusual metaphor.

Grade the published text of the catalog setting. The page becomes official when Theodore approves the scores and overall label and the catalog row is marked graded. A chord sheet is not required for that yes.

The report does not include a Status line. A published grade is the report on the song page. An unpublished grade is not shown.

Full lyrics stay off the site. CCLI covers congregational use, not a lyrics catalog here. Reports quote short lines for analysis. Use SongSelect or a printed chart for the complete text.
