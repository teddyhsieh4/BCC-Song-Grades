# Pattern catalog

Word lists go stale. Hunt the habit, not only the token.

Quoted lyrics are exempt. Identification fields (title, writers, CCLI, dates, slugs) are never filler.

## 1. Punctuation

- Em dash (`—`) and en dash used as an em dash
- Double hyphen (`--`) standing in for a dash
- Decorative symbols in authorial prose: `§` `¶` `→` `•` mid-sentence
- Curly quotes in the writer’s own sentences (straight quotes are fine)

Fix: period, comma, colon, parentheses, or a new sentence.

## 2. Negative parallelism and slogan pairs

Examples of the habit:

- It’s not X, it’s Y
- Not X, but Y
- This, not that
- Less a hammer, more a scalpel
- Not just X, but also Y

Fix: keep the true half. Drop the foil.

## 3. Banned cadence (authorial prose only)

Openers and glue: at its core, it’s important to note, it’s worth noting, in a world where, in today’s world, let’s dive in, here’s the thing, at the end of the day, in short, when it comes to, moreover, furthermore, additionally, notably.

Verbs and filler: unpacks, speaks into, leans into, sits with, holds space, invites us, breathes life, delve, leverage (verb), unlock, unleash, harness, foster, elevate.

Noun clichés: tapestry, rich tapestry, journey, landscape (figurative), realm, testament, cornerstone, paradigm, synergy, beacon.

Inflators: nothing short of, stands as a testament, deeply resonant, profoundly, robust yet accessible, pivotal, groundbreaking, transformative, seamless, holistic, myriad, plethora.

Fix: delete, or replace with the actual claim.

## 4. Structure tells

- Rule of three as a default (three examples, three beats, three “not only” clauses). Vary the count.
- Numbered scaffolding across sentences (“First… Second… Third…”) inside a paragraph. Report headings may stay numbered if the template uses them.
- Matched sentence twins of the same length and shape
- Wrap-up restatement that repeats the thesis under “In short”
- Significance inflation: a thin line treated as a legacy moment

Fix: cut one beat, join two sentences, or say the fact without the drumroll.

## 5. Telegram fragments

Three-word label sentences under a heading:

- The song teaches.
- Grammar is loose.
- That is an arrangement fact, not a lyric change.

Fix: write an ordinary paragraph. Mixed length.

## 6. Contrast-as-grade

Using another catalog title as a foil (“Unlike *In Christ Alone*, this song…”). Calibrate privately. Other titles only as setlist pairings.

## 7. What to keep

- Theological terms: substitution, propitiation, mediation, justification, sanctification, inerrancy, lordship, lament
- Ordinary biblical phrases, including “whosoever” and “for the world,” when the rest of the lyric is substitutionary
- Names, years, CCLI numbers, log counts
- Short quoted lines the grade needs
