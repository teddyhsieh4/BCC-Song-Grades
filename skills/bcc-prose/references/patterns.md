# Pattern catalog

Word lists go stale. Hunt the habit, not only the token.

Quoted lyrics are exempt. Identification fields (title, writers, CCLI, dates, slugs) are never filler.

## 1. Punctuation

- Em dash (`—`) and en dash used as an em dash
- Double hyphen (`--`) standing in for a dash
- Decorative symbols in authorial prose: `§` `¶` `→` `•` mid-sentence
- Curly quotes in the writer’s own sentences (straight quotes are fine)

Fix: period, comma, colon, parentheses, or a new sentence.

## 2. Negative parallelism and slogan pairs

Examples of the habit:

- It’s not X, it’s Y
- Not X, but Y
- This, not that
- Less a hammer, more a scalpel
- Not just X, but also Y

Fix: keep the true half. Drop the foil.

## 3. Banned cadence (authorial prose only)

Openers and glue: at its core, it’s important to note, it’s worth noting, in a world where, in today’s world, let’s dive in, here’s the thing, at the end of the day, in short, when it comes to, moreover, furthermore, additionally, notably.

Verbs and filler: unpacks, speaks into, leans into, sits with, holds space, invites us, breathes life, delve, leverage (verb), unlock, unleash, harness, foster, elevate.

Noun clichés: tapestry, rich tapestry, journey, landscape (figurative), realm, testament, cornerstone, paradigm, synergy, beacon.

Inflators: nothing short of, stands as a testament, deeply resonant, profoundly, robust yet accessible, pivotal, groundbreaking, transformative, seamless, holistic, myriad, plethora.

Fix: delete, or replace with the actual claim.

## 4. Structure tells

- Rule of three as a default (three examples, three beats, three “not only” clauses). Vary the count.
- Numbered scaffolding across sentences (“First… Second… Third…”) inside a paragraph. Report headings may stay numbered if the template uses them.
- Matched sentence twins of the same length and shape
- Wrap-up restatement that repeats the thesis under “In short”
- Significance inflation: a thin line treated as a legacy moment

Fix: cut one beat, join two sentences, or say the fact without the drumroll.

## 5. Telegram fragments

Three-word label sentences under a heading:

- The song teaches.
- Grammar is loose.
- That is an arrangement fact, not a lyric change.

Fix: write an ordinary paragraph. Mixed length.

## 6. Contrast-as-grade

Using another catalog title as a foil (“Unlike *In Christ Alone*, this song…”). Calibrate privately. Other titles only as setlist pairings.

## 7. Habits found across the published reports (Nov 2026 review)

These showed up in many reports at once. Each one reads fine in a single report. Across the catalog they make every song page sound the same.

**Stock sentences reused from report to report.** Examples:

- “It does not ask the room to confer on God what He already possesses.”
- “The language is plain.” / “The diction is plain.”
- “Singability is high and the teaching is the reason for the 5.”
- “The intensity stays inside the …”
- “The attributes are specific and tied to …”
- “The lines stay inside ordinary biblical sense.”
- “That is dense, precise, and Scripture-shaped.” / “That is why it is Excellent / Prefer.”
- “Those are small gaps.”
- “The claims are confessional and shareable.”
- “A vague theist cannot sing those lines without correction.”

Fix: say what is true of this lyric, or cut the sentence. If it would be true of most songs in the catalog, it tells the leader nothing.

**The doctrine list restated under every heading.** The Thesis names four doctrines, and then each later section names the same four in the same order. Fix: name them once (Thesis and gospel inventory). Later sections say only what is new for that heading.

**Contrast with the “not” second.** “The line is about God as He is, not about the worshiper’s feelings.” “The hymn is not anti-Trinitarian. It is simply a hymn to the Son.” Nobody raised the wrong reading. Fix: keep the true half. Keep the contrast only when it corrects a real mix-up, such as two songs with similar titles or two CCLI numbers.

**A sentence that lands on a bare auxiliary.** “Stanza 3 does.” “Burial is not.” “This church does not.” Fix: finish the sentence (“Stanza 3 completes it.”).

**Anaphora lists.** Four or more sentences in a row that open the same way (“They never confess … They never say … They never name …”). Fix: one list sentence.

**Checklist sentences.** Runs of sentences that each end “is named,” “is stated,” “is explicit,” or “is absent.” The gospel inventory table already holds that. Fix: say what the lyric does with the doctrine.

**Lists written as separate sentences.** “Doctrine of God. Creation. Exodus 3. Psalm 90.” Fix: use commas.

**Metaphors for the grade.** “Inner weather,” “true and underfed,” “sink the song,” “goes soft,” “smuggle in,” “floating free,” “flattens,” “carry” (a song carrying a doctrine), “stays inside,” “sits on,” “leans.” Fix: the plain word. “The singer’s feelings.” “True but thin.” “No line is false.” “The line is vague.”

**Coined terms.** “High-claim,” “life-project.” Fix: say what the lyric claims.

**“Follows the ordinary sense of” on every reference.** Fix: give the reference (“is Isaiah 53:10”). The rubric’s standard does not need repeating in each sentence.

**One-word openers under a heading.** “Reverent.” “No.” “One song.” “Mixed.” This is the telegram habit in section 5. Fix: start with a full sentence.

## 8. What to keep

- Theological terms: substitution, propitiation, mediation, justification, sanctification, inerrancy, lordship, lament
- Ordinary biblical phrases, including “whosoever” and “for the world,” when the rest of the lyric is substitutionary
- Names, years, CCLI numbers, log counts
- Short quoted lines the grade needs
